Cremation Costs in Miami, Florida (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Florida Cremation Rate: 62%

Miami is the most culturally and linguistically diverse cremation market in the country, and the pricing reflects that. Caballero Rivero — the largest Hispanic funeral home chain in South Florida — and Van Orsdel Family Funeral Chapels dominate the Miami-Dade flat-rate cremation space, while the Neptune Memorial Reef three miles off Key Biscayne offers a disposition option that exists nowhere else: an artificial-reef cremation burial that doubles as a scuba-diver destination. Direct cremation in Miami averages $2,250 — $450 above the Florida state median.

National context: the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA 2024 Annual Report put the U.S. cremation rate near 60.5 percent, and Miami providers operate under the oversight of the Florida Board of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services. Families ordering death certificates typically deal with the Miami-Dade County Health Department Vital Statistics office.

Miami's $2,250 direct-cremation figure is $450 above the Florida statewide average — the widest metro-to-state gap in the state. Higher Miami-Dade labor costs, limited crematory capacity in the urban core, and the Cuban- and Haitian-American preference for a full-service Catholic tradition all push base pricing up. Families looking for the Florida median typically need to call providers in Broward or Palm Beach, not in Miami proper.

Neptune Memorial Reef: Miami's Artificial-Reef Cremation Burial

Three and a quarter miles off the coast of Key Biscayne, 40 feet down, the Neptune Memorial Reef has since 2007 served as a permitted underwater mausoleum where cremated remains are mixed with a marine-grade concrete formula and cast into structures — columns, lions, gates, benches — that form a functioning artificial reef. Placement runs from roughly $1,000 for a mixed-remains placement to the mid-four-figures for named, individually located placements. The reef is open to scuba divers; families routinely schedule commemoration dives on anniversaries. Caballero Rivero and Van Orsdel both coordinate Neptune placements as an add-on to direct cremation, and the combined out-the-door cost typically lands under the full-service cremation figure. No other US cremation market offers this.

Florida Division of Funeral, Cemetery and Consumer Services

Florida regulates cremation through the Division of Funeral, Cemetery and Consumer Services rather than an independent board — the structure is housed inside the Department of Financial Services. The Division publishes the statewide licensure roster, investigates complaints, and enforces Florida's cremation authorization, identification, and crematory holding-period rules. Miami's provider density makes Division-level enforcement practical here in a way it is not in the Panhandle; complaints against Miami-Dade providers are typically resolved within 90 days of filing.

Miami's Cuban- and Haitian-American Cremation Shift

Historically, Miami's Cuban- and Haitian-American communities leaned strongly toward traditional Catholic burial, but the last fifteen years have brought a demographic shift in both communities toward cremation — driven in part by the cost pressure of urban cemetery plots in Hialeah and Little Havana. Caballero Rivero's Spanish-language visitation and rosary packages paired with cremation now represent the majority of their cremation revenue, a reversal from two decades ago. Haitian-American families still lean toward the full-service format, but cremation with a separate chapel visitation has displaced much of the market that once went to sealed-casket burial.

DCF Funeral Assistance for Low-Income Florida Residents

Florida's Department of Children and Families administers a narrow funeral benefit for eligible low-income residents, typically paying up to $1,000 toward direct cremation. The program covers Miami-Dade applicants, and most major Miami providers — including Caballero Rivero and Van Orsdel — accept DCF vouchers and bill the department directly. Eligibility is tied to state assistance status at the time of death, and applications go through DCF field offices.

Ocean Scattering Beyond Three Miles

Atlantic scattering beyond three nautical miles falls under EPA burial-at-sea rules, and dozens of Miami charter operators run scattering trips out of Key Biscayne, Virginia Key, and Miami Beach marinas. Biscayne National Park requires a Superintendent's permit for in-park scattering. The Intracoastal Waterway is not recommended for scattering — it is not the open ocean as defined by EPA rules, and some municipalities prohibit it outright.

Questions Worth Asking First

How much does cremation cost in Miami?

Miami direct cremation averages $2,250 through Caballero Rivero, Van Orsdel, and the independent Miami-Dade houses. Full-service cremation averages $7,750 — a figure that reflects the Cuban- and Haitian-American preference for Catholic visitation packages. The Miami pricing sits $450 above the Florida state median because of urban labor costs and limited crematory capacity in the city core.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Miami?

Yes. Direct cremation at $2,250 runs roughly $7,500 below a traditional Miami burial once you count the Woodlawn, Flagler Memorial, or Vista Memorial plot, plus vault and monument. Miami-Dade cemetery plot pricing has climbed faster than most Florida metros, widening the gap between cremation and burial year over year. Even full-service cremation at $7,750 comes in below a traditional burial.

Can I scatter ashes in Miami?

Atlantic scattering beyond three nautical miles is governed by EPA burial-at-sea rules — charter operators out of Key Biscayne, Virginia Key, and Miami Beach run family scattering trips. Biscayne National Park requires a Superintendent's permit for in-park scattering. Private land is straightforward with owner permission. Neptune Memorial Reef offers a fundamentally different alternative: permanent placement of remains in an artificial reef, open to scuba diving families on anniversaries.
