Burial Costs in Omaha, Nebraska (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Nebraska

Omaha carries a modest premium over the rest of Nebraska — about 10 percent on every major line — and funnels that premium through two large historic cemeteries and a handful of denominational grounds. Forest Lawn Memorial Park on North 48th Street and the landmark Prospect Hill Cemetery serve as reference points for the metro. A full Omaha burial in 2026 averages between $13,640 and $19,151, with a $7,370 funeral service and a $3,300 plot at a typical commercial cemetery.

Forest Lawn, Prospect Hill, and the metro map

  • Forest Lawn Memorial Park (North 48th Street). Founded 1885; the largest active cemetery in the Omaha metro, with a mix of traditional lawn sections, private estates, and a mausoleum complex.
  • Prospect Hill Cemetery. Omaha's oldest, dating to 1858, known for its 19th-century monuments and its role in the city's civic history. Inventory is limited and often restricted to existing lot owners.
  • Calvary Cemetery and Holy Sepulchre Cemetery. Catholic grounds of the Archdiocese of Omaha. Calvary at North 78th Street is the principal active Catholic cemetery.
  • Mount Hope Cemetery and Evergreen Memorial Park Cemetery. Mid-tier commercial grounds serving West Omaha and Bellevue.

Nebraska ADC Burial Assistance and how it reaches Omaha families

Nebraska's Aid to the Aged, Blind, and Disabled (ADC) program — administered through the Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services — includes a Burial Assistance component for qualifying recipients. Amounts are modest and paid directly to the funeral provider, and a family-contribution cap limits how much can be added to the service while remaining eligible. The HHS office in Douglas County handles most Omaha-area cases; ask the funeral director to coordinate the application, which must be filed promptly after the death.

Omaha National Cemetery and veteran benefits

Omaha National Cemetery in Sarpy County, a comparatively new VA national cemetery that opened in 2016, provides full interment benefits at no cost for eligible veterans and their spouses — plot, opening and closing, government marker, perpetual care. This cemetery has been an important addition for the metro because it consolidated veteran burial options previously split between smaller Nebraska grounds and more distant national cemeteries.

Winter interments on the Missouri River bluffs

Omaha cemeteries handle winter burials more routinely than rural northern Nebraska grounds — most metro cemeteries have the equipment to open graves through the winter. A cold-weather surcharge is not universal but is worth asking about, particularly at the older cemeteries with heavier landscape work. If the ground is temporarily unworkable, Forest Lawn and Calvary both maintain temporary vault storage.

Where an Omaha burial total bends

  • Call Dodge, Sarpy, and Douglas County township clerks. Nebraska's township cemeteries outside the immediate metro frequently price plots at $500 to $1,500 — the same land but a different administrative structure.
  • Independent Omaha funeral homes. Several long-established independents in Omaha still run below the chain-owned firms for the same package. Request GPLs by phone and compare directly.
  • Third-party casket. The FTC Funeral Rule covers every Omaha funeral home. An online casket delivered to the funeral home can reduce the casket line by 50 to 70 percent. Casket buying guide
  • Ask about liner versus vault. Forest Lawn and Calvary both accept concrete liners; the sealed-vault upgrade is optional at most price tiers.
  • Omaha National for veterans. Eligibility handled by the VA; the cemetery absorbs the plot, opening, and marker.
HHS burial assistance timing: Nebraska ADC Burial Assistance requires prompt filing after the death. Have the funeral director initiate the paperwork — waiting can foreclose the benefit.

Questions Families Ask

How much does burial cost in Omaha?

A traditional Omaha burial in 2026 runs $13,640 to $19,151 once every bill is on the table — $7,370 for the funeral service, $3,300 for a plot at a metro cemetery, $2,211 for a casket, $1,320 for a vault, $1,650 for opening and closing, and $1,500 to $3,500 for a marker. Outside Douglas and Sarpy counties, plots at township cemeteries regularly price under $1,000.

What are the cheapest burial options in Omaha?

A township cemetery plot outside the metro paired with a direct burial is the least expensive route for most families. A third-party casket delivered to the funeral home under the FTC Funeral Rule reduces the funeral-home portion further. Eligible veterans interred at Omaha National Cemetery avoid the plot, opening, and marker lines entirely.

How do Omaha burial costs compare to Nebraska?

The Omaha metro runs roughly 10 percent above the Nebraska state average. A metro funeral service averages $7,370 against $6,700 statewide, and the typical plot runs $3,300 against $3,000. Rural Nebraska grounds, especially in the Sandhills and along the western counties, often remain well below the statewide average.
