Burial Costs in New Hampshire (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Northeast

New Hampshire's per-capita cemetery count is one of the highest in the country — nearly every town has its own municipal or historic burial ground, many dating to the 1700s. That density is the reason Granite State burial pricing, while premium by any absolute measure at $17,160 to $24,200, still sits below most of southern New England. The town cemeteries across Hillsborough, Rockingham, and Merrimack counties keep the commercial memorial parks honest in ways they cannot in Massachusetts.

Embalming ($880) is not required under New Hampshire law when burial occurs promptly. Winter deaths add a frozen-ground or vault-storage line item almost invariably between mid-December and April.

Manchester, Concord, and the Town Cemeteries

Manchester's Valley Cemetery on Willow Street (established 1841) and Pine Grove Cemetery are the two most-used commercial grounds in the state's largest city. Concord centers on Blossom Hill Cemetery on North State Street, active since 1860. Portsmouth uses South Cemetery on South Street and the adjacent Proprietors Burying Ground. But the vast majority of New Hampshire burials happen at town cemeteries — Derry, Salem, Londonderry, Bedford, Nashua, Dover, Keene — each operated by the municipality with resident pricing that typically runs $1,500-$2,500 below commercial parks. The New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery in Boscawen, opened in 1997, handles eligible-veteran burials at no cost for veterans and reduced cost for spouses.

Winter Ground in Granite

Frost depth across New Hampshire runs 48-60 inches through most of the central and northern parts of the state, which means cemetery ground crews pause digging from roughly mid-December through early April. Funeral homes store remains in refrigeration (typically $40-$80 per day) or at the cemetery vault, and many families do not realize that an early-January death may produce a winter-storage invoice that does not appear on the original service estimate. Always ask, in writing, whether the quoted opening-and-closing assumes a winter hold and what the per-day charge runs.

Who Regulates the Industry

The New Hampshire Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers, housed within the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification in Concord, licenses every funeral home, director, and embalmer in the state. Pre-need contracts are regulated separately under RSA 325 and require 100% trust placement of pre-need funds — one of the stronger consumer protections in the country. The FTC Funeral Rule applies statewide: the General Price List, Casket Price List, and Outer Burial Container Price List must all be disclosed at the start of any in-person price discussion, and outside caskets must be accepted at no handling fee.

Financial Assistance

The New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services administers Burial Assistance for indigent decedents — the benefit is modest ($750 toward service, $250 toward interment) and subject to income eligibility, but it can combine with funeral-home accommodations. Town welfare offices have separate authority under RSA 165 to handle burials when no family can pay, and practice varies significantly by municipality. Veterans access the New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery in Boscawen at no cost. For families who can plan ahead, burial insurance or a pre-need contract (with 100% trust placement under state law) is one of the few states where pre-need protection is strong enough to rely on.

Where to Actually Save Money

  • Use the town cemetery when residency qualifies: Town-run cemeteries in places like Bedford, Nashua, Dover, and Derry typically price resident plots $1,500-$2,500 below commercial memorial parks. Residency rules vary — check current ordinance.
  • Ask about winter charges in writing: A February burial in New Hampshire can carry 10-12 weeks of vault or refrigerated storage before the ground opens. Confirm whether that is inside or outside the original quote.

Common Questions We Hear

What is the full price of burial in New Hampshire?

Adding every line — $8,800 service, $4,400 plot, $2,640 casket, $1,760 vault, $2,200 opening-and-closing, $1,000-$3,000 marker — a New Hampshire burial runs $17,160 to $24,200. Winter deaths can add $1,500-$3,500 in vault-storage fees before the ground opens in April, so the effective range in January and February often exceeds the nominal one.

Are burial vaults mandatory in New Hampshire?

State law does not mandate a vault, but frost heave through 48-60 inches of New Hampshire soil gives the cemeteries a practical reason to require one or a concrete grave liner. Every municipal and commercial cemetery in the state enforces this. The liner substitute is usually acceptable and is several hundred dollars cheaper — get the policy in writing from the specific cemetery.

What are the cheapest burial options in New Hampshire?

Three levers matter most: buy into your town's municipal cemetery where residency qualifies (resident pricing is typically $1,500-$2,500 below commercial parks in Manchester and Concord); buy the casket from an outside retailer the funeral home must accept at no handling fee under the FTC Funeral Rule; and choose direct burial at an independent funeral home — a direct burial in New Hampshire regularly clears under $4,500 all-in, memorial service held separately.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in New Hampshire?

The state's density of town-run cemeteries is the key unlock. Call the municipal clerk in the town where burial will happen and ask for the cemetery sexton's pricing sheet. Then compare against the commercial memorial parks and — if eligible — the New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery in Boscawen. Cemeteries are not covered by the FTC Funeral Rule, so ask in writing for plot, opening-and-closing, vault-or-liner policy, winter hold charges, perpetual care, and any residency restriction.

What is an all-in burial estimate for New Hampshire?

Full arithmetic: service ($8,800) + plot ($4,400) + casket ($2,640) + vault ($1,760) + opening-and-closing ($2,200) + marker ($1,000-$3,000) = $17,160 to $24,200. The one variable not captured in that list is winter storage, which runs $40-$80 per day and can accumulate over four months between December and April for inland New Hampshire burials.

Is green burial available in New Hampshire?

Yes. New Hampshire has several cemeteries with dedicated green burial sections, most notably in the Monadnock region and the Upper Valley, plus a handful of town cemeteries that permit biodegradable-container burial without embalming in older sections. State law permits home funeral care and gives families substantial flexibility on timing. A genuine green burial in New Hampshire typically totals well below the conventional $17,160-$24,200 range.
