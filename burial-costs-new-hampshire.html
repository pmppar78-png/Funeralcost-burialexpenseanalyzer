Burial Costs in New Hampshire (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: Northeast

New Hampshire's burial traditions are deeply woven into the fabric of New England's oldest communities. Nearly every town in the Granite State maintains at least one historic cemetery — some dating back to the 1600s — alongside modern memorial parks serving the growing southern tier. With rocky soil that can make grave excavation challenging and a climate that limits winter burials, New Hampshire families face unique practical considerations alongside moderate-to-high burial costs. This guide covers the specific pricing, cemetery options, and consumer rights that apply to burial in New Hampshire.

What Drives Burial Costs in New Hampshire

New Hampshire's burial costs are shaped by the state's geology, seasonal weather, and New England traditions. The Granite State earns its name — rocky, ledge-filled soil in many areas makes grave excavation significantly more labor-intensive and expensive than in states with softer ground. Some cemeteries must use specialized rock-breaking equipment, which adds to opening and closing fees.

Seasonal challenges also affect pricing. New Hampshire winters bring deep frost from late November through early April, and many cemeteries either do not perform winter burials or charge substantial surcharges ($500-$1,000 or more) for the additional equipment and labor required. Some families choose temporary vault storage at the funeral home with burial scheduled for spring.

Cemetery plot prices vary by location. Cemeteries in Nashua, Manchester, and Concord charge $3,000 to $8,000 or more for plots, while small-town cemeteries in the Lakes Region, White Mountains, and North Country often price plots between $1,000 and $3,000. Many New Hampshire towns maintain their cemeteries through town budgets, keeping costs lower for residents.

Cemetery Types Available in New Hampshire

  • Town cemeteries — Almost every New Hampshire town operates at least one municipal cemetery, managed by elected cemetery trustees. These are often the most affordable option and reflect centuries of local tradition. Pine Hill Cemetery in Dover and Blossom Hill Cemetery in Concord are prominent examples.
  • Historic colonial-era cemeteries — Many New Hampshire towns maintain cemeteries dating to the 1700s with slate and granite headstones. While most are no longer accepting new burials, some expanded sections remain active.
  • Catholic cemeteries — The Diocese of Manchester operates several cemeteries across the state, including Mount Calvary in Manchester, serving New Hampshire's significant Catholic community.
  • Veterans cemeteries — The New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery in Boscawen provides free burial for eligible veterans, their spouses, and dependent children. It is one of the most actively used state veterans cemeteries in New England.
  • Memorial parks — Modern lawn-style memorial parks in the southern tier offer flat markers, mausoleum crypts, and cremation gardens at higher price points.

Casket and Vault Options

New Hampshire funeral homes charge an average of $2,640 for caskets — above the national average, reflecting the state's higher cost of living. Families can save $1,000 or more by purchasing caskets from online retailers or warehouse-style sellers. The FTC Funeral Rule guarantees that all New Hampshire funeral homes must accept third-party caskets without charging handling fees.

Most New Hampshire cemeteries require a burial vault or grave liner. Given the rocky soil and harsh freeze-thaw cycles, vaults play a practical role in preventing grave settling and ground heaving. Concrete grave liners start around $800, while protective vaults with sealing features range from $1,500 to $10,000. Some smaller rural cemeteries may waive the vault requirement on a case-by-case basis.

Consumer Rights for Burial in New Hampshire

New Hampshire families are protected by both federal and state regulations:

  • FTC Funeral Rule — All New Hampshire funeral homes must provide itemized General Price Lists. You cannot be required to buy package deals and can select only the services you need.
  • NH RSA 325 — New Hampshire's funeral directors and embalmers are regulated under state statute, overseen by the NH Board of Registration of Funeral Directors and Embalmers.
  • Embalming not required — New Hampshire does not require embalming by law. If refrigeration is available, embalming can be declined even if there will be a delay before burial.
  • Home funeral rights — New Hampshire law allows families to care for their own dead and arrange home funerals without a funeral director, provided proper documentation is filed.
  • Preneed protections — New Hampshire requires preneed funeral funds to be held in trust, with consumer rights to cancel and receive refunds under specific conditions outlined in state law.
  • Cemetery trustee oversight — Town cemetery trustees in New Hampshire have statutory authority to set rules and fees for town cemeteries, providing local accountability.

Green and Natural Burial in New Hampshire

New Hampshire has shown growing interest in green burial, consistent with the state's independent, environmentally conscious ethos. While there are no Green Burial Council-certified cemeteries in the state as of 2026, several town cemeteries have established or are developing natural burial sections that allow biodegradable caskets or shroud burials without vaults. New Hampshire's home funeral movement is also active, with organizations like the Funeral Consumers Alliance of New Hampshire advocating for families' rights to simpler, more environmentally friendly burial options. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in New Hampshire?

A traditional burial in New Hampshire costs approximately $8,800 for the funeral service plus $4,400 for a cemetery plot. With a vault ($800-$10,000), headstone ($500-$5,000+), and grave opening fees ($800-$2,500), total costs typically range from $17,160 to $24,200. Southern New Hampshire near the Boston metro is most expensive.

Is a burial vault required in New Hampshire?

New Hampshire has no state law requiring burial vaults, but most cemeteries require them as a matter of policy. The state's rocky soil and harsh freeze-thaw cycles make vaults particularly important for preventing ground settling and heaving. Some smaller rural cemeteries may have more flexible policies.

What are the cheapest burial options in New Hampshire?

Direct burial in a small-town cemetery — without viewing, embalming, or a formal service — is the most affordable option. Combined with an online-purchased casket, total costs can range from $6,000 to $10,000. Green burial using a simple shroud or pine casket saves additional costs where cemeteries permit it.

Does rocky soil affect burial costs in New Hampshire?

Yes. New Hampshire's granite bedrock and rocky soil make grave excavation more difficult and expensive than in states with softer ground. Some cemetery locations require specialized rock-breaking or drilling equipment, which can add hundreds of dollars to the opening and closing fee. Always confirm these fees with your specific cemetery.

Can I arrange a home funeral in New Hampshire?

Yes. New Hampshire law allows families to handle funeral arrangements, including washing and dressing the body, conducting home vigils, and transporting remains to the cemetery, all without hiring a funeral director. A death certificate must be properly filed. The Funeral Consumers Alliance of New Hampshire can provide guidance for families interested in this option.
