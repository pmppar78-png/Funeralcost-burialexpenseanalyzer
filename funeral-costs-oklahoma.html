What Drives Funeral Costs in Oklahoma

Oklahoma law shapes the bill in one specific way. Under 59 O.S. §396.12b, every funeral must be carried out under the personal supervision of a licensed funeral director from the first call until interment, and embalming may only be done by a licensed embalmer. That rule does not apply to relatives by blood or marriage who handle a burial themselves, which leaves room for a family-directed service. Paid supervision is still the norm, so the quote you receive reflects local competition and facility costs. Oklahoma sits in the South, where pricing generally runs below the US median of $8,300, and metro and rural homes in the state often differ by 10-30%.

At 45%, Oklahoma's cremation rate is 18.4 points below the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

Your Consumer Rights in Oklahoma

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Oklahoma must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Oklahoma has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What's Specific to Oklahoma (South)

Homegoing services, repast meals, and strong church involvement remain part of Oklahoma funeral culture, and many congregations will cover or reduce the service-venue portion of the cost for members. When pricing a traditional funeral in Oklahoma, ask explicitly whether the quote assumes the service at the funeral home versus at a church; the latter often drops the facility fee by $500-$1,500.

Payment Assistance in Oklahoma

  • Oklahoma county commissioners, acting as overseers of the indigent, arrange the burial, and the county treasurer pays the grave opening and closing on their order (56 O.S. § 54).
  • To collect the one-time $255 from Social Security, an Oklahoma widow, widower or eligible child must call 1-800-772-1213, and the claim has to be made within two years.
  • For an Oklahoma veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. Funeral rules and help in Oklahoma

The Oklahoma Funeral Board licenses funeral directors and establishments in Oklahoma; call 405-522-1790 to check a license or file a complaint.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Oklahoma among the nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so an Oklahoma family may arrange much of a funeral itself. The Oklahoma death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

Cremation timing: Oklahoma sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (63 O.S. § 1-329.1).

Oklahoma has no statewide funeral payment; the county commissioners, acting as overseers of the indigent, must arrange and supervise the burial of a person who dies in the county without means, and the county treasurer pays necessary grave opening and closing costs on their order (56 O.S. § 54). (Source: 56 O.S. § 54.) The $255 Social Security lump sum goes only to an eligible spouse or child and must be claimed within two years; it does not go to the Oklahoma funeral home.

An Oklahoma funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Fort Gibson National Cemetery (918-478-2334) or Fort Sill National Cemetery (580-492-3200) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The tribal veterans cemeteries, Seaman First Class Billy Turner Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 580-490-6489), Seminole Nation and Veterans Memorial Cemetery (tribal, 405-295-4549), or White Eagle Cemetery (tribal, 580-762-8104), take requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in Oklahoma

  1. Ask every Oklahoma funeral home for its General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule says it must give you one when you ask in person.
  2. Consider an Oklahoma memorial service after cremation or burial, which avoids embalming and viewing fees.
  3. Call the Oklahoma board of county commissioners about indigent burial before contracting with a funeral home.
  4. If the person served, have the funeral home ask about Fort Gibson National Cemetery before you buy a private plot.
  5. Report a funeral home that hides or ignores its price list to the board at 405-522-1790.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in Oklahoma right now?

Current averages in Oklahoma come in at $6,100 for a traditional funeral, $5,000 for cremation with a service, and from $1,400 for direct cremation, with the traditional figure sitting 27% below the $8,300 US median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $2,700 when an Oklahoma family chooses burial.

Does cremation save money compared with burial in Oklahoma?

A direct cremation in Oklahoma runs about $1,400, whereas a traditional funeral with burial averages $6,100 before adding the plot, so the gap can easily exceed $4,700. Oklahoma's cremation rate of 45% sits below the 63.4% national rate NFDA projected for 2025.

What are my consumer rights at Oklahoma funeral homes?

The federal FTC Funeral Rule gives Oklahoma families the right to an itemized price list, to prices over the phone, and to buy only the goods and services they want. Oklahoma is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. The Oklahoma Funeral Board, 405-522-1790, licenses Oklahoma funeral homes and takes complaints.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Oklahoma?

Three moves cut the most: compare at least three Oklahoma General Price Lists side by side before choosing a provider, strip packages down to only the services you actually want, and choose direct cremation ($1,400) if cost is the priority.

Is embalming legally required in Oklahoma?

Not in Oklahoma when the body is refrigerated instead, though the time limits are short. The Funeral Board's rule (Okla. Admin. Code 235:10-11-1) requires an unembalmed body to be buried or cremated within 24 hours of death unless it is kept at 40°F or less, and once it leaves refrigeration it must be embalmed or disposed of within eight hours. The rule also bars public viewing of an unembalmed body more than 24 hours after death.

So a viewing held more than a day after death means paying for embalming, about $610 on our Oklahoma estimate, while direct cremation or a prompt burial does not. The Oklahoma Funeral Board (405-522-1790) licenses the homes that charge for it.

How do funeral costs in Oklahoma compare to other states?

Oklahoma's traditional-funeral average of $6,100 sits 27% below the $8,300 national median, and the South region overall trends toward moderate to lower pricing than the rest of the country.

Which funeral option costs the least in Oklahoma?

Direct cremation carries the lowest price tag in Oklahoma, from roughly $1,400. Direct burial is the next step up at about $3,050, which is $3,050 below a traditional Oklahoma funeral.
