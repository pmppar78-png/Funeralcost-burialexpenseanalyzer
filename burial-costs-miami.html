Burial Costs in Miami, Florida (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Florida

Miami is one of the most cremation-heavy metros in the country — roughly three-quarters of Miami-Dade dispositions are cremations, well above the Florida state rate. That context matters when you are asking a funeral home about burial, because burial volume is smaller, cemetery land is scarcer, and the full-service Miami burial now runs $18,300 to $25,725 for a Cuban-American, Haitian-American, or Anglo Miami family choosing a traditional in-ground interment.

The gap between $18,300 and $25,725 mostly reflects cemetery choice. Woodlawn Park North on Calle Ocho, Miami Memorial Park in Doral, and Our Lady of Mercy (the Catholic cemetery on NW 25th Street) each price differently, and the difference shows up most on the plot and interment lines.

Why Miami Burial Costs Sit Above the Florida Average

Miami pricing runs meaningfully above the statewide average — $9,750 funeral service versus $7,800, and $4,500 plot versus $3,600. Two forces drive that gap. First, cemetery land in Miami-Dade is some of the scarcest in Florida; Woodlawn Park and the Catholic cemeteries opened new sections slowly because existing acreage near the urban core is already full. Second, traditional-burial volume has thinned as cremation took over the local market, which means the fixed costs of running a funeral home spread across fewer burial cases. The Funeral Consumers Alliance of South Florida tracks these disparities; the local FCA chapter is a useful starting point before committing to a provider.

Above-Ground Entombment Is the Florida Norm for a Reason

Miami-Dade's high water table and porous limestone make above-ground entombment a practical alternative, not a luxury one. Most Miami cemeteries offer mausoleum crypts in addition to ground plots, and the math sometimes favors the crypt — you skip the vault entirely. When requesting cemetery quotes, ask for both the single-grave plot price and the single-crypt price. Families often discover the crypt is only modestly more expensive once the vault line is subtracted.

Disposition Paths Miami Families Actually Choose: Woodlawn Park, Caballero Rivero, and Beyond

  • Traditional burial — Viewing, service, casket, vault, ground interment. Still the pattern many Cuban-American and Catholic families default to.
  • Direct burial — Burial without viewing, embalming, or facility ceremony. Priced separately at most Miami funeral homes.
  • Mausoleum entombment — The South Florida practical option. Skips the vault, priced competitively with ground burial at many Miami cemeteries.
  • Green burial — Limited within Miami-Dade; available at Prairie Creek in the Ocala area and a handful of North Florida grounds.
Casket sourcing: Under the FTC Funeral Rule, Miami funeral homes must accept a casket from an online retailer without a handling fee. Compare online casket retailers.

Where Miami Families Actually Cut the Bill

  • Request the crypt quote alongside the plot quote. In Miami's high-water-table terrain, a mausoleum crypt can undercut a ground plot once vault cost is included.
  • Compare two Catholic and two commercial cemeteries. Our Lady of Mercy, Woodlawn Park, and a Doral or Miami Gardens commercial park will return meaningfully different itemized sheets.
  • Drive to Broward or Homestead. Plots in Dade Memorial Park south of Homestead or Lauderdale Memorial Gardens routinely run below Miami-Dade urban cemeteries.
  • Unbundle the casket and monument. Independent retailers save 50 to 70 percent on caskets; monument dealers typically beat cemetery in-house pricing on markers.
  • Compare at least two funeral homes. Miami's Cuban-American, Haitian-American, and Anglo funeral homes each price traditional service differently; a single quote rarely reveals the floor.

Reader Questions

How much does burial cost in Miami?

A Miami traditional burial totals between $18,300 and $25,725 in 2026 — notably above the Florida average. Funeral service near $9,750, plot $4,500, casket $2,925, vault $1,800, opening and closing $2,250, marker $1,500 to $3,000. Miami-Dade's scarce cemetery land and thinned traditional-burial volume drive the premium.

Is a burial vault required in Miami?

Florida law does not require an outer burial container. Most Miami cemeteries require one by internal policy because of the area's high water table. A sealed vault is common; a concrete grave liner is sometimes accepted at roughly half the cost. Confirm with Woodlawn Park, Our Lady of Mercy, or whichever cemetery you are considering — policies vary.

What are the cheapest burial options in Miami?

Direct burial arranged through a Miami funeral home skips embalming, viewing, and facility charges. A mausoleum crypt often beats a ground plot once the vault cost is removed — South Florida's high water table makes entombment more than a luxury choice. Driving to Homestead or Broward drops plot pricing. Sourcing the casket independently cuts another 50 to 70 percent off showroom prices.

How do Miami burial costs compare to Florida?

Miami runs roughly 25 percent above the statewide average — $9,750 funeral service versus $7,800, $4,500 plot versus $3,600. Scarce cemetery land in Miami-Dade and a cremation-heavy local market that spreads funeral home fixed costs across fewer burial cases together explain the premium. Jacksonville, Tampa, and Orlando all price closer to the state average than Miami does.
