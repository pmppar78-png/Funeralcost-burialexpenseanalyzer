Cremation Costs in South Carolina (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: South Carolina cremation guide rewritten around the coroner's permit and county funding, replacing a vague scattering answer and an unsourced discount figure. Cremation Rate: 45%

A South Carolina cremation costs about $1,600 by our estimate when there is no service and about $5,400 when a funeral comes first, a $3,800 difference. Roughly 45% of deaths in the state end in cremation. This page explains what the money buys at each level, the coroner's permit and what counties charge for it, the 24-hour wait, and where to turn when the family cannot pay.

Planning Your Own Cremation in South Carolina

About 45% of South Carolina deaths now end in cremation, still under the national rate, and the savings against a traditional burial usually run $5,100 or more. You can settle the question yourself in advance: state law lets a person sign a cremation authorization form for their own cremation, revocable at any time by written notice to both the funeral home and the crematory, and whoever holds that form at death must see that its instructions are followed (S.C. Code § 32-8-315).

Before You Sign in South Carolina

When considering cremation in South Carolina, keep these important points in mind:

  • Start with the General Price List. Its direct cremation line must spell out what is included, and that should be the transfer, the cremation and a simple container for the ashes.
  • No casket is needed for a direct cremation in South Carolina; under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR 453.3(b)) the provider must let the family use an unfinished wood box or another alternative container.
  • Ask when the ashes will be ready and how they come back, and whether a certified death certificate and the coroner's permit fee are part of the price.
An urn bought elsewhere is fine in South Carolina; the FTC Funeral Rule bars a provider from refusing it or charging to use it, and the South Carolina Board of Funeral Service takes complaints.

Keeping a South Carolina Cremation Affordable

Two moves that lower the cost:

  • Ask three homes for price lists. South Carolina funeral homes have to give out their General Price List, so compare the direct cremation figures in writing.
  • Ask about the permit fee. The coroner's permit is often passed on as a cash advance item, so check whether it is in the quote or will be added later.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cost of cremation in South Carolina?

On our estimates South Carolina cremation runs from about $1,600 for a direct cremation to about $5,400 with a full funeral first.

Which cremation services are offered in South Carolina?

South Carolina providers usually offer three: a direct cremation at about $1,600 with no service; a cremation and then a memorial at a time and place the family chooses; and a funeral with viewing followed by cremation at about $5,400.

What paperwork does a South Carolina cremation need?

South Carolina forbids cremation until 24 hours after death, and the crematory needs the death certificate, the next of kin's authorization and a coroner's cremation permit (S.C. Code § 32-8-340).

Problems with how a crematory or funeral home handled these steps go to the South Carolina Board of Funeral Service (803-896-4429).

Where may ashes be scattered in South Carolina?

Permission depends on the place. Private land needs the owner's consent, and at National Park Service sites such as Congaree National Park, 36 CFR 2.62 requires a permit or a designated area. Some families choose a columbarium niche, a burial of the urn or keepsakes instead.

Why is cremation gaining ground in South Carolina?

Cost counts for a lot. NFDA's 2023 national medians are $6,280 for a funeral with cremation and $8,300 for one with burial, and a South Carolina direct cremation costs less than either. Cremation also means no cemetery plot unless the family wants one.

How long does a South Carolina cremation take?

Nothing can happen in the first 24 hours, and the crematory needs the death certificate, the next of kin's authorization and the coroner's permit (S.C. Code § 32-8-340). Board regulations require refrigeration at 40°F for bodies held 24 hours or longer (S.C. Code Regs. ch. 57), and a crematory whose retort is down for more than a day must turn bodies away unless it can refrigerate them.

In the retort, the Cremation Association of North America says, a body takes anywhere from half an hour to over two hours, and the remains still have to cool and be processed afterwards. The funeral home can give you a collection date.

May a South Carolina family hold a funeral before cremating?

Yes. Our estimate for a South Carolina funeral and then cremation is about $5,400, usually $4,320 to $7,020, against about $1,600 for a direct cremation.

The Board of Funeral Service says cremation does not need embalming, though a family planning a viewing may want it. With the 24-hour wait and the coroner's permit (S.C. Code § 32-8-340), many families hold a viewing on the first evening and cremate the next day.

What a South Carolina coroner's cremation permit costs

In South Carolina the person who arranges a cremation must get the permit from the coroner or medical examiner of the county where the death happened. Wilfully skipping it is a misdemeanor (S.C. Code § 17-5-600). Coroners set their own charges, so the price varies by county. Richland County takes requests online only, and they must include the signed death certificate, the burial-removal-transit permit and the signed cremation authorization. The office emails the permit back and charges $20 (Richland County Coroner's Office; checked October 29, 2026).

Smaller counties may charge more. Bamberg County's coroner began charging $35 for a cremation permit on January 1, 2026. Funeral homes usually pass this fee on as a cash advance item. A bill introduced in 2023 (H. 3017) would have banned coroners from charging for cremation permits. It was referred to the House Labor, Commerce and Industry Committee and was not enacted in the 2023-24 session.

Who regulates South Carolina crematories, and paying for one

South Carolina's crematories and funeral homes hold licenses from the South Carolina Board of Funeral Service (803-896-4429), which is also where complaints go.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list South Carolina among the nine states that require a funeral director, yet a crematory still will not start without the death certificate, the coroner's permit and the next of kin's signed authorization.

Cremation must wait 24 hours after death, and the death certificate, next-of-kin authorization and coroner's cremation permit are all required (S.C. Code § 32-8-340).

When the estate cannot pay

South Carolina has no state funeral payment. An unclaimed body goes to the county coroner if the anatomical board declines it, the estate pays first, and the county is charged only after that; county councils may also fund indigent burial or cremation as a public purpose (S.C. Code § 17-5-570; S.C. Code § 4-9-130). Social Security's $255 lump sum goes only to an eligible spouse or child and has to be claimed within two years.

A South Carolina veteran who is cremated can still be placed in a national cemetery at no charge: Fort Jackson National Cemetery (803-699-2246), Beaufort National Cemetery (843-524-3925) and Florence National Cemetery Annex on Stockade Drive (843-669-8783), which is the Florence site taking new interments. The state's M.J. Dolly Cooper Veterans Cemetery near Anderson is at 864-332-8022; see cem.va.gov.

South Carolina checklist

  1. Ask each provider to list the transfer, the container, the crematory fee and the coroner's permit fee.
  2. Allow for the 24-hour wait when setting travel or memorial dates.
  3. If the estate cannot pay, ask the county council whether it funds indigent cremation.
  4. Report a provider that misstates prices to the Board at 803-896-4429.
