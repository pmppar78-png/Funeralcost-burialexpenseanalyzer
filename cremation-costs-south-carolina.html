Cremation Costs in South Carolina (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 45%

South Carolina's cremation rate sits at about 45% — below the national average but climbing. Direct cremation through J. Henry Stuhr in Charleston or the Cremation Society of Carolina runs roughly $1,600; a cremation with a full service tops out near $5,400. The state's 24-hour cremation hold, imposed under Title 32 of the South Carolina Code, and the required Cremation Authorization Form are the two procedural items that most affect timing.

What to Know Before Choosing Cremation in South Carolina

South Carolina Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation (LLR) oversees the Board of Funeral Service. The key provisions families run into are these:

  • 24-hour hold: Under SC Code Section 32-8-365, no cremation may occur within 24 hours of death unless a coroner authorizes otherwise.
  • Cremation Authorization Form: The next of kin, in the order set by state statute, must sign a specific form. Disputes among siblings or family members can pause the process.
  • Coroner release: In many counties the coroner or medical examiner must sign off before a funeral home can transfer the body to the crematory.
  • Pacemaker and device removal: Required before the retort for safety reasons; the funeral home handles this as part of preparation.
Charleston-area tip: J. Henry Stuhr has operated in Charleston since 1865 and owns multiple chapel locations. Calling two or three Stuhr locations will not give you independent price quotes — they share pricing across the company. For a true comparison, call Stuhr, an independent operator like Fielding Home for Funerals, and a direct-cremation specialist like the Cremation Society of Carolina.

Where to Turn for Help With Cremation Costs

South Carolina Department of Social Services administers a modest burial/cremation benefit for indigent decedents whose estates cannot pay and whose families qualify financially. Amounts vary by county and are in the $500 to $900 range in most cases. Veterans' families should inquire with Beaufort National Cemetery and Florence National Cemetery about no-cost inurnment, and file VA Form 21P-530 for the burial allowance ($978 non-service-connected, $2,000 service-connected). Social Security's one-time $255 lump sum death benefit applies regardless of disposition method.

The Cremation-Versus-Burial Gap in the Palmetto State

The savings relative to a traditional burial are significant — in most South Carolina counties a casket burial with plot, vault, marker, and opening/closing runs $7,000 to $10,000. Direct cremation at $1,600 beats that by more than $5,000. Even so, many low-country families still choose burial out of faith tradition; the AME, Southern Baptist, and historic African American Gullah-Geechee communities lean burial. The cremation rate has ticked up each of the last ten years but hasn't matched the Pacific Northwest's.

Scattering Along the Coast

South Carolina's coastline is broad and the Atlantic scattering options are used heavily by Charleston and Hilton Head families. Ocean scattering beyond three nautical miles is legal under EPA regulation; charter operators out of Charleston Harbor and Shem Creek handle attended scatterings for $250 to $800. Francis Marion National Forest permits dispersal with a free permit. ACE Basin scatterings on public water are permitted; private-property scatterings need the landowner's written consent.

Short Answers to Common Questions

How much does cremation cost in South Carolina?

Direct cremation in South Carolina costs about $1,600 through J. Henry Stuhr in Charleston, the Cremation Society of Carolina, and similar operators. Full-service cremation — ceremony and viewing before the cremation — averages $5,400. Charleston and Columbia tend to price slightly above the state average; upstate markets around Greenville run a bit lower. The mandated 24-hour wait under SC Code 32-8-365 applies regardless of price tier.

What kinds of cremation services can I choose in South Carolina?

Three structures are typical: direct cremation at $1,600 with no ceremony, cremation with a memorial held afterward at a church or family location, and full-service cremation at $5,400 with open-casket viewing before cremation. Several South Carolina providers, including Stuhr, offer witness cremation for families who want to be present at the retort committal.

What legal requirements apply to cremation in South Carolina?

South Carolina requires a 24-hour hold after death before cremation can proceed (SC Code Section 32-8-365). The legal next of kin must sign a Cremation Authorization Form, and in most counties the coroner or medical examiner must release the body. Implanted devices like pacemakers are removed beforehand. The SC Board of Funeral Service under LLR enforces these rules and licenses every funeral establishment in the state.

Can I scatter ashes in South Carolina?

Ocean scattering beyond three nautical miles is legal under EPA rule; Charleston and Hilton Head charter operators handle attended scatterings for $250 to $800. Francis Marion National Forest and Sumter National Forest allow dispersal with a free permit. Private land needs the owner's written permission. State parks generally require ranger approval. Scattering in the ACE Basin on public water is widely practiced.

What is driving South Carolina's cremation rate?

South Carolina's 45% rate lags the national average but has risen steadily. The 3,800-plus savings over traditional burial is the biggest driver; the second is generational change as baby-boomer families increasingly choose simpler dispositions. Cultural resistance remains strong in the low-country's African American and AME communities and in parts of the upstate, which keeps the state rate below the Northeast and Pacific Northwest figures.

What is the timeline for cremation in South Carolina?

State law imposes a minimum 24-hour wait from death to cremation. Most South Carolina providers complete the full process — transfer, paperwork, coroner release, cremation, and return of remains — within 5 to 10 days. Direct cremation through the Cremation Society of Carolina is typically fastest because no ceremony sits in the timeline. Peak summer weeks in Charleston and Hilton Head sometimes extend return-of-remains timing by a few days.

Can I hold a funeral service before cremation in South Carolina?

Yes — this is the $5,400 option. Stuhr, Fielding Home for Funerals in Hilton Head, McAlister-Smith in North Charleston, and most full-service funeral homes in the state offer viewing and ceremony before the cremation. Families who prefer a memorial service after cremation pay less and gain scheduling flexibility, since the cremation doesn't hold the calendar.
