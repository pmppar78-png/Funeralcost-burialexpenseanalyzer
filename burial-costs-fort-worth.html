Burial Costs in Fort Worth, Texas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Texas

Fort Worth's burial market does something specific: it prices slightly below Dallas even though the two cities share a metro economy. A 2026 traditional interment here totals $15,026 to $21,142, a roughly $400-$500 discount on the service line versus East Dallas. Part of the reason is the city's dense mix of century-old independents along Hemphill and Camp Bowie and a handful of historic church cemeteries still accepting new burials. Texas has no state embalming requirement, and the Texas Funeral Service Commission enforces price disclosure under the same FTC Funeral Rule logic — which gives Fort Worth families meaningful leverage.

Totaled line by line, a Fort Worth burial bill in 2026 falls between $15,026 and $21,142. The lower half is routinely reachable by pricing the funeral home and cemetery as two separate negotiations rather than buying a bundled package.

The Texas Funeral Service Commission and what it actually does

The Texas Funeral Service Commission licenses funeral establishments and directors, investigates consumer complaints, and publishes disciplinary records. Three things the commission does that matter for a Fort Worth family: it enforces the FTC Funeral Rule's General Price List requirement, it takes complaints about embalming misrepresentation, and it publishes the disciplinary file on each funeral home so you can check a provider's history before signing. Texas law does not require embalming; if a mortuary claims otherwise, file the complaint.

Where Fort Worth plots actually sit, price-wise

  • Greenwood Memorial Park and other White Settlement-area memorial parks: $4,500-$6,500 per plot.
  • Oakwood Cemetery (historic, east of downtown): plot prices heavily dependent on section; older sections often unavailable, newer sections in the $3,500-$4,800 range.
  • Catholic diocese cemeteries in Tarrant County: $2,200-$3,800 per plot.
  • Small Johnson and Parker County cemeteries, 25-40 minutes out: $800-$2,000 per plot, often with lower opening-and-closing fees.

Tarrant County indigent burial: The county's indigent burial program runs through the Medical Examiner's office with income-eligibility thresholds. It provides a basic interment and a government-provided marker. The application window is narrow and requires documentation of insolvency — start with the county clerk.

Veterans' options in Fort Worth

Dallas-Fort Worth National Cemetery (in southern Dallas County) serves the entire metro. Eligible veterans and their qualifying spouses bury at no cost: plot, opening-and-closing, and a federal-issue marker are all covered. Several Tarrant County cemeteries also maintain veterans' sections with reduced pricing. Our veteran burial benefits guide walks through the VA paperwork and eligibility.

The casket line is still where most families overpay

A 20-gauge steel casket at a Fort Worth funeral home's showroom runs $2,600-$3,200. The same casket from a reputable online retailer — which the funeral home must accept without a handling fee under the FTC Funeral Rule — lands between $950 and $1,500 with two- to four-day delivery. Independent casket showrooms in the metro occasionally undercut online pricing for traditional hardwood models. Fort Worth has more than one independent casket retailer that sells to the public.

What Readers Typically Ask

How much does burial cost in Fort Worth?

A full Fort Worth burial in 2026 lands between $15,026 and $21,142. That is the funeral service ($7,920), plot ($3,740), casket ($2,376), vault ($1,496), opening-and-closing ($1,870), and a marker ($1,500-$3,000) added together. Fort Worth prices slightly below Dallas on the service line, and families willing to look at Catholic diocese cemeteries or small Johnson County grounds can cut the plot by $1,500-$2,500.

What are the cheapest burial options in Fort Worth?

Direct burial through an independent Fort Worth funeral home, combined with a plot at a Catholic diocese cemetery or a small rural cemetery in Johnson or Parker County, is the shortest path to a low total. Buying the casket from an online retailer routinely trims $1,000-$1,800. Veterans should check eligibility at Dallas-Fort Worth National Cemetery, where plot, opening-and-closing, and marker are provided at no cost.

How do Fort Worth burial costs compare to the rest of Texas?

Fort Worth runs slightly above the Texas state average but below Houston and Dallas. The service line here ($7,920) is roughly $400 under Dallas; the plot line ($3,740) is about $170 under. The gap mostly reflects older, established independent funeral homes in Fort Worth competing on service pricing more aggressively than some Dallas operators.
