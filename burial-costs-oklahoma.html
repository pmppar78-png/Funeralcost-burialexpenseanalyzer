Burial Costs in Oklahoma (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 24, 2026 Region: South

Whether Oklahoma burial is a family tradition or simply the right choice for your loved one, knowing the real numbers before you walk into a funeral home matters. Average totals in Oklahoma run from $13,560 to $18,060, but careful comparison, especially on caskets and cemetery choice, can cut thousands off that bill.

How to Reduce Burial Costs in Oklahoma

Burial costs in Oklahoma can be managed with smart planning:

  • Compare cemetery prices directly: Cemeteries are not covered by the FTC Funeral Rule but most in Oklahoma will share a price sheet on request.
  • Check church and family cemeteries: Active church and family-owned cemeteries are still common in rural Oklahoma and routinely price plots 30-50% below commercial memorial parks.
  • County veterans' sections: Many Oklahoma counties maintain dedicated veterans' sections separate from national cemeteries.

Regional Context for Burial in Oklahoma

In much of Oklahoma, family and church cemeteries still operate alongside commercial ones, and many congregations keep section pricing below market. If you have a church or family plot in Oklahoma, the difference from a private cemetery may be more than $1,080 (our estimate).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Oklahoma?

A traditional burial in Oklahoma costs approximately $6,100 for the funeral service plus $2,700 for a cemetery plot. When you add a burial vault ($1,080), opening and closing fees ($1,350), casket ($1,830), and headstone ($500-$5,000), the Oklahoma total typically ranges from $13,560 to $18,060.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Oklahoma?

Oklahoma law does not require a burial vault. A cemetery can require an outer container in its rules, and a liner is often the cheaper way to satisfy that; ask for the rule in writing and whether you may supply the container yourself.

An Oklahoma funeral home cannot tell you the law requires a vault when only the cemetery does (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(c)). Questions about a perpetual-care cemetery's trust fund go to the Oklahoma Insurance Department (800-522-0071), which has jurisdiction over those funds under 36 O.S. § 7103.

How can I reduce burial costs in Oklahoma?

Direct (immediate) burial skips embalming, viewing and a ceremony at the funeral home, so it is usually the lowest-cost burial on an Oklahoma price list. You may supply your own casket in Oklahoma; the FTC Funeral Rule forbids a handling fee for it. A VA national cemetery burial costs eligible Oklahoma veterans nothing for the plot, opening and closing, or headstone.

How do I compare cemetery costs in Oklahoma?

Ask each cemetery for its prices for the burial space, opening and closing, vault or liner and marker setting, and whether it is a perpetual-care cemetery. Under Oklahoma's Perpetual Care Fund Act (36 O.S. §§ 7101–7112), those cemeteries must put 10% of each burial-space sale into a trust or pledged deposit for upkeep, and the Oklahoma Insurance Department issues their permits.

Complaints about cemetery upkeep go to the Attorney General's Consumer Protection Unit, not the Funeral Board. The Oklahoma Funeral Board (405-522-1790) covers the funeral home side of the same burial.

What is an all-in burial estimate for Oklahoma?

Adding every line item, burial in Oklahoma usually totals $13,560 to $18,060: funeral service about $6,100, plot about $2,700, casket about $1,830, vault about $1,080, opening-and-closing about $1,350, and headstone $500-$5,000.

Can I choose a natural or green burial in Oklahoma?

A growing number of Oklahoma cemeteries now offer green burial sections (biodegradable container, no embalming, no vault), and the total cost is usually below a traditional burial. If a dedicated green burial ground is not nearby, some families in Oklahoma use a hybrid approach (traditional plot without embalming).

Burial rules and help in Oklahoma

For a license check or complaint about a funeral home arranging a burial in Oklahoma, call the Oklahoma Funeral Board at 405-522-1790; Oklahoma cemeteries can have a separate regulator.

Because the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count Oklahoma among the nine states that require a funeral director, an Oklahoma family may arrange a burial without one; the cemetery still sets its own rules for the grave, and the burial permit must be in hand.

No cremation wait affects a burial, but if plans change to cremation, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (63 O.S. § 1-329.1).

An Oklahoma county pays the reasonable cost of opening and closing the grave for an indigent burial on the commissioners' order (56 O.S. § 54). (Source: 56 O.S. § 54.) A surviving spouse or child may claim Social Security's one-time $255 death payment within two years; it is paid to them, not to the Oklahoma cemetery.

Eligible Oklahoma veterans get a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. In Oklahoma, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Fort Gibson National Cemetery (918-478-2334); Fort Sill National Cemetery (580-492-3200); tribal veterans cemeteries: Seaman First Class Billy Turner Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 580-490-6489); Seminole Nation and Veterans Memorial Cemetery (tribal, 405-295-4549); White Eagle Cemetery (tribal, 580-762-8104) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps for a burial in Oklahoma

  1. Get the funeral home's General Price List and the Oklahoma cemetery's own price sheet before you agree to a burial.
  2. Ask the Oklahoma cemetery whether it requires an outer burial container before you shop for one.
  3. Ask the Oklahoma commissioners about grave-opening costs, which the county treasurer pays on their order.
  4. If the person served, ask Fort Gibson National Cemetery what paperwork it needs, usually starting with the DD214 discharge record.
  5. Report a funeral home that misprices a burial to the board at 405-522-1790.
