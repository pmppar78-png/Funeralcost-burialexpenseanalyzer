Burial Costs in Sacramento, California (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: California

Sacramento's oldest continuously operating cemetery — Sacramento Historic City Cemetery on Broadway and 10th, opened 1850 during the Gold Rush — is maintained by the City of Sacramento and still accepts burials in a handful of reserved sections. East Lawn Memorial Park in East Sacramento (1904) and Sacramento Memorial Lawn (now part of East Lawn's portfolio) are the two largest private cemeteries serving the metro. A 2026 traditional burial in Sacramento runs about $12,190 for the funeral service and $5,980 for a plot, with all-in totals between $23,552 and $33,189 — pricing that reflects both California's cost structure and the way the state regulates funeral and cemetery services through a combined Department of Consumer Affairs board.

California's Cemetery and Funeral Bureau and What It Enforces

California folds cemetery, funeral, crematory, and pre-need oversight into one regulator: the California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau, part of the Department of Consumer Affairs. That consolidation matters because California has some of the strongest pre-need trust and endowment-care requirements in the country — endowment-care cemeteries must deposit a state-specified percentage of every plot sale into a perpetual trust, and pre-need funeral contracts must be fully funded with qualifying trust deposits or insurance. Before signing any Sacramento pre-need contract, search the provider's license on cfb.ca.gov for disciplinary history, and make sure the written contract identifies the trust or insurer and disclosed cancellation rights. California additionally requires funeral homes to provide a written, itemized General Price List on request and to accept third-party caskets without a handling fee under the FTC Funeral Rule.

Sacramento's Cemetery Map: East Lawn, St. Mary's, and Sacramento Valley National

Sacramento Historic City Cemetery is the oldest and most affordable burial option in the metro, though availability is limited to reserved sections and specific eligibility. East Lawn Memorial Park on Folsom Boulevard (1904) is the largest private cemetery and handles most non-denominational burials in the metro. St. Mary's Cemetery on 21st Avenue, run by the Diocese of Sacramento, handles most Catholic burials at parishioner-friendly pricing. Home of Peace (Jewish) and Mount Vernon Memorial Park (Fair Oaks) round out the metro's major grounds. For eligible veterans, Sacramento Valley National Cemetery in Dixon (opened 2006, 30 minutes west of downtown) provides plot, vault, opening-and-closing, and upright marker at no cost — roughly $10,000 off a Sacramento burial given local price levels.

Sacramento tip: California's endowment-care requirement means the plot fee at any licensed endowment-care cemetery has a built-in perpetual-maintenance component. That is not an optional fee — it is set by the state's CPA-audited trust deposit requirement. Focus your negotiation on the service and merchandise lines, not on the endowment-care component of the plot.

Cutting the Sacramento Bill

  • Check Sacramento Valley National Cemetery eligibility: The Dixon facility provides everything plot-side at no cost for qualifying veterans — the single biggest lever available in the metro.
  • Ask St. Mary's Cemetery for parishioner pricing: Diocese of Sacramento plots typically undercut commercial memorial parks by $1,500–$3,000.
  • Consider Sacramento Historic City Cemetery eligibility: When available, the City-owned cemetery's pricing is well below the private market.
  • Request three written General Price Lists: California's strict disclosure rules make the three-GPL comparison particularly useful — the spread in Sacramento regularly exceeds $3,000 on identical packages.
  • Ask about natural burial sections: Several Sacramento-area cemeteries have added green or hybrid burial sections without advertising them; those plots typically price 30–50 percent below traditional lawn.
  • Use cremation market leverage: California's cremation rate is around 65 percent; burial-focused providers have real pricing flexibility they rarely publish.
  • Sacramento County indigent burial: Sacramento County operates an indigent disposition program through the Coroner's Office for decedents without means and without family to pay. See our payment assistance guide.

Answers to Common Questions

How much does burial cost in Sacramento?

A 2026 traditional burial in Sacramento averages $12,190 for the funeral service plus $5,980 for a plot, with all-in totals between $23,552 and $33,189. East Lawn Memorial Park and Mount Vernon Memorial Park handle most of the metro's non-denominational burials at the upper end; St. Mary's (Diocese of Sacramento) and Sacramento Historic City Cemetery sit at the lower end. Veterans eligible for Sacramento Valley National Cemetery in Dixon save roughly $10,000 on the plot-side costs.

Is a burial vault required in Sacramento?

California does not require vaults by statute. Most Sacramento-area cemeteries require either a vault or the cheaper grave liner as written policy, enforced as part of their endowment-care and maintenance agreements. Natural burial sections at certain Sacramento cemeteries allow burial without either. Always ask the specific cemetery for the written interment specification before paying a deposit.

How do Sacramento burial costs compare to California?

Sacramento runs about 15 percent above the California state average ($12,190 / $5,980 versus $10,600 / $5,200) but below Los Angeles, San Diego, and San Jose. Sacramento is the cheapest of California's large metros, reflecting Central Valley land values relative to the coast. The California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau's endowment-care and pre-need rules apply uniformly across the state regardless of metro.
