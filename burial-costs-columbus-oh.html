Burial Costs in Columbus, Ohio (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Ohio

Columbus burial costs sit roughly where Cincinnati's do, and both run a touch above Cleveland. Columbus families budgeting for a full-service burial should plan on a range of about $14,916 to $20,999 before flowers, obituaries, reception costs, or any veterans' benefits are factored in. This guide explains where each dollar goes and where Franklin County families actually have room to cut.

That $14,916 to $20,999 window assumes a mainstream Franklin County cemetery — Green Lawn, Union, Resthaven Memory Gardens, Sunset, or one of the Mount Calvary Catholic properties — along with a mid-range casket and headstone. The upper end of the range is typical for a Dignity Memorial–affiliated chapel and a premium casket; the lower end is more common at independent family-owned funeral homes in places like Bexley, Clintonville, and Grove City. Winter burials in Franklin County generally do not trigger the frozen-ground surcharges that Cleveland cemeteries add, although late-January openings sometimes carry a small premium when the ground is particularly hard.

Green Lawn and the Historic Columbus Cemeteries

Green Lawn Cemetery, established in 1848 on what is now the near west side, is by far the largest and most historically significant cemetery in Columbus — 360 acres, a designated arboretum, and the resting place of Samuel Medary, Eddie Rickenbacker, James Thurber, and the Ohio statesmen whose names appear on streets downtown. Green Lawn still actively sells plots across a range of price points. Union Cemetery on Olentangy River Road is the large commercial cemetery serving the OSU area and north side. Mount Calvary Cemetery in Columbus and St. Joseph Cemetery in Lockbourne are the primary Catholic options for the Diocese of Columbus. Camp Chase Confederate Cemetery is an unusual historical site: a small National Park Service–administered burial ground holding Confederate soldiers who died as Union POWs at Camp Chase during the Civil War, no longer open to new interments but worth knowing as a Columbus cemetery landmark.

Veterans Cemetery Access From Columbus

For Columbus-area veterans, the nearest VA national cemetery is Dayton National Cemetery, about an hour west via I-70. Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery in Rittman is farther — about two hours north via I-71 — and primarily serves northeast Ohio, though Columbus families can use it. Interment at either is free for eligible veterans and spouses and includes plot, opening and closing, outer burial container, perpetual care, and a government-furnished headstone. Ohio also operates Ohio State Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Lawton (Allen County, near Lima) for a similar benefit closer to mid-state families than Rittman. Columbus funeral homes arrange transportation to any of these cemeteries routinely; the typical cost is a modest transportation surcharge that is still dwarfed by the savings from the cemetery-side benefit.

Franklin County Indigent and Assistance Programs

Franklin County Job and Family Services administers indigent burial and cremation assistance for qualifying decedents. Coverage is limited — typically a direct cremation with interment of ashes in a designated section, or in limited cases a basic direct burial — and the application must be filed before arrangements are finalized. The program is needs-tested on the decedent's resources, not the survivors'. Columbus also has several congregation-based funds (Catholic parishes, the Columbus Jewish Federation, various Baptist associations) that will occasionally bridge shortfalls for members in hardship. The Social Security $255 lump-sum death benefit applies to qualifying surviving spouses regardless of location, and the funeral home can file on the family's behalf.

Columbus vs. Other Ohio Cities

Columbus and Cincinnati now match almost exactly on burial pricing; Cleveland is a few hundred dollars cheaper on most lines. The smaller Ohio cities — Dayton, Toledo, Youngstown, Akron — generally run closer to the state average, and rural township cemeteries anywhere south or east of Columbus metro can be 30–40% below Franklin County pricing.

Practical Ways Columbus Families Cut the Bill

  • Start with Dayton National Cemetery for any veteran. The one-hour drive west buys roughly $6,000 in cemetery-side savings for eligible veterans and spouses.
  • Use Green Lawn's published itemization as a benchmark. Green Lawn publishes detailed pricing; use those numbers when comparing Union, Resthaven, Sunset, and the other large commercial cemeteries.
  • Shop independent funeral homes. Columbus has a strong bench of independents — Schoedinger, Egan-Ryan, Cook & Son-Pallay, and others — that typically quote closer to the NFDA national average than Dignity Memorial–affiliated locations.
  • Ask about township cemeteries. Franklin, Licking, Fairfield, Delaware, and Pickaway County townships still operate cemeteries with plot and opening-and-closing fees well below metro commercial pricing. The township clerk has the fee schedule.
  • Request a grave liner quote, not only a full vault. Some Columbus cemeteries accept a concrete liner, which runs about half the cost of a sealed vault.
  • Order the casket online. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, Columbus funeral homes must accept a third-party casket without handling fees. Casket guide | Retailers
  • Price the headstone through an independent monument dealer. Columbus has several long-established monument companies that deliver-and-install at area cemeteries at substantially lower prices than in-house cemetery monuments. Headstone guide

Answers to Common Questions

How much does burial cost in Columbus?

A full-service burial in Columbus runs about $7,810 for the funeral service and $3,740 for a plot at Green Lawn, Union, or another major Franklin County cemetery. Add a $1,496 vault, $1,870 opening and closing, a mid-range casket ($2,343), and a headstone ($1,500–$3,500), and the total sits between $14,916 and $20,999. Eligible veterans interred at Dayton National Cemetery one hour west pay essentially nothing on the cemetery side of the bill.

Is a burial vault required in Columbus?

Ohio state law does not require a vault. Green Lawn, Union, Resthaven, Sunset, Mount Calvary, and most other major Franklin County cemeteries require one as a maintenance policy to prevent ground settling. Some older township and parish cemeteries accept a concrete grave liner, which costs roughly half as much as a sealed vault. Always confirm with the specific cemetery before accepting a vault line on the funeral home's itemization.

What are the cheapest burial options in Columbus?

Veterans and spouses should start with Dayton National Cemetery or the Ohio State Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Lawton — both are free for eligible families. For non-veteran families, direct burial at a township cemetery in outlying Licking, Fairfield, Delaware, or Pickaway County using a casket ordered online is the lowest-cost path. Franklin County Job and Family Services administers indigent burial or cremation for qualifying decedents, and the Social Security $255 lump-sum benefit remains available for surviving spouses.
