Burial Costs in Arkansas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: South

Arkansas sits comfortably below the national average on funeral costs, but "below average" still lands most families at eleven or twelve thousand dollars by the time a burial is complete. The state's rural geography — most counties still have church cemeteries operating alongside a single commercial funeral home — means the spread between the most expensive and the most affordable paths to a dignified burial is unusually wide here. Families who call around tend to save meaningfully; families who accept the first funeral home package generally overpay.

Adding every invoice together, Arkansas burials typically total $11,420 to $16,050. The state's two VA national cemeteries (Fayetteville and Little Rock) can eliminate most of the cemetery side for eligible veterans.

The Arkansas State Board of Embalmers, Funeral Directors, Cemeteries and Burial Services

Arkansas is one of the few states whose funeral regulator also has cemetery oversight in its name — and that's the practical difference consumers see here. The Arkansas State Board of Embalmers, Funeral Directors, Cemeteries and Burial Services licenses both sides of the industry and accepts complaints on either. If a commercial cemetery refuses to itemize its fees, the board is a legitimate escalation path. Board meeting minutes and disciplinary actions are public on its website. Before committing to a large pre-need purchase, searching the board's actions page for the provider's name is a cheap sanity check.

Cemetery pricing and which Arkansas cemeteries are worth knowing about

  • Mount Holly Cemetery — Little Rock's historic downtown cemetery since 1843; limited inventory but notable for heritage burials.
  • Roselawn Memorial Park — Little Rock; large memorial-park style, mid-market pricing.
  • Fairview Memorial Park — Fayetteville; one of the primary Northwest Arkansas cemeteries.
  • Forest Hills Memorial Park — multiple Arkansas locations; commercial chain pricing.
  • Fayetteville National Cemetery and Little Rock National Cemetery — both operated by the VA. Eligible veterans and spouses pay nothing for plot, vault, opening-and-closing, or a government-issue marker.

Small-town church cemeteries — Methodist, Baptist, Church of Christ — still operate actively across the Arkansas Delta, the Ozarks, and the River Valley, and their plot prices frequently come in at half or less of what a commercial memorial park in Little Rock, Fayetteville, or Fort Smith charges.

The Arkansas embalming rule worth knowing

Under state rules, embalming is not required if disposition occurs within a tight timeframe and refrigeration is available. Most Arkansas funeral homes offer refrigeration as an option, but the default quote usually assumes embalming. Decline in writing if it doesn't fit your plans — this is a $500-$1,500 swing on most bills. The FTC Funeral Rule requires the funeral home to obtain your express written authorization before embalming; they cannot assume consent.

The Costco casket play: Arkansas has Costco warehouses in North Little Rock, Fayetteville, and Fort Smith, and the Costco casket catalog delivers to most of the state within a few business days at 50-70% below a funeral home showroom. The funeral home cannot refuse the casket or charge a handling fee under the FTC Funeral Rule, and Arkansas enforces this rule actively.

Veterans, county programs, and family cemeteries

Eligible Arkansas veterans have the two VA national cemeteries plus the Arkansas State Veterans Cemeteries at North Little Rock and Birdeye. County-level indigent burial programs exist but are administered unevenly — some Arkansas counties fund them through the county judge's discretionary fund and some through their welfare office. When funds are scarce, starting with the county judge's office often gets faster answers than calling the funeral home first. Arkansas also permits family burial grounds on private property with proper permitting through the county clerk and the state health department; this is more commonly used in the rural parts of the state than most urban-based funeral homes will mention.

Green burial in Arkansas

Dedicated green burial grounds are limited in Arkansas, but hybrid sections that waive the vault and embalming requirements exist at several cemeteries in the Fayetteville-Bentonville corridor and in the Little Rock metro. Pricing for a natural burial typically runs $3,000-$5,500 all-in — materially below the $11,420 floor for a traditional burial. Call each cemetery and ask specifically about "natural" or "hybrid" sections; the option may exist even if the website doesn't list it.

Reader Questions

What is the full price of burial in Arkansas?

Arkansas burials bill out at roughly $6,100 for the funeral service, $2,800 for a plot, $1,830 for a casket, $1,120 for a vault, $1,400 for opening-and-closing, and $1,000-$3,000 for a headstone, for an all-in $11,420-$16,050. A traditional church cemetery burial in rural Arkansas paired with an online casket purchase can pull the total closer to $7,000. Eligible veterans using Fayetteville or Little Rock National Cemetery pay essentially nothing on the cemetery side.

Is a burial vault required in Arkansas?

Arkansas state law doesn't mandate vaults; individual cemeteries do. Commercial memorial parks in Little Rock, Fayetteville, and Fort Smith typically require at least a concrete liner. Small church and municipal cemeteries across the Delta and the Ozarks are more flexible. Liners run $800-$1,500; premium vaults reach $10,000. Ask the specific cemetery for their vault spec in writing — not the funeral home's default recommendation.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in Arkansas?

Direct burial at a church or rural cemetery with an online-purchased casket and no embalming is the cheapest full-service path, often under $5,000. VA eligibility at Fayetteville or Little Rock National Cemetery is essentially free for the veteran and spouse. Hybrid green burial sections at a few Northwest Arkansas cemeteries avoid vault costs entirely. Buying the casket from Costco or an online retailer rather than the funeral home is the single biggest casket-side saving.

What is the total cost of burial in Arkansas including everything?

Funeral service ($6,100) plus plot ($2,800) plus casket ($1,830) plus vault ($1,120) plus opening-and-closing ($1,400) plus headstone ($1,000-$3,000) equals $11,420-$16,050. The monument is often billed separately weeks later — don't consider the funeral "paid for" until you've settled that invoice too. Cash-advance items (death certificates, obituary fees, clergy honorarium) should pass through the funeral home at cost without markup under the FTC Funeral Rule.

Does Arkansas have green burial cemeteries?

Hybrid green burial sections exist at several cemeteries in Northwest Arkansas and the Little Rock metro, though fully dedicated conservation grounds remain rare in the state. A natural burial typically runs $3,000-$5,500 all-in, with no vault, no embalming, and a biodegradable container. Ask each cemetery directly about "natural" or "hybrid" sections; many cemeteries offer the option without advertising it on their website.
