Burial Costs in Arkansas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 24, 2026 Region: South

If you are arranging a burial in Arkansas, the costs can add up faster than most families expect. Between the funeral home bill and the separate cemetery invoice, total burial expenses in Arkansas typically range from $13,750 to well over $18,250. This guide lays out what drives each line item and where Arkansas families have room to save.

How to Reduce Burial Costs in Arkansas

Burial costs in Arkansas can be managed with smart planning:

  • Request itemized pricing from every provider: Funeral homes in Arkansas must provide a General Price List. Cemeteries are not required to, but most in the Arkansas area will share one if you ask specifically for the itemized price sheet rather than a "package."
  • Check church and family cemeteries: Active church and family-owned cemeteries are still common in rural Arkansas and routinely price plots 30-50% below commercial memorial parks.
  • County veterans' sections: Many Arkansas counties maintain dedicated veterans' sections separate from national cemeteries.

Regional Context for Burial in Arkansas

Families in Arkansas often assume the funeral home handles everything, but the cemetery bill is separate and usually smaller at church, fraternal, or municipal sites than at commercial memorial parks. Calling around costs nothing: every Arkansas funeral home must quote its prices by phone under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR 453.2(b)(1)), so a family can line up two or three quotes before choosing one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the full price of burial in Arkansas?

A full burial in Arkansas comes in at about $6,100 for the funeral service, $2,800 for the cemetery plot, $1,830 for a casket, $1,120 for a vault, $1,400 for opening and closing, and $500-$5,000 for a headstone. Arkansas totals generally land between $13,750 and $18,250 depending on choices.

Is a burial vault required in Arkansas?

Burial vaults are required by most cemeteries in Arkansas as a matter of cemetery policy (to prevent ground settling), though they are rarely mandated by state law. Vault costs in Arkansas typically range from $800 to $10,000.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in Arkansas?

Direct burial is the lowest-cost path in Arkansas — it skips viewing, ceremony, and embalming. Green or natural burial is the next rung up, available at a growing number of Arkansas cemeteries with biodegradable containers and no embalming.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Arkansas?

Arkansas cemeteries price plots, opening and closing, and vault rules separately, so ask each one for a written price list. The FTC Funeral Rule covers funeral homes, not most cemeteries, so an Arkansas cemetery may not be required to give an itemized price list; ask for one anyway. VA national cemeteries in Arkansas include Fayetteville National Cemetery and Fort Smith National Cemetery.

What is the total cost of burial in Arkansas including everything?

The first answer above lists each item; in Arkansas, cash advance items such as certified death certificates, an obituary notice and flowers are added on top of the $13,750 to $18,250 range.

Does Arkansas have green burial cemeteries?

There are green burial options in parts of Arkansas, though coverage is not statewide. Some Arkansas cemeteries have hybrid sections rather than fully dedicated grounds. Check our green burial guide and call local Arkansas cemeteries to confirm what they currently offer.

Burial rules and help in Arkansas

Funeral homes handling burials in Arkansas are licensed by the Arkansas Insurance Department, Funeral Services Division (501-682-0574). Cemetery sales in Arkansas may fall under a different office, so ask the board who oversees the cemetery you are considering.

Because the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count Arkansas among the nine states that require a funeral director, an Arkansas family may arrange a burial without one; the cemetery still sets its own rules for the grave, and the burial permit must be in hand.

A burial in Arkansas goes ahead once the permit is issued; for cremation, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (17 CAR § 30-205).

When no one in Arkansas claims a body, the person holding it must search for relatives and check for military service, and the county court decides on a pauper burial (Ark. Code Ann. § 20-17-702). (Source: Ark. Code Ann. § 20-17-702.) A surviving spouse or child may claim Social Security's one-time $255 death payment within two years; it is paid to them, not to the Arkansas cemetery.

An Arkansas veteran's grave in a national cemetery costs the family nothing for the plot, opening and closing, or marker. In Arkansas, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Fayetteville National Cemetery (479-442-2566); Fort Smith National Cemetery (479-783-5345); state veterans cemeteries: Arkansas State Veterans Cemetery at Birdeye (870-588-4608); Arkansas State Veterans Cemetery at North Little Rock (501-683-2259) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps for a burial in Arkansas

  1. Price the Arkansas cemetery separately: plot, opening and closing, vault or liner, and marker are often sold apart from the funeral home.
  2. Ask the Arkansas cemetery whether it requires an outer burial container before you shop for one.
  3. Ask the Arkansas county judge's office about a county-paid burial plot before buying one.
  4. If the person served, ask Fort Smith National Cemetery what paperwork it needs, usually starting with the DD214 discharge record.
  5. Report a funeral home that misprices a burial to the board at 501-682-0574.
