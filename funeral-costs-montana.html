What the Law Guarantees You in Montana

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Montana must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. The Montana Board of Funeral Service licenses morticians and mortician interns, mortuaries, crematory operators and technicians, crematories and for-profit cemeteries, and its members include a cemetery company representative and one member of the public.

If you believe a funeral home in Montana has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Complaints against a Montana licensee go to the Department of Labor and Industry's complaint line, (406) 841-2362.

Why Funeral Prices Vary in Montana

What you pay for a funeral in Montana depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in Montana, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether Montana state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. Mountain pricing is generally moderate than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in Montana routinely differs by 10-30%.

At 72%, Montana's cremation rate is 8.6 points above the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

Payment Assistance in Montana

  • Montana counties are not required to offer indigent funeral help, and a county that does can seek repayment from the estate (Mont. Code Ann. § 53-3-116).
  • Funeral homes in Montana usually report the death to Social Security, but the $255 lump sum still has to be claimed by the spouse or child (1-800-772-1213, within two years).
  • For a Montana veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. How Montana Differs From Its Mountain Neighbors

Mountain-state funeral pricing in Montana (avg $7,100) is usually moderate, but the Billings area can run noticeably higher than rural parts of the state. Fuel and transportation appear on GPLs in Montana more often than in denser regions; if the funeral home is more than 30 miles from the residence or cemetery, mileage charges can add $100-$400 to the final bill.

Montana death certificates: a flat $16

Montana Vital Records charges $16 for each certified death certificate, and the fee is not refunded (Montana DPHHS, vital records fee schedule, effective September 21, 2024; checked October 29, 2026). Three copies cost $48.

  • Any county clerk: every Montana County Clerk and Recorder can sell a death certificate, whichever county the death happened in.
  • Cause of death: if the certificate still says "pending autopsy" or "pending investigation," the state issues a copy with the cause removed.
  • By mail: Montana Vital Records, P.O. Box 4210, Helena, MT 59604 (406-444-2685), with a copy of both sides of your ID or a notarized application. VitalChek and VitalRecordsOnline are the only approved online sellers.

Funeral rules and help in Montana

Funeral homes in Montana are licensed by the Montana Department of Labor and Industry (406-841-2359). That office takes Montana complaints about pricing, misleading sales, or a home that will not hand over its price list.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Montana among the nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so a Montana family may arrange much of a funeral itself. The Montana death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

If the family chooses cremation, Montana requires a 24-hour wait before cremation (MCA 37-19-705).

Montana counties may, but are not required to, provide indigent burial, entombment or cremation help, where indigent means the person could not pay the amount the county negotiated with the funeral home (Mont. Code Ann. § 53-3-116); the county can seek repayment from the estate and draw on the state indigent-funeral account. (Source: Mont. Code Ann. §§ 53-3-116, 7-4-2924.) The $255 Social Security lump sum goes only to an eligible spouse or child and must be claimed within two years; it does not go to the Montana funeral home.

A Montana funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Yellowstone National Cemetery (406-647-2746) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state and tribal veterans cemeteries, Apsaalooke Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 406-620-3504), Eastern Montana State Veterans Cemetery - Miles City (406-218-2945), Montana State Veterans Cemetery - Fort Harrison (406-324-4793), or Western Montana State Veterans Cemetery - Missoula (406-721-2995), take requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in Montana

  1. Ask every Montana funeral home for its General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule says it must give you one when you ask in person.
  2. Consider a Montana memorial service after cremation or burial, which avoids embalming and viewing fees.
  3. Ask the Montana county commissioners whether the county offers indigent funeral help before choosing a funeral home.
  4. If the person served, have the funeral home ask about Yellowstone National Cemetery before you buy a private plot.
  5. Report a funeral home that hides or ignores its price list to the board at 406-841-2359.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a typical Montana funeral cost in 2026?

Most Montana families paying for a full traditional service see the bill settle around $7,100, 14% below the $8,300 national benchmark. Cremation with ceremony runs closer to $5,600, while a stripped-down direct cremation in Montana can land near $1,600. Cemetery plot fees in Montana add about $3,200. Actual prices shift a lot depending on which Montana provider you call first, which is why comparing is non-negotiable.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in Montana?

Direct cremation in Montana starts around $1,600; a traditional funeral with burial averages $7,100, not counting the cemetery plot or vault. For most Montana families, that is a difference of at least $5,500.

What are my consumer rights at Montana funeral homes?

In Montana, as everywhere in the US, the FTC Funeral Rule requires an itemized General Price List, phone price quotes, and no fee for using a casket bought elsewhere. Montana is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. You can confirm a Montana license or file a complaint with the Montana Department of Labor and Industry at 406-841-2359.

What is the best way to reduce funeral expenses in Montana?

The biggest lever is comparison shopping: two or three GPLs from different Montana providers, compared line by line, consistently saves families $1,000 or more. Home and family burial is legal in parts of Montana with permits and can eliminate cemetery fees entirely. At $1,600, direct cremation is the cheapest disposition option available in Montana.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Montana?

The FTC Funeral Rule bars a Montana funeral home from saying embalming is required by law when it is not, and its price list must state that embalming is not required except in certain special cases. If a Montana provider says embalming is needed, ask which law or cemetery rule it is citing.

How do funeral costs in Montana compare to other states?

Montana's traditional-funeral average of $7,100 sits 14% below the $8,300 national median, and the Mountain region overall trends toward moderate to lower pricing than the rest of the country.

What is the least expensive way to handle a funeral in Montana?

In Montana, direct cremation at about $1,600 is the most affordable path: just transport, cremation, and the return of the remains. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $3,550 in Montana before cemetery fees and is the cheapest burial option.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in Montana?

Call several Montana funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Montana home cannot charge a fee for it.
