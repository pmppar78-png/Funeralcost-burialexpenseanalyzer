What the Law Guarantees You in Montana

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Montana must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. The Montana Board of Funeral Service licenses morticians and mortician interns, mortuaries, crematory operators and technicians, crematories and for-profit cemeteries, and its members include a cemetery company representative and one member of the public.

If you believe a funeral home in Montana has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Complaints against a Montana licensee go to the Department of Labor and Industry's complaint line, (406) 841-2362.

Why Funeral Prices Vary in Montana

What you pay for a funeral in Montana depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in Montana, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether Montana state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. Mountain pricing is generally moderate than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in Montana routinely differs by 10-30%.

At 72%, Montana's cremation rate is 8.6 points above the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

Payment Assistance in Montana

  • Montana counties are not required to offer indigent funeral help, and a county that does can seek repayment from the estate (Mont. Code Ann. § 53-3-116).
  • Funeral homes in Montana usually report the death to Social Security, but the $255 lump sum still has to be claimed by the spouse or child (1-800-772-1213, within two years).
  • For a Montana veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. How Montana Differs From Its Mountain Neighbors

Mountain-state funeral pricing in Montana (avg $7,100) is usually moderate, but the Billings area can run noticeably higher than rural parts of the state. Fuel and transportation appear on GPLs in Montana more often than in denser regions; if the funeral home is more than 30 miles from the residence or cemetery, mileage charges can add $100-$400 to the final bill.

Montana death certificates: a flat $16

Montana Vital Records charges $16 for each certified death certificate, and the fee is not refunded (Montana DPHHS, vital records fee schedule, effective September 21, 2024; checked October 29, 2026). Three copies cost $48.

  • Any county clerk: every Montana County Clerk and Recorder can sell a death certificate, whichever county the death happened in.
  • Cause of death: if the certificate still says "pending autopsy" or "pending investigation," the state issues a copy with the cause removed.
  • By mail: Montana Vital Records, P.O. Box 4210, Helena, MT 59604 (406-444-2685), with a copy of both sides of your ID or a notarized application. VitalChek and VitalRecordsOnline are the only approved online sellers.

Montana funeral licensing, county help and veterans cemeteries

In Montana, funeral homes are licensed by the Department of Labor and Industry, which answers at 406-841-2359. It handles complaints about pricing, misleading sales and homes that will not hand over a price list, and it can confirm a license before you commit.

Montana law does not require a funeral director, and the Funeral Consumers Alliance leaves Montana off its list of nine states with that rule. A family can therefore take on parts of the work, such as a home wake or a service at the ranch or church, and pay a home only for what it chooses. The death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

The 24-hour cremation rule

A cremation cannot take place within 24 hours of death, and the crematory needs a signed cremation authorization (MCA 37-19-705). In winter, when travel to a crematory can be slow, ask the home whether its quote includes transport and any days of refrigeration.

County indigent funerals are optional here

Unlike some states, Montana does not make counties pay. A county may provide indigent burial, entombment or cremation help, and "indigent" means the person could not pay the amount the county negotiated with the funeral home (Mont. Code Ann. § 53-3-116). The county can seek repayment from the estate and draw on the state indigent-funeral account (§ 7-4-2924). Ask the county commissioners whether their county takes part before choosing a home. Social Security's $255 goes only to an eligible spouse or child, must be claimed within two years, and is not paid to the funeral home.

Montana's veterans cemeteries

Yellowstone National Cemetery near Laurel (406-647-2746) is booked by the funeral home through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state runs three more: Eastern Montana State Veterans Cemetery in Miles City (406-218-2945), Montana State Veterans Cemetery at Fort Harrison (406-324-4793) and Western Montana State Veterans Cemetery in Missoula (406-721-2995). The tribal Apsaalooke Veterans Cemetery answers at 406-620-3504. Ask any of them what an eligible veteran's family would pay before buying a private plot, which averages $3,200 in the table.

A Montana to-do list

  1. Get each home's General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule requires it when you ask in person.
  2. Price a memorial gathering after cremation or burial instead of embalming and a viewing.
  3. Ask the county commissioners whether indigent funeral help is offered.
  4. For a veteran, ask about Yellowstone National Cemetery before buying a private plot.
  5. Complaints about a hidden or ignored price list go to 406-841-2359.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a typical Montana funeral cost in 2026?

Our 2026 Montana estimate is $7,100 for a traditional funeral with burial, $5,600 for a funeral with cremation and from about $1,600 for direct cremation, plus roughly $3,200 for a plot. Prices in Billings, Missoula and small towns differ, so ask nearby homes for their General Price Lists.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in Montana?

Direct cremation near $1,600 is about $5,500 less than a $7,100 traditional funeral in Montana, and the gap widens past $8,000 once a $3,200 plot is added. Montana requires 24 hours between death and cremation (MCA 37-19-705).

What are my consumer rights at Montana funeral homes?

The FTC Funeral Rule requires a Montana home to give you an itemized price list in person, quote prices by phone, and accept a casket or urn from another seller with no handling fee. Montana law does not require you to hire a funeral director. The Department of Labor and Industry (406-841-2359) takes complaints.

What is the best way to reduce funeral expenses in Montana?

Ask for every home's price list and buy only the parts you need, since Montana does not require a funeral director. Direct cremation from about $1,600 with a later memorial is the lowest-cost route. A veteran can be buried at Yellowstone National Cemetery or one of the state veterans cemeteries, and some counties offer indigent funeral help under MCA 53-3-116.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Montana?

In most Montana cases, no. Embalming is an item you may decline, and the FTC requires the home's printed price list to tell you it is optional apart from special cases. Some homes ask for it before an open-casket viewing; a closed casket, a quick burial or direct cremation avoid the question. That keeps the $710 average off the bill.

How do funeral costs in Montana compare to other states?

Montana sits below the middle. Our $7,100 traditional-funeral estimate is about 14% under the $8,300 national median (NFDA 2023); see the national cost index for every state's figure.

What is the least expensive way to handle a funeral in Montana?

In Montana, direct cremation at about $1,600 is the most affordable path: just transport, cremation, and the return of the remains. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $3,550 in Montana before cemetery fees and is the cheapest burial option.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in Montana?

Price shopping by phone is allowed and homes must answer. Line up quotes for direct cremation and immediate burial from more than one community, and check whether removal from the place of death and mileage are in the quote. The Department of Labor and Industry confirms licenses.
