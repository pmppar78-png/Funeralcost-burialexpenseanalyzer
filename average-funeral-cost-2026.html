Understanding Average Funeral Cost 2026

NFDA surveyed its member funeral homes in July 2023 and released the results on December 8, 2023. The headline medians are $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, $9,995 when a burial vault is included, and $6,280 for a funeral with viewing and cremation. A median means half of the surveyed homes charged less and half charged more, so it is a midpoint, not a typical bill in any one city.

None of those figures include cemetery charges such as the plot, opening and closing, or a headstone, and they do not include cash advance items like death certificates, flowers or an obituary. A family's real total is the funeral home's itemized bill plus those outside costs.

What You Need to Know

How to read the national numbers:

  • The $8,300 burial median assumes a metal casket and no vault. Most cemeteries require some outer container, which is why the $9,995 figure with a vault is often closer to a real burial bill.
  • Direct cremation has its own medians: $2,645 with a container the family supplies and $2,750 with one from the funeral home. Direct cremation costs
  • NFDA's statistics page, updated September 29, 2025, cites a projected 2025 United States cremation rate of 63.4% from its Cremation and Burial Report, so cremation prices now describe most funerals. Cremation vs. burial comparison
  • The non-declinable basic services fee had a 2023 median of $2,495. It is part of every arrangement, including direct cremation. Read our FTC guide
  • State and city prices vary a great deal around the national median; see the state pages for local estimates. Funeral costs by state
  • The 2026 figures on this page are our estimate, adjusted with the BLS funeral expenses price index, not new survey data.
  • Help with the bill can come from Social Security's $255 lump-sum payment, VA burial benefits and some state programs. Explore payment assistance
  • The NFDA figures are medians from 809 member funeral homes that answered a survey mailed to 5,219 homes on July 7, 2023, so they describe NFDA members rather than every provider.
  • The cremation median of $6,280 includes a $400 cremation fee, a $160 alternative container and a $295 urn, along with the same viewing and ceremony charges as a burial.
  • Regional medians for a funeral with viewing and burial ranged from $7,390 in the Mountain states to $8,985 in New England.

Costs and Pricing

The table below converts the 2023 medians into estimated 2026 dollars, and the callout lists the main official figures.

NFDA 2023 national medians: funeral with viewing and burial $8,300; with a vault $9,995; funeral with viewing and cremation $6,280; direct cremation $2,645 with a family-supplied container and $2,750 with the funeral home's. Cemetery costs are not included in any of them.

To compare a local quote with these numbers, use the same type of service: a direct cremation quote against the direct cremation median, not against the full-service figure. Ask each funeral home for its General Price List and record the matching lines on the funeral cost comparison worksheet. Within the $8,300 burial median, the largest pieces are the $2,500 metal casket and the basic services fee; the vault adds a median of $1,695.

What's changed in 2026: estimated 2026 dollars

NFDA's 2023 study is still the most recent official national survey of funeral home prices. To show roughly what those medians are worth at 2026 prices, we adjusted them with the Bureau of Labor Statistics consumer price index for funeral expenses. These are our estimates, not new survey data, and your local prices may be higher or lower.

How we calculated this: The BLS funeral expenses index averaged 379.301 across the 12 months of 2023 and stood at 418.227 in September 2026, the latest month published as of October 21, 2026. That is an increase of about 10.3% (418.227 ÷ 379.301 = 1.1026). We multiplied each NFDA median by 1.1026 and rounded to the nearest $10. The CPI tracks price changes for funeral services in general, not one funeral home, so treat these as ballpark figures. Neither median includes cemetery costs such as a plot, vault, opening and closing, or a headstone.

Your Consumer Rights

The federal Funeral Rule is what makes these national comparisons possible:

  • Every funeral home must give an itemized General Price List, so its prices can be compared with the medians line by line. Full FTC guide
  • Prices must be given over the phone on request. How to compare prices
  • Apart from the basic services fee, every item can be declined, which is how families bring a bill below the median.
  • A funeral home must accept a casket bought elsewhere without a handling fee; the 2023 median metal casket on a funeral home price list was $2,500.
  • Embalming is not required by law in most cases, and a home cannot embalm for a fee without permission.

If a funeral home will not provide prices, report it to the Federal Trade Commission or your state attorney general, or ask the Funeral Consumers Alliance for help.

Practical Steps You Can Take

  1. Decide between burial, cremation with a service, or direct cremation, since each has its own median.
  2. Look up your state page to see how local estimates compare with the national figure.
  3. Call two or three funeral homes and ask for the same lines from their price lists.
  4. Add cemetery costs separately: plot, opening and closing, vault or liner, and marker.
  5. Add cash advance items such as certified death certificates and an obituary.
  6. Subtract any benefits the family qualifies for, such as the Social Security payment or a VA allowance.
  7. Compare the total with the median for the same type of service, not a different one.
  8. Get the itemized statement in writing before paying.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average funeral cost in 2026?

The latest national survey, NFDA 2023, puts the median funeral with viewing and burial at $8,300 and with cremation at $6,280. Adjusted with the BLS funeral price index, those are roughly $9,150 and $6,920 in 2026 dollars, by our estimate.

Why does this page use 2023 data?

NFDA has not released a newer national General Price List Study. Its statistics page still cited the 2023 figures as of September 29, 2025.

Do these figures include the cemetery?

No. The NFDA medians price what a funeral home sells. The plot, opening and closing the grave, and the headstone are bought from the cemetery or a monument dealer, and items the funeral home pays for on your behalf, such as death certificates, are cash advance items (16 CFR 453.1(b)). The one partial exception is the $9,995 median, which adds a vault but still leaves out the plot and the grave work.

The FTC Funeral Rule mostly stops at the cemetery gate. It applies to a "funeral provider" that sells or arranges both funeral goods and funeral services (16 CFR 453.1(i)), so a cemetery that sells only plots and opening fees is generally not bound by it. Ask the cemetery for its own written price list, and check your state's cemetery rules for what it must disclose.

What does direct cremation cost nationally?

NFDA's 2023 medians are $2,645 when the family supplies the container and $2,750 when the funeral home does. Adjusted with the same BLS funeral price index used on this page (up about 10.3% since 2023), that is roughly $2,920 and $3,030 in September 2026 dollars, as our estimate rather than a new survey.

Every funeral home has to show these choices on its General Price List: a price range for direct cremation, a separate price when you provide the container, and a price and description for each alternative container it offers (16 CFR 453.2(b)(4)(ii)(C)). Compare those lines rather than a package price, and remember the crematory fee may be listed separately.

How was the 2026 estimate calculated?

We multiplied each 2023 median by the change in the BLS consumer price index for funeral expenses from the 2023 average to September 2026, about 10.3%.

Why are local prices so different from the median?

Labor, real estate and the number of competing funeral homes differ from one area to the next, and state law adds its own costs. On this site's state estimates, a traditional funeral ranges from about $5,900 in Mississippi to about $11,000 in Hawaii, and those are estimates built around the national figures, not separate surveys.

State rules also change what a family must pay for. Connecticut, for example, makes a family wait 48 hours before cremation, while Colorado requires a body to be refrigerated or embalmed if it is not buried or cremated within 24 hours (C.R.S. 12-135-106), so refrigeration shows up as a charge on the bill. The funeral costs by state pages list each state's own rules next to its estimates.

What are my rights when comparing prices?

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, every funeral home must give itemized prices, by phone or in person, and let you choose only what you want.

Which region of the country is most expensive?

In NFDA's 2023 study, New England had the highest regional median for a funeral with viewing and burial at $8,985, and the Mountain region had the lowest at $7,390.
