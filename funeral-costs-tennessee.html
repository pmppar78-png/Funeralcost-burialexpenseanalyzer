Funeral Costs in Tennessee: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: South

Tennessee's funeral market runs along the state's three-grand-divisions structure — East, Middle, and West — with distinct pricing and provider cultures in each. R.S. Lewis & Sons in Memphis, a historic Black-owned firm that handled arrangements for Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. after his 1968 assassination, remains one of the state's most significant institutions. Marshall Donnelly Combs anchors Nashville's independent market, Dignity Memorial's consolidation shows up more heavily in the suburbs, and Tennessee's 42% cremation rate (per NFDA 2024 GPL Study tracking) sits below most of the country. The Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers enforces Tennessee Cemetery Act Title 46 governing perpetual care and cemetery sales, and the federal FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) applies to every provider statewide. Funeral Consumers Alliance (FCA) price surveys and NFDA data inform the averages throughout this page.

The Tennessee Cemetery Act and Title 46 oversight

Tennessee Cemetery Act Title 46 governs the sale of cemetery lots, perpetual-care trust requirements, and cemetery operator licensing. The TN Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers shares enforcement with the TN Department of Commerce and Insurance. Perpetual-care cemeteries must maintain a trust deposit of at least 10% of gross plot sales, and the Department publishes cemetery financial filings that families can request. Before signing a cemetery contract in Tennessee, verifying the cemetery's Title 46 registration and trust-compliance status is a 10-minute call that has saved many families from post-purchase headaches.

Chattanooga, Nashville, and Mountain Home national cemeteries

Tennessee hosts three active national cemeteries: Chattanooga National, Nashville National, and Mountain Home National in Johnson City. All three provide free burial for eligible veterans. Mountain Home, co-located with the VA Medical Center, has substantial available capacity and serves the eastern third of the state. Nashville National is near full interment capacity in its older sections, so new Middle Tennessee veteran burials increasingly route to the state Middle Tennessee Veterans Cemetery in Nashville or the East Tennessee Veterans Cemetery in Knoxville.

Mount Olivet, Elmwood, and the state's antebellum cemetery stock

Mount Olivet Cemetery in Nashville, founded in 1856, holds the remains of multiple Tennessee governors and Civil War figures, and it still accepts new interments in its expanded sections. Elmwood Cemetery in Memphis (1852) is one of the most architecturally distinct Victorian-era cemeteries in the South. Both typically price plots below comparable suburban memorial parks, and families wanting a historic setting often save $1,500 to $2,500 by choosing an established association cemetery over a new Dignity-owned park in Franklin, Collierville, or Farragut.

Larkspur Conservation and Tennessee's green-burial option

Larkspur Conservation near Westmoreland, about an hour north of Nashville, is Tennessee's only dedicated conservation burial ground. It offers natural burial with no embalming, no vault, and biodegradable shrouds or caskets — and the land itself is protected conservation acreage. Plot pricing is modest compared with traditional cemeteries, and Larkspur coordinates directly with funeral homes that handle natural preparation. For cost-conscious Tennessee families drawn to green burial, Larkspur plus a direct-transport funeral home package frequently totals under $3,500.

Small line items that become big ones

Most of the pricing surprises in Tennessee come from a predictable list of add-ons. Look for these specifically when reviewing any funeral home quote:

  • Casket handling fee — Some funeral homes add a surcharge for caskets purchased elsewhere, despite this being illegal under the FTC Funeral Rule
  • Mandatory embalming — Funeral homes may claim embalming is required. In Tennessee, it is almost never legally required. Know your rights
  • Cemetery "perpetual care" fees — An ongoing maintenance charge, typically $200–$1,000, added on top of the plot price
  • Vault/liner surcharge — Cemeteries may require a specific vault brand or type, inflating costs by $500–$2,000+
  • Documentation and filing fees — Death certificates, permits, and filing fees can add $200–$600
  • Weekend/holiday premium — Services held outside business hours may carry a 15–25% surcharge
  • "Package" markups — Bundled packages often include services you don't need. Always compare line-by-line. What funeral homes don't tell you

Buyer Protections to Remember

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Tennessee must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in Tennessee has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Your state attorney general's office can also assist with consumer protection complaints.

R.S. Lewis & Sons, Beale Street history, and Memphis funeral culture

Memphis's Black funeral tradition is one of the richest in the American South. R.S. Lewis & Sons Funeral Home, established 1918, has served the Memphis community for more than a century — including arrangements for Dr. King's body after his assassination at the Lorraine Motel — and it continues to coordinate the elaborate homegoing services that define West Tennessee's Black Baptist and AME communities. These services routinely involve multi-day wakes, processional services, and extensive repast meals; Lewis and peer firms like N.J. Ford & Sons price these bundles competitively versus what families unfamiliar with the tradition would face on a la carte pricing elsewhere.

Funds, Grants, and Public Programs

If funeral costs in Tennessee feel overwhelming, there are several assistance options to explore:

  • Social Security death benefit — A one-time $255 lump sum under SSA 42 U.S.C. § 402(i) for eligible surviving spouses or children. Learn more
  • Veteran burial benefits — VA 38 CFR § 3.1600 governs burial allowances; Chattanooga, Knoxville, Memphis, Mountain Home, and Nashville National Cemeteries provide free interment for eligible veterans. Veteran benefits guide
  • State assistance programs — Tennessee Department of Human Services administers limited funeral aid via county-level indigent burial programs; TennCare (Medicaid) coverage may apply for eligible recipients under T.C.A. § 71-5-101 et seq. Medicaid funeral assistance
  • Burial insurance — Final expense policies can cover funeral costs from $5,000 to $25,000. Compare the best burial insurance companies
  • Crowdfunding — Platforms like GoFundMe are increasingly used to cover funeral costs. Crowdfunding guide

What to nail down first

When an immediate Tennessee funeral arrangement lands on you, the order of operations matters. This sequence tends to save both money and emotional bandwidth:

  1. Take a breath. Unless there are legal or medical time constraints, you typically have 24–72 hours before decisions must be finalized.
  2. Request General Price Lists from at least 2–3 funeral homes in your area of Tennessee. They are legally required to provide them. Questions to ask funeral homes
  3. Decide on disposition: cremation in Tennessee or burial in Tennessee. This is the single biggest cost decision.
  4. Choose only the services you need. Embalming, premium caskets, and elaborate arrangements are optional. What funeral homes don't tell you
  5. Explore payment assistance if cost is a concern: Social Security benefits, veteran benefits, Medicaid assistance, or crowdfunding.

Answers to the Questions We Hear Most

What is the average price of a funeral in Tennessee right now?

Per the NFDA 2024 GPL Study and Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers licensure filings, current Tennessee averages come in at $6,700 for a traditional funeral, $5,400 for cremation with a service, and from $1,600 for direct cremation — with the traditional figure sitting 15% below the $7,848 US median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $3,000 when applicable. Because Tennessee pricing is not standardized, the same service can swing several thousand dollars between neighboring funeral homes, so always request the itemized GPL required under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

Does cremation save money compared with burial in Tennessee?

It does. A direct cremation in Tennessee runs about $1,600, whereas a traditional funeral with burial averages $6,700 before adding the plot — so the gap can easily exceed $5,100. Tennessee's 42% cremation rate sits below the ~60% national figure reported by NFDA. Many Southern families have strong traditions around homegoing services and church-based funerals, which can affect service choices and costs.

What are my consumer rights at Tennessee funeral homes?

The FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) protects every consumer in Tennessee. Funeral homes must hand you an itemized General Price List on request, let you pick and choose services (packages cannot be forced on you), accept a casket or urn purchased elsewhere with no handling fee, and never misrepresent legal requirements. The Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers layers additional state protections on top.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Tennessee?

Three moves cut the most: compare at least three Tennessee General Price Lists side by side before choosing a provider, strip packages down to only the services you actually want, and choose direct cremation ($1,600) if cost is the priority. Check with local churches and fraternal organizations — in Tennessee, congregation-owned cemeteries and family sections are often priced well below commercial memorial parks. Veteran burial benefits apply at any national cemetery and some state veterans' cemeteries in Tennessee. Then confirm eligibility for veteran, Medicaid, and Social Security survivor benefits.

Is embalming legally required in Tennessee?

No — embalming is rarely required by Tennessee law. Most cases allow refrigeration as an alternative, and the decision is yours. Some Tennessee funeral homes have internal rules for open-casket viewings, but that is provider policy rather than state law. Some Southern funeral homes may emphasize traditional open-casket services, but embalming remains optional by law in most cases. The Funeral Rule specifically prohibits providers from falsely claiming embalming is legally mandated.

Which funeral option costs the least in Tennessee?

Direct cremation carries the lowest price tag in Tennessee, from roughly $1,600. You skip the viewing, ceremony, and embalming — transport, cremation, and return of the ashes are all that are included. Direct burial is the next step up at about $4,020 and is still considerably cheaper than a traditional service.

Where do I look for low-cost funeral providers in Tennessee?

Start with GPL requests from at least three Tennessee funeral homes — that alone exposes the outliers. Reach out to the Funeral Consumers Alliance for Tennessee for member-run lists, shortlist dedicated direct cremation providers, ask every funeral home about their simplest no-frills package, and see if a nonprofit or cooperative provider serves your area of Tennessee.
