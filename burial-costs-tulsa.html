Burial Costs in Tulsa, Oklahoma (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Oklahoma

Tulsa's burial history is shaped by oil money and by the 1921 Race Massacre, and both still mark the city's cemeteries. Oaklawn Cemetery near downtown holds mass graves from the massacre that were formally identified only in recent excavations; Rose Hill Memorial Park off East 11th Street and Memorial Park Cemetery anchor the modern burial economy. Prices here run close to the Oklahoma state average at $11,792 to $16,548, among the lower total burial costs in the country.

Oaklawn, Rose Hill, and the weight of 1921

Tulsa's cemetery landscape is shaped partly by history most other cities do not carry. Oaklawn Cemetery, near downtown, contains mass-grave sections tied to the 1921 Tulsa Race Massacre — sections that have been the subject of city-sponsored forensic work in recent years. Rose Hill Memorial Park on East 21st Street is the larger corporate memorial park and handles the majority of contemporary mainstream burials. Memorial Park Cemetery sits further east, offering a mid-range option with an on-site mortuary. For veterans, Fort Gibson National Cemetery south of Muskogee — roughly 55 miles from Tulsa — is the nearest VA burial ground.

Oklahoma Funeral Board and the two-regulator problem

The Oklahoma Funeral Board licenses funeral directors, establishments, and handles mortuary complaints. Cemeteries are regulated separately — perpetual-care trusts fall under the Oklahoma Department of Securities for larger commercial cemeteries, and smaller church and fraternal cemeteries operate with essentially no state supervision at all. That means Tulsa-area cemetery pricing varies more than funeral-home pricing. Before buying preneed plots, verify the cemetery's trust status if it is a commercial operator.

A Tulsa-specific negotiating tactic

Get three Tulsa funeral homes to fax, email, or hand you their General Price Lists on the same day. The professional-services fee spread between a corporate-chain home on the south side and an independent family-owned mortuary on the north side often runs $1,500–$2,500 for functionally identical service. The Oklahoma Funeral Board does not regulate what homes charge — only that they disclose. Walk-in comparison shopping works here better than in most markets because Tulsa has a healthy independent-home tradition and little price collusion. Church and rural-cemetery savings around Tulsa

Rural counties around Tulsa — Rogers, Wagoner, Creek, Okmulgee — still have active church and township cemeteries with plot fees in the $500–$1,000 range, frequently without vault requirements. A family with church or community ties in those counties can cut the plot-plus-opening-and-closing line item by $3,000 or more. Call congregations and the county clerk's office directly; most of these options are not indexed online. Combined with direct burial and a third-party casket, the Tulsa floor for a basic burial lands near $4,500.

Veterans, green burial, and payment assistance

  • Fort Gibson National — free for eligible veterans and qualifying dependents; grave, liner, marker, and opening and closing covered. See veteran burial benefits.
  • Green burial — no certified conservation burial ground yet in Oklahoma, but hybrid sections at several Tulsa-area cemeteries accept shroud or biodegradable-container burials. The green burial options guide covers what to ask for.
  • Oklahoma Department of Human Services — limited burial allowance for qualifying indigent cases.

Common Questions

How much does burial cost in Tulsa?

A traditional burial in Tulsa costs approximately $6,405 for the funeral service plus $2,835 for a cemetery plot. Including vault ($1,134), opening/closing fees ($1,418), casket ($1,922), and headstone ($1,500-$3,000), the total ranges from $11,792 to $16,548.

Is a burial vault required in Tulsa?

Burial vaults are required by most cemeteries in the Tulsa area as a matter of cemetery policy to prevent ground settling. They are rarely required by Oklahoma state law. A grave liner — a less expensive alternative — may meet the cemetery's requirements. Always ask your specific cemetery about their vault policy and whether cheaper alternatives are accepted.

How do Tulsa burial costs compare to Oklahoma?

Burial costs in Tulsa are close to the Oklahoma state average. At $6,405 for a funeral service (vs. $6,100 statewide) and $2,835 for a cemetery plot (vs. $2,700), the Tulsa metro premium reflects higher real estate and operating costs.
