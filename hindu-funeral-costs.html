Hindu Funeral Costs: Traditions and Pricing in 2026

Reviewed: March 2026 Sources: NFDA, Religious Organizations, Consumer Surveys Editorial: Our Standards

Hindu funeral traditions center on cremation (Antyesti), which is considered essential for releasing the soul (atman) from the body. The ceremony typically includes ritual washing, dressing the body in white, a funeral pyre or modern cremation, and the scattering of ashes in a sacred body of water. A period of mourning follows, concluding with a memorial ceremony on the 13th day.

Hindu Funeral Requirements and Traditions

Understanding Hindu funeral traditions helps families honor their loved ones while making informed decisions about services and costs. Here are the key requirements and customs:

  • Cremation is required (except for young children and saints, who may be buried)
  • Cremation should ideally occur within 24 hours of death
  • The eldest son or closest male relative traditionally lights the funeral pyre or initiates cremation
  • The body is bathed, dressed in white (or red for married women), and adorned with garlands
  • Ashes should be scattered in a sacred river or body of water
  • A 13-day mourning period is observed

Ways to Save on a Hindu Funeral

Hindu cremation costs are often lower than traditional Western funerals because no casket or cemetery plot is typically needed. Hindu temples and community organizations often provide priest services at minimal cost or by donation. Families can reduce costs by choosing direct cremation and holding the ritual ceremony separately. Community support during the mourning period often includes donated food and services.

For additional cost-saving strategies, see our guides to affordable funeral options and payment assistance programs. Comparing prices from multiple providers using their FTC-required General Price Lists can also save families significant money.

Know Your Rights: Regardless of religious tradition, the FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to receive itemized pricing, choose only the services you want, and use caskets or urns purchased from third parties. These federal protections apply at every funeral home in the United States.
