Burial Costs in Missouri (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Midwest

Missouri sits squarely in the American middle on burial pricing — not cheap like the Delta South, not expensive like the coasts. The state's 114 counties between them operate hundreds of township, fraternal, and religious cemeteries whose plot prices run a fraction of what Bellefontaine in St. Louis or Mount Washington in Kansas City will quote. Across all of it, a conventional Missouri burial totals $12,690 to $17,830 in 2026, with independent family-owned funeral homes still dominant enough to keep the service half of the bill within reasonable range.

Adding the full column — $6,800 service, $3,100 plot, $2,040 casket, $1,240 vault, $1,550 opening-and-closing, plus a $1,000-$3,000 marker — a standard Missouri burial lands between $12,690 and $17,830. Embalming ($680) is not required by Missouri law when burial happens within a reasonable window, though most funeral homes will recommend it if there will be a public viewing.

St. Louis, Kansas City, and the Middle of the State

The metropolitan cemeteries set the ceiling. In St. Louis, Bellefontaine Cemetery (opened 1849 on the north side) and Calvary Cemetery next door price plots well above the state average; Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery south of the city handles the bulk of veteran burials in eastern Missouri. In Kansas City, Mount Washington Cemetery in Independence, Forest Hill Calvary in the Raytown area, and Leavenworth National Cemetery across the Kansas line are the three pivot points. Between the metros — in Columbia, Springfield, Joplin, and Cape Girardeau — commercial memorial parks coexist with town cemeteries whose plot prices can be half the metro rate for a similar piece of ground.

Missouri's Regulatory Framework

The Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors, housed within the Division of Professional Registration in Jefferson City, licenses every funeral establishment in the state and maintains public records of disciplinary actions. Pre-need contracts are governed separately by the Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors' pre-need program, which requires 100% of pre-need funds to be placed in trust or used to buy an insurance policy — a stronger consumer protection than most states. The FTC Funeral Rule binds every Missouri funeral home to price-list disclosure and the acceptance of outside caskets at no handling fee.

Help When the Money Is Not There

Missouri does not have a dedicated state funeral assistance fund, but two doors are worth knocking on. MO HealthNet (the state's Medicaid program) allows a small burial reserve to be excluded from asset limits, and the Department of Social Services administers limited emergency assistance through Family Support Division offices for families meeting TANF thresholds. County commissioners are statutorily responsible for indigent burials when no family can be located or when the family cannot pay — practice varies sharply by county, and the coroner's office is usually the right first call. Veterans have three national cemeteries (Jefferson Barracks, Springfield, Fort Leavenworth across the border) and the Missouri State Veterans Cemetery system in Bloomfield, Higginsville, Jacksonville, Fort Leonard Wood, and Springfield — all no-cost for eligible veterans and reduced-cost for spouses.

Leverage Points on Price

  • Call the county clerk before the memorial park: Missouri township and fraternal cemeteries rarely appear in Google results. The county clerk's office can point you to public cemetery fee schedules that routinely cut plot costs by $1,500 or more.
  • Favor independent funeral homes over chains: Missouri retains a strong independent family-owned sector — these firms price closer to NFDA national averages than the three national chains with Missouri locations. Ask who owns the building.

Questions and Answers

What do Missouri families spend on burial?

A conventional Missouri burial runs between $12,690 and $17,830 once every line is added: $6,800 funeral service, $3,100 plot, $2,040 casket, $1,240 vault, $1,550 opening-and-closing, and a $1,000-$3,000 marker. The state's strong independent funeral home sector keeps service pricing in the middle of the national range, and the township/fraternal cemetery layer pulls the plot average below the coastal markets.

What are the cheapest burial options in Missouri?

Three approaches cut the most in Missouri: direct burial (no viewing, no embalming, interment within a day or two — a memorial service can be held later), a plot in a township, fraternal, or congregational cemetery rather than a commercial memorial park (found through the county clerk, usually $1,500+ cheaper), and an outside casket bought online that the funeral home is required to accept under the FTC Funeral Rule at no handling fee.

Once I add every line item, what does burial actually cost in Missouri?

Full arithmetic: service ($6,800) + plot ($3,100) + casket ($2,040) + vault ($1,240) + opening-and-closing ($1,550) + marker ($1,000-$3,000) = $12,690 to $17,830. Comparing two independent funeral homes and two cemeteries — with the county clerk's public cemetery list as a third option — is the single move that moves this number the most.

What help is available for low-income Missouri families?

Missouri does not run a named state funeral assistance fund, but three channels exist. MO HealthNet allows a burial reserve exemption; the Department of Social Services Family Support Division handles limited emergency aid; and county commissioners are responsible under Missouri statute for indigent burials when no family is able to pay. Veterans get the strongest coverage — Jefferson Barracks, Springfield National, and the Missouri State Veterans Cemeteries in Bloomfield, Higginsville, Jacksonville, Fort Leonard Wood, and Springfield all offer no-cost burial for eligible veterans.
