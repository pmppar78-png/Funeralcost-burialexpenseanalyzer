Burial Costs in Washington, DC (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 24, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 What changed: Disinterment in the District of Columbia (new section) Area: Washington, DC Metro

Most Washington DC families underestimate burial costs because the funeral home quote is only one of several bills. Cemetery invoices, monument company invoices, and administrative fees are separate. Across all of them, Washington DC burial totals generally land between $18,063 and $20,063, with casket and cemetery choice driving most of the variation.

Where the DC numbers come from. The $8,023 funeral figure, casket included, is the South Atlantic median from the NFDA 2023 General Price List Study, as tabulated by Choice Mutual; NFDA reports by region, not for the District alone. The $5,252 plot, $1,204 concrete liner, and $2,084 regular-depth opening are from the St. Paul’s Rock Creek Cemetery 2025 price list and Rock Creek burial options page (sites there run $5,252 to $8,957, and Congressional Cemetery lists full plots at $8,000 to $14,000). Embalming ($750) is the median in the DC Attorney General funeral home price survey (August 2017). The $1,500–$3,500 headstone range and the whole Virginia column are our estimates, not survey data.

The total cost of burial in Washington DC, including all cemetery fees, typically ranges from $18,063 to $20,063 depending on the services and products chosen.

Ways to Reduce Burial Costs in Washington DC

  • Request itemized pricing from every provider: Funeral homes in the District must provide a General Price List under the FTC Funeral Rule. Cemeteries are not required to, but most in the DC area will share one if you ask specifically for the itemized price sheet rather than a "package."
  • Check church cemeteries: St. Paul’s Rock Creek Cemetery is run by an Episcopal parish and publishes its prices, so ask any church or nonprofit cemetery in the District for the same sheet.
  • Veterans: eligible veterans can be buried at no charge in a VA national cemetery; the closest VA-run one with open burial space is Quantico National Cemetery in Virginia. Arlington National Cemetery, run by the Army, is much closer, but its in-ground burial eligibility is the strictest of any U.S. national cemetery.

Regional Context: Burial in Washington DC and the South

The District has few active cemeteries, so a small number of operators set plot prices, and the price lists cited above show how high they run. That is why a space already owned in a family or church section can save several thousand dollars.

Save on Caskets: Under the FTC Funeral Rule, funeral homes must accept caskets purchased from third-party retailers without charging handling fees. Compare online casket retailers to save significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Washington DC?

A traditional burial in Washington DC costs approximately $8,023 for the funeral, casket included, plus $5,252 for a cemetery plot. Including a concrete liner ($1,204), opening/closing fees ($2,084), and a headstone ($1,500-$3,500), the total ranges from $18,063 to $20,063.

Is a burial vault required in Washington DC?

Burial vaults are required by most cemeteries in the Washington DC area as a matter of cemetery policy to prevent ground settling. They are rarely required by District of Columbia law. A grave liner, a less expensive alternative, may meet the cemetery's requirements. Always ask your specific cemetery about their vault policy and whether cheaper alternatives are accepted.

What are the cheapest burial options in Washington DC?

The most affordable burial options in Washington DC include: direct burial (no viewing or ceremony), which can substantially reduce costs for embalming and facility fees; green burial using a biodegradable container and no embalming; and purchasing a casket from an independent retailer, which often costs less. Plot prices also differ a lot between District cemeteries: St. Paul's Rock Creek lists plots from $5,252, while Congressional Cemetery sites run $8,000 to $14,000.

How do Washington DC burial costs compare to Virginia?

The funeral itself costs about the same: $8,023 in DC against our $8,200 Virginia estimate. The plot is where DC costs more, at $5,252 against $4,000, so the difference sits mostly in the land, not the funeral home.

Disinterment in the District of Columbia

In the District, no remains can be disinterred until the Registrar of Vital Records issues an authorization to a licensed funeral director. The authorization includes a sworn statement, under penalty of perjury, giving the reason for the disinterment. If the disinterment could be a public health threat, the DC Health Director has to be told before it is approved (D.C. Code § 7-231.17(g); checked October 29, 2026).

  • Who signs: DC Health's application needs signatures from the informant, next of kin or legal representative and from the cemetery manager, and the DC Health Director has to approve it.
  • Moving the remains: the permit has to travel with the remains to the new grave. A false statement on vital records paperwork can bring a fine of up to $12,500.

Burial rules and help in Washington, DC

The DC Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection (DLCP), Occupational and Professional Licensing (202-671-4500) licenses the funeral homes that arrange burials in DC. It can also tell you which DC office handles cemetery complaints.

Because the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count DC among the nine states that require a funeral director, a DC family may arrange a burial without one; the cemetery still sets its own rules for the grave, and the burial permit must be in hand.

A DC burial needs a disposition permit. If you switch to cremation instead, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (D.C. Code § 7-231.17).

DC stopped paying for low-income burials when its DHS Burial Assistance program closed on September 30, 2025; for questions about other District benefits, the Economic Security Administration answers at 202-727-5355. (Source: DC DHS Burial Assistance notice.) A surviving spouse or child may claim Social Security's one-time $255 death payment within two years; it is paid to them, not to the DC cemetery.

Veterans can be buried at no cost for the grave in a VA national cemetery; VA has no national cemetery with open burial space inside the District itself (its Congressional Cemetery government lots are closed). The VA cemetery locator lists the nearest open ones, including Quantico National Cemetery in Virginia.

Next steps for a burial in DC

  1. Price the DC cemetery separately: plot, opening and closing, vault or liner, and marker are often sold apart from the funeral home.
  2. Ask the DC cemetery for its rules on grave opening times, vaults and markers.
  3. Budget a DC burial without the old DHS benefit, which closed September 30, 2025, and ask a VA cemetery about a veteran's free grave.
  4. Report a funeral home that misprices a burial to the board at 202-671-4500.
