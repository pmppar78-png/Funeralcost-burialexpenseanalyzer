Burial Costs in Wisconsin (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Wisconsin burial guide reworked around the Cemetery Board, WFCAP eligibility and veterans cemeteries, and unsourced fee, family-owned and markup claims dropped. Region: Midwest

We estimate a Wisconsin burial at $16,320 to $20,820, counting a $7,200 funeral service, a $3,400 plot and the cemetery's other charges. Wisconsin regulates cemeteries through a Cemetery Board at the Department of Safety and Professional Services, and its Funeral and Cemetery Aids Program can help a family that qualifies. Below we cover both, along with the county authorization needed to move a grave and the veterans cemeteries at Harshaw, King, Spooner and Union Grove.

Town and Church Cemeteries in Wisconsin

Wisconsin has many town, church and association cemeteries as well as commercial memorial parks, and each sets its own prices. Ask the town or county clerk which cemeteries serve your area, then get written prices from more than one. Every cemetery must keep a record of each burial, so it can also confirm where family members lie.

Keeping a Wisconsin Burial Affordable

Where a Wisconsin family can save:

  • Get the cemetery's prices in writing. The FTC Funeral Rule does not reach cemeteries, so ask each one for the plot, liner, opening and perpetual care charges and compare them with our estimates of $3,400, $1,360 and $1,700.
  • Ask the town clerk. Many Wisconsin towns own or oversee a local cemetery, and the clerk can tell you who sells its lots.
  • Compare two or three funeral homes. Each must give you its General Price List, in person or by phone, and the direct burial line shows the lowest price it offers.
  • Choose direct burial. With no embalming, viewing or service, it drops several charges from the General Price List, and a memorial can follow later.
  • Price the headstone separately. A monument dealer can quote against the cemetery; ask the cemetery first what size and material it allows.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cost of burial in Wisconsin?

On our estimates a Wisconsin burial costs $16,320 to $20,820: a $7,200 funeral service, a $3,400 plot, a $2,160 casket, a $1,360 vault or liner, $1,700 to open and close the grave and a headstone between $500 and $5,000.

Must a Wisconsin grave have a vault?

No Wisconsin statute requires one; a cemetery may. A plain liner usually meets the rule for less than a vault, so ask what the cemetery accepts before paying the $1,360 we estimate. Vaults range from $800 to $10,000.

How can a Wisconsin burial cost less?

Pick direct burial, the cheapest burial on most General Price Lists, and supply your own casket if you like; the FTC Funeral Rule bars a handling fee. An eligible veteran can be buried free at Northwoods National Cemetery or one of the three state veterans cemeteries.

Why do Wisconsin cemetery prices differ?

Owners differ: towns, churches, associations and private companies each set their own fees, and the FTC Funeral Rule does not apply to them. Ask for an itemized sheet with the plot, opening and closing, vault rule, perpetual care and any membership or residency limits, and compare at least two.

What does a Wisconsin burial total?

Add $7,200 for the service, $3,400 for the plot, $2,160 for a casket, $1,360 for a vault and $1,700 for the opening, then $500 to $5,000 for a headstone: $16,320 to $20,820 before cash advance items.

Where is green burial offered in Wisconsin?

Yes. Natural Path Sanctuary in Verona, near Madison, is run by the Farley Center (608-845-8724). Burials there use no embalming fluid and no cement vaults.

Elsewhere in Wisconsin, the question to put to a local cemetery is whether it has a natural section or will waive its vault rule for one burial. A Wisconsin funeral home cannot charge a handling fee for a shroud or casket you supply.

Who signs to move a grave in Wisconsin

In Wisconsin the coroner or medical examiner of the county where a body is buried issues the authorization to disinter and rebury it. The application must be signed by the person in charge of the disinterment and by a relative or other authorized person in the law's order of priority. That order starts with the person named in the deceased's health care power of attorney, then the spouse, then an adult child, a parent, an adult sibling, a guardian and anyone else responsible for the disposition. A court order also works. The authorization cannot issue if the deceased left contrary instructions, or if someone of the same or higher priority objects (Wis. Stat. § 69.18(4); checked October 29, 2026).

Cemeteries themselves may move buried remains under a separate section, Wis. Stat. § 157.112, without this authorization. Every cemetery must also keep a written record of each burial: the name, the place of death, the burial date and the funeral director in charge. That record is where to start if a family is unsure where a relative lies.

Wisconsin burial regulators and the aids program

The Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services (608-266-2112) licenses the funeral homes that arrange burials in Wisconsin. It can also tell you which Wisconsin office handles cemetery complaints. Cemeteries themselves come under the Cemetery Board housed at DSPS, whose six members, four representing licensed cemetery authorities and two from the public, are appointed by the Governor for four-year terms.

No funeral director is required for a Wisconsin burial; Wisconsin is not among the nine states on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list, so a family can arrange the plot, opening and container with the cemetery.

A disposition permit is needed for burial. If the family chooses cremation instead, Wisconsin requires a 48-hour wait, unless death was from a contagious disease, and a coroner's or medical examiner's permit (Wis. Stat. 979.10).

Funeral and Cemetery Aids Program

For an eligible person, WFCAP pays up to $1,500 toward the funeral home and up to $1,000 toward the cemetery, with limits on the total bill (Wisconsin DHS). Social Security's $255 goes only to an eligible spouse or child.

The program pays the funeral home, cemetery or crematory directly for costs the estate, family and other resources leave unpaid, and only the provider can file the application. A person usually qualifies by having been enrolled at death in a program such as BadgerCare Plus, Medicaid for the elderly, blind or disabled, Family Care, Supplemental Security Income or a paid W-2 placement. If not, the family can ask the local agency for a posthumous decision on Medicaid eligibility before the provider applies. Providers reach the program at 888-859-0611.

Veterans

Northwoods National Cemetery in Harshaw (906-396-9276) is the one Wisconsin national cemetery the VA lists as open for new burials; Wood National Cemetery in Milwaukee is closed. The state runs veterans cemeteries in King (715-256-5000), Spooner (715-635-5360) and Union Grove (262-878-5660), and the plot, opening and marker are free for an eligible veteran (VA National Cemetery Administration).

Wisconsin burial checklist

  1. Get the cemetery's charges separately from the funeral home's.
  2. Ask if a headstone from a monument dealer is allowed.
  3. If the family qualifies for WFCAP, check that the cemetery bill fits the $1,000 limit.
  4. For a veteran, call Northwoods or the nearest state veterans cemetery before buying a plot.
  5. Report funeral home problems to DSPS at 608-266-2112.
