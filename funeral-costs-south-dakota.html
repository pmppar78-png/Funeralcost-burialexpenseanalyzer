A South Dakota funeral is rarely a single bill. It is a funeral home invoice, a cemetery invoice (if burial), and a handful of third-party charges, and the way those numbers add up in South Dakota can surprise families who only saw the headline quote. Help Paying for a Funeral in South Dakota

  • A South Dakota county-paid burial or cremation becomes a lien on the person's property, including a homestead (SDCL 28-17-1), so families with a house should ask about that before applying.
  • In South Dakota the $255 lump sum is not sent just because the home reported the death; the spouse or eligible child has two years to request it at 1-800-772-1213, and it is paid separately from any county help under SDCL 28-17-1.
  • South Dakota law expects every body to be decently buried within a reasonable time (SDCL 34-26-15); a person charged with that duty who neglects it commits a Class 1 misdemeanor and owes whoever steps in treble the burial expense (SDCL 34-26-18). For a veteran, the payer files VA Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. Eligibility details for South Dakota families are in our veteran burial benefits guide.
  • Because county help under SDCL 28-17-1 becomes a lien on the estate, it is worth checking Medicaid and charities first in our funeral payment assistance guide and how to pay for a funeral with no money.

Your Consumer Rights in South Dakota

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in South Dakota must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. The South Dakota Board of Funeral Service, listed with the Department of Health's licensing boards, takes consumer complaints, inspects funeral homes and crematories, and registers funeral service trainees; its office is in Spearfish (605-642-1600). It is not the South Dakota Funeral Association, which is a trade group that promotes the profession.

What Drives Funeral Costs in South Dakota

A South Dakota funeral bill is shaped by a short statute list as much as by the market. Every funeral establishment has to be run by a licensed manager (SDCL 36-19-30) and the State Board of Funeral Service inspects each one at least once every three years (SDCL 36-19-31). Within those rules, Midwest pricing generally lands under the US median of $8,300, and a Sioux Falls or Rapid City home can price 10-30% away from a rural South Dakota one.

At 52%, South Dakota's cremation rate is 11.4 points below the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

Planning a Funeral in South Dakota: Where to Start

Before a home is chosen in South Dakota, settle these points one at a time, starting with the county lien if the family owns a house:

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in South Dakota or burial in South Dakota.

South Dakota death certificates: $15 a copy

The South Dakota Department of Health charges $15 for each certified or informational copy of a death record, and phone or internet orders add an $11.50 expedite fee that covers regular-mail shipping (SD Department of Health, vital records fees; ARSD 44:09:06:02; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Example: three copies ordered by mail cost $45.
  • Office: Vital Records, 221 W. Capitol Avenue, Pierre (605-773-4961). The department offers a fee waiver form for military requests.

South Dakota's funeral board, county help and veterans' graves

Licensing in South Dakota sits with the South Dakota State Board of Funeral Service, which works under the Department of Health. Call 605-642-1600 to check that a funeral home is licensed, or to complain about a hidden price list, misleading sales talk or charges you never agreed to.

There is no rule that a family must use a funeral director for every step; the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count South Dakota among its nine restrictive states. Many families still hire a home for transport and paperwork and then run the service themselves at a church, a lodge or the family farm.

Paperwork from Pierre

The death certificate and the disposition permit must be filed whichever way the family goes. Certified copies come from Vital Records at the South Dakota Department of Health, 221 West Capitol Avenue in Pierre (605-773-4961), which accepts orders in person, by mail, by phone or online.

Timing a cremation

South Dakota will not allow a cremation until 24 hours have passed since the death, and the crematory also needs a signed authorization and a disposition permit (S.D. Codified Laws § 34-26A-13.1). If a viewing is planned first, that day usually passes anyway; if not, ask whether the home charges for refrigeration while you wait.

County poor relief and its catch

When someone dies without funds and no relative or friend can pay, the county commissioners have to arrange the burial or cremation (SDCL 28-17-1). The county of residence pays, or the county of death if residence is unknown. The cost then becomes a lien on what the person owned, a homestead included, so the help works more like a loan against the estate than a grant (SDCL ch. 28-14). The $255 Social Security payment does not offset this: it goes only to an eligible spouse or child who claims it within two years.

Where South Dakota veterans are buried

Black Hills National Cemetery near Sturgis (605-347-3830) is the state's national cemetery, and a funeral home schedules the burial through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run South Dakota Veterans Cemetery answers at 605-277-8094. Three tribal veterans cemeteries also take requests directly: Akicita Owicahe Lakota Freedom (605-455-1497), Sicangu Akicita Owicahe (605-259-3044) and Sisseton-Wahpeton Oyate (605-698-3388). The Sicangu number is from the Rosebud Sioux Tribe's office directory.

Before you choose a South Dakota home

  1. Ask each home for its General Price List in writing.
  2. Weigh a memorial service after cremation, with no embalming or viewing.
  3. Call the county commissioners about poor relief, and ask about the lien, before a home is picked.
  4. For a veteran, ask about Black Hills National Cemetery before paying for a private plot.
  5. If a home will not show its prices, tell the board at 605-642-1600.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in South Dakota?

We estimate $6,400 for a traditional South Dakota funeral with burial and $5,200 for a funeral with cremation, while direct cremation starts near $1,500. A cemetery plot is extra, about $2,800 on our figures. Ask homes in Sioux Falls, Rapid City or your own county for their price lists, since individual homes can be well above or below these numbers.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in South Dakota?

Direct cremation, at roughly $1,500, is about $4,900 below a $6,400 traditional funeral in South Dakota, and the difference grows once a plot near $2,800 is bought. The state's 24-hour wait before cremation (SDCL 34-26A-13.1) rarely adds cost, but ask about refrigeration.

What rights do South Dakota families have when arranging a funeral?

Under the FTC Funeral Rule a South Dakota home must hand you an itemized General Price List, give prices by phone, and accept a casket or urn you bought elsewhere without charging extra. No state law forces you to use a funeral director for every step. Complaints go to the State Board of Funeral Service at 605-642-1600.

How can I save on funeral costs in South Dakota?

Get each home's price list, choose only the services you want, and consider direct cremation with a later memorial. Eligible veterans can be buried at Black Hills National Cemetery or a state or tribal veterans cemetery instead of a private plot. County poor relief exists for those without funds, but it is repaid through a lien on the estate.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in South Dakota?

South Dakota families can normally say no. Federal rules forbid a funeral home to present embalming as a legal must when no law calls for it, and the General Price List has to carry a written notice to that effect. A home that wants it for a public viewing can say so, but that is its policy, not the state's. Leaving it out drops the $640 average line in our table.

Is South Dakota cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Below average. Our $6,400 South Dakota traditional-funeral figure is roughly 23% less than the $8,300 national median reported by the NFDA for 2023.

What is the cheapest funeral option in South Dakota?

The lowest South Dakota price is for direct cremation, around $1,500, which can happen once the 24-hour wait has passed. Direct burial is the next step up at about $3,200; it skips the service and embalming, yet the plot, about $2,800 on our figures, is a separate cost.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in South Dakota?

Ring three homes, the closest plus two in neighboring towns, and ask each for the same three prices: direct cremation, immediate burial and a memorial without a viewing. Ask too what they charge to collect the body from the place of death. Before paying a deposit, the State Board of Funeral Service at 605-642-1600 can confirm the license.
