Burial Costs in Seattle, Washington (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Washington

Seattle occupies a peculiar spot in American burial economics. Washington legalized natural organic reduction — human composting — in 2020, the first state to do so, and Recompose runs its facility in the SoDo district. That change has reshaped the alternatives available to Seattle families even when they ultimately choose traditional burial. The city's historic cemeteries — Lake View on Capitol Hill, Evergreen-Washelli in North Seattle, Calvary in the University District — still handle most conventional interments, with costs running roughly a quarter above the Washington state average.

Seattle-area burial costs generally total $20,975 to $29,525. The midpoint applies to a full-service ceremony at Bonney-Watson or Flintoft's with burial at Evergreen-Washelli; the low end is realistic for direct burial at one of the smaller King County cemeteries.

Natural organic reduction: the Seattle invention

Washington's Natural Organic Reduction law took effect in 2020, and Recompose — the first company to offer it at scale — is based in Seattle. The process transforms a body into soil over roughly two months and costs approximately $7,000 all in, inclusive of state filing fees. That puts it above direct cremation (under $1,500) but well below traditional burial (starting around $20,975). Herland Forest and Return Home offer variations. Any Seattle family weighing cremation against burial should understand this third option before signing anything — the Washington State Board of Funeral Directors & Embalmers regulates all three disposition paths identically, which means price shopping is legitimate across them.

The veteran option: Tahoma National in Kent

Tahoma National Cemetery sits about 20 miles south of downtown Seattle and handles the majority of King County veteran interments. For eligible veterans and qualifying dependents, the grave, opening and closing, outer burial container, and government marker are all provided at no charge. A family with a veteran parent who did not realize this eligibility existed can cut a full Seattle burial bill by $15,000 or more. The veteran burial benefits page walks through active-duty thresholds and spouse coverage — both wider than most families assume.

Honest negotiating tactics for the Seattle market

  • Call three cemeteries in one afternoon. Evergreen-Washelli, Lake View, and Tahoma generally yield enough spread on the plot-plus-opening side to save several thousand dollars.
  • Ask for the General Price List in writing. Bonney-Watson and Flintoft's both publish detailed itemizations; comparing them side-by-side is the cleanest way to see where the professional-services fee differs.
  • Liner versus vault. Some Seattle cemeteries accept a concrete grave liner at roughly a third of a full vault's $2,100 price. Ask in writing.
  • Third-party casket. Seattle funeral homes cannot surcharge a casket you bring in from elsewhere. Markup is often 300%+. See the casket buying guide and best online casket retailers.
  • Independent monument work. Cemetery-vendor markups on markers commonly run 30–50%; outside work is priced in the headstone cost guide.
FTC Funeral Rule in Washington: Every licensed Seattle funeral home must hand you an itemized General Price List before any arrangement conference, must accept a casket you supply without surcharge, and cannot bundle services you did not ask for. The FTC Funeral Rule guide covers enforcement if a provider pushes back.

Seattle vs. Washington and the West Coast

Washington's cremation rate — roughly 76% — is among the highest in the country, and the Pacific Northwest broadly has been receptive to natural burial and composting. That market pressure has forced traditional burial providers to compete differently: more hybrid sections, more published pricing, more willingness to sell the plot independently from the mortuary services. Seattle runs about 25% above the Washington state average — $11,000 against $8,800 for service, and $5,250 against $4,200 for a plot. The premium is concentrated in King County real estate; Spokane, Tacoma, and eastern Washington generally come in closer to the statewide figure.

King County indigent burial and payment help

King County Medical Examiner's Indigent Burial program assists with limited disposition costs for qualifying low-income decedents; cremation is the default under that program. Families with a veteran decedent should always check Tahoma eligibility first — the Department of Veterans Affairs benefit supersedes almost every other assistance path. For civilian families outside the county program, the funeral payment assistance guide covers state DSHS burial assistance, FEMA funeral reimbursement where applicable, and nonprofit options. Households choosing the least expensive path entirely should also weigh cheapest funeral options and green burial sections, both of which strip out several mid-bill line items simultaneously.

Common Questions We Hear

How much does burial cost in Seattle?

A traditional burial in Seattle costs approximately $11,000 for the funeral service plus $5,250 for a cemetery plot. Including vault ($2,100), opening/closing fees ($2,625), casket ($3,300), and headstone ($1,500-$3,000), the total ranges from $20,975 to $29,525.

Is a burial vault required in Seattle?

Washington law does not require a burial vault. The policy is set by each cemetery — Evergreen-Washelli, Lake View, and Calvary each enforce their own rule, mostly to stabilize the ground for maintenance. A concrete grave liner is often accepted as a lower-cost substitute and runs roughly 60% below a full vault.

How do Seattle burial costs compare to Washington?

Seattle runs about 25% above the Washington state average — $11,000 for service against $8,800 statewide, and $5,250 for a plot against $4,200. The premium is concentrated in King County real estate; Spokane, Tacoma, and the eastern Washington markets generally come in closer to the state average.
