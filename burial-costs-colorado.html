Burial Costs in Colorado (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Mountain

If you are arranging a burial in Colorado, the costs can add up faster than most families expect. Between the funeral home bill and the separate cemetery invoice, total burial expenses in Colorado typically range from $15,220 to well over $21,420. This guide lays out what drives each line item and where families have room to save.

Those numbers assume a commercial cemetery in the Front Range corridor. Add the fact that many mountain cemeteries close ground operations between mid-November and mid-April — the elevation makes winter grave opening impractical or impossible — and the real annual cost range widens further, because a December death in Summit County usually means either hold-over vault storage or shipment to a lower-elevation cemetery.

The Colorado Licensing Quirk That Saves Savvy Families Money

Colorado is one of only a small handful of states — Alabama, Arkansas, and a couple of others — that does not license individual funeral directors. The state instead registers funeral establishments and crematories through the Colorado Office of Funeral Home and Crematory Registration within the Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA). That means the person arranging your burial in Denver, Boulder, or Grand Junction does not have to hold a state license; the business where they work does. For consumers, the practical implication is that price variation between Colorado funeral homes is larger than in most states, because there is no state-mandated education or apprenticeship bar entry. It also means shopping aggressively — getting three GPLs before deciding — is more important here than in, say, New York or Ohio. DORA does maintain a public complaint database; it is worth a quick look before signing anything.

Front Range Cemeteries Worth Knowing

Riverside Cemetery in north Denver, established in 1876, is Colorado's oldest continuously operating cemetery and a Denver historic landmark; it is still actively selling plots. Fairmount Cemetery near Lowry, opened in 1890, is the large historic Denver memorial park with the Ivy Chapel and an arboretum collection. Crown Hill Cemetery in Wheat Ridge and Mount Olivet Cemetery in Wheat Ridge (Catholic, Archdiocese of Denver) are the large west-metro options. For veterans, Fort Logan National Cemetery in southwest Denver is the major regional VA cemetery; interment is free for eligible veterans and spouses and Fort Logan has been one of the most active national cemeteries in the system for years. Colorado also operates state veterans cemeteries — Veterans Memorial Cemetery of Western Colorado in Grand Junction and Pikes Peak National Cemetery in Colorado Springs (opened 2018) — which give mountain and western-slope families a closer option than Fort Logan.

Natural and Green Burial Is More Developed Here Than Almost Anywhere Else

Colorado is arguably the epicenter of the American natural-burial movement. The Crestone End of Life Project in Saguache County operates the only licensed outdoor open-air cremation ground in the United States — a Vedic-inspired pyre site available to community members and, with approval, to outside families — and also supports natural burial. Seven Stones in Littleton and Natural Funeral in Lafayette, among others, offer greener burial options on the Front Range. Colorado also permits human composting (terramation) and has some of the most permissive family- and home-burial laws in the country: with the right paperwork and land, a family plot on private property is legal in many counties. For families with the acreage and the inclination, that path eliminates commercial cemetery fees entirely.

Denver Metro vs. Mountain Counties vs. Rural Plains

Front Range burial costs, especially in Denver and Boulder counties, run materially above the state average. Mountain counties are more expensive still, largely because of labor and access. Rural eastern and southern Colorado — Yuma, Kiowa, Bent, Prowers, Baca counties — typically runs 30–50% below Denver. If a family has flexibility on location, the cemetery line item alone can shift by several thousand dollars.

How Colorado Families Actually Reduce the Bill

  • Use Fort Logan, Pikes Peak, or the Grand Junction state veterans cemetery. Free interment for eligible veterans and spouses, plus a government-furnished headstone. This is the biggest single lever for families with any veteran connection.
  • Because Colorado does not license individual funeral directors, shop aggressively. Get three itemized price lists. The spread between Front Range independents and corporate chains is usually larger than in licensed-director states.
  • Explore green, terramation, or open-air cremation at Crestone. Colorado's regulatory environment makes paths available here that simply are not offered in most states. Prices are typically well below a traditional burial.
  • Consider the plains if you do not need a Front Range location. Yuma, Kit Carson, Bent, and Prowers County cemeteries can price plots 40–60% below Denver.
  • Plan around seasonal ground closures. Many mountain cemeteries above 7,500 feet hold remains in vault storage over winter and inter in spring. Ask about storage fees before you assume a standard timeline.
  • Family or home burial where permitted. Colorado's home-funeral paperwork, administered county-by-county, is more permissive than most states. The home funeral guide and green burial options page cover specifics.

Questions Families Ask

What is the full price of burial in Colorado?

Adding a Colorado funeral service ($8,000), a cemetery plot ($3,800), a casket ($2,400), vault ($1,520), opening and closing ($1,900), and a headstone ($1,500–$3,000) yields a total between $15,220 and $21,420 for a full commercial burial. Denver metro families pay toward the upper end; rural Eastern Plains families often come in 30–40% below that floor. Veterans buried at Fort Logan or Pikes Peak National Cemetery pay only the funeral home service fees.

Is a burial vault required in Colorado?

Colorado state law does not require a vault. Most Front Range commercial cemeteries — Fairmount, Crown Hill, Mount Olivet, Riverside — require one as a maintenance policy to prevent ground settling. Natural burial grounds and some rural county cemeteries accept a concrete grave liner or no vault at all; Colorado's natural burial providers (Seven Stones, Natural Funeral, the Crestone End of Life Project) deliberately allow a shrouded or biodegradable container with no vault. Always confirm the specific cemetery's policy.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in Colorado?

Colorado has unusually wide floor-to-ceiling variation because the state's burial laws and natural-burial providers are permissive. Cheapest paths: family burial on private land where county law allows; natural burial at a dedicated green cemetery; direct burial at a rural Eastern Plains cemetery using a casket ordered online; or — for eligible veterans — Fort Logan, Pikes Peak, or Grand Junction veterans cemeteries. Terramation (human composting) and the Crestone open-air cremation ground are Colorado-specific options that sit well below a traditional burial.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Colorado?

Get itemized price sheets from at least three cemeteries: plot, opening-and-closing, vault or liner policy, perpetual care, monument-installation fee, residency or denominational restrictions, and — critically for Colorado — any seasonal ground-closing window at elevation. Rural plains county cemeteries are often administered by the county clerk rather than listed online. Because Colorado does not license individual funeral directors, price variation among funeral homes is wider than most states; shop funeral homes aggressively too.

What is the total cost of burial in Colorado including everything?

A Colorado burial with funeral service ($8,000), plot ($3,800), casket ($2,400), vault ($1,520), opening and closing ($1,900), and headstone ($1,500–$3,000) typically totals $15,220 to $21,420. Winter interments at mountain elevations may add vault-storage fees if the cemetery is closed to ground operations until spring. Veterans eligible for Fort Logan or Pikes Peak National Cemetery remove the cemetery-side lines almost entirely.

Does Colorado have green burial cemeteries?

Yes — Colorado is arguably the most developed green-burial state in the country. Seven Stones in Littleton, Natural Funeral in Lafayette, and the Crestone End of Life Project in Saguache County all offer natural burial without embalming or vaults. Colorado also legally permits terramation (human composting) and, uniquely, Crestone operates the only licensed open-air outdoor cremation ground in the United States. For families open to these alternatives, pricing typically sits well below a conventional commercial burial.
