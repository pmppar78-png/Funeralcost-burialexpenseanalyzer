Burial Costs in Los Angeles, California (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: California

Los Angeles has the most expensive traditional-burial market in the United States. The combination of Forest Lawn's multi-location tier pricing, limited cemetery capacity relative to county population (10.1 million people), California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau oversight, and metro-area labor and real estate costs has pushed the 2026 LA County range to $25,600 to $36,075 — nearly double the national average. Hollywood Forever, Forest Lawn Hollywood Hills and Glendale, Westwood Village Memorial Park (where Marilyn Monroe, Natalie Wood, Carroll O'Connor, and Truman Capote are buried), and Inglewood Park Cemetery are the largest operators. Each sets its own pricing tier and the difference between them on a single plot can exceed $10,000.

The 2026 Los Angeles total ranges from $25,600 to $36,075. The spread is driven almost entirely by cemetery selection — Westwood Village, Forest Lawn Hollywood Hills premium sections, and Hollywood Forever garden plots can push totals north of $50,000, while Riverside National Cemetery burial removes most of the cemetery bill entirely for eligible veterans.

Forest Lawn's tier pricing and the LA cemetery hierarchy

Forest Lawn Memorial-Parks operates six locations across Southern California — Glendale (the original, opened 1906), Hollywood Hills, Covina Hills, Cypress, Long Beach, and Cathedral City. Each location has its own pricing tier. Glendale's historic sections (Whispering Pines, Sunrise Slope, Terrace of Dawn, and the ultra-premium Memorial Terrace) price single plots anywhere from $4,500 to more than $45,000 depending on section, sightlines, and proximity to mausoleums. Hollywood Hills, which opened in 1948, prices similarly for its garden-court sections. Forest Lawn does not publish complete pricing online — the walk-in consultation is where the tiering surfaces.

Westwood Village Memorial Park in West LA — 2.5 acres surrounded by Wilshire Boulevard office towers — quotes premium single plots well above $40,000 when inventory is available. Hollywood Forever on Santa Monica Boulevard (where Johnny Ramone, Judy Garland, Rudolph Valentino, and Cecil B. DeMille are buried) runs $5,500–$18,000 on standard sections. Mount Sinai Memorial Park (Hollywood Hills and Simi Valley), serving LA's Jewish community, prices $5,000–$12,000 for standard plots with end-of-life services. Rose Hills Memorial Park in Whittier — the largest cemetery in North America by acreage at 1,400 acres — is the most accessible mid-tier option, with standard plots at $4,500–$8,000.

Inglewood Park Cemetery (home to Ray Charles, Ella Fitzgerald, and Ethel Waters) and Angeles Abbey Memorial Park in Compton round out the broader metro. The Catholic Archdiocese of Los Angeles operates numerous cemeteries — Holy Cross (Culver City), Calvary (East LA), and San Fernando Mission (Mission Hills) among them — typically priced $3,800–$7,500 for practicing Catholics, with diocesan burial packages bundled.

Riverside and Los Angeles National Cemeteries

Los Angeles National Cemetery in Westwood (near the VA Medical Center on Wilshire) is the historic VA cemetery — established 1889 but effectively closed to new first interments since 1978 due to capacity. Riverside National Cemetery, in western Riverside County about 60 miles east of downtown LA, is the primary active VA cemetery for Southern California veterans. It covers plot, vault, marker, and opening-and-closing at no cost for eligible veterans; spouses are eligible for burial alongside. With 265,000 interments, Riverside National is the largest VA cemetery in the country. Bakersfield National Cemetery provides additional capacity for the northern reaches of the LA region.

Shop the state line and county line. LA County prices are the peak; Orange County (Pacific View Memorial Park in Corona del Mar, Harbor Lawn in Costa Mesa) runs 10–20% below. Riverside and San Bernardino County cemeteries — Montecito Memorial Park, Green Acres, Desert Lawn — price another 15–25% below Orange County. A family willing to bury outside LA County can recover $5,000–$12,000 on plot and opening-and-closing alone. California funeral homes across county lines will transport for a modest fee.

Where Los Angeles burial totals actually move

  1. Cemetery tier. The largest single variable. Westwood Village or a Forest Lawn Glendale premium section can exceed the entire out-of-pocket range on this page by itself.
  2. Service scope. Direct burial strips chapel use, visitation, and embalming. In the LA market direct burial saves $5,000–$9,000 versus full visitation-plus-service.
  3. Casket sourcing. California funeral homes cannot refuse a third-party casket or charge a handling fee under the FTC Funeral Rule. Titan Casket, Overstock, Walmart, and Costco all ship to LA. Savings $1,500–$4,500.
  4. Independent vs. corporate chain. SCI/Dignity Memorial owns Hollywood Forever, Forest Lawn is a nonprofit but priced comparably, and corporate chains operate widely in the LA funeral-home market. Independents — Malloy-Mitten, Pierce Bros. (now SCI), McCormick & Son, Hollywood Forever-adjacent independents — typically price $2,000–$5,000 below chain quotes on equivalent packages.
  5. Pre-need contracts. California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau regulates pre-need trusting; Business and Professions Code Section 7735 requires 70% of pre-need funds to be held in trust. A family signing pre-need at an LA cemetery should confirm the trust percentage and the consumer-refund terms before paying.

Green burial and sea scattering in LA

Joshua Tree Memorial Park in the Morongo Valley (about 2.5 hours east) operates a dedicated natural-burial section; Eternal Valley Memorial Park in Newhall has offered hybrid green-burial inventory. True conservation burial in LA County is extremely limited. For families considering cremation-with-scattering, California permits at-sea scattering beyond 3 nautical miles under EPA rules; several Santa Monica Bay and Catalina Channel charter operators conduct scattering voyages at $500–$1,500 per family. Private-land burial is legal in California under Health and Safety Code Section 7500 subject to county health department approval, though practical application within LA County is rare given land use and zoning.

Reader Questions

How much does burial cost in Los Angeles?

The 2026 LA County traditional burial range is $25,600 to $36,075 — the highest in the country. A direct burial with a Rose Hills Whittier or archdiocesan Holy Cross plot can close near $15,500. A full service at Westwood Village Memorial Park or a Forest Lawn Glendale premium section can exceed $55,000 when mausoleum crypts, monuments, and premium caskets are factored in. Riverside National Cemetery removes most of the bill entirely for eligible veterans.

What are the cheapest burial options in Los Angeles?

The lowest-cost routes in LA County: direct burial through an LA independent funeral home paired with an archdiocesan cemetery (Holy Cross, Calvary, San Fernando Mission) or Rose Hills Whittier; casket purchased online under the FTC Funeral Rule; liner rather than sealed vault where accepted; and, for families willing to travel, a cemetery in Riverside, San Bernardino, or Orange County. For eligible veterans, Riverside National Cemetery eliminates plot, vault, marker, and opening-closing costs.

How do Los Angeles burial costs compare to California?

Los Angeles runs about 25% above the California statewide average — funeral service $13,250 vs. $10,600, plot $6,500 vs. $5,200. San Francisco runs slightly higher than LA on plot pricing; San Jose matches LA closely. San Diego is 4–5% below LA. California's inland counties (San Bernardino, Riverside, Kern, Tulare) drop to around the state average or below. Forest Lawn's tier pricing in particular distorts LA County averages upward — premium sections can exceed statewide medians by an order of magnitude.
