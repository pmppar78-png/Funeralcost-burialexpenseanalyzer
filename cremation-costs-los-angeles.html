Cremation Costs in Los Angeles, California (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: California Cremation Rate: 65%

Los Angeles runs 25% above the California statewide average for direct cremation — the largest premium of any California metro we track — and the dynamics behind that premium are specific to LA County's 10.2 million residents. Forest Lawn operates six massive campuses across the metro, from Glendale and Hollywood Hills to Cypress and Covina; Pierce Brothers runs multiple locations including Westwood Village Memorial Park (where Marilyn Monroe is interred); Neptune Society operates its LA flagship in Burbank; and Cremation Society of Los Angeles publishes flat rates below $1,500 as a direct counterweight to the full-service chains.

Forest Lawn, Pierce Brothers, and the LA cremation oligopoly

Forest Lawn Memorial-Parks & Mortuaries operates the largest cemetery-funeral-crematory network in the metro with locations in Glendale, Hollywood Hills, Long Beach, Cypress, Covina Hills, and Coachella Valley. Pierce Brothers (Dignity Memorial/SCI) runs Westwood Village, Hollywood Forever, Valley Oaks, and Valhalla Memorial Park. Rose Hills Memorial Park in Whittier is the largest single cemetery in North America with an on-site mortuary and crematory. Beyond the chains, Neptune Society, Cremation Society of Los Angeles, Tulip Cremation, and Solace Cremation dominate the flat-rate direct segment — most under $1,500. Small independent operators like Groman Mortuary in Hollywood and Angelus Funeral Home in Crenshaw still serve specific community niches.

Los Angeles County cremation permits

California Health and Safety Code requires a 48-hour waiting period between death and cremation, a permit from the local registrar of vital statistics, and a signed Declaration for Disposition of Cremated Remains. In LA County, the Department of Public Health handles death registration; permits typically issue within 24-72 hours of the death certificate being signed. Deaths referred to the LA County Department of Medical Examiner (the "Los Angeles County Coroner") at 1104 North Mission Road extend the timeline to two or three weeks when autopsy or toxicology is ordered — an outsize share of LA cases given the county's population and the ME office's 24/7 intake. The California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau at cfb.ca.gov licenses crematories and funeral establishments.

The Pacific, Catalina, and forest scattering

LA's scattering geography is unusually rich. Pacific Ocean scattering three nautical miles offshore is permitted under EPA regulations with notification filed within 30 days; charter boats out of Marina del Rey, San Pedro, and Long Beach charge $350 to $1,500 for group scattering. Catalina Island allows scattering with the landowner's consent (most of the island is managed by the Catalina Island Conservancy, which grants limited permits). Angeles National Forest and Los Padres National Forest permit scattering with ranger-district written permission. California State Parks — Malibu Creek, Griffith Park, Topanga — require park-office approval. Beach scattering is generally not permitted on LA County beaches below the high-tide line. Los Angeles National Cemetery off Wilshire Boulevard is closed to new interments; Riverside National Cemetery, an hour east, provides free inurnment for eligible veterans.

LA-specific cost levers

  • Flat-rate direct-cremation providers beat chain pricing by $1,000+. Tulip Cremation, Solace Cremation, Cremation Society of Los Angeles, and Neptune Society all publish direct cremation under $1,500 — roughly half the metro average of $2,750 charged by full-service chains.
  • The California preneed funeral trust rule (Business and Professions Code 7735) requires 100% of preneed funds to be deposited in a master trust managed by the California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau — one of the strongest consumer protections in the country.
  • LA County Department of Public Social Services operates an indigent cremation program for residents below income thresholds, contracted through rotating county providers; eligibility verified through DPSS.
  • Veterans qualify for Riverside National Cemetery (LA National is closed to new interments), which offers free inurnment and a government-issued niche cover.

Common Questions

How much does cremation cost in Los Angeles?

Direct cremation through a full-service LA funeral home averages $2,750 — but flat-rate providers like Tulip, Solace, Cremation Society of Los Angeles, and Neptune Society price direct cremation under $1,500. Full-service cremation with viewing and ceremony runs $9,750 at Forest Lawn or Pierce Brothers, reflecting LA's elevated venue and staffing costs. Urns, memorial services, and scattering add $200 to $3,000.

What types of cremation are available in Los Angeles?

Los Angeles families can choose from: direct cremation ($2,750) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service — a ceremony held after cremation; and traditional cremation ($9,750) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Los Angeles?

By a massive margin. Burial plots at Forest Lawn Glendale or Westwood Village Memorial Park run from $15,000 to well over $100,000 (a single plot at Westwood sold for more than $300,000 in recent years). Complete burial packages at LA's mid-tier cemeteries still total $12,000 to $20,000 before endowment fees. Direct cremation at $2,750 — or $1,500 through a flat-rate provider — saves at least $10,000. Riverside National Cemetery offers free inurnment for eligible veterans.

Where can I compare cremation prices in Los Angeles?

Every LA provider is required by the FTC Funeral Rule to share a General Price List on request, by phone or in person. Forest Lawn, Pierce Brothers, Rose Hills, Neptune Society, Tulip Cremation, Solace, and Cremation Society of Los Angeles all publish quotable pricing. The California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau at cfb.ca.gov maintains the licensing registry and complaint database. Funeral Consumers Alliance of Los Angeles runs periodic price surveys.

Can I scatter ashes in Los Angeles?

Pacific scattering three nautical miles offshore is permitted under EPA rules with 30-day written notification; Marina del Rey, San Pedro, and Long Beach charters run $350-$1,500. Angeles National Forest and Los Padres National Forest require ranger-district permits. LA County beaches below the high-tide line are generally off-limits. Catalina Island, Griffith Park, Malibu Creek, and other state-park lands require written permission from the managing agency. Private property scattering is permitted with the landowner's consent.
