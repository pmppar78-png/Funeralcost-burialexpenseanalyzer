Cremation Costs in Wisconsin (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 58%

Wisconsin's 58% cremation rate sits comfortably above the national midpoint, and the state runs a distinctive two-regulator structure where the Funeral Directors Examining Board handles mortuaries and a separate Cemetery Association registration governs burial grounds. Direct cremation starts at $1,700 in 2026, Milwaukee and Madison anchor the market, and winter frozen-ground scheduling still shapes memorial timing. Here's what Wisconsin families actually pay and how the regulatory split affects them.

Wisconsin's two-regulator model: Examining Board and Cemetery Association registration

Wisconsin splits funeral oversight. The Wisconsin Funeral Directors Examining Board licenses mortuaries, embalmers, and cremation authorization paperwork. Cemeteries operate under a separate Cemetery Association registration requirement administered by the Department of Safety and Professional Services. For a Wisconsin family, the practical consequence is that a cremation-pricing dispute goes to the Examining Board, while a columbarium niche or perpetual-care question goes to DSPS. Consumers regularly file with the wrong body; both agencies accept written complaints and will redirect, but the handoff adds weeks.

Krause in Milwaukee, Cress in Madison, and the Wisconsin chain landscape

Krause Funeral Home runs multiple Milwaukee-area locations and typically lists direct cremation at or slightly above the $1,700 state benchmark. Cress Funeral Service anchors the Madison metro with a similar pricing posture. Prasser-Kleczka and the regional cooperative providers serve the Fox Valley and northern Wisconsin. Wisconsin is unusual in that most crematories are operated by funeral homes rather than standalone facilities, which means direct cremation in the state typically routes through a funeral home intake even when no service is attached. That does not change the $1,700 floor materially, but it does mean families should ask specifically for the "direct cremation only" figure rather than a bundled package.

Wood National Cemetery at Zablocki VA and Wisconsin veteran options

Wood National Cemetery, located on the Zablocki VA Medical Center grounds in Milwaukee, accepts eligible-veteran cremated remains at no cost and is one of the oldest national cemeteries in the Midwest. Central Wisconsin Veterans Memorial Cemetery at King and the Southern Wisconsin Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Union Grove extend coverage for families outside the Milwaukee area. Combining a Krause or Cress $1,700 direct cremation with inurnment at one of the veteran cemeteries lands the total out-of-pocket below $2,000 for an eligible family.

Forest Home, Forest Hill, and Wisconsin cemetery pricing

Forest Home Cemetery in Milwaukee (established 1850) and Forest Hill Cemetery in Madison (1858) are Wisconsin's historic benchmarks. Both accept cremation inurnments in ground sections and, increasingly, in columbaria built in recent decades. Historic-section pricing at either runs above the Wisconsin median; open-section cremation inurnment sits in the $1,200-$3,000 range including opening-and-closing. County-owned cemeteries and township burial grounds across rural Wisconsin routinely price niches below $800, a meaningful spread if the family is flexible on location.

Winter frozen-ground scheduling and County Human Services assistance

Wisconsin winter ground conditions affect burial far more than cremation, but they do push many winter-death families to schedule a spring interment of ashes rather than a December or January one. Cremation providers can hold ashes for the family indefinitely at no additional cost in most cases. For financial assistance, Wisconsin's county-level Human Services offices administer an indigent burial benefit that applies to cremation arrangements; the cap is modest, but stacked with the Social Security lump-sum death payment, it closes most of the gap on a $1,700 direct cremation for a qualifying family.

Answers to the Questions We Hear Most

What is the typical price of cremation in Wisconsin?

Wisconsin cremation pricing lives in two tiers: direct cremation — transport, cremation, and return of ashes — starting around $1,700, and full-service cremation with a ceremony landing near $5,800. Urns ($50-$3,000), memorial venues ($500-$2,000), and scattering or inurnment fees ($200-$600) are separate line items most providers will quote on request.

What types of cremation are available in Wisconsin?

Families in Wisconsin can choose from three main types: direct cremation ($1,700) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service (ceremony held after cremation, often more flexible on timing); and traditional cremation ($5,800) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each option can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget.

Does Wisconsin have specific rules for cremation?

Yes. Wisconsin requires written authorization from the legal next of kin, a standard 24-48 hour waiting period, and in some counties a permit from the medical examiner before cremation can proceed. Pacemakers and certain implants must be removed first. The FTC Funeral Rule (federal) separately guarantees you do not need a casket for cremation — a basic cremation container suffices.

Where are ashes allowed to be scattered in Wisconsin?

Wisconsin scattering rules depend on the specific location. Private land is generally fine with the owner's permission; at sea requires being at least 3 nautical miles offshore per EPA rules; many state and national parks need a permit (and in some cases prohibit it entirely). Check the local ordinance before choosing a spot. Families in Wisconsin also often opt for columbarium niches, burial of the urn, or keepsake jewelry in place of scattering.

Why is cremation so popular in Wisconsin?

The 58% figure for Wisconsin lines up with what most states are seeing: affordability first, a more flexible memorial window second, and slow cultural normalization of cremation as a standard choice rather than a departure.

How quickly can cremation be completed in Wisconsin?

From start to ashes, most cremations in Wisconsin take 4 to 10 days. The cremation itself is 2-3 hours, but the mandatory 24-48 hour pre-cremation waiting period and the 3-7 business day processing window dominate the timeline. Direct cremation at $1,700 moves fastest since there is no viewing or service to schedule beforehand.

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Wisconsin?

Yes. Wisconsin funeral homes regularly arrange visitation and a ceremony before cremation takes place — the full-service option typically runs around $5,800. If the ceremony timing is flexible, a memorial service after cremation (at any venue, anytime) is another option and usually costs less than the full traditional route.
