Burial Costs in Virginia Beach, Virginia (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Virginia

Virginia Beach's burial market reflects the Virginia cost-of-living multiplier layered on national averages. A full traditional burial here — funeral service, cemetery plot, vault, casket, headstone — commonly runs $16,590 to $23,373. The funeral home portion ($8,610) and cemetery portion ($4,200 for the plot alone) are the two largest line items.

Hampton Roads water tables and what they do to a burial

Virginia Beach and the surrounding Hampton Roads region — Norfolk, Portsmouth, Chesapeake, Suffolk — sit on some of the shallowest water tables on the East Coast, and coastal subsidence means the situation is trending worse, not better. Cemeteries here overwhelmingly require reinforced concrete vaults for in-ground burial, and several have shifted marketing toward mausoleum entombment and high-rise columbaria for practical reasons. Princess Anne Memorial Park on Virginia Beach Boulevard, Rosewood Memorial Park, and Virginia Beach Memorial Garden all publish vault-inclusive packages; always ask whether above-ground placement would be cheaper in your specific section — in low-lying ground it routinely is.

The VA Board of Funeral Directors and Perpetual Care Trust law

The Virginia Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers regulates funeral homes; cemetery perpetual-care funds are separately governed under Virginia's Perpetual Care Trust Law, which requires commercial cemeteries to deposit a portion of each plot sale into a trust dedicated to long-term maintenance. Before buying a preneed plot at Princess Anne Memorial or Rosewood, verify the cemetery's trust status through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. It is a layer of consumer protection that many families do not realize exists.

FTC Funeral Rule in Hampton Roads: Every Virginia Beach funeral home must provide an itemized General Price List, cannot surcharge a third-party casket, and must allow you to decline any service you do not want. Local casket markup often runs 300–400% — the casket buying guide and best online casket retailers show the alternative pricing. This applies identically in Norfolk, Portsmouth, and Chesapeake.

Veterans, Albert G. Horton, and the Hampton National limit

Hampton National Cemetery — established during the Civil War and home to thousands of Union soldiers — has limited new casket inventory; columbarium niches remain but full-casket plots are scarce. The Albert G. Horton Jr. Memorial Veterans Cemetery in Suffolk, about 30 miles west of Virginia Beach, is the state-run option now handling most regional veteran burials with full free interment for eligible veterans and qualifying dependents. For any family with a military decedent this is the single largest available cost reduction. Full eligibility details in veteran burial benefits.

An honest negotiating tactic for Virginia Beach

Request three General Price Lists in person on the same day — Virginia Beach has sufficient funeral-home density that comparison shopping works — then do the same with cemeteries. Ask every cemetery for a side-by-side quote of in-ground-with-vault versus mausoleum entombment, because in low-lying Princess Anne Memorial and Rosewood sections, above-ground is frequently cheaper than the plot-plus-vault combination. Pairing the cheapest mortuary professional-services fee with the cheapest disposition method and a third-party casket cuts $3,500 or more from the state midpoint. Monument work via an independent stone company beats cemetery-vendor pricing 25–35% (headstone cost guide).

Green burial and payment help

Virginia Beach Memorial Garden has developed green-burial sections, joining a small but growing set of coastal Virginia cemeteries allowing shroud or biodegradable-container interment without vault or embalming. Green burial here typically totals $4,500–$7,500, a meaningful reduction against the traditional midpoint of $19,900. The green burial options guide covers what to ask for. For payment assistance, Virginia Beach Department of Human Services administers limited indigent-burial help; broader paths (Social Security lump-sum, FEMA, church-community) are covered in funeral payment assistance and cheapest funeral options.

Questions and Answers

How much does burial cost in Virginia Beach?

A traditional burial in Virginia Beach costs approximately $8,610 for the funeral service plus $4,200 for a cemetery plot. Including vault ($1,680), opening/closing fees ($2,100), casket ($2,583), and headstone ($1,500-$3,000), the total ranges from $16,590 to $23,373.

Is a burial vault required in Virginia Beach?

Burial vaults are required by most cemeteries in the Virginia Beach area as a matter of cemetery policy to prevent ground settling. They are rarely required by Virginia state law. A grave liner — a less expensive alternative — may meet the cemetery's requirements. Always ask your specific cemetery about their vault policy and whether cheaper alternatives are accepted.

How do Virginia Beach burial costs compare to Virginia?

Burial costs in Virginia Beach are close to the Virginia state average. At $8,610 for a funeral service (vs. $8,200 statewide) and $4,200 for a cemetery plot (vs. $4,000), the Virginia Beach metro premium reflects higher real estate and operating costs.
