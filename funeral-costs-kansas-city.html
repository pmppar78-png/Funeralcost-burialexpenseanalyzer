Funeral Costs in Kansas City, Missouri (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Missouri

Kansas City spans a state line, and so does its funeral market. The Missouri side — Jackson, Clay, Platte, and Cass counties — runs roughly $7,480 for a full traditional service, while the Kansas side in Wyandotte and Johnson counties trims that to around $6,900 thanks to lower licensing and facility overhead. Major operators include D.W. Newcomer's Sons (the Speaks Chapels family, founded in Kansas City in 1873), Muehlebach Funeral Home in the Northland, McGilley & Sheil, Passantino Bros., and the low-cost specialist Heartland Cremation & Burial Society in Raytown. Mount Moriah Cemetery on Holmes, Forest Hill on Troost (the Satchel Paige gravesite), and Memorial Park in Independence anchor the Missouri-side plot market; cremation rates across the bi-state metro are running close to 55 percent, ahead of Missouri's 52 percent figure.

The State-Line Arbitrage: Missouri vs. Kansas Pricing

Few metros let you change states with a turn signal. A funeral home five minutes apart — say, McGilley & Hoffmann in Kansas City, Missouri versus Porter Funeral Home in Lenexa, Kansas — can differ $600-$1,100 on an identical traditional service. The driver is a combination of Missouri's stricter facility inspection schedule (roughly annual), higher property costs in the downtown and Plaza districts, and Missouri's pre-need trust rules that push more administrative cost into retail pricing. For families without a strong denominational attachment to a specific Kansas City chapel, pulling GPLs from both sides of State Line Road is the single largest lever available. Cremation is transportable across state lines; a body can cross from Kansas to Missouri for a cheaper direct cremation with only the usual transit permit from the originating county.

Mount Moriah, Forest Hill, and the Cemetery Map

Kansas City's cemetery landscape is unusually rich for a metro its size. Mount Moriah Cemetery & Funeral Home operates locations on Holmes and in Gashland; Forest Hill on Troost Avenue holds Satchel Paige and several Negro Leagues figures; Memorial Park in Independence and Resurrection on I-435 serve the Catholic market; Floral Hills in Kansas City, Missouri holds a lot of the east-side volume. Plot pricing now runs $1,800-$3,200 in standard sections and $4,500-$7,000 in family or feature lots. Leavenworth National Cemetery (established 1886) and Kansas City's Fort Leavenworth Main Post Cemetery provide VA-eligible veterans no-cost burial, opening and closing, liner, and marker. The Missouri Veterans Cemetery in Higginsville, roughly an hour east, is another no-cost option.

Line Items Kansas City Families Miss on the First Read

  • Basic services of funeral director and staff — typically $2,100-$3,200 Missouri-side, $1,800-$2,700 Kansas-side.
  • State-line transfer fees — if the death occurs in one state and services are in the other, some providers add a crossover charge. Ask upfront.
  • Facility use for visitation — often split from service use and billed per half-day. Sunday surcharges are common.
  • Cash advance items — Kansas City Star obituaries, clergy honoraria (Catholic Diocese of Kansas City-St. Joseph rates vs. independent), certified death certificates ($15 in Missouri via Jackson County, $15 in Kansas via the Office of Vital Statistics).
  • Third-party caskets — Missouri and Kansas both follow the FTC Funeral Rule. Costco, Walmart, and online retailers must be accepted without a handling surcharge.

Jackson County and Bi-State Burial Assistance

Missouri's burial assistance landscape runs through the Missouri Funeral Assistance Program administered via MO HealthNet, which can contribute up to $1,000-$1,200 toward indigent burial or cremation for qualifying Medicaid recipients. Jackson County also operates a limited indigent disposition fund via the medical examiner's office. On the Kansas side, Wyandotte County's General Assistance program and Johnson County's Multi-Service Center can sometimes contribute toward direct cremation. The Social Security lump-sum death benefit ($255) applies bi-state. Crime Victims Compensation funds up to $5,000 for funeral costs are available in both Missouri (through the Department of Public Safety) and Kansas (through the Attorney General's office) when death resulted from a criminal act. Kansas City Hospice & Palliative Care and Catholic Charities of Kansas City-St. Joseph both maintain emergency funeral assistance funds with limited budgets.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical funeral price in Kansas City?

Traditional funerals in Kansas City run about $7,480 Missouri-side and closer to $6,900 Kansas-side, with direct cremation starting near $1,760 and plots averaging $3,410. A realistic bracket: about $1,000 for Heartland Cremation's basic direct disposition through roughly $13,100 for a full traditional service at McGilley & Hoffmann or D.W. Newcomer's with a Mount Moriah or Memorial Park plot and a monument.

What is Kansas City's cremation-versus-burial split?

Bi-state Kansas City cremation runs near 55 percent, slightly ahead of Missouri's 52 percent and Kansas's 50 percent figures. The cost math drives it: Heartland Cremation and Cremation Society of Kansas & Missouri publish direct rates under $1,500, versus $7,480 plus $3,410 for full traditional burial. Memorial services at a church, a family home, or Liberty Memorial Park after cremation are now the dominant bi-state pattern.

How do Kansas City funeral costs compare to the national average?

Kansas City sits below the national traditional-funeral average of $7,848. It's cheaper than Chicago or Minneapolis, on par with St. Louis and Omaha, and more expensive than smaller Missouri or Kansas markets like Springfield or Wichita. The bi-state GPL comparison is the single sharpest tool a Kansas City family has: spreads of $1,500-$2,500 on identical services are routine between Missouri-side and Kansas-side providers.

How do I compare funeral quotes in Kansas City?

The FTC Funeral Rule requires every Kansas City funeral home on both sides of State Line Road to give you an itemized General Price List on request — no appointment, no commitment. Call at least one Missouri-side provider (D.W. Newcomer's, McGilley & Sheil, Muehlebach), one Kansas-side provider (Porter Funeral Home, Amos Family, Johnson County Funeral Chapel), and one cremation specialist (Heartland Cremation, Cremation Society of Kansas & Missouri). The Funeral Consumers Alliance of Mid-America covers the Kansas City metro.

What does direct cremation include in Kansas City?

Direct cremation here means transport from the place of death, the cremation itself at a licensed crematory, and return of the cremated remains in a basic container. No embalming, no visitation, no service at the funeral home. Heartland Cremation & Burial Society in Raytown, Cremation Society of Kansas & Missouri, and Newcomer Cremations all publish rates starting near $995-$1,400, meaningfully below the $1,760 metro average. Families typically hold a memorial later at a church, a park like Loose or Liberty Memorial, or a private home.

Are there affordable funeral options in Kansas City?

The floor is direct cremation at about $1,000 through Heartland Cremation or Cremation Society of Kansas & Missouri. Alternatives include direct burial without visitation, memorial-only gatherings at a church or park, and — in rural parts of Clay, Platte, or Cass counties in Missouri, and Leavenworth or Miami counties in Kansas — home funerals under each state's lay-care laws. Missouri Funeral Assistance through MO HealthNet and Kansas General Assistance can help indigent families. Veterans qualify for no-cost burial at Leavenworth National Cemetery.
