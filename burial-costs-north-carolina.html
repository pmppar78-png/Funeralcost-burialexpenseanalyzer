Burial Costs in North Carolina (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: South

North Carolina sits squarely in the middle of the national burial-cost map, but the state average of roughly $7,100 in funeral-home charges and $3,200 for a plot conceals a wide range between regions. The Triangle and Charlotte metros run meaningfully above the average, while much of the Piedmont and the eastern coastal plain run well below it. Add casket, vault, opening, and marker and a North Carolina burial in 2026 lands between $13,180 and $18,510.

The all-in range for a traditional burial in North Carolina runs from $13,180 in a small-town setting to $18,510 in an urban historic cemetery with full services. Casket and cemetery choice explain most of the gap.

The Piedmont-to-coast price spread

Charlotte and Raleigh anchor the top of North Carolina's cost curve. Oakwood Cemetery in Raleigh, Elmwood Cemetery in Charlotte, and the historic grounds alongside them have limited inventory and price accordingly. Move into the rural Piedmont or out to coastal counties like Carteret and Brunswick and the numbers change: family cemeteries and rural municipal grounds often price plots at $1,500 to $2,500, and local funeral homes set service fees below $6,000. A family that is flexible about the location of interment — especially in the coastal plain, where family burial grounds remain active — can reduce the total by several thousand dollars without changing the ceremony at all.

Notable grounds and their character

  • Oakwood Cemetery, Raleigh. Founded 1869. Historic nonprofit with tiered sections; new burials still accepted in designated areas.
  • Elmwood Cemetery, Charlotte. Early 19th-century municipal cemetery adjacent to Pinewood; mostly at capacity but limited availability remains.
  • New Bern Cedar Grove and Wilmington Oakdale. Coastal historic grounds that reflect traditional Southern burial architecture and generally lower pricing than the major metros.
  • Church burial grounds across the Piedmont. United Methodist, Presbyterian, and Baptist congregations still maintain active cemeteries throughout the state, often at a fraction of commercial pricing for members and their families.

North Carolina Board of Funeral Service rules you want to know

The North Carolina Board of Funeral Service licenses funeral directors and embalmers, enforces the federal FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and administers the state's pre-need trust statute. Pre-need funeral contracts in North Carolina are subject to an escrow requirement: funds paid toward goods and services must be deposited in a trust or funded through an insurance policy, and the Board publishes a consumer lookup for verifying that deposits were made. Before paying toward a pre-need contract, ask for the trust receipt or insurance policy number.

Burial assistance at the county level

North Carolina does not administer a statewide burial assistance program. Instead, each county's Department of Social Services operates its own program under state general assistance guidance. Mecklenburg, Wake, Guilford, and Durham counties are among those with formal published rules; smaller counties handle requests case by case. Amounts are modest — typically under $1,000 — and programs pay the funeral provider directly rather than the family. Contact the specific county DSS office for current forms and eligibility thresholds.

Where a North Carolina burial number bends

  • Call church and family cemeteries first. In rural North Carolina, a plot in a churchyard or family ground can cost under $1,000, frequently with no opening-and-closing charge beyond the sexton's fee. Start with the congregation nearest to the burial location, not the funeral home.
  • Veterans and spouses. The state maintains veterans cemeteries at Jacksonville, Black Mountain, Spring Lake, and Goldsboro; eligible veterans and spouses receive the plot and opening-and-closing at no charge. Eligibility and benefits fall under the VA National Cemetery Administration (38 U.S.C. § 2402) and the VA burial allowance rules at 38 CFR § 3.1600.
  • Source the casket outside the funeral home. The FTC Funeral Rule applies statewide — a North Carolina funeral home cannot refuse or surcharge a third-party casket. Savings of $1,000 or more are routine. Casket buying guide
  • Ask about liner-versus-vault. Many North Carolina cemeteries accept a concrete liner in place of a sealed vault. The cost difference can exceed $1,000 on a single purchase.
  • Compare three funeral homes. Urban and suburban North Carolina markets have enough competition that prices on the same service can vary by $2,000 to $3,000 between firms.
Verify the pre-need trust: The North Carolina Board of Funeral Service enforces escrow rules for pre-need contracts. If you pre-pay, ask for the specific trust receipt and check it against the Board's guidance before you sign anything.

Commonly Asked Questions

What is the full price of burial in North Carolina?

A full traditional burial in North Carolina averages $7,100 for the funeral service, $3,200 for the cemetery plot, $2,130 for a casket, $1,280 for a vault, $1,600 for opening and closing, and $1,000 to $3,000 for a marker. Statewide totals land between $13,180 and $18,510. Raleigh, Charlotte, and Asheville run above that; rural Piedmont and coastal counties come in well under it.

Is a burial vault required in North Carolina?

State law does not require a vault. The cemetery's own rules usually do, and most cemeteries in North Carolina — including Oakwood in Raleigh and Elmwood in Charlotte — accept a concrete liner in place of a full sealed vault. The difference is material: a basic liner is typically $800 to $1,000 while a premium sealed vault can exceed $10,000. Always ask the cemetery for the written container specification before buying.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in North Carolina?

Get itemized price sheets from at least three North Carolina cemeteries covering plot, opening and closing, container requirements, perpetual care, and any residency restriction. Cemeteries are not bound by the FTC Funeral Rule, so these details are only provided when asked. Church and family cemeteries in rural North Carolina frequently price plots at a small fraction of commercial rates — often the single biggest lever for reducing the total.

Does North Carolina have green burial cemeteries?

Yes. Several North Carolina cemeteries are certified by the Green Burial Council or operate dedicated natural-burial sections, including grounds in the Triangle and in the western counties. Green burial in North Carolina typically means no embalming, a biodegradable container, and a plot without a vault or liner, which can bring the total meaningfully below a traditional burial. Availability is denser in the Triangle and Asheville than in the coastal plain.
