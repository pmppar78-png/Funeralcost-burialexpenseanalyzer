Burial Costs in North Carolina (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: North Carolina page rewritten by hand, with a new regulators and public help section. Region: South

A North Carolina burial is really three bills from three sellers. The funeral home charges about $7,100 for its services, the cemetery starts near $3,200 for a plot before it opens the grave, and a headstone or marker adds $500 to $5,000. Church and family cemeteries are still common in the state, so the cemetery line is often the easiest one to bring down.

Church, Family and Commercial Cemeteries in North Carolina

Commercial memorial parks are only part of the picture in North Carolina; many congregations and families still run their own cemeteries and charge less for a section. If your family already holds a church or family plot, using it could save more than $1,280 compared with a private cemetery, by our estimate.

Ways to Pay Less for a North Carolina Burial

Four places where a North Carolina family can usually save:

  • Get two sets of prices. In North Carolina the funeral home and the cemetery bill separately, so compare each one on its own before agreeing to a package.
  • Ask about church and family grounds. Rural North Carolina still has many working church and family cemeteries, and they often price a plot 30 to 50% below a commercial memorial park.
  • Look for a county veterans' section. Many North Carolina counties keep veterans' sections of their own, apart from the national and state veterans cemeteries.
  • Price a mausoleum in coastal areas. Where the ground sits low, as along the North Carolina coast, entombment is common and can cost less than a plot plus a vault at the same cemetery.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does each part of a North Carolina burial cost?

In our North Carolina estimate the funeral home's service is about $7,100, the plot $3,200, a casket $2,130, a vault $1,280 and the opening and closing of the grave $1,600, plus $500 to $5,000 for a headstone. All together that comes to $15,810 to $20,310.

Will a North Carolina cemetery insist on a vault?

Most will, but the rule is the cemetery's, not the state's. A vault or liner keeps the ground over a grave from caving in. In North Carolina expect $800 to $10,000; a church or family cemetery may have looser rules than a memorial park.

How can a North Carolina burial be kept inexpensive?

Choose direct burial. With no embalming, viewing or service it is the cheapest burial a North Carolina funeral home offers. Natural burial, in a biodegradable container and without embalming, usually costs a little more and is offered at more North Carolina cemeteries each year.

How should I compare North Carolina cemeteries?

Ask each cemetery for its prices in writing: the plot, opening and closing, and whatever vault or liner it requires. Most cemeteries fall outside the FTC Funeral Rule, so a written list is not guaranteed, but it is worth requesting. For a veteran, the Salisbury National Cemetery Annex is the VA national cemetery in North Carolina taking burials.

Are there costs beyond the North Carolina burial estimate?

Yes: cash advance items. Certified death certificates, an obituary and flowers are paid by the funeral home on your behalf and added to the bill, outside the $15,810 to $20,310 North Carolina range.

Is green burial possible in North Carolina?

In some parts of the state. A green burial ground is not within reach everywhere in North Carolina, and some cemeteries offer a hybrid section rather than a fully natural site. Read our green burial guide, then call the cemeteries near you to ask what they allow.

How deep a North Carolina grave must be

North Carolina sets one statewide depth rule for every interment: the top of the burial vault or other container must sit at least 18 inches below the ground surface (N.C.G.S. § 65-77; checked October 29, 2026).

The law carves out two cases. It does not apply when no part of the vault touches the ground, as in an above-ground crypt, and it lets a family cemetery deeded before January 1, 1989 place a surface vault beside a spouse who was buried that way before 1981. Anyone thinking about a family plot on their own land should also ask the county about zoning and well or septic setbacks, which are set locally.

North Carolina's burial regulators and public help

For a license check or complaint about a funeral home arranging a burial in North Carolina, call the North Carolina Board of Funeral Service at 919-733-9380; North Carolina cemeteries can have a separate regulator. For most cemeteries that regulator is the N.C. Cemetery Commission, which supervises cemeteries, their sales organizations and brokers, and preneed cemetery salespeople under Chapter 65, Article 9; cemeteries run by churches or government agencies fall outside that law.

Families in North Carolina can arrange a burial without a funeral director; the state is not one of the nine the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring one. The plot, the grave opening and the container can be arranged with the cemetery itself. Burial has no waiting period, though a switch to cremation brings a 24-hour wait (N.C.G.S. 90-210.129, 130A-388).

Paying for it

When a body is unclaimed, a North Carolina county pays for burial only after the estate has been used, and the person's residence decides which county is responsible (Source: N.C. Gen. Stat. § 130A-415). Social Security pays $255 only to a surviving spouse or child, a small fraction of the $3,200 plot we estimate for North Carolina.

A veteran's plot and marker in a national cemetery are free. North Carolina's VA national cemetery taking burials is the Salisbury National Cemetery Annex (704-636-2661), and the state runs four veterans cemeteries: Coastal Carolina (910-347-4550), Eastern Carolina (919-648-0510), Sandhills (919-664-1175) and Western Carolina (828-669-0684) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

A short checklist for North Carolina

  1. Get the cemetery's plot, opening and closing, vault and marker prices separately; they are often sold apart from the funeral home.
  2. Ask the cemetery about its grave opening times and its vault and marker rules.
  3. If no one can claim the body, ask the county DSS about a county-paid burial before buying a plot.
  4. For a veteran, call Salisbury National Cemetery Annex or a state veterans cemetery about eligibility before buying a private grave.
  5. If a funeral home misleads you about burial prices, a call to the Board at 919-733-9380 costs nothing.
