Burial Costs in Milwaukee, Wisconsin (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Wisconsin

Milwaukee is one of the last major American cities where traditional burial still measurably outpaces cremation. German-Catholic, Polish-Catholic, and Lutheran traditions on the South Side and in Cudahy, West Allis, and South Milwaukee keep demand steady at Forest Home Cemetery and the surrounding grounds. A 2026 Milwaukee burial totals between $15,026 and $21,142 for most families, and the gap between those two numbers reflects cemetery choice more than any other single factor.

Forest Home Cemetery and the Historic Milwaukee Pricing Ceiling

Forest Home Cemetery, founded in 1850 on Milwaukee's South Side, is the oldest nonsectarian cemetery in Wisconsin and the resting place of many of the city's founding families. Its section pricing carries a premium that reflects both its historical standing and the scarcity of new space in its older areas. Holy Cross on 70th Street and Pinelawn Memorial Park in Milwaukee County generally sit at mid-range for Milwaukee ground burial. Traditional Polish and Lutheran cemeteries in the South Side serve specific congregations and sometimes quote below commercial parks for members.

Wood National Cemetery: Free Burial for Eligible Milwaukee Veterans

Wood National Cemetery, on the grounds of the Zablocki VA Medical Center on National Avenue, provides no-cost burial for eligible Milwaukee-area veterans and their spouses — grave, opening and closing, liner, and government-furnished marker all included. Scheduling runs through the funeral director you select; Wood does not offer direct family reservations. For veterans preplanning in Milwaukee, confirming eligibility with the VA before commitment to a private cemetery is worth the phone call. See veteran burial benefits.

Burial Options Available in Milwaukee

  • Traditional burial — Viewing, service, casket, vault, interment. Still dominant in Milwaukee County.
  • Direct burial — Burial without viewing, embalming, or facility ceremony. Widely accepted and published by most Milwaukee funeral homes.
  • Green burial — Limited in Milwaukee County proper; a handful of rural Wisconsin cemeteries accept shrouded or biodegradable-container interments.
  • Mausoleum entombment — Available at Forest Home, Holy Cross, and most larger Milwaukee cemeteries; skips vault requirements.
Casket sourcing: Under the FTC Funeral Rule, Milwaukee funeral homes must accept caskets purchased from third-party retailers without charging handling fees. Compare online casket retailers.

Where Milwaukee Families Cut the Bill

  • Compare three cemeteries. Forest Home, Holy Cross, and a public cemetery in Cudahy or West Allis will return three meaningfully different itemized sheets. Ask for plot, opening and closing, vault policy, and perpetual care.
  • Check Wood National Cemetery for veterans. Free burial is the single biggest lever for eligible Milwaukee veterans — call before signing a private cemetery contract.
  • Call the township or county clerk. Waukesha, Washington, and Ozaukee County cemeteries publish plot fees that commercial search results miss entirely.
  • Use independent funeral homes. Milwaukee still has a strong independent family-owned funeral home market; these providers generally price closer to NFDA averages than corporate-chain locations.
  • Source the casket independently. 50 to 70 percent savings versus the funeral home showroom are routine. Casket buying guide.

Common Questions We Hear

How much does burial cost in Milwaukee?

A Milwaukee traditional burial totals between $15,026 and $21,142 in 2026. Funeral service near $7,920, plot $3,740, casket $2,376, vault $1,496, opening and closing $1,870, marker $1,500 to $3,000. Forest Home family-lot pricing pushes toward the upper end; Holy Cross or a Cudahy public cemetery holds the number near the lower.

Is a burial vault required in Milwaukee?

Wisconsin state law does not require an outer burial container. Forest Home, Holy Cross, Pinelawn, and most Milwaukee County cemeteries require one by internal policy. A concrete grave liner typically satisfies the rule at roughly half the cost of a sealed vault. Confirm in writing with the specific cemetery.

What are the cheapest burial options in Milwaukee?

For eligible veterans, Wood National Cemetery provides no-cost burial — grave, opening, liner, and government marker. Direct burial through an independent Milwaukee funeral home removes embalming and viewing fees. Township cemeteries in Waukesha and Washington Counties frequently quote plots below Milwaukee commercial parks, and the county clerk is the reliable source for those prices. Sourcing the casket independently cuts another 50 to 70 percent off showroom pricing.

How do Milwaukee burial costs compare to Wisconsin?

Milwaukee runs roughly 10 percent above the statewide average — $7,920 funeral service versus $7,200, $3,740 plot versus $3,400. Milwaukee County labor rates and cemetery land pricing explain the premium. Rural Wisconsin township cemeteries routinely price opening and closing fees $400 to $900 versus $1,500 to $2,500 at Milwaukee private cemeteries.
