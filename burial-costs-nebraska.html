Burial Costs in Nebraska (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Midwest

Nebraska holds some of the most affordable burial pricing in the Great Plains, and a big part of that is the lingering dominance of independent, family-owned funeral homes across the state's 93 counties. Total spend on a conventional burial here runs from $12,400 to $17,410 — close to the NFDA national median — with Omaha and Lincoln charging modest premiums over the rural counties west of Grand Island. Township cemeteries across the Platte Valley still sell plots at price points the commercial parks cannot match.

Embalming ($670) is not required under Nebraska law when burial occurs within a reasonable timeframe and refrigeration is available at the funeral home.

Omaha, Lincoln, and the Rural Counties

In Omaha, Forest Lawn Memorial Park on State Street (opened 1885) and Prospect Hill Cemetery north of downtown are the two most-used grounds, with Calvary Cemetery under the Archdiocese of Omaha handling most Catholic burials. Lincoln concentrates on Wyuka Cemetery on O Street — a state-chartered 140-acre cemetery whose per-plot pricing is frequently 20% below comparable Omaha private cemeteries. Beyond the two metros, small-town municipal cemeteries in Grand Island, Kearney, North Platte, and Scottsbluff are run by the towns themselves, and the price disparity against commercial parks in the same county can exceed $1,500 on the plot alone.

The Rules That Actually Bind Nebraska Practice

The Nebraska Funeral Directing and Embalming Board, inside the Department of Health and Human Services in Lincoln, licenses every funeral establishment, director, and embalmer in the state. Pre-need contracts in Nebraska are governed by the Burial Pre-Need Sale Act, which requires 85% of pre-need funds to be placed in trust (or applied to a regulated insurance product) — a meaningful consumer protection compared to states with weaker pre-need rules. The FTC Funeral Rule applies to every Nebraska funeral home: the General Price List must be handed over at the start of any in-person price discussion, and an outside casket cannot be refused or surcharged.

Help for Nebraska Families Who Cannot Pay

Nebraska's Department of Health and Human Services administers the Aid to the Aged, Blind, or Disabled (AABD) burial assistance program for eligible low-income decedents — payment is modest (a few hundred dollars toward burial expenses) but can be combined with funeral-home fee reductions some Nebraska directors still offer for AABD cases. Counties have separate indigent-burial responsibilities when no family can be found or no family can pay, and the county attorney's office is typically the right point of contact. Veterans get two substantial options: the Omaha National Cemetery in Sarpy County (opened 2016) and Nebraska Veterans' Cemetery at Alliance, both no-cost for eligible veterans.

Getting the Bill Down in Nebraska

  • Call the county or township clerk before the commercial cemetery: Nebraska township and rural municipal cemetery schedules live with the clerk's office, not online. Plot pricing differences against Forest Lawn or Wyuka regularly clear $1,500.
  • Use the FTC Funeral Rule on the casket: An outside casket must be accepted at no handling fee. Online retailers routinely list identical models 50-70% below mortuary pricing. Casket buying guide | Best online casket retailers
  • Favor independent funeral homes: Nebraska retains a strong independent sector. These firms price closer to NFDA national averages than the two national chains operating locations in the state — ask who owns the building.
  • Ask about grave liners: Most Nebraska cemeteries accept a concrete liner where a full vault is not required. The gap can approach $600.
  • Price the marker from an independent monument yard: Nebraska monument companies in Omaha, Kearney, and Lincoln typically underprice funeral-home markers by 30-50%. Headstone cost guide
  • Direct burial where acceptable to the family: Direct burial in Nebraska clears under $3,500 all-in at many independent funeral homes and can be followed by a memorial service held separately. Green burial options

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the full price of burial in Nebraska?

Adding every line on a conventional Nebraska burial — $6,700 service, $3,000 plot, $2,010 casket, $1,200 vault, $1,500 opening-and-closing, $1,000-$3,000 marker — the total sits between $12,400 and $17,410. The state's strong independent funeral home sector keeps service pricing close to the NFDA national median rather than pulling toward coastal metros.

Is a burial vault required in Nebraska?

Nebraska law does not require a vault, but virtually every cemetery in the state requires either a sealed vault or a concrete liner. The liner is the cheaper route by several hundred dollars and is accepted at most Omaha, Lincoln, and municipal cemeteries. Ask for the specific cemetery's written policy before the plot is purchased — rules vary by cemetery, not by state.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in Nebraska?

Direct burial at an independent Nebraska funeral home (no viewing, no embalming, memorial service held separately) regularly clears under $3,500 all-in. A township or rural municipal cemetery can drop plot costs $1,500 below Forest Lawn or Wyuka. And buying the casket from an outside retailer — legally protected under the FTC Funeral Rule — cuts 50-70% off the mortuary casket markup.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Nebraska?

Start with the county or township clerk's office in the county where burial will happen — Nebraska has many publicly run cemeteries that do not appear online. Then ask each cemetery in writing for the plot fee, opening-and-closing, vault-or-liner policy, perpetual care, and any residency restriction. Cemeteries are not covered by the FTC Funeral Rule, so disclosure happens only when requested. Compare at least three: one metro private, one municipal or township, and one religious cemetery.

What is the total cost of burial in Nebraska including everything?

Full arithmetic: service ($6,700) + plot ($3,000) + casket ($2,010) + vault ($1,200) + opening-and-closing ($1,500) + marker ($1,000-$3,000) = $12,400 to $17,410. Comparing two or three independent funeral homes plus the county clerk's public cemetery list is the single move that reliably bends the total downward.

Does Nebraska have green burial cemeteries?

Yes, though coverage is patchy. Nebraska has a small number of dedicated green burial sections, most notably around Omaha and Lincoln, and several traditional cemeteries accept a hybrid approach — no embalming, biodegradable container, no vault requirement in designated sections. A genuine green burial in Nebraska typically lands below a conventional burial on total cost, and the state's relatively permissive rules on home funeral care give families flexibility on timing.
