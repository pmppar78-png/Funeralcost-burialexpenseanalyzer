Federal Funeral Rule Protections

Separately from state licensing, the FTC Funeral Rule binds every Illinois home: written itemized prices, quotes over the phone, and no forced packages.

To report an Illinois home that breaks the rule, use ftc.gov, or ask the Funeral Consumers Alliance what to do.

Money for an Illinois Funeral

  • The DHS payment is split in two, services and burial or cremation, so make sure the funeral home claims both.
  • Social Security usually hears about the death from the funeral home, but the $255 lump sum is paid only when a qualifying spouse or child applies (1-800-772-1213, within two years).
  • An Illinois veteran's family can recover part of the bill on VA Form 21P-530EZ, filed by the person who paid: for a death on or after October 1, 2026 that was not service-related, up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot, as our veteran burial benefits guide explains.
  • For Medicaid, charities and other help in Illinois, read our overview of funeral aid or the page on paying for a funeral with no savings.

What's Specific to Illinois (Midwest)

Independent, family-owned funeral homes still anchor Illinois's market more than in the coasts, and they generally price closer to the NFDA national averages than corporate-chain locations. Asking an Illinois provider whether they are independently owned or part of a national group is a quick way to predict where their GPL will sit. Illinois publishes no comparison of chain and independent prices; the Department of Financial and Professional Regulation licenses both kinds alike, so put two homes' General Price Lists, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to hand you, side by side.

Death certificates in Illinois: price, timing and eligibility

A certified copy of an Illinois death certificate from the state's Division of Vital Records costs $19 for the first copy and $4 for each additional copy of the same record ordered at the same time. Genealogical copies, which cannot be used for legal purposes, are $10 and $2. Orders placed through VitalChek, the state's online and phone vendor, carry an extra $15 handling fee, and a mailed request can take around 12 weeks (Illinois Department of Public Health; checked October 29, 2026).

Illinois death records are not public. You must show a personal or property right interest, which in practice means a relative, the executor, or someone who needs the record to settle a claim. The Division of Vital Records moved on September 14, 2026 to 110 West Lawrence Avenue in Springfield, so older forms and websites may list the wrong address. Most families get copies faster from the county clerk where the death happened, or through the funeral home.

  • Order every copy you need at once: each extra copy is $4, against $19 to start over.
  • Allow for the 12-week mail time if an insurer or bank is waiting.
  • In Cook County, the County Clerk's office also issues certified copies for deaths that occurred there, often faster than ordering from Springfield.
  • A genealogical copy is only for deaths at least 20 years old and cannot be used to settle an estate or an insurance claim.
  • Check the funeral home's per-copy price against the state fee.

Licensing, state aid and veterans' graves in Illinois

The Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (217-524-1981) licenses funeral directors and embalmers and takes complaints about prices, misleading sales and homes that will not hand over a price list.

Illinois is one of nine states where a licensed funeral director has to handle certain steps: filing the death certificate, moving the body, being present at burial or cremation, and getting permits (410 ILCS 535, as summarized by the Funeral Consumers Alliance). Washing and dressing the body or holding the service at home can still be done by the family. Cremation may not happen sooner than 24 hours after death (410 ILCS 18/35).

The DHS funeral and burial payment

For eligible people, the Illinois Department of Human Services pays up to $1,370 for funeral services and up to $686 for burial or cremation, a combined $2,056. The claim has to be filed within 180 days of the death (Illinois DHS).

Veterans cemeteries

Illinois has six VA national cemeteries: Abraham Lincoln in Elwood, southwest of Chicago (815-423-9958); Camp Butler (217-492-4070); Danville (217-554-4550); Fort Sheridan (224-610-7296, cremated remains only); Mound City (618-694-1391); and Rock Island (309-784-0000). Graves are booked by the funeral home through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). Sunset Veterans Cemetery, run by the state, takes requests directly at 217-222-8641.

An Illinois checklist

  1. Ask two or more Illinois homes for written prices before agreeing to anything.
  2. Decide early about embalming and a viewing; leaving them out is often the biggest saving.
  3. Have the funeral home file the DHS claim, both parts, well inside 180 days.
  4. For a veteran, compare Abraham Lincoln National Cemetery with a private plot.
  5. Keep IDFPR's number, 217-524-1981, handy if a home strays from its price list.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is an Illinois funeral in 2026?

Our estimate for a traditional Illinois funeral is about $8,500, close to the $8,300 national median. Cremation with a service is about $6,600, direct cremation about $2,000, and a plot adds roughly $4,200. Prices vary widely between homes, so compare before choosing.

How much less does cremation cost in Illinois?

About $6,500 less: $2,000 for direct cremation against $8,500 for a traditional funeral. Counting a $4,200 plot, the gap grows to roughly $10,700.

Which federal and state rules protect Illinois families?

The FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR 453) entitles you to an itemized General Price List and phone quotes, and lets you bring a casket or urn from elsewhere with no handling fee. Illinois adds a rule of its own: a licensed funeral director must file the death certificate, move the body, attend the burial or cremation and get the permits (410 ILCS 535, per the Funeral Consumers Alliance). IDFPR, 217-524-1981, checks licenses and takes complaints.

How can an Illinois family spend less?

Illinois requires a fixed place of business behind every license. Under 225 ILCS 41/10-5, a funeral director and embalmer must work from an establishment devoted to the care and preparation of the dead, and an intern may assist only under a licensee's immediate personal supervision. Comparing price lists from two or three providers often saves $1,000 or more. Township and county cemeteries often charge half what private ones do, and at $2,000, direct cremation is the lowest-cost choice in Illinois. Family-owned homes are still common across the state, and many post clearer prices than the chains.

Is embalming required in Illinois?

No Illinois statute requires embalming for burial. The rule that touches it is in the Crematory Regulation Act: a crematory may not take an unembalmed body it cannot cremate within 24 hours unless it has refrigeration below 40°F (410 ILCS 18/35(p)).

So refrigeration is the legal alternative. Embalming costs about $850 in Illinois on this page's estimate, close to NFDA's 2023 national median of $845. Complaints go to the Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (217-524-1981).

How do Illinois prices compare nationally?

About in the middle. Our $8,500 Illinois estimate for a traditional funeral is close to the $8,300 national median, in line with moderate living costs across the Midwest.

What is the cheapest choice in Illinois?

Direct cremation at about $2,000: transport, the cremation and return of the ashes, nothing else. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $4,250 in Illinois before cemetery fees and is the cheapest burial option.

How do I find an Illinois funeral home that charges less?

Line up three General Price Lists and read the direct cremation and immediate burial prices. If you buy the casket or urn elsewhere, the home has to accept it without a fee.
