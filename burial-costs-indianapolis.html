Burial Costs in Indianapolis, Indiana (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Indiana

Indianapolis sits in a market where Crown Hill Cemetery — the third-largest non-government cemetery in the country — anchors pricing on the north side, while neighborhood and township cemeteries across Marion County routinely come in thousands below the big memorial parks. The result is a wide spread: depending on whether a family ends up at a historic mausoleum space on 34th Street or a modest township plot in Decatur or Wayne, Indianapolis burial totals in 2026 land anywhere from about $14,278 to $20,075. The Indiana State Board of Funeral and Cemetery Service, housed at the Professional Licensing Agency downtown, licenses funeral homes and cemeteries separately — which is why the bill comes from at least two distinct companies.

Indianapolis totals sit in a band from $14,278 at the low end to $20,075 at the upper end. Where you land inside that band is driven primarily by cemetery choice (Crown Hill, Washington Park East and Floral Park price very differently than Oaklandon or Acton township grounds) and whether you opt for an independent casket purchase or buy through the funeral home.

Crown Hill and the geography of Indianapolis cemeteries

Crown Hill Cemetery, opened in 1864 and covering 555 acres on the near north side, is a category of its own. James Whitcomb Riley, Benjamin Harrison, and John Dillinger are buried here; its Gothic Chapel and Community Mausoleum are the reason plot and crypt prices run well above the Marion County average. Crown Hill commonly quotes traditional single-depth plots between $3,500 and $6,000, with premium sections and mausoleum crypts exceeding $10,000.

Washington Park East on the far east side, Floral Park on the west, and Memorial Park in Fort Benjamin Harrison tend to price in the $2,500–$4,000 range for a standard plot. The Catholic Cemeteries of the Archdiocese of Indianapolis — principally Calvary and Holy Cross & St. Joseph on West Bluff Road — are available to practicing Catholics and generally run $2,200–$3,800, including the standard diocesan burial package.

The lowest published plot fees in Marion County come from the township cemeteries: Pike, Wayne, Decatur, Franklin, Warren, Lawrence, Perry, Center, and Washington townships each maintain public burial grounds, and several still list single plots under $1,500 for township residents. The township trustee's office is the right first call.

Indiana 15-mile rule. Under IC 23-14-49, if a family cemetery or small rural burial ground becomes inaccessible, remains can be moved to another cemetery within 15 miles with county commissioner authorization. For Indianapolis families with relatives buried in outlying Hendricks, Hancock, Hamilton, Johnson, Morgan, or Boone county plots, this rule matters when planning consolidated future interments.

Winter scheduling and the Marion County frost line

Frost regularly drives 18–30 inches into Marion County soil between late December and early March. Most Indianapolis cemeteries still open graves year-round because grounds crews have the equipment for frozen ground, but a winter surcharge of $150–$500 is routinely added to the opening & closing fee. Smaller township and rural cemeteries outside the Beltway sometimes pause burials until thaw, holding remains in a receiving vault at no additional charge, which can be the right choice when the family is not rushed.

Assistance paths specific to Marion County

  1. Township trustee emergency assistance. Each of Marion County's nine township trustees administers poor relief funds under Indiana Code 12-20. Burial assistance is among the allowable uses. The trustee can approve a direct-pay grant, typically up to $1,000–$1,500, applied to the funeral home or cemetery invoice. Call the trustee for the decedent's township of residence.
  2. Marion County Coroner indigent program. If no family steps forward and no assets are available, the Marion County Coroner arranges cremation through a contracted provider at Crown Hill's crematory. Remains are held for eventual family claim.
  3. Indiana Veterans' Memorial Cemetery, Madison. The state veterans' cemetery in Madison (about 90 minutes south) provides no-cost burial for Indiana-resident veterans and a $300 plot allowance for spouses. Combined with VA burial benefits, a veteran family's out-of-pocket can fall below $3,000.
  4. SSA lump-sum death benefit. $255 to the surviving spouse or dependent child — not meaningful standalone, but worth claiming.
  5. FEMA COVID-19 funeral assistance. Still open for deaths related to COVID-19 on or after January 20, 2020; up to $9,000 per deceased.
The independent funeral home advantage in Indianapolis. Indianapolis has a strong market of family-owned funeral homes — Shirley Brothers, Indiana Funeral Care, Flanner Buchanan (still family-held), and Crown Hill Funeral Home among them. Corporate chain-owned locations also operate in the metro. Families pricing 2–3 GPLs side-by-side from each category typically see $1,500–$3,500 of movement on identical service packages. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, every funeral home in Indiana must provide the GPL on request.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Indianapolis?

In 2026, an Indianapolis traditional burial lands between $14,278 at the low end and about $20,075 at the upper end. A direct burial at an independent funeral home using a township cemetery can come in under $9,000. A Crown Hill Cemetery mausoleum placement with a premium casket and full visitation can exceed $25,000. The range is real and widely published on Indianapolis funeral home General Price Lists.

Is a burial vault required in Indianapolis?

Indiana state law does not require a burial vault. The requirement — where it exists — comes from the individual cemetery. Crown Hill, Washington Park East, Floral Park, and essentially every memorial park in Marion County require either a vault or a concrete grave liner to prevent ground settlement. A liner typically runs $900–$1,200 versus $2,000–$3,500 for a sealed vault and is widely accepted. Township cemeteries sometimes allow direct-in-ground interment without a liner on special request — confirm in writing before ordering.

What are the cheapest burial options in Indianapolis?

Direct burial through an independent Indianapolis funeral home — Flanner Buchanan, Indiana Funeral Care, Shirley Brothers — combined with a township cemetery plot (Decatur, Franklin, Wayne, Perry) and a concrete liner typically totals $6,500–$9,500 all in. Purchasing the casket online through a retailer like Costco or Overstock and shipping to the funeral home under the FTC Funeral Rule can trim another $800–$2,000. The Indiana Veterans' Memorial Cemetery in Madison is free for qualifying veterans and removes the plot and opening-closing fees entirely.

How do Indianapolis burial costs compare to Indiana?

Indianapolis prices run roughly 8–12% above the Indiana statewide average. Funeral service charges sit at $7,590 in Marion County versus $6,900 statewide; plots average $3,520 in the metro versus $3,200 across Indiana. The premium reflects higher real estate and labor costs inside the I-465 loop. Moving as little as 20 miles out — to Hendricks, Hancock, Johnson, or Boone County funeral homes and cemeteries — often recovers most of that difference for families who are willing to travel for arrangements.
