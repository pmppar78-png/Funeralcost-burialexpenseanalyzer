Payment Assistance in Kansas

  • A Kansas coroner pays for a disposition first from property found with the person, then from state assistance if the person qualified under K.S.A. 39-713d, and only then from the county general fund.
  • Funeral homes in Kansas usually report the death to Social Security, but the $255 lump sum still has to be claimed by the spouse or child (1-800-772-1213, within two years).
  • For a Kansas veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. What the Law Guarantees You in Kansas

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Kansas must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. State licensing belongs to the Kansas State Board of Mortuary Arts, whose stated mission is to make sure its licensees work in a way that gives the people of Kansas maximum protection of their health, safety and welfare.

If you believe a funeral home in Kansas has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Questions or complaints about a Kansas funeral home can go to the board at 700 SW Jackson Street, Suite 904, in Topeka (785-296-3980).

Why Funeral Prices Vary in Kansas

What you pay for a funeral in Kansas depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in Kansas, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether Kansas state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. Midwest pricing is generally lower than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in Kansas routinely differs by 10-30%.

At 50%, Kansas's cremation rate is 13.4 points below the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

How Kansas Differs From Its Midwest Neighbors

Independent, family-owned funeral homes still anchor Kansas's market more than in the coasts, and they generally price closer to the NFDA national averages than corporate-chain locations. Asking a Kansas provider whether they are independently owned or part of a national group is a quick way to predict where their GPL will sit. No Kansas agency tracks the price gap between the two: the State Board of Mortuary Arts licenses chain-owned and family-owned homes under the same rules, which leaves each home's General Price List under the FTC Funeral Rule as the way to see it.

Funeral rules and help in Kansas

If a funeral home in Kansas refuses to show prices or pressures you, the licensing body is the Kansas State Board of Mortuary Arts, reachable at 785-296-3980.

Hiring a funeral home is a choice, not a legal requirement, for most steps of a funeral in Kansas; the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count Kansas among the nine states with funeral-director rules, and you can buy only the services you want.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Kansas sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (K.S.A. 65-1762; 65-2426a).

In Kansas the coroner arranges burial or cremation when no family claims the body (K.S.A. 22a-215); the cost comes first from any property found with the person, then from the state assistance program if the person qualified under K.S.A. 39-713d, and otherwise from the county general fund. (Source: K.S.A. 22a-215; K.S.A. 39-713d.) Social Security's one-time $255 death payment, paid only to a qualifying spouse or child, is small next to the $6,600 average Kansas funeral in the table.

A Kansas funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Fort Leavenworth National Cemetery (913-684-4348, cremated remains only), Fort Scott National Cemetery (913-212-4612), or Leavenworth National Cemetery (913-727-1376) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemeteries, Kansas Veterans Cemetery at Fort Dodge (620-682-7564), Kansas Veterans Cemetery at Fort Riley / Manhattan (785-717-3050), Kansas Veterans Cemetery at WaKeeney (785-743-5685), or Kansas Veterans Cemetery at Winfield (620-705-6300), take requests directly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a typical Kansas funeral cost in 2026?

Most Kansas families paying for a full traditional service see the bill settle around $6,600, 20% below the $8,300 national benchmark. Cremation with ceremony runs closer to $5,300, while a stripped-down direct cremation in Kansas can land near $1,500. Cemetery plot fees in Kansas add about $2,900. Actual prices shift a lot depending on which Kansas provider you call first, which is why comparing is non-negotiable.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in Kansas?

Direct cremation in Kansas starts around $1,500; a traditional funeral with burial averages $6,600, not counting the cemetery plot or vault. For most Kansas families, that is a difference of at least $5,100.

What are my consumer rights at Kansas funeral homes?

In Kansas, as everywhere in the US, the FTC Funeral Rule requires an itemized General Price List, phone price quotes, and no fee for using a casket bought elsewhere. According to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, Kansas does not require families to hire a funeral director for every step. The Kansas State Board of Mortuary Arts, 785-296-3980, licenses Kansas funeral homes and takes complaints.

What is the best way to reduce funeral expenses in Kansas?

The biggest lever is comparison shopping: two or three GPLs from different Kansas providers, compared line by line, consistently saves families $1,000 or more. Township and county cemeteries in Kansas rarely appear in online searches but are often half the price of private cemeteries; call the clerk's office in the county where burial will occur. Independent, family-owned funeral homes still dominate Kansas and tend to price more transparently than chain providers. At $1,500, direct cremation is the cheapest disposition option available in Kansas.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Kansas?

The FTC Funeral Rule bars a Kansas funeral home from saying embalming is required by law when it is not, and its price list must state that embalming is not required except in certain special cases. Ask any Kansas home that insists on embalming to name the rule in writing.

How do funeral costs in Kansas compare to other states?

Kansas's traditional-funeral average of $6,600 sits 20% below the $8,300 national median, and the Midwest region overall trends toward moderate to lower pricing than the rest of the country.

What is the least expensive way to handle a funeral in Kansas?

In Kansas, direct cremation at about $1,500 is the most affordable path: just transport, cremation, and the return of the remains. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $3,300 in Kansas before cemetery fees and is the cheapest burial option.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in Kansas?

Ask at least three Kansas providers for their General Price Lists and compare direct cremation and immediate burial lines. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Kansas home cannot charge a fee for it.
