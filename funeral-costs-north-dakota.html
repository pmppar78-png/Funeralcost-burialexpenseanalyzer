What Drives Funeral Costs in North Dakota

North Dakota funeral prices are usually below the US median of $8,300, and city and rural quotes can differ by 10-30%. Every funeral home is overseen by a small board: the state health officer or a designee plus three governor-appointed embalmers, each with at least three years of practice in North Dakota. Each place of business needs its own establishment license, a crematorium needs an annual license costing no more than $100, and the board writes the rules for what happens to cremated remains a funeral home is left holding when no one claims them (N.D.C.C. ch. 43-10).

At 55%, North Dakota's cremation rate is 8.4 points below the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

Your Consumer Rights in North Dakota

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in North Dakota must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

What's Specific to North Dakota (Midwest)

Independent, family-owned funeral homes still anchor North Dakota's market more than in the coasts, and they generally price closer to the NFDA national averages than corporate-chain locations. Asking a North Dakota provider whether they are independently owned or part of a national group is a quick way to predict where their GPL will sit. North Dakota's State Board of Funeral Service (701-776-6222) licenses both kinds of home but reports no prices, so ask each one for the General Price List the FTC Funeral Rule requires and compare the basic services fee first.

Payment Assistance in North Dakota

  • North Dakota's human service zone reimburses $3,500 for a final disposition, which is a cremation unless a burial is requested for religious reasons (N.D.C.C. § 23-06-03).
  • Funeral homes in North Dakota usually report the death to Social Security, but the $255 lump sum still has to be claimed by the spouse or child (1-800-772-1213, within two years).
  • For a North Dakota veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. Eligibility rules for North Dakota families are in our veteran burial benefits guide.

Funeral rules and help in North Dakota

Funeral homes in North Dakota are licensed by the North Dakota State Board of Funeral Service (701-776-6222). That office takes North Dakota complaints about pricing, misleading sales, or a home that will not hand over its price list.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list North Dakota among the nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so a North Dakota family may arrange much of a funeral itself. The North Dakota death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that North Dakota sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (N.D.C.C. § 23-06-07).

When a North Dakota death leaves too little to pay and no responsible relative, the human service zone must arrange the final disposition within seven days of the application, and the reimbursement is $3,500 (N.D.C.C. § 23-06-03); it is a cremation unless a burial is requested for religious reasons. (Source: N.D.C.C. § 23-06-03(6).) The $255 Social Security lump sum goes only to an eligible spouse or child and must be claimed within two years; it does not go to the North Dakota funeral home.

A North Dakota funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Fargo National Cemetery (701-451-4650) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state and tribal veterans cemeteries, All Nations Veterans Cemetery - Fort Yates (tribal, 701-854-8689) or North Dakota Veterans Cemetery (701-667-1418), take requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in North Dakota

  1. Request itemized prices from North Dakota funeral homes by phone or in person; under the FTC Funeral Rule you only pay for what you choose.
  2. Ask the North Dakota funeral home whether embalming is actually needed for your plans.
  3. Apply to the North Dakota human service zone; it must act within seven days of the application.
  4. If the person served, have the funeral home ask about Fargo National Cemetery before you buy a private plot.
  5. Report a funeral home that hides or ignores its price list to the board at 701-776-6222.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a typical North Dakota funeral cost in 2026?

Most North Dakota families paying for a full traditional service see the bill settle around $6,700, 19% below the $8,300 national benchmark. Cremation with ceremony runs closer to $5,300, while a stripped-down direct cremation in North Dakota can land near $1,500. Cemetery plot fees in North Dakota add about $3,000. Actual prices shift a lot depending on which North Dakota provider you call first, which is why comparing is non-negotiable.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in North Dakota?

Direct cremation in North Dakota starts around $1,500; a traditional funeral with burial averages $6,700, not counting the cemetery plot or vault. For most North Dakota families, that is a difference of at least $5,200.

What are my consumer rights at North Dakota funeral homes?

The federal FTC Funeral Rule protects every consumer in North Dakota. North Dakota may layer additional state protections on top through its funeral regulatory board.

What is the best way to reduce funeral expenses in North Dakota?

The biggest lever is comparison shopping: two or three GPLs from different North Dakota providers, compared line by line, consistently saves families $1,000 or more. Township and county cemeteries in North Dakota rarely appear in online searches but are often half the price of private cemeteries; call the clerk's office in the county where burial will occur. Independent, family-owned funeral homes still dominate North Dakota and tend to price more transparently than chain providers. At $1,500, direct cremation is the cheapest disposition option available in North Dakota.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in North Dakota?

The FTC Funeral Rule bars a North Dakota funeral home from saying embalming is required by law when it is not, and its price list must state that embalming is not required except in certain special cases. If a North Dakota provider says embalming is needed, ask which law or cemetery rule it is citing. The North Dakota State Board of Funeral Service, 701-776-6222, licenses North Dakota funeral homes and takes complaints.

How do funeral costs in North Dakota compare to other states?

North Dakota funerals are near or slightly below the national average, reflecting moderate costs of living across the region. North Dakota's traditional-funeral average of $6,700 sits 19% below the $8,300 national median, and the Midwest region overall trends toward moderate to lower pricing than the rest of the country.

What is the least expensive way to handle a funeral in North Dakota?

In North Dakota, direct cremation at about $1,500 is the most affordable path: just transport, cremation, and the return of the remains. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $3,350 in North Dakota before cemetery fees and is the cheapest burial option.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in North Dakota?

Call several North Dakota funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the North Dakota home cannot charge a fee for it.
