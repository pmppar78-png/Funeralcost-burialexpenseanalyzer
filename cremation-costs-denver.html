Cremation Costs in Denver, Colorado (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Colorado Cremation Rate: 72%

Denver has one of the highest cremation rates of any major U.S. metro — Colorado hit 72% statewide years ago, and Front Range demographics push the Denver number a touch higher still. Direct cremation averages $2,070 across the Denver metro in 2026, with full-service cremation landing near $7,130. The providers that dominate are familiar: the Cremation Society of Colorado, Horan & McConaty, and Feldman Mortuary in the Jewish community. Because Colorado licenses establishments but not individual funeral directors, shopping here feels slightly different than in neighboring Kansas or Wyoming — more on that below.

National context: the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA 2024 Annual Report put the U.S. cremation rate near 60.5 percent, and Denver providers operate under the oversight of the Colorado Office of Funeral Home and Crematory Registration. Families ordering death certificates typically deal with the Denver Department of Public Health and Environment Vital Records office.

Denver runs about $270 above the Colorado statewide direct-cremation average because the metro absorbs higher retort operating costs, stricter air-quality compliance under the Regional Air Quality Council rules, and real estate expenses that do not apply to operators in Greeley or Pueblo. The $7,130 full-service figure is closer to the Colorado Springs number than to Fort Collins.

Why Colorado does not license individual funeral directors

Colorado is one of only a handful of states whose regulatory scheme licenses the funeral establishment and the crematory — through the Colorado Office of Funeral Home and Crematory Registration — but does not credential the individual funeral director. Practically, this means the person arranging a Denver cremation may not hold a state-issued mortuary license. It does not mean providers are unregulated: establishments have to meet requirements for facilities, recordkeeping and consumer disclosures, and the FTC Funeral Rule still applies. But families should know that references, online reviews and membership in organizations like the Colorado Funeral Directors Association matter more here than in states with individual licensure.

The Crestone exemption and Denver scattering options

Colorado is unique in permitting open-air cremation at a single licensed facility — Crestone End of Life Project in the San Luis Valley — though the service is ceremonial, serves regional residents, and is not a Denver option. For Denver-area families, the practical scattering choices are:

  • National forest land: Pike, Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests permit ash scattering without a permit if done discreetly and away from water sources and trails.
  • Rocky Mountain National Park: Requires a free Superintendent's Compendium permit, typically approved within a week.
  • 14er summits: Popular and legal on most peaks; Colorado Fourteeners Initiative asks families to scatter below the summit cairn.
  • Private property: Always legal with owner consent.

Altitude and body-return logistics

Denver is a regional hub for mountain-town deaths. When a traveler dies in Breckenridge, Vail, Aspen or Estes Park, the body typically travels to a Denver-area crematory because mountain communities lack capacity. Horan & McConaty and the Cremation Society of Colorado handle much of this inbound transport, and the logistics add $400-$900 to the base cremation price depending on distance and whether an airport transfer (often via DIA for out-of-state return of ashes) is involved. Altitude itself has no mechanical effect on cremation — the retort controls its own atmosphere — but death-certificate paperwork crossing county lines (Summit, Pitkin, Eagle to Denver) can add 48 to 72 hours.

Denver provider snapshot

  • Cremation Society of Colorado — volume-driven direct cremation, frequently quoted at the low end of the Denver range.
  • Horan & McConaty — multiple locations across the metro, full-service and direct options, newer cremation-specific facilities in Aurora and Highlands Ranch.
  • Feldman Mortuary — the primary Jewish funeral provider in Denver, handles cremation where the family's practice permits but with specific religious protocols.

County indigent cremation for Denver residents

Denver County operates an indigent burial and cremation program through Human Services for residents without resources or next of kin willing to take responsibility. Adams, Arapahoe and Jefferson counties run parallel programs. These are reimbursements to participating funeral homes, not direct family payments, and approval requires documented financial hardship. Statewide, the Colorado Funeral Directors Association keeps a list of participating providers.

Urn purchase tip: You are not required to buy your urn from the Denver funeral home handling the cremation. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, providers must accept any container you furnish without charging a handling fee.

Reader Questions

How much does cremation cost in Denver?

Direct cremation averages $2,070 in the Denver metro in 2026, with the Cremation Society of Colorado frequently quoting the lowest prices and the more ceremonial chapels at Horan & McConaty and Feldman Mortuary at the upper end. Full-service cremation — viewing, service, then cremation — runs about $7,130. Mountain-town transfers into Denver add $400 to $900 on top.

What types of cremation are available in Denver?

Three standard paths. Direct cremation ($2,070) is fastest and cheapest — no ceremony, ashes returned within 7-10 days. Cremation with a later memorial lets families hold a service at a church, a mountain cabin, or a park once they've chosen the urn and disposition. Full-service cremation ($7,130) replicates a traditional funeral experience through the ceremony, followed by cremation. Jewish families using Feldman should discuss religious observance requirements up front.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Denver?

Yes, decisively. A Denver cremation at $2,070 runs about $7,100 under a traditional burial once you add cemetery plot costs at Fairmount or Mount Olivet, a vault, and a headstone. That math is a big reason Colorado's cremation rate hit 72% before most of the country crossed 60%.

Where can I compare cremation prices in Denver?

The cheapest path in Denver is to phone three or four direct-cremation specialists first, then one full-service funeral home, and ask each for the itemized General Price List the FTC Funeral Rule requires. Parting.com and the Funeral Consumers Alliance chapter covering Denver can supplement those quotes with independent comparisons.

Can I scatter ashes in Denver?

Most Denver-area families scatter in the Rockies. Pike, Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests allow discreet scattering without a permit; Rocky Mountain National Park requires a free superintendent's permit; 14er summits are widely used (Colorado Fourteeners Initiative asks for a location below the cairn). Private property is legal with owner consent. Water scattering in rivers is legal under EPA rules but requires common-sense distance from drinking-water intakes.
