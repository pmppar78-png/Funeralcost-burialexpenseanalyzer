Burial Costs in Kansas City, Missouri (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Missouri

Kansas City straddles a state line — State Line Road separates Jackson County, Missouri from Johnson and Wyandotte Counties, Kansas — and burial pricing shifts noticeably depending on which side of that line a family ends up on. Missouri cemeteries sit under the Office of Endowed Care Cemeteries at the Division of Professional Registration; Kansas cemeteries report to the Kansas State Board of Mortuary Arts. This guide focuses on Kansas City, Missouri pricing, but families routinely cross into Overland Park, Olathe, Shawnee, or Kansas City, Kansas for better cemetery math. The realistic 2026 total for a Jackson County burial is $13,959 to $19,613.

Jackson County totals run $13,959 to $19,613 in 2026. Crossing the state line into Wyandotte or Johnson County, Kansas typically saves $500–$1,500 on plot and opening-closing — and changes the regulatory framework from Missouri's endowed-care statute to Kansas' separate board.

Kansas City's historic cemeteries

Mount Washington Cemetery in Independence (1871) and Elmwood Cemetery on Truman Road (1872) are the two oldest active Kansas City-area cemeteries, and both carry the historic-section premium that comes with century-and-a-half-old memorial parks. Standard plots at Mount Washington and Elmwood typically run $2,500–$4,500 with opening-and-closing adding $1,500–$2,200 depending on weekend timing and depth. Forest Hill Cemetery at 69th and Troost — where Negro Leagues founders, Satchel Paige, and several of the city's most recognized civic figures are buried — sits in a similar tier.

Memorial Park Cemetery on Holmes, Mount Moriah & Freeman on Holmes Road, and Green Lawn Cemetery in south Kansas City round out the broader metro inventory. Each lists plot pricing at or slightly above the $3,410 Jackson County average. The Catholic Cemeteries of the Diocese of Kansas City-St. Joseph — Mount Olivet, Resurrection, and St. Mary's — price closer to $2,800–$3,600 for practicing Catholics and bundle several standard services.

Leavenworth National Cemetery and the veterans option

Leavenworth National Cemetery, 45 minutes northwest of downtown Kansas City, covers grave, marker, vault, and opening-and-closing at no cost for eligible veterans. For spouses buried alongside, fees are waived under the standard VA spouse benefit. Fort Leavenworth National Cemetery (a separate, smaller installation on Fort Leavenworth itself) has capacity constraints and is closed to new first interments in many sections — Leavenworth National is the broader-access site. Both operate under the same VA scheduling process.

The state-line play. Several Kansas City metro families arrange the funeral service at a Missouri independent (often for proximity to the church or the family home), then use a cemetery across the line — Mount Calvary in KCK, Shawnee Mission Memory Gardens in Shawnee, or one of the older Johnson County church cemeteries. The Missouri funeral director can deliver to any Kansas cemetery; a cross-state transport fee applies but is usually under $350, and the plot savings can exceed $1,200.

Where the Kansas City bill actually moves

  1. Cemetery side of State Line Road. Missouri averages $3,410; Kansas averages $2,900. Small Kansas church and township cemeteries can drop to $1,200–$1,800.
  2. Corporate chain versus independent funeral home. Dignity Memorial and SCI own several Kansas City area chapels. Independent operators — Muehlebach, Longview, Johnson County Funeral Chapel, McGilley — typically come in $1,500–$3,500 below the corporate price on identical service packages. Always ask whether the funeral home is independently owned.
  3. Casket sourcing. The FTC Funeral Rule applies in both Missouri and Kansas. Third-party online retailers ship to both states. Savings $800–$2,500 on the casket line.
  4. Vault versus liner. Most Kansas City memorial parks require concrete enclosure. A liner typically runs $900–$1,200; a sealed vault $2,000–$3,500. Confirm the minimum in writing.
  5. Winter scheduling. Frost penetration is shallow in the metro (usually 12–24 inches), and most Kansas City cemeteries open graves year-round. Winter surcharges are typically $150–$400, lower than Iowa or Minnesota rates.

Assistance programs in Jackson County

Jackson County Missouri does not operate a formal indigent burial fund, but the Jackson County Medical Examiner's Office handles disposition for unclaimed decedents, typically through contracted cremation at low cost. For Missouri families with assets below state thresholds, the Missouri Funeral Trust and the state's pre-need consumer protection fund administered by the Division of Professional Registration offer recovery paths when a pre-need contract is mismanaged. The Missouri Crime Victims Compensation Program covers up to $5,000 for families of homicide victims. Social Security's $255 lump-sum death benefit and FEMA's up-to-$9,000 COVID-19 funeral assistance both remain available to qualifying Kansas City families.

Short Answers to Common Questions

How much does burial cost in Kansas City?

A 2026 Jackson County burial lands between $13,959 and $19,613 for a traditional service. A direct burial at an independent such as McGilley or Muehlebach, paired with a Kansas-side church cemetery plot, can close under $8,500. A full visitation at Mount Washington or Elmwood with a premium casket and granite monument can exceed $22,000. Leavenworth National Cemetery removes most of the bill for eligible veterans.

Is a burial vault required in Kansas City?

Neither Missouri nor Kansas state law requires a vault. The requirement comes from individual cemeteries. Mount Washington, Elmwood, Memorial Park, Forest Hill, Mount Moriah, and nearly every Jackson County memorial park require either a concrete vault or a liner. A liner is the lower-cost option where accepted. Some Kansas-side church cemeteries still permit direct-in-ground interment without a vault or liner — confirm in writing before ordering.

What are the cheapest burial options in Kansas City?

Direct burial through a Kansas City independent (McGilley, Muehlebach, Longview, Johnson County Funeral Chapel) combined with a Kansas-side township, church, or fraternal cemetery is the lowest-cost route — totals can close under $7,500. Online casket purchase under the FTC Funeral Rule trims another $800–$2,500. For eligible veterans, Leavenworth National Cemetery eliminates plot, vault, opening-and-closing, and marker costs entirely.

How do Kansas City burial costs compare to Missouri?

Kansas City runs about 10% above the Missouri statewide average on both funeral service and plot. Funeral service averages $7,480 in the metro versus $6,800 statewide; plots average $3,410 versus $3,100. St. Louis tracks close to Kansas City. The rural Missouri counties (Howell, Texas, Shannon, Dent, Reynolds, and the southern Ozark tier) regularly list plots under $1,500 at township and community cemeteries, which is where much of the state's affordability lives.
