Your Consumer Rights in Massachusetts

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Massachusetts must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Massachusetts has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What's Specific to Massachusetts (Northeast)

Massachusetts has one of the highest funeral-home densities in the country, which usually helps consumers: more providers means more pricing competition. However, ownership consolidation by Service Corporation International (SCI) and similar groups has concentrated some of the Boston market under a handful of brands. Ask each Massachusetts provider whether they are independently owned; independent operators in Massachusetts often undercut branded ones by 10-25%.

Payment Assistance in Massachusetts

  • Because the Massachusetts DTA limit applies to the whole funeral bill, a simple burial or direct cremation is the usual way to stay at or under $3,500.
  • Funeral homes in Massachusetts usually report the death to Social Security, but the $255 lump sum still has to be claimed by the spouse or child (1-800-772-1213, within two years).
  • VA reimburses part of a Massachusetts veteran's funeral through Form 21P-530EZ, filed by whoever paid; deaths not connected to service on or after October 1, 2026 qualify for up to $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for the plot, as our veteran burial benefits guide explains.
  • More options for Massachusetts families, including Medicaid and charity help, are in our funeral payment assistance guide and how to pay for a funeral with no money.

What to Do When Planning a Funeral in Massachusetts

Families arranging a funeral in Massachusetts for the first time often move faster than they need to.

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in Massachusetts or burial in Massachusetts.

Funeral rules and help in Massachusetts

Funeral homes in Massachusetts are licensed by the Massachusetts Board of Embalming and Funeral Directing (617-701-8628). That office takes Massachusetts complaints about pricing, misleading sales, or a home that will not hand over its price list.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Massachusetts among the nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so a Massachusetts family may arrange much of a funeral itself. The Massachusetts death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Massachusetts requires a 48-hour wait before cremation (M.G.L. ch. 114, § 44).

The Massachusetts Department of Transitional Assistance pays up to $1,100 toward a funeral for eligible people, but only when the total cost is no more than $3,500. (Source: Massachusetts DTA.)

A Massachusetts funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Massachusetts National Cemetery (508-563-7113) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemeteries, Massachusetts State Veterans Cemetery - Agawam (413-821-9500) or Massachusetts State Veterans Cemetery - Winchendon (978-297-9501), take requests directly. Massachusetts National Cemetery is in Bourne, on Cape Cod.

Next steps for a funeral in Massachusetts

  1. Ask every Massachusetts funeral home for its General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule says it must give you one when you ask in person.
  2. Ask the Massachusetts funeral home whether embalming is actually needed for your plans.
  3. Ask the Massachusetts Department of Transitional Assistance about burial assistance while the total is still under $3,500.
  4. If the person served, have the funeral home ask about Massachusetts National Cemetery before you buy a private plot.
  5. Report a funeral home that hides or ignores its price list to the board at 617-701-8628.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a typical Massachusetts funeral cost in 2026?

Most Massachusetts families paying for a full traditional service see the bill settle around $10,400, 25% above the $8,300 national benchmark. Cremation with ceremony runs closer to $7,800, while a stripped-down direct cremation in Massachusetts can land near $2,500. Cemetery plot fees in Massachusetts add about $5,200. Actual prices shift a lot depending on which Massachusetts provider you call first, which is why comparing is non-negotiable.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Massachusetts?

In Massachusetts, direct cremation at $2,500 costs about $7,900 less than the $10,400 traditional funeral, and roughly $13,100 less once about $5,200 in cemetery costs is added.

What does the FTC Funeral Rule mean for me in Massachusetts?

In Massachusetts, as everywhere in the US, the FTC Funeral Rule requires an itemized General Price List, phone price quotes, and no fee for using a casket bought elsewhere. Massachusetts is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. Complaints about a Massachusetts funeral home go to the Massachusetts Board of Embalming and Funeral Directing (617-701-8628).

What is the best way to reduce funeral expenses in Massachusetts?

The biggest lever is comparison shopping: two or three GPLs from different Massachusetts providers, compared line by line, consistently saves families $1,000 or more. Request frozen-ground pricing separately if the death is between December and March; Massachusetts cemeteries often charge extra in winter. Municipal, Catholic, and Jewish cemeteries in Massachusetts generally price below private memorial parks by $1,000 or more. At $2,500, direct cremation is the cheapest disposition option available in Massachusetts.

Does Massachusetts require embalming?

Not usually. Under 239 CMR 3.10, an unembalmed body that will be buried or cremated in Massachusetts within 50 hours of death must still be washed, disinfected and wrapped by a licensed embalmer. If disposition will take longer than 50 hours, the body must also be kept at 34 to 39°F; if that is not possible, it must be embalmed unless the family directs otherwise in writing.

A local board of health can order embalming in cases of advanced decomposition. At about $1,040 on our Massachusetts estimate, embalming here costs more than NFDA's 2023 national median of $845, so if you do not want it, give the written direction mentioned above.

Where does Massachusetts rank nationally for funeral costs?

Massachusetts's $10,400 traditional-funeral estimate is 25% above the $8,300 national median and the fourth highest of the 50 states on this site's estimates, behind Hawaii, New York and California. It is also above NFDA's 2023 New England regional median of $8,985.

Direct cremation in Massachusetts is estimated at about $2,500, which ranks near the top as well. The national $8,300 figure is NFDA's survey median, while the state figures are this site's estimates.

What is the least expensive way to handle a funeral in Massachusetts?

In Massachusetts, direct cremation at about $2,500 is the most affordable path: just transport, cremation, and the return of the remains. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $5,200 in Massachusetts before cemetery fees and is the cheapest burial option.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Massachusetts?

Call several Massachusetts funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Massachusetts home cannot charge a fee for it.
