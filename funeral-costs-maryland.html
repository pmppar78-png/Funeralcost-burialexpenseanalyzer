Funeral Costs in Maryland: What Families Pay in 2026

Reviewed: February 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Maryland Funeral Board Region: Northeast

Maryland's funeral market is a study in contrasts. The Baltimore metro area and the affluent Washington D.C. suburbs of Montgomery and Prince George's counties drive the state's above-average costs, while western Maryland's rural communities maintain significantly more affordable pricing. Overseen by the Maryland Board of Morticians and Funeral Directors, the state's industry reflects mid-Atlantic diversity at its fullest: Baltimore's historic African-American funeral homes, the Catholic parishes of the Baltimore-Washington corridor, a significant Jewish community with specific funeral requirements, and a growing Korean-American population with distinctive customs. With a 55% cremation rate near the national average, Maryland families are evenly divided between traditional and contemporary approaches to honoring their loved ones.

Funeral Cost Breakdown in Maryland

Maryland's funeral costs exceed the national median of $7,848, reflecting the mid-Atlantic region's higher cost of living. The Baltimore metro and the D.C. suburbs are the primary cost drivers -- Montgomery County, in particular, has some of the most expensive funeral services in the region due to its affluent demographics and high real estate costs. Western Maryland (Allegany, Garrett, and Washington counties) offers pricing that is 20-30% below the state average, comparable to neighboring West Virginia. The state's diverse religious landscape creates demand for a wide range of service types, from traditional Catholic funeral masses to Jewish tahara preparation to Korean ancestral rites.

What Drives Funeral Costs in Maryland

D.C. suburbs premium. Montgomery and Prince George's counties, part of the Washington D.C. metropolitan area, have high real estate costs, affluent demographics, and a concentration of both independent and corporate funeral homes. Funeral costs in these counties can exceed the state average by 15-25%, with premium cemetery plots in Montgomery County reaching $8,000 or more.

Baltimore's funeral heritage. Baltimore has a storied African-American funeral home tradition dating back generations. These businesses serve their communities with homegoing celebrations, extended visitations, and services that honor both the individual and the community. Baltimore's unique neighborhoods each have their own funeral home traditions, and the city's overall funeral costs, while lower than the D.C. suburbs, remain above the national average.

Maryland Board of Morticians and Funeral Directors. The Board licenses all funeral professionals and establishments, maintains strong consumer protection standards, and oversees preneed contract compliance. Maryland's regulatory environment includes specific requirements for price disclosure beyond the federal Funeral Rule.

Religious and cultural diversity. Maryland's large Catholic population supports extensive funeral mass traditions. The significant Jewish community (concentrated in Baltimore and Montgomery County) requires specific funeral customs including tahara (ritual washing), simple pine caskets, and prompt burial. A growing Korean-American community in Howard and Anne Arundel counties observes distinct ancestral rites. Each of these traditions influences the range and pricing of services available.

Strong consumer protection. Maryland has robust consumer protection laws that go beyond federal requirements, which adds modest compliance costs but provides important safeguards for families.

Cremation vs. Burial in Maryland

Maryland's 55% cremation rate aligns closely with the national average. The D.C. suburbs and the state's more secular communities have higher cremation rates, while Baltimore's African-American communities and Catholic parishes tend toward traditional burial. The state's Jewish community follows Jewish law (halacha), which traditionally requires burial, though some Reform congregations have become more accepting of cremation. Maryland's high cemetery costs -- particularly in Montgomery County and parts of Baltimore -- are a significant factor pushing families toward cremation as an affordable alternative.

Maryland Funeral Regulations

The Maryland Board of Morticians and Funeral Directors oversees the state's funeral industry with comprehensive regulations:

  • Licensing — All morticians and funeral directors must hold active Maryland licenses with continuing education requirements.
  • Preneed protections — Maryland requires preneed contract funds to be placed in trust. Consumers have cancellation and transfer rights with provisions for refund.
  • No mandatory embalming — Maryland does not require embalming by law. Refrigeration is the accepted alternative.
  • Price transparency — Maryland enforces price disclosure requirements beyond the federal FTC Funeral Rule.
  • Cemetery oversight — Maryland regulates cemeteries, including requirements for perpetual care funds and proper maintenance.

Your Consumer Rights in Maryland

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Maryland must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

Maryland provides strong consumer protections beyond the federal Funeral Rule. The Board of Morticians and Funeral Directors investigates complaints and can impose disciplinary action. The Maryland Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division is active in enforcing fair business practices. Maryland's Consumer Protection Act provides additional remedies for deceptive practices. File complaints with the Board, the AG at marylandattorneygeneral.gov, the FTC at ftc.gov, or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Maryland?

The average traditional funeral in Maryland costs approximately $9,200, above the national median. The D.C. suburbs (Montgomery, Prince George's counties) are the most expensive areas, with costs reaching $10,000-$12,000. Western Maryland (Frederick, Hagerstown, Cumberland) is 20-30% more affordable. Direct cremation averages $2,200.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Maryland?

Yes, substantially. Direct cremation at $2,200 saves families $7,000 or more compared to a full traditional burial with all associated costs. Given Maryland's high cemetery plot costs (averaging $4,600, and much higher in Montgomery County), cremation offers significant savings. Maryland's 55% cremation rate reflects growing acceptance of this more affordable option.

What are my consumer rights at Maryland funeral homes?

Maryland consumers are protected by both the federal FTC Funeral Rule and state-level protections through the Board of Morticians and Funeral Directors and the Maryland Consumer Protection Act. You must receive an itemized price list, select only the services you want, and bring your own casket without penalty. Maryland's preneed contract protections include trust requirements and transfer rights.

Why are D.C. suburb funeral costs higher than the rest of Maryland?

Montgomery and Prince George's counties are part of the Washington D.C. metropolitan area, with high real estate costs, affluent demographics, and significant operating expenses for funeral homes and cemeteries. Premium cemetery plots in these counties can exceed $8,000. If cost is a primary concern, comparing providers in adjacent counties like Frederick or Howard may reveal more affordable options.

Does Maryland require embalming?

No. Maryland does not legally require embalming. Refrigeration is an accepted alternative. However, if you are planning an open-casket viewing, some funeral homes may recommend embalming as a practical matter. Always ask about alternatives.

What Jewish funeral options are available in Maryland?

Maryland's significant Jewish community is well-served by funeral homes experienced in Jewish funeral customs. Traditional Jewish funerals involve tahara (ritual washing), a simple pine casket (aron), burial within 24 hours when possible, and shiva observance. Several Baltimore and Montgomery County funeral homes specialize in or are fully experienced with Jewish funeral arrangements. Jewish cemeteries throughout the state offer dedicated burial space.
