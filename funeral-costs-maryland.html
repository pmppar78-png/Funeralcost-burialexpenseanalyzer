Funeral Costs in Maryland: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 What changed: Reworded the page in plainer, state-specific language and added the section “Maryland Funeral Charges by Line Item”. Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: Northeast

Maryland funerals cost more than most. Our estimate for a traditional service is $9,200, 11% above the $8,300 national median, driven by Baltimore and Washington-suburb costs, and 55% of Maryland deaths now end in cremation.

Where Maryland Families Find Funeral Money

  • Relatives can pay for the plot, vault, grave digging or clothing themselves without breaking Maryland's $2,500 limit, because those items don't count toward it.
  • Only a spouse or child who meets Social Security's rules can get its one-time $255, and the claim must be made at 1-800-772-1213 within two years.
  • When a Maryland veteran's death on or after October 1, 2026 was not service-connected, the person who paid can claim up to $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for a plot on VA Form 21P-530EZ; see our veteran burial benefits guide.
  • For Medicaid and charity help, see our assistance guide and the page on a funeral with no money.

What Moves Maryland Prices

Geography, mostly. Baltimore and the Washington suburbs typically price 10-30% above western Maryland and the Eastern Shore, and the state as a whole runs above the $8,300 US median.

Maryland Buyers and the FTC Rule

The FTC Funeral Rule applies in Maryland as everywhere: a price list you can keep, prices by phone, and no compulsory packages.

Report a Maryland home that breaks it to ftc.gov; the Funeral Consumers Alliance can help too.

Planning in Maryland, in Order

Follow these roughly in sequence to avoid paying for mistakes:

  1. First, burial or cremation: cremation in Maryland or burial in Maryland.

Maryland death certificates: $10, then $12

Maryland prices death certificates unusually: the first copy costs $10, and every additional copy issued in the same transaction costs $12 (Md. Health-Gen. § 4-217). Ordering online through VitalChek adds a $13 internet surcharge, and expedited shipping, available only online, is $20. If no record is found, the fee is kept and you get a Certification of Record Search instead (Maryland Division of Vital Records; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Which years: the Division of Vital Records issues certificates for deaths in Maryland from 2012 on. For earlier deaths, contact the Maryland State Archives.
  • Who may apply: a surviving relative or authorized representative, or the funeral director who handled the disposition.
  • By mail: mail the application with ID copies, proof of entitlement, payment by check or money order and a stamped envelope for the reply to Maryland's Division of Vital Records at P.O. Box 68760, Baltimore, MD 21215-0036. Mail orders cannot be expedited.
  • In person: the Baltimore lobby serves customers by appointment only; some local health departments also issue copies, and under a 2024 change to § 4-217 the MVA can issue them too.
  • Questions: call 410-767-6500 or toll-free 1-877-463-3464.

Because each extra copy costs more than the first, Maryland is one of the few states where ordering one at a time costs less per copy; still, a single order saves trips and processing time. Insurers, banks, retirement plans and the MVA for a title transfer usually each want a certified copy. Five copies in one order cost $58, $10 plus four at $12. Funeral homes can also order copies for you through the state's funeral facility portal or by mail, so ask what the home charges per copy before it adds them to the bill.

Maryland's regulator, burial assistance and veterans cemeteries

Funeral directors and funeral establishments are licensed through the Maryland Department of Health; the number for license checks and complaints is 410-764-4792.

Maryland does not appear on the Funeral Consumers Alliance's list of nine states that make a funeral director part of after-death care, so families can handle much of a funeral themselves, though the death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed. A cremation cannot take place until 12 hours after death (Md. Health-Gen. § 5-503).

Burial Assistance

The state's Burial Assistance program pays up to $650 toward the funeral of an eligible person, such as a public-assistance recipient, as long as the whole funeral costs $2,500 or less. A plot, vault, grave digging or clothing paid for by family or friends is left out of that $2,500 (COMAR 07.03.20.03). Apply before the bill is paid, because the program does not reimburse a paid funeral. The $255 Social Security payment is separate, goes only to an eligible spouse or child within two years, and is not paid to the funeral home.

Veterans

Baltimore National Cemetery (410-644-9696) takes cremated remains only and is booked through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office, 800-535-1117. Maryland's five state veterans cemeteries handle their own requests: Cheltenham (301-372-6398), Crownsville (410-987-6320), Eastern Shore (410-943-3420), Garrison Forest (410-363-6090) and Rocky Gap (301-777-2185).

Before you sign in Maryland

  1. Ask every Maryland funeral home for its General Price List; under the FTC Funeral Rule it must give you one in person. Prepaying is regulated by Md. Health Occ. §7-405: the contract must say whether it is guaranteed, and within 10 days the seller must deposit 100% of service payments, 80% of a casket or vault price, and all other goods payments in escrow or trust.
  2. Settle the embalming and viewing question early; Maryland requires express permission to embalm.
  3. Apply to the local department of social services before paying anything.
  4. A veteran may qualify for Crownsville or another state veterans cemetery, so check that first and buy a private plot only if not.
  5. If a home does not honor its price list, call the Department of Health at 410-764-4792.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Maryland?

We estimate about $9,200 for a traditional funeral with burial, $7,000 for a cremation with a service and $2,200 for direct cremation, plus roughly $4,600 for a plot. For your own figure, compare price lists from two or three homes near you.

How much does cremation save in Maryland?

Often $7,000 or more: about $2,200 for direct cremation against $9,200 for a funeral with burial, before the plot. Maryland's 55% cremation rate is below NFDA's 63.4% national projection for 2025.

What rules protect Maryland funeral buyers?

The FTC Funeral Rule gives you an itemized price list, phone quotes and the right to bring a casket from elsewhere with no fee. Maryland doesn't force families to hire a funeral director, according to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, and it bans embalming without express permission. Complaints go to the Maryland Department of Health, 410-764-4792.

How do Maryland families spend less?

Collect two or three price lists first. For a winter death, ask cemeteries about frozen-ground surcharges, common from December to March. Municipal, Catholic and Jewish cemeteries usually undercut private memorial parks by $1,000 or more, and direct cremation, about $2,200, is the cheapest choice anywhere in Maryland.

Must a body be embalmed in Maryland?

No. Maryland goes further than most states: under Health-General § 5-513(d), a body may not be embalmed at all without the express permission of the person authorized to arrange disposition, or a court order. If an unembalmed body will be stored more than 48 hours, the funeral home or crematory must refrigerate it (§ 5-513(b)).

Embalming costs about $920 in Maryland on this page's estimate, above NFDA's 2023 national median of $845.

How does Maryland compare with other states?

Maryland's $9,200 traditional-funeral estimate sits 11% over the $8,300 national median, eighth highest of the 50 states we estimate. It is above every neighboring state here: Delaware is $8,600, Pennsylvania $8,500 and Virginia $8,200.

The national $8,300 figure is NFDA's 2023 survey median; the state numbers are this site's estimates, not a separate survey. Maryland's direct cremation estimate is $2,200.

What's the least expensive choice in Maryland?

Direct cremation, from about $2,200. Direct burial comes next at around $4,600 and still avoids viewing, ceremony, and embalming, though the $4,600 Maryland plot estimate still applies.

How can I find a cheaper Maryland funeral home?

Compare the direct cremation lines from several Maryland funeral homes first; this site's estimate starts around $2,200. Maryland also requires express permission before embalming, so a low-cost provider should not be adding that charge unless you asked for it.

Maryland's State Anatomy Board runs a body donation program at no cost to the family when the donor dies in Maryland: it pays for transport, preparation and cremation. If the death happens in a neighboring state, the family pays to bring the body into Maryland and to file the death and cremation certificates.
