Cremation Costs in Virginia (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 50%

Virginia's 50% cremation rate sits almost exactly at the national midpoint, but the pricing map inside the state is anything but average. Direct cremation runs $1,900 statewide, yet the same line item at a Woody Funeral Home in Richmond, an Altmeyer in Norfolk, and a rural Southwest Virginia provider can vary by $600 or more. This guide walks through what Virginia families pay in 2026 and how the state's two-regulator cemetery structure, Hollywood and Arlington, and Hampton Roads water-table economics shape the real bill.

Cost ranges on this page draw on the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report, which documents a national cremation rate of roughly 60.5%. Consumer price disclosures follow the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and Virginia-specific licensing is administered by the Virginia Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers.

Virginia's three cremation markets: Richmond, Hampton Roads, and Northern Virginia

Virginia does not have a single cremation market. Woody Funeral Home anchors the Richmond area and tends to run at the mid-range of the $1,900 direct cremation benchmark. Altmeyer Funeral Home's Norfolk and Virginia Beach locations compete with a cluster of standalone cremation societies in Hampton Roads that routinely undercut the regional chains on direct cremation. Northern Virginia — Arlington Funeral Home and the Fairfax-area providers — runs roughly $300-$600 above the statewide median because of DC-adjacent operating costs. A family comparing a Richmond provider to a Norfolk provider to a Fairfax provider for the same service will almost always see a spread wider than $500.

The Perpetual Care Trust Fund Law and Virginia's dual regulators

Mortuaries in Virginia are regulated by the Virginia Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers, while cemeteries answer to the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation under the Perpetual Care Trust Fund Law. A cremation cost concern at the mortuary side — price-list transparency, casket surcharge, FTC Funeral Rule compliance — goes to the Funeral Board. A concern about a columbarium niche at a Virginia cemetery, a perpetual-care trust shortfall, or a plot misrepresentation goes to DPOR. Families often file with the wrong regulator and wait months for a referral; knowing which body has jurisdiction matters.

Hollywood, Oakwood, and Virginia National: where cremation inurnment sits

Hollywood Cemetery in Richmond (final resting place of Presidents Monroe and Tyler, and Jefferson Davis) and Oakwood Cemetery (the Confederate-military counterpart) both accept cremation inurnments in designated columbarium and garden sections. Hollywood pricing runs well above the state average — the historic inventory carries a premium. Oakwood sits lower. For Virginia veterans, Virginia National Cemetery at Amelia and the Hampton and Quantico national cemeteries accept cremated remains at no cost to the eligible veteran; Arlington National, across the Potomac, has far more restrictive eligibility than most families assume.

Hampton Roads water-table and the scattering-at-sea question

The Hampton Roads water table affects burial more than cremation, but it nudges Virginia Beach-area families toward cremation at the margin and makes ocean scattering a genuine option. Scattering from a Virginia charter boat beyond 3 nautical miles is straightforward under EPA rules and does not require a permit, though the captain typically files a notification. Atlantic scattering runs $250-$800 through Virginia charter operators. For the Piedmont and Shenandoah Valley counties, national forest and BLM scattering with a permit is free.

Local DSS funeral assistance and Virginia's burial benefit

Virginia's Department of Social Services administers a modest state burial assistance benefit through local DSS offices for qualifying indigent cases, and it applies to cremation as well as burial. The benefit caps well below a full direct cremation, but stacked with Social Security's lump-sum death payment and a provider willing to accept the assistance benefit as full payment, a Virginia family with no resources can arrange direct cremation with minimal out-of-pocket cost. The fastest route is contacting the local DSS before the family signs a provider contract.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does cremation cost in Virginia in 2026?

Cremation in Virginia runs from roughly $1,900 for the simplest direct option up to about $6,400 when paired with a viewing or ceremony. Urns, memorial services, and interment of ashes are priced separately — typically $50-$3,000 for an urn, $500-$2,000 for a memorial, and $200-$600 for scattering or inurnment. Your final number depends on which tier you pick.

Which cremation options exist in Virginia?

Virginia providers typically offer three tiers. Direct cremation at $1,900 is the simplest — no service, no viewing, body cremated and ashes returned. Cremation with a later memorial keeps the ceremony but shifts it to a venue of your choice, offering timing flexibility. Traditional cremation at $6,400 is a full funeral home service followed by cremation. Each can be adjusted to match family preferences and budget.

Is ash scattering legal in Virginia?

In most cases yes, but the specifics depend on where. In Virginia you can generally scatter on private land (with owner permission) or at sea beyond 3 nautical miles under EPA rules. National park scattering often requires a free permit. Check Virginia local ordinances and any park-specific rules before proceeding. Some families prefer columbarium niches, urn burial, or memorial jewelry to avoid the permitting question entirely.

How long does cremation take in Virginia?

In Virginia, the cremation process itself takes 2 to 3 hours. However, there is typically a mandatory 24 to 48 hour waiting period before cremation can proceed. After cremation, ashes are usually ready for pickup or delivery within 3 to 7 business days. Direct cremation ($1,900) has the shortest total timeline since no viewing or ceremony is involved.

Do I have to skip the service if I choose cremation in Virginia?

Not at all. Virginia providers offer cremation with a full service — visitation, ceremony, and then cremation instead of burial — for about $6,400. Memorial services after cremation are also common, letting families choose any venue or date. Only direct cremation at $1,900 omits the service entirely.
