Cremation Costs in Virginia (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Virginia cremation guide reworded throughout, covering medical examiner sign-off, scattering permits and local help. Cremation Rate: 50%

About half of Virginia families, roughly 50% by the rate we use, now choose cremation. Our estimate puts direct cremation at about $1,900 and cremation with a viewing or ceremony at about $6,400. Virginia adds one step most states do not: a medical examiner must clear every death before cremation. This page covers the prices, that requirement, scattering rules and the help a city or county can give.

Saving on a Virginia Cremation

How a Virginia family can spend less:

  • Get three price lists. Each Virginia funeral home must hand over its General Price List, and comparing three shows quickly where each sits in the $1,330 to $2,660 range for direct cremation.
  • Call a direct cremation provider. Providers that focus on simple cremation can cost less than a full-service home; check that any provider is licensed with the Virginia board.

City and Rural Cremation Prices in Virginia

Prices are not the same across Virginia. In metro areas such as Hampton Roads and Northern Virginia, several cremation providers often compete for the same families, which tends to keep direct cremation toward the lower part of the $1,330-$2,660 range. In rural counties with only one funeral home, quotes can sit higher, so it can be worth calling a provider in the nearest city as well.

What to Ask a Virginia Provider

Before choosing, confirm these with the Virginia provider:

  • What is included. The price list has to spell out what the direct cremation fee covers, normally collecting the body, the cremation and a temporary urn.
  • The container. A casket is not required; under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR 453.3(b)) a Virginia provider must allow an unfinished wood box or other alternative container.
  • The timing. Ask when the ashes will be ready after the medical examiner signs off, how they will be returned, and whether a certified death certificate is included.
In Virginia you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Virginia bring a container, and the VA Board of Funeral Directors & Embalmers is who you call if a home in Virginia refuses it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is cremation in Virginia?

Our estimate is about $1,900 for a direct cremation in Virginia and about $6,400 for cremation with a viewing or ceremony.

What cremation choices do Virginia providers offer?

Most Virginia providers price three arrangements. Direct cremation, about $1,900, has no service or viewing; the body is cremated and the ashes returned. Cremation with a memorial afterwards sits in between. Traditional cremation, about $6,400, adds a full funeral home service first, roughly $4,500 more than direct cremation.

What are Virginia's cremation regulations?

Virginia sets no minimum number of hours, but one step is easy to overlook: no one who died in the state may be cremated until a medical examiner has decided the death needs no further medicolegal inquiry and has signed a certificate on a form from the Office of the Chief Medical Examiner (Va. Code § 32.1-309.3). That applies whatever the cause and manner of death, and the examiner's fee, capped by the Board of Health, is paid by whoever applies. For the exact Virginia citation, the funeral-director question and who takes complaints, see the cremation rules and help section lower on this page.

Can I scatter ashes in Virginia?

Generally yes. No Virginia statute says where a family may scatter ashes, so the requirement is permission from whoever owns or manages the land. The one law that mentions scattering, Va. Code § 54.1-2808.1, applies to providers: a funeral establishment may scatter remains left unclaimed for 120 days and must keep a permanent record of how and where.

For Shenandoah National Park or the Blue Ridge Parkway, get a permit under 36 CFR 2.62. At sea, the scattering must be at least three nautical miles out and reported to EPA Region 3 within 30 days. Complaints about a provider go to the Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers (804-367-4479).

How many Virginia families choose cremation?

Roughly half, about 50% by this guide's estimate, which is below the 63.4% national rate NFDA projected for 2025. Price drives many of those decisions: direct cremation is about $1,900 here, against a funeral service averaging near $8,200 before the plot and vault. Every Virginia cremation must be authorized by the Office of the Chief Medical Examiner (Va. Code § 54.1-2818.1).

How long does a Virginia cremation take?

Only 2 to 3 hours of it is the cremation. The wait before it is mostly paperwork: Virginia sets no minimum time, so the medical examiner's authorization and the permit decide the date (Va. Code 54.1-2818.1). Direct cremation ($1,900) is quickest because there is no service to schedule.

Can I still have a funeral if I choose cremation in Virginia?

No. A Virginia family can hold a visitation and funeral and then cremate rather than bury, for about $6,400 on our estimate. Only direct cremation, at about $1,900, leaves the service out altogether, and a memorial can still be held later.

Virginia's medical examiner clearance, needed for every cremation

Virginia requires a medical examiner to clear every cremation of a death in the state, whatever the cause: the Chief Medical Examiner, an assistant or an appointed local medical examiner must certify on the Office of the Chief Medical Examiner's form that no further medicolegal inquiry is needed. The examiner is entitled to a fee set by the Board of Health, paid by whoever applies for the certificate, which in practice is usually the funeral home (Va. Code § 32.1-309.3; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Identification: the next of kin or their representative must also identify the person visually, or from a photograph, before the cremation (Va. Code § 54.1-2818.1).
  • Waiting at the crematory: if a crematory cannot cremate within 24 hours of taking custody, it must keep unembalmed remains refrigerated at about 40°F or below (18VAC65-20-436).

Virginia's board, medical examiner sign-off and local help

The Virginia Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers (804-367-4479) licenses cremation providers; call it to check a license or report a problem.

Virginia does not appear on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that require a funeral director. Even so, no Virginia crematory starts without a death certificate, a permit and written consent from the next of kin.

Virginia sets no waiting period. What it does require, under Va. Code 54.1-2818.1, is a next-of-kin identification of the body and an authorization from the Office of the Chief Medical Examiner for each cremation.

If the family cannot pay

When the person claiming the body cannot afford it, a Virginia city or county may pay for the cremation (Va. Code § 32.1-309.1). (Source: Va. Code §§ 32.1-309.1, 32.1-309.2.) A spouse or child who qualifies can also apply to Social Security for the $255 death payment, as long as it is within two years.

A qualifying veteran's ashes can be placed in a national cemetery columbarium or in-ground site with no charge for the space. Virginia's options include the National Memorial Cemetery at Quantico (703-221-2183), Culpeper National Cemetery (540-825-0027) and Danville National Cemetery (704-636-2661, cremated remains only); see cem.va.gov.

Virginia checklist

  1. Ask whether death certificates and the permit fee are included in the price.
  2. Ask how long the medical examiner's authorization usually takes before setting a memorial date.
  3. If the family cannot pay, ask the city or county whether it will cover the cremation.
  4. Report misstated prices to the board at 804-367-4479.
