Burial Costs in Idaho (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: Mountain

Idaho offers some of the most affordable burial options in the western United States, shaped by the state's rural character, strong community ties, and the influence of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, whose members make up a significant portion of the population. From the growing Boise metro area — where costs are rising with rapid population growth — to the quiet small-town cemeteries of the Snake River Plain, the Magic Valley, and the northern panhandle, Idaho's burial landscape is defined by simplicity, practicality, and reverence for family. The state's many LDS-affiliated cemeteries, municipal graveyards, and community burial grounds keep costs well below the national average for most families.

What Drives Burial Costs in Idaho

LDS Cemetery Traditions

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints has a significant presence in Idaho, and many communities have cemeteries affiliated with or influenced by LDS traditions. LDS funerals emphasize simplicity and family involvement, often keeping costs lower. Many small-town cemeteries in southeastern Idaho serve predominantly LDS populations.

Plot Prices by Area

Boise metro (including Meridian, Nampa, Caldwell): $1,500-$4,500. Idaho Falls/Pocatello: $1,000-$3,000. Twin Falls: $800-$2,500. Coeur d'Alene/North Idaho: $1,200-$3,500. Small-town Idaho: $400-$1,500.

Notable Idaho Cemeteries

Idaho State Veterans Cemetery in Boise serves eligible veterans at no cost. Morris Hill Cemetery in Boise, established in 1882, is the city's oldest and most historic cemetery. Many small-town cemeteries across Idaho offer beautiful mountain or valley settings.

Cemetery Types Available in Idaho

  • Municipal cemeteries — The most common type in Idaho cities and towns. Affordable and well-maintained.
  • Church-affiliated cemeteries — Including LDS, Catholic, and Protestant cemeteries, particularly in southeastern Idaho.
  • Veterans cemeteries — Idaho State Veterans Cemetery in Boise. Learn about veteran burial benefits
  • Community cemeteries — Small-town and rural burial grounds maintained by local associations.
  • Private memorial parks — Limited options, primarily in the Boise area.

Casket and Vault Options in Idaho

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, Idaho families can buy caskets from any retailer. Online options save 50-80%. Idaho has no state law requiring vaults, though most commercial cemeteries require them.

Consumer Rights for Burial in Idaho

  • FTC Funeral Rule — Itemized pricing required.
  • Idaho Bureau of Occupational Licenses — Oversees funeral home licensing.
  • Embalming not required by Idaho law.
  • Home funerals permitted — Idaho allows families to handle burial without a funeral director.

Green and Natural Burial in Idaho

Idaho's natural beauty and outdoor culture make green burial a growing interest. Some municipal and community cemeteries accommodate natural burial. The state's rural character means many cemeteries have never required vaults, making them naturally suited to green burial. Learn more about green burial options

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Idaho?

A traditional burial in Idaho costs approximately $6,700 for the funeral service plus $3,000 for a cemetery plot. Total costs range from $12,400 to $17,410. Small-town cemeteries offer the most affordable options.

Is a burial vault required in Idaho?

No state law requires vaults in Idaho. Most commercial cemeteries require them, but many small-town and community cemeteries do not.

What are the cheapest burial options in Idaho?

Direct burial at a small-town municipal cemetery is most affordable, potentially $5,000-$8,000 total. Many Idaho communities have very affordable plot prices. Veterans receive free burial at the Idaho State Veterans Cemetery in Boise.

Can I be buried on private property in Idaho?

Yes. Idaho permits burial on private property with compliance to local regulations. Check with your county for specific requirements.
