Medicaid Funeral Assistance: What Families Need to Know in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 22, 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Government Sources Editorial: Our Standards

Learn how Medicaid can help pay for funeral costs including state programs, eligibility requirements, and application steps. Medicaid itself does not pay a funeral bill in most states. What it does is let a person set money aside for a funeral without losing eligibility, and some states run a separate burial program for people who were on Medicaid.

Understanding Medicaid Funeral Assistance

Most of the confusion comes from mixing up two different things. The first is the asset rule: SSI and most state Medicaid programs do not count certain burial funds and burial spaces when deciding whether someone qualifies. The second is direct help with the bill, which comes only from state or county programs, and only in some places.

For SSI, 20 CFR § 416.1231 excludes up to $1,500 in funds set aside for burial expenses, and excludes burial spaces such as a plot, a vault or a casket regardless of value. Many states apply the same or similar limits for Medicaid. A person applying for long-term care Medicaid often buys an irrevocable prepaid funeral contract for this reason, because an irrevocable contract usually is not counted as an asset; the state Medicaid agency decides what qualifies.

What You Need to Know

The facts that change what a family can plan for:

  • A revocable prepaid funeral plan can be cashed out, so Medicaid usually counts it as an asset. An irrevocable plan generally is not counted, but it also cannot be cancelled for a refund.
  • Life insurance with a cash value can count as an asset. Term life insurance has no cash value and is treated differently; check the state's rule before you change a policy.
  • Some states pay a capped amount toward the funeral of a Medicaid recipient. Indiana, for example, pays up to $1,200 toward the funeral director's charges and up to $800 toward cemetery charges for recipients in certain Medicaid categories, with the claim filed by the provider.   • Where a state program exists, it usually pays the funeral home or cemetery directly and may limit what the total funeral can cost.
  • After a recipient dies, federal law requires states to seek repayment from the estates of people who were 55 or older for certain Medicaid costs (42 U.S.C. § 1396p(b)). Money placed in an irrevocable funeral contract is typically spent on the funeral, not recovered.
  • A county indigent burial or cremation program is separate from Medicaid and has its own application, often through the county coroner, social services office or human services department.
  • The FTC Funeral Rule still applies when public money pays part of the bill. Ask for the General Price List and compare. Read our FTC guide

Costs and Pricing

State burial programs are capped and modest, so it helps to know what a basic funeral costs before talking to a funeral home.

The NFDA 2023 General Price List Study reports a national median of $2,645 for direct cremation with a container supplied by the family, and $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, not counting cemetery costs. A state payment in the low thousands covers a direct cremation in many areas but not a full traditional funeral.

If a state program will contribute, ask the funeral home whether it accepts that program's payment and whether the program caps the total cost of the funeral. Get the price for direct cremation and immediate burial, which every funeral home must list, and compare at least two or three homes with the funeral cost comparison worksheet.

Your Consumer Rights

Protections that apply when a funeral is paid partly by Medicaid or a public program:

  • The funeral home must give the family an itemized General Price List, even if a state program is paying. Full FTC guide
  • You can choose only the goods and services you want, so a program's cap can be met by leaving out optional items. How to compare prices
  • A provider cannot require a casket for direct cremation; an unfinished wood box or alternative container must be offered.
  • For a prepaid plan, ask for the contract in writing and confirm whether it is irrevocable, who holds the money, and what happens if the funeral home closes.
  • If a Medicaid application is denied because of a funeral plan or policy, the applicant has the right to a fair hearing with the state agency.

Complaints about a funeral home go to the state funeral board or the Federal Trade Commission; disputes about eligibility go to the state Medicaid agency.

Practical Steps You Can Take

  1. Find out which Medicaid program the person is on, because state burial payments are often limited to certain categories.
  2. Call the state Medicaid agency or county social services office and ask whether it pays any funeral or burial costs, and how to claim it.
  3. Ask about deadlines; Indiana, for example, requires the funeral home or cemetery to file within 90 days.
  4. If the person is still living and applying for Medicaid, ask the agency how it treats irrevocable funeral contracts and burial funds before buying one.
  5. Get General Price Lists from two or three funeral homes and ask which of them accept the state's payment.
  6. Check for other help at the same time: the Social Security $255 payment, a VA burial allowance, or a county indigent burial program.
  7. Keep copies of every invoice and the death certificate; programs usually ask for both.
  8. Read any estate recovery notice carefully and ask the agency about hardship waivers if the estate includes a home where family still lives.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Medicaid pay for funerals?

In most states Medicaid does not pay a funeral bill directly. Some states run burial assistance for recipients, often with a fixed cap paid to the funeral home or cemetery; others leave it to counties.

Can I keep a prepaid funeral plan and still qualify for Medicaid?

Usually, if the plan is irrevocable. Revocable plans can be cashed in, so they are generally counted as assets. Ask the state Medicaid agency before buying.

How much can be set aside for burial under SSI?

Under 20 CFR § 416.1231, up to $1,500 in designated burial funds is excluded, and burial spaces such as a plot or casket are excluded regardless of value. State Medicaid rules can differ.

What does Indiana's program pay?

Up to $1,200 toward the funeral director's charges, including cremation, and up to $800 toward cemetery charges, for recipients in certain Medicaid categories. The provider files the claim within 90 days.

Will the state take money back from the estate?

Federal law requires states to seek recovery from the estates of recipients who were 55 or older for certain costs, under 42 U.S.C. § 1396p(b). Hardship waivers and exemptions exist.

Who applies for a state burial payment?

It varies. In some states the funeral home files; in others the family applies through county social services. Ask before the funeral is arranged.

What if the family cannot pay and there is no Medicaid benefit?

Ask the county about indigent burial or cremation, and see our guide to paying for a funeral with no money.
