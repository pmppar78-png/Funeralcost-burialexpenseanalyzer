Average Cost of a Funeral in Tennessee (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 22, 2026 Region: South

Burial costs in Tennessee are shaped by three separate bills: the funeral home's service fee (around $6,700), the cemetery's plot and interment charges (starting near $3,000), and the headstone or marker (typically $500–$5,000).

How to Reduce Burial Costs in Tennessee

Burial costs in Tennessee can be managed with smart planning, especially for families facing funeral costs without insurance coverage:

  • Compare cemetery prices directly: Cemeteries are not covered by the federal trade commission Funeral Rule but most in Tennessee will share a price sheet on request, while a funeral provider must give consumers a general price list or complete price list showing the services shown and only the items selected.
  • If pricing rules aren't followed: You can file a complaint with the Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors if a funeral home does not provide required pricing disclosures or a written statement to provide describing the funeral goods and services selected, including any legal or other requirements.
  • Check church and family cemeteries: Active church and family-owned cemeteries are still common in rural Tennessee and routinely price plots 30-50% below commercial memorial parks.
  • County veterans' sections: Many Tennessee counties maintain dedicated veterans' sections separate from national cemeteries.
  • In some regions, families may weigh this against direct cremation and other affordable cremation options depending on local pricing.

  • Budget for paperwork: A death certificate in Tennessee costs $15 per copy, and families often need several copies for banks, insurance, and estate matters. Some families also seek financial help to cover funeral costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the full price of burial in Tennessee?

A full burial in Tennessee comes in at about $6,700 for the service charge, which typically covers basic services from the funeral director and staff plus a proportionate share of overhead costs, $3,000 for the cemetery plot, $2,010 for a casket, $1,200 for a vault, $1,500 for opening and closing, and $500-$5,000 for a headstone. Totals generally land between $14,910 and $19,410 depending on choices, and extras like a funeral ceremony or graveside service can raise the total.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Tennessee?

Some cemeteries require an outer burial container, which may be a vault or a less expensive grave liner, to keep the ground stable over time. A liner is usually an acceptable substitute — always ask the specific Tennessee cemetery for their written policy.

What are the cheapest burial options in Tennessee?

A loved one's remains may still require transportation to the funeral home, cemetery, or crematory, so ask what services are included before you pay. Many funeral homes may also offer payment flexibility or local referrals for assistance. FEMA may provide financial assistance for funeral expenses in certain cases. Low-income families may also qualify for indigent burial programs, typically if they earn under federal poverty guidelines, and local charities may help cover burial expenses. Eligible veterans may receive burial benefits through the VA, and some families may also receive a small social security survivor payment.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Tennessee?

Tennessee cemeteries price plots, opening and closing, and vault rules separately, so ask each one for a written price list. The FTC Funeral Rule covers funeral homes, not most cemeteries, so a Tennessee cemetery may not be required to give an itemized price list; ask for one anyway. VA national cemeteries in Tennessee include Chattanooga National Cemetery and Knoxville National Cemetery.

How can you plan funeral arrangements in Tennessee ahead of time?

Pre-planned funeral arrangements are called preneed contracts in Tennessee, and the funds are generally held in trust accounts. A Totten Trust is another way to set aside money to pay funeral costs. If you receive Medicaid, be aware of the $6,000 limit on this type of plan. Writing down final wishes can make things easier for family, and arrangements should be reviewed and updated over time.

What is an all-in burial estimate for Tennessee?

Adding every line item, burial in Tennessee usually totals $14,910 to $19,410: funeral service about $6,700, plot about $3,000, casket about $2,010, vault about $1,200, opening-and-closing about $1,500, and headstone $500-$5,000.

Is green burial available in Tennessee?

Not all cemeteries in Tennessee offer green burial sections, so you may need to research options in your area.

Burial rules and help in Tennessee

For a license check or complaint about a funeral home arranging a burial in Tennessee, call the Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers at 615-741-5062; Tennessee cemeteries can have a separate regulator.

Tennessee is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict families from caring for their own dead. You can buy only the services you need, but someone must still file the Tennessee death certificate and get the permit.

No cremation wait affects a burial, but if plans change to cremation, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (Tenn. Code Ann. 62-5-504).

Tennessee help with funeral costs, where it exists, is usually run by counties, and we could not confirm a statewide figure. Start with the Tennessee county social-services office; our public assistance guide covers what to ask. Separately, Social Security pays Tennessee survivors $255 once, if they are an eligible spouse or child.

Eligible Tennessee veterans get a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. In Tennessee, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Chattanooga National Cemetery (423-855-6590); Knoxville National Cemetery (423-979-3535, cremated remains only); Memphis National Cemetery (901-386-8311, cremated remains only); state veterans cemeteries: East Tennessee State Veterans Cemetery (865-577-3228); Middle Tennessee State Veterans Cemetery (615-532-2238); Tennessee State Veterans Cemetery at Parkers Crossroads (731-967-4127). Source: VA National Cemetery Administration.

What to do next

  1. Request itemized prices from Tennessee providers by phone or in person; under the FTC Funeral Rule you only pay for what you choose.
  2. Ask whether the Tennessee cemetery lets you buy the headstone elsewhere.
  3. Ask your Tennessee county human-services office about indigent burial help before you commit.
  4. For a veteran, ask Chattanooga National Cemetery about eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. Keep the board's number, 615-741-5062, in case a provider will not honor its price list.
