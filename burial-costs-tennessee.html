Burial Costs in Tennessee (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: South

Tennessee's burial market splits roughly three ways — the Nashville basin around Mount Olivet, the Memphis riverfront at Elmwood, and the Appalachian eastern counties where family and church cemeteries still carry much of the load. Elmwood Cemetery in Memphis, established in 1852, and Mount Olivet in Nashville, opened in 1856, together hold a substantial fraction of the state's historically prominent graves. Total burial costs in Tennessee run $12,400 to $17,410, with the lower end increasingly realistic if rural cemetery pricing is available.

A Tennessee traditional burial generally totals $12,400 to $17,410. Marshall-Donnelly or Woodlawn-Roesch-Patton in Nashville and Memorial Park Funeral Home in Memphis anchor the full-service end; a small-county funeral home paired with a church cemetery can easily come in under $9,000.

Tennessee's Cemetery Act and why it matters

Tennessee's Cemetery Act (Title 46) sets rules for perpetual care, cemetery company licensing, and trust funds for endowment care. The state requires that a portion of every plot sale be deposited in an irrevocable trust for long-term maintenance — a consumer protection that did not exist before the 1968 law. The Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors & Embalmers regulates mortuaries; cemetery complaints go to the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance's Regulatory Boards division. Before buying preneed plots, verify that the cemetery is registered and current on its endowment-care reporting.

Three Tennessees, three burial economies

East Tennessee's Appalachian counties retain the strongest church-cemetery tradition in the state and some of the lowest burial costs in the Southeast — plot fees of $500–$1,200 are still common in Hamblen, Greene, and Sullivan counties. Middle Tennessee, centered on Nashville, has the widest corporate-cemetery footprint and the strongest growth in premium garden and estate sections; Mount Olivet Cemetery (established 1856) anchors the historic end, Spring Hill handles the volume end. West Tennessee, anchored by Memphis, has some of the oldest cemeteries in the South — Elmwood (founded 1852) alone has more than 75,000 documented interments. A Knoxville family and a Memphis family facing identical line items will routinely see $3,000 of spread, driven mostly by cemetery choice.

Veterans: three national cemeteries plus state option

Tennessee is unusually well-served for eligible veterans. Memphis National, Mountain Home National (in Mountain Home near Johnson City), and Chattanooga National all provide grave, opening and closing, outer burial container, and marker at no cost. The state-run Middle Tennessee State Veterans Cemetery in Pegram serves the Nashville area. For qualifying veterans and dependents this is the single largest available cost reduction in the state — the veteran burial benefits page walks through eligibility, which is wider than most families expect.

Larkspur Conservation and Tennessee's green-burial option

Larkspur Conservation at Taylor Hollow in Middle Tennessee is the state's first certified conservation burial ground. Total cost for a Larkspur interment typically lands between $3,000 and $5,000, with no vault, no embalming, and no hardwood casket. For families open to a different aesthetic, it is a bundled cost reduction that strips three line items out at once. The green burial options guide compares hybrid and conservation approaches; traditional cemeteries in Nashville and Chattanooga have begun opening green sections as well.

FTC Funeral Rule in Tennessee: Every licensed Tennessee funeral home must provide an itemized General Price List, cannot charge a handling fee on third-party caskets, and must unbundle services you did not request. Cemetery pricing is not covered — ask anyway; most Tennessee cemeteries will itemize. Start with the casket buying guide and the best online casket retailers.

An honest Nashville negotiating tactic

In Nashville, call the cemetery before the funeral home. The plot-plus-opening spread between Mount Olivet, Spring Hill, and the Catholic cemeteries is typically $2,000–$3,500 for functionally comparable interment. Once the cemetery is locked in, the funeral-home quote becomes apples-to-apples, and the largest remaining variance is the casket markup — often 300–400%. Pairing the cheapest cemetery with a mid-price independent funeral home and a third-party casket routinely shaves $4,000 from the quoted midpoint. The headstone cost guide shows similar spread on monument work.

Paying for it: state assistance and church resources

The Tennessee Department of Human Services administers a limited burial assistance program for qualifying low-income cases; allowances are modest and aimed at direct burial. In East Tennessee especially, church cemeteries and congregational help often fill the gap — a tradition worth checking even if the family has not been active for years. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the full price of burial in Tennessee?

A typical Tennessee burial: service $6,700, plot $3,000, casket $2,010, vault $1,200, opening and closing $1,500, headstone $1,000–$3,000. Total lands between $12,400 and $17,410. East Tennessee church cemeteries bring the low end down; Nashville premium sections push the high end up.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Tennessee?

State law does not require a vault. Mount Olivet, Elmwood, Spring Hill, and the state's commercial memorial parks require either a vault or a concrete grave liner under their own policies. Many rural church cemeteries have no vault requirement at all. The liner option usually runs 50–60% cheaper than a full vault.

What are the cheapest burial options in Tennessee?

Memphis National Cemetery, Middle Tennessee State Veterans Cemetery, and Mountain Home National Cemetery are free for eligible veterans. For civilians, direct burial at a rural church cemetery with a third-party casket often comes in well under $6,000. Larkspur Conservation at Taylor Hollow offers certified green burial at $3,000–$5,000 all in.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Tennessee?

Tennessee cemeteries must operate under the Cemetery Act and maintain endowment-care trust funds. The Department of Commerce and Insurance publishes a list of registered cemeteries — use it to verify the cemetery is current on its reporting before buying preneed. Most cemeteries will provide itemized pricing on request even though the FTC Funeral Rule does not compel it.

What is an all-in burial estimate for Tennessee?

Sum the individual line items and a complete Tennessee burial costs $12,400–$17,410: service $6,700, plot $3,000, casket $2,010, vault $1,200, opening and closing $1,500, headstone $1,000–$3,000. Comparing Mount Olivet or Elmwood against a county or church cemetery is the fastest route to meaningful reductions.

Is green burial available in Tennessee?

Yes. Larkspur Conservation at Taylor Hollow, south of Nashville, is Tennessee's first certified conservation burial ground. Several traditional cemeteries also maintain hybrid natural burial sections with no vault and no embalming requirement. Green burial in Tennessee typically totals $3,000–$5,000 with no vault, embalming, or traditional casket.
