Cremation Jewelry Guide: What Families Need to Know in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 22, 2026 Last reviewed: October 22, 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Government Sources Editorial: Our Standards

Explore cremation jewelry and memorial keepsakes including types, costs, materials, and options. Cremation jewelry holds a very small portion of ashes, usually in a sealed chamber inside a pendant, ring or bracelet. This page covers how the pieces work, what to check before buying, and how they fit with the rest of the cremation arrangements.

Understanding Cremation Jewelry Guide

Most pieces have a hollow chamber closed with a tiny screw, and many sellers recommend sealing the threads with adhesive so the ashes cannot escape. Others set the ashes into glass or resin so they are permanently enclosed. Only a pinch is needed, so families commonly keep the rest in an urn, scatter it, or place it in a columbarium or grave.

Jewelry is merchandise, like an urn. A funeral home may offer it, but under the FTC Funeral Rule you can buy it, or the urn, from any seller, and the funeral home cannot charge a fee for handling items you bought elsewhere. Filling the jewelry is usually done by the family at home with a small funnel, or by the seller or funeral home for a fee.

What You Need to Know

Before ordering a piece:

  • Check the metal. Stainless steel and titanium resist corrosion; sterling silver tarnishes; plated pieces can wear through. Ask what the chamber is made of, not only the outside.
  • Ask how the chamber is sealed and whether the piece can be worn in water. A screw seal without adhesive can loosen with wear.
  • Glass or resin pieces that contain ashes cannot be opened again, so keep some ashes aside if you might want them later.
  • Ask the crematory or funeral home to set aside a small amount for jewelry before the rest goes into a permanent urn.
  • For air travel, the TSA says cremated remains can go through the checkpoint in a container that X-rays clearly, and officers will not open the container even if asked. Some airlines do not allow remains in checked bags. Read our FTC guide
  • Keepsake urns and jewelry can let several relatives share the ashes when the family cannot agree on one place.
  • If the rest of the ashes will be scattered at sea, EPA rules under 40 CFR § 229.1 require it to be at least three nautical miles from shore, with notice to EPA within 30 days. Cremation vs. burial comparison
  • The NFDA 2023 median price of an urn on a funeral home price list was $295. Keepsake jewelry is usually bought in addition to an urn, not instead of one.
  • Ask how the remaining ashes will be returned after a jeweler takes a portion, and keep the crematory's identification paperwork with them.

Costs and Pricing

Jewelry is usually a small part of the cost of a cremation, but it is easy to overpay when it is bundled into a package.

The NFDA 2023 General Price List Study puts the national median for direct cremation at $2,645 when the family supplies the container, and $6,280 for a funeral with viewing and cremation. Urns and keepsake jewelry are separate merchandise and must be priced individually on request.

Compare the funeral home's price for the same piece with online and local jewelers, and ask whether a filling or engraving fee is extra. If the cremation package includes jewelry you do not want, ask for the itemized price without it, which the Funeral Rule lets you do. Note all of it on the funeral cost comparison worksheet. Because jewelry is a separate purchase, it does not change the direct cremation price; NFDA's 2023 median cremation fee alone was $400.

Your Consumer Rights

Rights that apply to urns and cremation keepsakes:

  • You can buy an urn or keepsake jewelry from any seller; the funeral home cannot refuse it or charge for using it. Full FTC guide
  • For direct cremation, the funeral home must make an alternative container available and cannot require a casket. How to compare prices
  • Package prices are optional; you can buy only the items you want from the General Price List.
  • Online jewelry sellers are covered by ordinary consumer protection law, so get the return policy and the materials in writing.
  • Ask the crematory how it identifies remains and returns them, and get the cremation authorization and receipt.

Complaints about a funeral home or crematory go to the state funeral or cemetery board or the Federal Trade Commission; complaints about an online seller go to your state attorney general.

Practical Steps You Can Take

  1. Decide what will happen to most of the ashes, since jewelry holds only a small amount.
  2. Ask the crematory or funeral home to set aside a portion for keepsakes.
  3. Choose a material that suits daily wear, and confirm how the chamber is sealed.
  4. Compare the funeral home's price with independent sellers.
  5. Fill the piece carefully over a tray, or ask the seller or funeral home to fill and seal it.
  6. Keep the cremation certificate with the remaining ashes in case of travel or later interment.
  7. Check airline rules before flying with remains.
  8. Tell relatives who has which keepsake so the pieces are not lost later.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much ash goes into cremation jewelry?

Very little, typically a pinch. The rest stays in an urn or is scattered or interred.

Can I fill cremation jewelry myself?

Yes. Most pieces come with a small funnel and screw. Seal the screw with adhesive if the seller recommends it.

Does the funeral home have to accept jewelry or an urn I bought elsewhere?

Yes. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, it cannot refuse merchandise bought elsewhere or charge you to use it.

Can I fly with cremation jewelry?

The TSA allows cremated remains through the checkpoint if the container can be X-rayed clearly, and officers will not open it. Check the airline's rules as well.

Can the ashes be removed later?

From a screw-chamber piece, yes. Ashes set into glass or resin cannot be removed.

Is cremation jewelry waterproof?

Some pieces are sealed for water, many are not. Ask the seller and remove the piece for swimming if unsure.

What other keepsake options exist?

Small keepsake urns, glass art and memorial diamonds made from carbon are other options; see direct cremation costs for the cremation itself.

Can I buy cremation jewelry from a seller other than the funeral home?

Yes. Independent jewelers and online retailers sell cremation jewelry, and the funeral home's selection is only one option; compare the metal, the seal and the return policy before buying.
