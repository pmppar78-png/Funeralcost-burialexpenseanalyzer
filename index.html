Cost ranges are compiled from publicly available data including NFDA consumer surveys (2021–2023), state funeral board price surveys, Funeral Consumers Alliance reports, and published General Price Lists. Many tools, including a final expenses calculator, start with national medians and average costs before applying regional adjustments; some also fill fields with national figures that may not reflect local prices. Regional adjustments are based on cost-of-living indices and metropolitan vs. rural pricing differentials. All figures represent typical ranges and are for illustrative purposes only; your actual costs will depend on the specific provider, location, and choices you make. Frequently asked questions

How much does a funeral cost on average?

The median cost of a funeral with viewing and burial is approximately $8,300; cremation averages around $6,280 (NFDA data). Costs vary widely by state and provider. A typical direct cremation costs roughly $2,200, though prices still vary by provider and area. Request itemized price lists from several local funeral homes; they're required by law to provide them, and transportation services can add significantly to the total.

Traditional funeral vs. direct cremation?

A traditional funeral includes embalming, viewing, ceremony, and burial. Direct cremation skips these: the body is cremated shortly after death, and families may hold a memorial later. Cremation is generally more affordable than burial.

Do I have to buy a casket from the funeral home?

No. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, funeral homes must accept caskets from third-party retailers without a handling fee. Comparing prices can save hundreds or thousands of dollars.

What is the FTC Funeral Rule?

A federal regulation requiring funeral providers to give you itemized price lists, let you choose only what you want (no forced packages), and prohibit false claims about legal requirements. The basic services fee is mandatory and non-declinable even when other items are optional. If violated, you can file a complaint with the FTC.

Is embalming legally required?

In most states, no. It may be required for open-casket viewings by funeral home policy. Alternatives include refrigeration or dry ice. If told it's mandatory, ask for the specific law; it's usually a policy, not a legal requirement.

Can I plan a funeral without a funeral home?

In many states, families can handle arrangements themselves ("home funeral"). Laws vary; some states require a licensed director for certain steps. Research your state's laws or consult your state's funeral board.

What is a General Price List and how do I use it?

A General Price List (GPL) is an itemized list of every service and product a funeral home offers, with prices. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, funeral homes must provide this to you upon request, in person or over the phone. The GPL is your most powerful tool for comparison shopping. Request GPLs from 2–3 providers and compare line items directly. If a funeral home refuses to provide one, they are violating federal law.

Should I prepay for a funeral?

Prepaying can lock in current prices and reduce the burden on your family, but it also carries risks. Consumer protections for prepaid funeral contracts vary significantly by state. Before signing any preneed contract, ask: What happens if the funeral home closes or is sold? Is the money held in trust? Can you cancel and get a refund? Can the contract be transferred to another provider? Consider consulting with a financial advisor and reviewing your state's preneed regulations before committing.

Glossary of common terms

Terms you may encounter when speaking with funeral homes and cemeteries.

General Price List (GPL)
A printed list of all goods, services, and prices from a funeral home. They must give you this by law.
Embalming
Chemical preservation for viewing or transport. Not legally required in most states.
Direct cremation
Cremation without viewing or ceremony. Usually the least expensive option.
Direct burial
Burial shortly after death, skipping embalming and formal ceremony; immediate burial usually excludes visitation or a formal ceremony. Burial typically also involves a casket, grave plot, and vault or liner.
Casket vs. coffin
Casket: rectangular, split lid. Coffin: tapers at feet. "Casket" is more common in the U.S.
Vault or grave liner
Container placed in the ground before the casket. These outer burial container options, sometimes called burial containers, are often required by cemeteries, and burials require additional costs for grave plots and vaults.
Preneed contract
Pre-planning agreement for funeral services. Consumer protections vary by state.
Opening & closing fees
Cemetery charges for digging and filling the grave. Separate from the plot cost, these cemetery fees are often additional costs and may cover digging and fill.

How we source and verify cost data

Our 2026 cost estimates blend several named public sources to keep ranges honest and reproducible:

  • NFDA 2023 General Price List Study: The National Funeral Directors Association's latest nationwide survey of itemized funeral-home pricing forms the core of our national medians.
  • NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report & CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report: Used to project cremation-rate adoption and service-fee spread.
  • BLS Consumer Price Index: Funeral Services (CUUR0000SEGD02): Used to forward-project 2024 figures into 2026 dollars at the currently published rate of change.
  • CDC NCHS mortality data: Used to weight regional figures by death counts per state.
  • State funeral board reports and locally-filed General Price Lists: Obtained under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) to validate and supplement survey data; funeral homes must provide a written estimate for cash advance items before arrangements are finalized, including charges paid on your behalf.
  • Funeral Consumers Alliance research: Consulted for market context, pricing trends, and consumer-advocacy findings.
  • SSA lump-sum death payment (42 U.S.C. § 402(i)) and VA burial allowance (38 CFR § 3.1600): Referenced when quantifying available offsets for eligible families.

All data is reviewed periodically. Last comprehensive review: 2026-10-22 by Paul Paradis, Founder & Editor. We are transparent about the limitations of aggregated data; your local costs may differ. Always request a General Price List directly from providers for current pricing.

Average funeral cost in 2026: what families actually pay

The average funeral cost in 2026 depends on the type of service, your location, and the choices you make, and many full-service funerals fall in the $7,000 to $12,000 range depending on the choices made. According to the National Funeral Directors Association, the median cost of a funeral with viewing and burial is approximately $8,300. A funeral with cremation averages $6,280. These figures do not include cemetery charges, which can add $2,000 to $8,000 more.

Here is what families typically pay by service type:

Funeral prices rose about 2.7% a year over the past decade (30.5% from August 2016 to August 2026 in the BLS funeral-expenses price index, CUUR0000SEGD02), a little slower than the 3.4% a year for all consumer prices. The primary cost drivers include funeral home service fees, casket selection, cemetery charges (plot, vault, opening/closing), and embalming. Cremation vs burial cost: which is more affordable?

Cremation is significantly less expensive than burial in every U.S. state. Direct cremation, the most affordable option, averages about $1,400 to $3,000 by state (NFDA 2023 national median: $2,645), against $7,000 to $15,000+ for a traditional funeral with burial. Even cremation with a full funeral service ($4,000 – $12,000) costs less than burial when you factor in cemetery charges. For example, readers can compare cremation and burial costs using interactive industry tools for their situation.

How to save money on funeral costs

Families who research their options before contacting a funeral home save an average of 20–30% on total funeral costs. Here are the most effective strategies:

  1. Compare General Price Lists from 2–3 funeral homes: Prices vary by 200–300% between providers in the same area. This is the single most effective way to save. Learn how to compare prices
  2. Consider direct cremation: At roughly $1,400–$3,000 in most states, it is the most affordable disposition option. A memorial service can be held separately at any location. Direct cremation guide
  3. Purchase caskets and urns from independent retailers: Funeral homes mark up caskets by 200–400%. Buying from a third-party retailer saves 50–70%. The FTC Funeral Rule protects this right. Best online casket retailers
  4. Decline unnecessary services: Embalming, premium caskets, and elaborate flowers are optional. Choose only what matters to your family. What funeral homes don't tell you
  5. Explore financial assistance: Social Security death benefits, veteran burial benefits, Medicaid funeral assistance, and crowdfunding can help offset costs. How to pay for a funeral with no money
