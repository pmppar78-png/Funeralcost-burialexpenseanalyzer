Cremation Costs in Jacksonville, Florida (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Florida Cremation Rate: 62%

Jacksonville sits at the low end of Florida metro cremation pricing — roughly 15% below Miami — even though the city has one of the largest funeral-home footprints in the state. Hardage-Giddens operates at least eight locations across Duval County, Quinn-Shalz serves the beaches, and the Jacksonville Memory Gardens complex on Ramona Boulevard has run a combined funeral-home-and-crematory since 1955. At 62% cremation adoption, Northeast Florida outpaces the national average, and the Duval County Medical Examiner handles a significant share of military-related deaths given Naval Station Mayport and NAS Jacksonville.

The Hardage-Giddens footprint

Hardage-Giddens is effectively Jacksonville's default funeral-home chain, with locations including Oaklawn Chapel, Greenlawn Chapel, Riverside Memorial Park, Mandarin Chapel, and the Town & Country Chapel in Orange Park. Most are Dignity Memorial properties under Service Corporation International. Jacksonville Memory Gardens combines a cemetery, funeral home, and crematory at 111 Blanding Boulevard. Neptune Society's Jacksonville location specializes in direct cremation and pre-need packages; Quinn-Shalz Family Funeral Home in Neptune Beach handles beaches-area arrangements. Independent operators like Giddens-Reed Funeral Home and Hall Funeral Home serve historically Black communities on the Eastside and Arlington.

Florida's 48-hour hold and the St. Johns County effect

Florida Statute 497.385 imposes a mandatory 48-hour hold between death and cremation, plus a medical examiner release requirement. In Jacksonville the Duval County Medical Examiner's Office at 2100 Jefferson Street clears most non-suspicious deaths within 24 hours, but cases involving active-duty military, drownings in the St. Johns River, or unattended deaths in rural Nassau County can extend the wait. Families with St. Johns County ties — Ponce Vedra, St. Augustine — often find that moving the death certificate through St. Johns County's smaller ME office runs faster than Duval. Providers cannot proceed with cremation until the ME's release clears and the signed authorization from the legally authorized person is on file.

Scattering at Mayport, the Atlantic, and the St. Johns

Offshore scattering three nautical miles east of Jacksonville Beach, Mayport, or Fernandina Beach is permitted under EPA regulations with subsequent notification to the agency within 30 days. Charter boats out of Mayport and the Beaches typically charge $350 to $1,200 for group scattering services. Scattering in the St. Johns River requires no specific permit but should be done respectfully away from city-use areas. The Timucuan Ecological Preserve, Little Talbot Island State Park, and Fort Clinch State Park all require written permission from the park superintendent for scattering. Jacksonville National Cemetery off Lannie Road provides free inurnment in the columbarium wall for eligible veterans and spouses.

Lowering the bill in Duval County

  • Request the Hardage-Giddens "direct cremation only" package. Across Dignity Memorial locations, the direct-cremation tier is typically 30% to 40% below the "simple cremation with memorial" tier when priced as a standalone service.
  • Neptune Society and Jax-based cremation-only providers quote direct cremation under $1,500 in many cases — meaningfully below the $1,890 metro average for full-service funeral homes.
  • Pre-need buyers are protected by Florida's 70% trust requirement, meaning at least 70% of the pre-paid funds must be placed in a state-regulated trust administered under Chapter 497.
  • Veterans and active-duty military qualify for full-expense burial or cremation through the Jacksonville National Cemetery and VA burial allowance (up to $2,000 for service-connected deaths).

Answers to the Questions We Hear Most

How much does cremation cost in Jacksonville?

Direct cremation in Jacksonville averages $1,890, against a full-service cremation figure of $6,510. That spread is close to Florida's statewide pattern but sits below Miami and Tampa, which reflects Duval County's lower overhead and the competitive presence of Neptune Society, Hardage-Giddens locations, and Jacksonville Memory Gardens. Urns, memorial services, and scattering add $200 to $3,000 depending on choices. Veterans eligible for Jacksonville National Cemetery inurnment avoid interment and marker fees entirely.

What types of cremation are available in Jacksonville?

Jacksonville families can choose from: direct cremation ($1,890) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service — a ceremony held after cremation; and traditional cremation ($6,510) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Jacksonville?

Yes. A standard burial at Evergreen, Oaklawn, or Riverside Memorial Park in Jacksonville runs between $8,000 and $11,000 once plot, vault, opening-closing, and monument are added. Direct cremation at $1,890 undercuts that by roughly $6,300; even full-service cremation at $6,510 remains below the burial total. Arlington National Cemetery and Jacksonville National Cemetery both offer free interment or inurnment for eligible veterans.

Where can I compare cremation prices in Jacksonville?

Start with written General Price Lists from Hardage-Giddens, Quinn-Shalz, Jacksonville Memory Gardens, and Neptune Society — all are required by the FTC Funeral Rule to share prices on request, including by phone. The Florida Department of Financial Services Division of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services publishes licensee and complaint records at myfloridacfo.com. Funeral Consumers Alliance of Northeast Florida also maintains periodic local surveys.

Can I scatter ashes in Jacksonville?

Offshore Atlantic scattering three nautical miles east of Jacksonville Beach, Mayport, or Neptune Beach is permitted under EPA rules with written notice filed within 30 days. St. Johns River scattering has no specific permit requirement but should be done well away from drinking-water intakes and municipal areas. Little Talbot Island State Park and the Timucuan Ecological Preserve both require written permission from park administration. Private property scattering is permitted with the landowner's consent.
