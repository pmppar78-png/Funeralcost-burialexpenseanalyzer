Burial Costs in Raleigh, North Carolina (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: North Carolina

Raleigh's oldest active cemetery is Historic Oakwood on Oakwood Avenue — founded 1869 and still operating as a nonprofit with a published fee schedule and a large Confederate section that doubles as a state historical site. The fact that Wake County's best-known cemetery is a nonprofit has a downstream effect on burial pricing across the metro: it sets a ceiling that the commercial memorial parks in north Raleigh and Apex have to price against. A 2026 traditional burial in Raleigh runs about $8,165 for the funeral service and $3,680 for a plot, with all-in totals between $15,157 and $21,287.

Oakwood, Montlawn, and the Raleigh Cemetery Map

Historic Oakwood Cemetery anchors the metro's nonprofit tier and publishes its fee schedule on request — that sheet is the best anchor for comparing what a fair plot plus opening-and-closing should cost in Wake County. Montlawn Memorial Park (south Raleigh) and Raleigh Memorial Park (north Raleigh) are the two largest commercial memorial parks. Pine Forest Memorial Gardens in Wake Forest and Bethel Cemetery in Apex cover the outlying suburbs. Historic City Cemetery on New Bern Avenue is the city's oldest burial ground (1798) and maintained by the City of Raleigh. Raleigh National Cemetery on Rock Quarry Road — an annex maintained by the VA — currently accepts only cremated remains and second interments, so most Wake County veterans are directed instead to North Carolina State Veterans Cemetery Goldsboro or to the Eastern Carolina State Veterans Cemetery in Spring Lake, either of which provides plot, vault, opening-and-closing, and marker at no cost to eligible veterans and spouses.

North Carolina Funeral Market Rules

The North Carolina Board of Funeral Service licenses and audits funeral homes and cemeteries in the state, and North Carolina has stricter pre-need escrow requirements than most Southern states — pre-need funds must sit in a dedicated trust account with regular audits. Before signing a pre-need contract with any Raleigh funeral home, search the provider on nceasfs.org for disciplinary history. In the Raleigh market specifically, Brown-Wynne Funeral Home (three locations) and Mitchell Funeral Home are among the established independents; Service Corporation International and Carriage Services operate several of the suburban memorial-park-plus-mortuary combinations. Three written General Price Lists almost always surface a meaningful spread on identical service packages.

Where the Raleigh Bill Gets Cut

  • Start with Historic Oakwood's fee schedule: Published pricing from the nonprofit anchor tells you where every other Raleigh cemetery should land.
  • Call congregational and family cemeteries in Wake, Durham, and Johnston counties: Plots in working church cemeteries routinely price $1,000–$2,500 below commercial memorial parks, and many still have available sections.
  • Check state veterans cemetery eligibility: Goldsboro and Spring Lake provide plot, vault, opening-and-closing, and marker at no cost to eligible veterans and spouses — roughly $7,500 off a Raleigh burial.
  • Request three written General Price Lists: Raleigh's funeral-home market is large and competitive; the spread on identical packages can exceed $2,000.
  • Ask about a liner instead of a vault: Many Wake County cemeteries accept a liner, which saves $500–$1,500 relative to a full vault.
  • Buy the casket independently: FTC Funeral Rule bars handling fees. Casket buying guide.
  • Wake County Social Services: Wake County DSS administers a modest burial assistance benefit for eligible low-income families; county parallel programs exist in Durham, Johnston, and Orange. See our payment assistance guide.

Questions From Families

How much does burial cost in Raleigh?

A 2026 traditional burial in Raleigh averages $8,165 for the funeral service plus $3,680 for a plot, with all-in totals between $15,157 and $21,287. Historic Oakwood Cemetery's nonprofit pricing typically sits at the low end of that range; Raleigh Memorial Park and Montlawn Memorial Park sit higher; a church or family cemetery plot in Wake, Durham, Johnston, or Orange County usually sits well below it.

Is a burial vault required in Raleigh?

North Carolina does not require a vault by statute. Most Raleigh-area cemeteries require either a vault or the cheaper grave liner as written policy. Historic Oakwood accepts a liner in lieu of a vault; most of the suburban commercial memorial parks require a full vault. Always ask the specific cemetery for the written interment specification before paying a deposit.

What are the cheapest burial options in Raleigh?

The lowest-cost Raleigh burials usually come from North Carolina State Veterans Cemetery Goldsboro or Spring Lake (eligible veterans receive plot, vault, and marker at no cost), a Wake or Johnston County church cemetery plot, or Historic Oakwood using its published nonprofit fee schedule. Direct burial booked against three written General Price Lists from Brown-Wynne, Mitchell, and one of the combined mortuary-memorial-park providers also consistently surfaces lower pricing.

How do Raleigh burial costs compare to North Carolina?

Raleigh runs roughly 15 percent above the North Carolina state average — $8,165 and $3,680 versus $7,100 and $3,200 — and is roughly level with Charlotte, the state's other high-cost metro. The Raleigh premium reflects Wake County land values and the density of combined mortuary-memorial-park operators built up during the metro's post-2000 growth; Historic Oakwood's nonprofit status provides the main offsetting lever.
