Cremation Trends Around Alaska

Alaska's 72% cremation rate is among the highest in the nation, which means the local market is mature and competitive. Direct cremation providers in and around Anchorage advertise transparent, flat-rate pricing and can often complete the process in under a week. The $2,800 price point in Alaska reflects real market competition, not a rack rate; there is rarely much room to negotiate lower.

How to Save on Cremation in Alaska

Cremation costs in Alaska can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Alaska: Request written General Price Lists.
  • Choose a flat-rate direct cremation provider: Alaska has a mature, competitive direct cremation market. Flat-rate providers in and around Anchorage publish transparent pricing and rarely leave much room to negotiate lower; pick on turnaround and reputation rather than haggling.
  • Look into outdoor scattering options: Private land (with permission), Pacific scattering services, national forests with a free permit, and several Alaska state parks allow ash scattering. DIY scattering with the required permits is effectively free; guided services run $200-$800.

Before You Sign for Cremation in Alaska

When considering cremation in Alaska, keep these important points in mind:

  • Get the Alaska provider's General Price List before you agree to anything; the direct cremation line must state what it includes, and the price should cover transport, the cremation itself and a basic container for the ashes.
  • Even the cheapest Alaska direct cremation lets you skip the casket: the provider must accept an unfinished wood box or other alternative container (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(b)). Paperwork sets the earliest date. Under AS 18.50.230, the death certificate has to be filed with the local registrar within three days and before final disposition, and the physician, advanced practice registered nurse or physician assistant must sign the medical certification within 24 hours, unless a medical examiner inquiry is required.
  • Before signing, ask the Alaska provider when the ashes will be ready, how they are returned, and whether a certified copy of the death certificate is in the price.
  • You can buy the urn anywhere and the Alaska provider cannot charge a handling fee; compare options in our urn buying guide.
Urns can be bought from any seller for less, and under the FTC Funeral Rule an Alaska funeral home cannot refuse one bought elsewhere.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Alaska?

Cremation costs in Alaska range from $2,800 for direct cremation to $7,200 for a full funeral service followed by cremation. On top of the $2,800 Alaska direct cremation price, families may pay for an urn ($50-$3,000), a memorial service ($500-$2,000), and scattering or inurnment fees ($200-$600). Before those extras, the Alaska base ranges from $2,800 for direct cremation to $7,200 with a service.

Which cremation options exist in Alaska?

Alaska providers typically offer three tiers. Direct cremation at $2,800 is the simplest Alaska tier: no service or viewing, the body cremated and the ashes returned. Traditional cremation at $7,200 adds a full funeral home service before the cremation, $4,400 more than the direct tier in Alaska.

Does Alaska have specific rules for cremation?

The FTC Funeral Rule (federal) separately bars any Alaska provider from requiring a casket for cremation; a basic cremation container suffices.

Can I scatter ashes in Alaska?

Alaska has no statute on where ashes may go, so the landowner or managing agency sets the terms. Alaska State Parks require a special park use permit under 11 AAC 18.010 for gatherings of more than 20 people or for uses that conflict with normal park activity, so a large memorial at a state park needs one; ask the Division of Parks and Outdoor Recreation about a small family scattering.

Denali, Kenai Fjords, Glacier Bay and the other National Park Service units follow 36 CFR 2.62: a permit or a designated area. If you scatter from a boat in open ocean, federal rules put the spot at least three nautical miles from shore and require a report to EPA Region 10 within 30 days.

Why do so many Alaska families choose cremation?

Cost is the first reason: direct cremation in Alaska starts around $2,800, against about $9,800 for a traditional funeral before any cemetery charges. Alaska's rate on this page, 72%, is above the 63.4% national rate NFDA projected for 2025, and NFDA's reports name cost, environmental concerns and lower religious affiliation among the national reasons.

Two Alaska facts fit that pattern. Only 46.3% of U.S.-born Alaskans live in the state where they were born, against 66.4% nationally (Census Bureau, 2023 American Community Survey), so many families have no family plot nearby, and ashes are easier to carry home than a body. About a third of Alaska adults (33%) are religiously unaffiliated, compared with 29% of all U.S. adults (Pew Research Center, 2023-24 Religious Landscape Study).

How quickly can cremation be completed in Alaska?

From start to ashes, most cremations in Alaska take 4 to 10 days. Direct cremation at $2,800 moves fastest: with no viewing or service to schedule, the only wait is the Alaska cremation permit (Alaska sets no fixed waiting period; AS 13.75.020).

Can I hold a funeral service before cremation in Alaska?

Yes. Expect about $7,200 for a service with the body present followed by cremation, within a typical Alaska range of $5,760 to $9,360. Direct cremation, at about $2,800, costs $4,400 less because it skips the visitation, the ceremony and the rented or purchased casket.

If the death happened off the road system, ask the funeral home to list air or long-distance transport as its own line, since the FTC Funeral Rule's itemized price list lets you see that cost before agreeing to a service. The person with the right to control disposition signs the authorization under AS 13.75.020.

When Alaska needs a burial-transit permit

Alaska's burial-transit permit covers far more than a final disposition. The state requires one before a cremation or burial, and also to move a body within Alaska, to ship it out of state, to keep it more than 72 hours after death, to send it by common carrier, or to hold a public funeral. The Department of Health says the permit also helps make sure the death was natural and that nothing calls for notifying the State Medical Examiner (Alaska Department of Health; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Not needed: no permit is required to take a body to the State Medical Examiner, or when a permit from another state or Canada comes with the body.
  • Medical examiner cases: sudden, unexpected, suspicious or unattended deaths go to the State Medical Examiner first, and cremation waits until the office releases the body.

Cremation rules and help in Alaska

To check that a cremation provider in Alaska is licensed, or to report a problem, contact the Alaska Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing (mortuary science program) at 907-465-2250.

Alaska is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring a funeral director, but no Alaska crematory will proceed without the death certificate, the cremation permit and the next of kin's signed authorization.

Timing and permits: Alaska sets no waiting period before cremation. The person with the right to control disposition must authorize it, and a burial-transit permit is required (AS 13.75.020).

An Alaska cremation for a person with no means can be paid through General Relief Assistance: the funeral home's basic services are capped at $1,250, and the crematory charge is one of the additional necessary costs the Division of Public Assistance may pay (7 AAC 47.130). (Source: 7 AAC 47.130.) Social Security's $255 lump sum is paid only to an eligible Alaska spouse or child, and only if claimed within two years of the death.

Choosing cremation does not give up an Alaska veteran's cemetery benefit; urns can be interred in national cemeteries free of charge. Options in Alaska include Fort Richardson National Cemetery (907-384-7075), Sitka National Cemetery (907-384-7075), and Metlakatla Veterans Memorial Cemetery (tribal, 907-886-1121). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Alaska

  1. Get written cremation prices from at least two Alaska providers and compare what each direct cremation price includes.
  2. Confirm who signs the Alaska cremation permit and when, so the memorial date is realistic.
  3. Ask the Alaska funeral home to request General Relief pre-authorization (form GEN-61) before the cremation is scheduled.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 907-465-2250.
