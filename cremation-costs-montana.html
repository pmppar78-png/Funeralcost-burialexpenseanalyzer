Cremation Costs in Montana (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 What changed: Who signs off on a Montana cremation (new section) Cremation Rate: 72%

About 72% of Montana deaths now end in cremation, and the price depends mainly on how much happens before it. A direct cremation starts near $1,600, while cremation after a full funeral service averages about $5,600, so the choice of service is worth $4,000. Below is what each option includes, what Montana law requires first, and how distance and scattering on public land affect the plans.

The Main Kinds of Cremation in Montana

Direct Cremation

$1,600
  • The lowest-cost choice on a Montana price list
  • Nothing is held before the cremation
  • The provider takes the body straight to the crematory
  • The family receives the ashes afterward
  • A gathering can still be planned for a later date

Cremation with Service

$5,600
  • A conventional funeral is held first
  • Visitation with the body can be arranged
  • The service can take place at a church or the funeral home
  • A plain urn is normally included
  • Closest to what people expect of a traditional funeral

Scattering Ashes on Montana's Public Land

Scattering is legal in most cases on Montana's national forest and BLM land, with a free permit. That, along with a 72% cremation rate, is why many Montana families choose cremation with an outdoor memorial in mind. A scattering service in Montana costs $200 to $800, while a family that scatters the ashes itself under a permit pays nothing.

Before You Sign for Cremation in Montana

Settle these questions with a Montana provider before you sign:

  • Montana's priority list in MCA 37-19-904 starts with the person a service member named on Defense Department Form 93, then anyone the decedent designated in a notarized affidavit, then the spouse. After that the decision passes to a majority of the surviving children, then the parents, then a majority of the siblings; fewer than half of the children may act only after reasonable efforts to notify the rest and with no known opposition from a majority.
  • No casket is needed. The FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR 453.3(b)) requires a Montana provider that sells direct cremation to accept an unfinished wood box or other alternative container instead.
  • Because a rural Montana crematory may be far away, ask how long the ashes take to come back once the permit is issued and whether the price includes bringing them to you or only pickup.
Montana families can skip the crematory's urns. The FTC Funeral Rule lets you supply your own container for the ashes, and a home that refuses it can be reported to the MT Board of Funeral Service.

Keeping a Cremation in Montana Affordable

Two things matter most for the price of a Montana cremation:

  • Get three written price lists. Ask at least three Montana providers for their General Price Lists; the FTC Funeral Rule requires a funeral home to give you one, and the direct cremation line shows what each charges for the same service.
  • Ask about mileage. In rural Montana the nearest crematory can be more than 100 miles from the funeral home. Some providers build that trip into the $1,600 base price and others bill mileage on top, so ask which applies.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a cremation cost in Montana?

There are two price levels in Montana. Direct cremation, covering transport, the cremation and return of the ashes, starts near $1,600; cremation with a full funeral ceremony first averages about $5,600.

What cremation options do Montana providers offer?

Montana providers generally offer three options: (1) direct cremation at about $1,600, with no service; (2) cremation followed by a memorial on a date and at a place the family picks, which costs somewhere in between; and (3) a full funeral with viewing before cremation, at about $5,600.

What does Montana law require before a cremation?

Montana requires 24 hours to pass after death before a cremation. On top of that wait, the crematory has to have the death certificate and the cremation permit in hand, along with an authorization signed by the next of kin or the agent the person named. The rules section lower on this page gives the statute, says whether a funeral director must take part, and lists the licensing office's phone number.

Where can ashes be scattered in Montana?

It depends on where. On national forest and BLM land a free permit usually covers it; private land needs the owner's permission. Many Montana families instead place the urn in a columbarium niche, bury it, or keep some ashes in memorial jewelry.

Why do so many Montana families choose cremation?

At 72%, Montana cremates a larger share of its dead than most states. Cost is a big part of it: our estimate puts direct cremation in Montana about $5,500 below a traditional funeral.

How long does a Montana cremation take?

The cremation itself lasts 2 to 3 hours. A direct cremation ($1,600) finishes soonest because nothing is scheduled beforehand, but Montana law still requires 24 hours after death before it can take place (MCA 37-19-705).

Can a Montana family hold a funeral before the cremation?

Yes. Many Montana providers will rent a casket so the family can hold a viewing or a funeral first. Schedule it with the law in mind: cremation cannot take place within 24 hours of death, and a signed cremation authorization is required, although Montana law does not require a funeral director (MCA 37-19-705).

Who signs off on a Montana cremation

Montana treats a planned cremation as a reason for the coroner to look at the death. When cremation is the only reason for that inquiry, the county coroner may approve it by phone, but the approval has to be put in writing within 24 hours (MCA 50-15-405; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Four copies: the written authorization is made in quadruplicate on a state form; the person handling the disposition, the coroner and the local registrar each keep one, and the fourth can travel with the body.
  • No state fee: Montana law does not set a statewide charge for this sign-off, so ask the funeral home whether the coroner in your county bills for it.

Montana cremation oversight and help with costs

The Montana Department of Labor and Industry (406-841-2359) oversees crematories and the funeral homes that sell cremation, and it is the place to check a license or make a complaint.

Montana does not appear on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict family-directed care. Once the cremation permit is issued, a Montana family may work with a crematory directly. No cremation can happen within 24 hours of death, and a signed cremation authorization is required, but the law does not require a funeral director (MCA 37-19-705).

When the family cannot pay

A Montana county can help pay for an indigent cremation through the state's indigent-funeral account, even if the county runs no general indigent program (Source: Mont. Code Ann. §§ 53-3-116, 7-4-2924). The $255 Social Security death payment, which only a qualifying spouse or child receives, covers part of the $1,600 direct cremation estimate on this page.

An eligible veteran's cremated remains can go into a VA national cemetery columbarium or an in-ground site with no charge for the space. In Montana the options include Yellowstone National Cemetery (406-647-2746), the tribal Apsaalooke Veterans Cemetery (406-620-3504) and the Eastern Montana State Veterans Cemetery in Miles City (406-218-2945). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps in Montana

  1. Ask two or more Montana providers for written direct cremation prices and check what each price covers, mileage included.
  2. Build in at least 24 hours after the death, plus time for the coroner's sign-off and the permit, when setting a date.
  3. If the county may pay, ask about its negotiated cremation rate under § 53-3-116.
  4. Keep the Department of Labor and Industry's number, 406-841-2359, handy before paying any provider.
