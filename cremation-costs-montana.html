Cremation Costs in Montana (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Cremation Rate: 72%

Across Montana's vast open landscapes, cremation has become the clear preference for families planning end-of-life arrangements. At 72%, Montana's cremation rate ranks well above the national average, driven in part by the state's rural geography, practical Western mindset, and the desire many Montanans express to have their remains returned to the land they loved. However, limited crematory availability outside of Billings, Missoula, and Great Falls means families in remote areas often face transportation surcharges that can add several hundred dollars to the total cost.

What Drives Cremation Costs in Montana

Several factors unique to Montana shape what families pay for cremation:

  • Geography and distance: Montana is the fourth-largest state by area, but only about one million people live here. Crematories are concentrated in a handful of cities, meaning remains often travel long distances. Transfer fees from remote locations in eastern Montana can exceed $500.
  • Limited crematory competition: With fewer standalone crematories than more populated states, pricing competition is limited in many regions. Most crematories are operated by funeral homes rather than independent facilities.
  • Seasonal considerations: Montana winters can complicate transportation and scheduling. Some families opt for cremation specifically because frozen ground makes winter burials extremely difficult and expensive in parts of the state.
  • Native American community needs: Montana is home to seven reservations, including the Crow, Blackfeet, and Northern Cheyenne. While most tribal traditions favor ground burial, some Native American families are now choosing cremation, and culturally sensitive providers in the state offer services that respect tribal customs and preferences.
  • 24-hour waiting period: Montana law requires a mandatory 24-hour waiting period after death before cremation can proceed. This is shorter than many states and helps keep costs slightly lower by reducing required refrigeration time.

Consumer Rights for Cremation in Montana

Montana families choosing cremation are protected by both federal and state regulations:

  • FTC Funeral Rule applies fully: All Montana cremation providers must give you a General Price List (GPL) before discussing arrangements, and you have the right to choose only the services you want.
  • No casket requirement: You are not required to purchase a casket for cremation. An alternative container made of unfinished wood, pressed wood, or cardboard is sufficient, and providers cannot refuse to perform cremation because you chose a less expensive container.
  • Authorization requirements: Montana requires written authorization from the legal next of kin before cremation can proceed. If the deceased left pre-arranged cremation instructions, those wishes carry significant weight under Montana law.
  • Right to provide your own urn: Cremation providers cannot charge a handling fee if you bring an urn purchased elsewhere. Many Montana families choose handcrafted urns from local artisans or simple containers that reflect the state's outdoor heritage.
  • Preneed protections: If you prepay for cremation in Montana, the funds must be placed in trust, protecting your money if the provider goes out of business.

Scattering and Memorial Options in Montana

Montana is one of the most permissive states in the country for scattering cremated remains, which is a major reason many families here choose cremation:

  • Public lands: Scattering is generally permitted on Bureau of Land Management (BLM) land and national forest land throughout Montana, which covers millions of acres. No permit is typically required, though notifying the local ranger district is considered courteous.
  • National parks: Glacier National Park and Yellowstone (Montana portion) allow scattering with a free permit from the superintendent's office. Scattering must occur away from trails, waterways, and developed areas.
  • Rivers and lakes: Scattering in Montana's rivers and lakes is generally permitted. Many families choose the Missouri River, Flathead Lake, or mountain streams. No state permit is required, but remains should be scattered in moving water rather than still pools.
  • Private land: Scattering on private property requires written permission from the landowner. Many Montana ranching families scatter on their own land as a deeply personal memorial.
  • Mountain memorials: Summit scattering on Montana peaks is popular, particularly in the Absaroka-Beartooth Wilderness and the Bob Marshall Wilderness Complex. Families should be mindful of Leave No Trace principles.
Cost-Saving Tip: Montana's vast public lands mean you can scatter remains at no cost in many beautiful locations. If a funeral home offers an expensive scattering service, ask whether you can handle scattering on your own — most Montana families do.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Montana?

Direct cremation in Montana averages $1,600, with prices ranging from $1,120 to $2,240 depending on your location and provider. Cremation with a full funeral service averages $5,600. Families in remote areas should budget an additional $200 to $500 for transportation to the nearest crematory, as crematories are concentrated in Billings, Missoula, Great Falls, and a few other cities.

What types of cremation are available in Montana?

Montana providers offer direct cremation (no service beforehand, the most affordable option), cremation with a memorial service (service held after cremation with the urn present), and traditional cremation (a full funeral with viewing before cremation). Direct cremation is by far the most popular choice in Montana, particularly among families in rural areas who plan separate memorial gatherings later.

What are Montana's cremation regulations?

Montana requires a 24-hour waiting period after death before cremation can occur. Written authorization from the legal next of kin is required, along with a cremation permit. Pacemakers and other battery-powered implants must be removed before cremation. Montana does not require embalming for cremation, and no casket is required — an alternative container is legally sufficient.

Can I scatter ashes in Montana?

Yes, Montana is one of the most permissive states for scattering. You may scatter on BLM land, national forest land, and most public land without a permit. Glacier and Yellowstone national parks allow scattering with a free permit. You can scatter on private property with the landowner's written permission. Many families scatter along Montana's rivers, on mountain summits, or on family ranch land.

Are there cremation considerations for Native American families in Montana?

Montana is home to seven tribal reservations, and cremation customs vary significantly by tribe. Many traditional beliefs favor ground burial, but an increasing number of Native American families are choosing cremation. Some Montana funeral homes offer culturally sensitive cremation services that incorporate tribal customs. If the deceased was an enrolled tribal member, the tribe's social services office may offer assistance with cremation costs.

Why is cremation so popular in Montana?

Montana's 72% cremation rate is driven by several factors: the practical, self-reliant mindset common in the rural West; the high cost of winter burials when the ground is frozen; the ability to scatter remains across Montana's beloved public lands; lower upfront costs compared to traditional burial; and a less formal approach to memorial customs in many Montana communities.
