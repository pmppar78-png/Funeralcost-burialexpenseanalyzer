Cremation Costs in Montana (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 Cremation Rate: 72%

Cremation pricing in Montana is simpler than traditional burial but can still vary by thousands of dollars between providers. In Montana, direct cremation starts around $1,600 while cremation with a full service averages $5,600, a gap of $4,000. This guide walks through what's included at each price point and how Montana's 72% cremation rate shapes local options.

Before You Sign for Cremation in Montana

When considering cremation in Montana, keep these important points in mind:

  • Montana's priority list in MCA 37-19-904 starts with the person a service member named on Defense Department Form 93, then anyone the decedent designated in a notarized affidavit, then the spouse. After that the decision passes to a majority of the surviving children, then the parents, then a majority of the siblings; fewer than half of the children may act only after reasonable efforts to notify the rest and with no known opposition from a majority.
  • A Montana provider offering direct cremation must let you use an unfinished wood box or other alternative container instead of a casket (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(b)).
  • Ask how long the Montana crematory usually takes to return the ashes once the permit is issued, and whether the quoted price includes delivery or only pickup.
In Montana you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Montana bring a container, and the MT Board of Funeral Service is who you call if a home in Montana refuses it.

Keeping a Cremation in Montana Affordable

Cremation costs in Montana can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Montana: Request written General Price Lists.
  • Account for transport distance: In rural Montana, the nearest crematory may be 100+ miles from the funeral home. Some Montana providers absorb this in the $1,600 base price; others charge mileage.

Regional Context for Cremation in Montana

Scattering ashes on public land in Montana, especially in national forests and BLM land, is legal in most cases with a free permit. Combined with the 72% state cremation rate, this makes cremation a natural choice for many Montana families who want an outdoor memorial. Dedicated scattering services run $200-$800 in Montana; a DIY scatter with a permit is free.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical price of cremation in Montana?

Montana cremation pricing lives in two tiers: direct cremation (transport, cremation, and return of ashes) starting around $1,600, and full-service cremation with a ceremony landing near $5,600.

What kinds of cremation services can I choose in Montana?

Three paths are standard in Montana: (1) direct cremation, $1,600, just the cremation itself, no service; (2) cremation with memorial, cremation first, ceremony later at a location of your choice, priced in between; (3) cremation with full funeral, $5,600, ceremony and viewing before cremation.

What are Montana's cremation regulations?

In short, Montana sets a 24-hour minimum before cremation, and the crematory also needs the death certificate, a cremation permit and a signed authorization from the next of kin or designated agent. The statute behind the Montana timing rule, whether a funeral director must be involved, and the licensing board's phone number are listed in the cremation rules section further down this page.

Where are ashes allowed to be scattered in Montana?

Montana scattering rules depend on the specific location. Families in Montana also often opt for columbarium niches, burial of the urn, or keepsake jewelry in place of scattering.

What is driving Montana's cremation rate?

Montana's 72% cremation rate reflects one of the highest cremation rates in the nation. The cost delta alone explains a lot: direct cremation costs an estimated $5,500 less than a traditional funeral in Montana.

How long does cremation take in Montana?

In Montana, the cremation process itself takes 2 to 3 hours. Direct cremation ($1,600) has the shortest timeline because no viewing or ceremony is involved, though Montana law still requires 24 hours to pass after death (MCA 37-19-705).

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Montana?

Yes. A Montana family can hold a viewing or service before cremation, and many providers rent a casket for it. Plan the date around the law: Montana bars cremation within 24 hours of death and requires a signed cremation authorization, though no funeral director is required by law (MCA 37-19-705).

Cremation rules and help in Montana

Crematories and the funeral homes that sell cremation in Montana answer to the Montana Department of Labor and Industry (406-841-2359).

Because Montana is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict family-directed care, the family may deal with a Montana crematory directly once the cremation permit has been issued.

Before a cremation can happen: Montana bars cremation within 24 hours of death and requires a signed cremation authorization, though no funeral director is required by law (MCA 37-19-705).

Montana counties can help pay for an indigent cremation through the state indigent-funeral account even if they run no general indigent program (Mont. Code Ann. §§ 53-3-116, 7-4-2924). (Source: Mont. Code Ann. §§ 53-3-116, 7-4-2924.) The $255 Social Security death payment to a qualifying Montana spouse or child would cover only part of the $1,600 direct cremation estimate above.

Cremated remains of an eligible Montana veteran can be placed in a VA national cemetery columbarium or in-ground site at no charge for the space. Options in Montana include Yellowstone National Cemetery (406-647-2746), Apsaalooke Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 406-620-3504), and Eastern Montana State Veterans Cemetery - Miles City (406-218-2945). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Montana

  1. Get written cremation prices from at least two Montana providers and compare what each direct cremation price includes.
  2. Allow at least 24 hours after death in Montana, plus time for the permit, before the cremation date.
  3. Ask the Montana county about its negotiated cremation rate under § 53-3-116.
  4. Note the board's number, 406-841-2359, before you pay a cremation provider.
