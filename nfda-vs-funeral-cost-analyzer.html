    What NFDA Measures

    NFDA’s consumer cost figures come from its Member General Price List Study. The study reports national medians, and medians for the nine U.S. census regions, of items that appear on a funeral home’s General Price List. A median is the midpoint of those surveyed prices: half the responding homes listed a lower figure, half a higher one. It is not a typical bill in any one city, and it is not a quote.

    Both package medians end at the funeral home’s bill. Those package figures leave out the cemetery contract itself, any monument, and pass-through items such as flowers or an obituary. That limit is why a family planning a burial still has to price the cemetery contract separately, even after reading the official $8,300 median.

    The latest official cost study is the 2023 Member General Price List Study, released on December 8, 2023, in a press release and a public PDF. The same study’s method section says the questionnaire was mailed on July 7, 2023, to 5,219 NFDA-member funeral homeowners, with an online option; 809 completed responses were received (474 by mail, 335 online), described as reflecting membership at 95% confidence, plus or minus 3.4%.

    NFDA also publishes cremation and burial rates, and some consumer-preference headlines, in later Cremation and Burial Reports. Those rate figures are not a new price survey. The full 2025 Cremation and Burial Report and the full 2023 General Price List Report are sold to nonmembers; this page uses only figures that appear in the public statistics page, the press releases, and the two public PDFs named below.

    The 2023 National Medians, Item by Item

    NFDA’s published national midpoints from that 2023 study are $8,300 when the funeral includes a viewing and burial, and $6,280 when it includes a viewing and cremation. Including a vault brings the published burial total to $9,995. As of November 19, 2026, no later official cost medians had been released.

    The same study reported that the 2021 median for a funeral with viewing and burial was $7,848, and that the 2023 figure of $8,300 was 5.8% higher. The 2021 cremation-with-viewing median was $5,810, and the 2023 figure of $6,280 was 8.1% higher. Inflation over those two years was reported at 13.6%. The $7,848 figure is the 2021 burial median, not the current official number.

    What the 2025 Public Headlines Cover — and What They Do Not

    The 2025 Cremation and Burial Report has public headlines in an 18 September 2025 press release and on NFDA’s statistics page, which notes that it was last updated September 29, 2025. Those headlines are about rates and preferences, not a new General Price List study.

    The public 2025 figures we cite are: a projected 2025 U.S. cremation rate of 63.4% and a projected burial rate of 31.6%; by 2045, cremation at 82.3% and burial expected to decline to 13.0%; and deaths projected to climb 26% over the next two decades, reaching about 3.91 million annually by 2045. Among people who would prefer cremation for themselves, the statistics page, attributing the 2025 report, lists 37.8% who would bury or inter in a cemetery, 37.1% who would keep an urn at home, 33.5% who would scatter in a sentimental place, 10.5% who would split among relatives, 10.0% who would keep the remains in a place of worship, and 9.6% who have not decided. People can prefer more than one option, so those shares add to more than 100%.

    A separate 2025 Consumer Awareness and Preferences Report, as quoted on the same statistics page, says 61.4% would be interested in exploring “green” funeral options, up from 55.7% in 2021. That 61.4% figure is not the older 67.7% green-interest number from the 2024 consumer study, and this page does not mix the two.

    No 2025 package-price median is used here, because none was found in those public sources. Official cremation rates for individual states are published by the Cremation Association of North America; we do not invent a 50-state rate table from NFDA’s national headlines.

    What Funeral Cost Analyzer Adds

    NFDA is the source we cite for official national and regional funeral-home medians, and for the public 2025 rate headlines. What this site adds is the consumer layer those public NFDA pages do not publish: state-by-state cost estimates, a few city pages, situation guides, and calculators. Our state and metro prices are estimates, anchored to the NFDA national study and adjusted for regional costs. They are not quotes. The price that matters locally is the one on that funeral home’s current General Price List.

    The calculators on this site are our own tools. They are not NFDA products, and they do not replace a funeral home’s General Price List.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the latest official NFDA funeral cost median?

    The latest official national cost medians are from the 2023 Member General Price List Study, released on December 8, 2023: $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, and $6,280 for a funeral with viewing and cremation. The same study lists a $9,995 total when a burial vault is included. No later public cost median had been published as of November 19, 2026.

    Does the $8,300 median include a cemetery plot?

    No. The 2023 study covers items on a funeral home’s General Price List. Plot or crypt charges, a monument or marker, and cash advances such as flowers or an obituary sit outside the $8,300 package. Our burial-plot guide and state burial totals therefore list those cemetery lines on their own.

    Are Funeral Cost Analyzer’s state figures official NFDA numbers?

    No. NFDA publishes national and nine-region medians. Our state, county and city cost figures are our own estimates, anchored to those official national benchmarks and adjusted for regional costs and, for 2026, for inflation using the BLS funeral-expense CPI, as our methodology page explains. They are not an NFDA state survey. For a local price, ask the funeral home for its current General Price List.

    Is there a 2025 NFDA funeral cost median?

    Not in the public materials we can cite. The 2024 Cremation and Burial Report noted that the next GPL Study would be conducted in 2025, but no 2025 cost medians appeared on the public statistics page or in a public press release as of November 19, 2026. The 2025 Cremation and Burial Report headlines that are public are rates and preferences, not new package prices. The full 2025 report is sold to nonmembers; this page does not use paywalled figures.

    Is Funeral Cost Analyzer affiliated with NFDA?

    No. This site has no affiliation with the National Funeral Directors Association and does not claim its endorsement. NFDA remains the standard public source for national funeral-home medians, and the official pages are linked so you can read them yourself.

    Where do the $2,645 and $2,750 direct-cremation figures come from?

    They are 2023 General Price List Study medians. They were not in the December 8, 2023 press-release item tables; they are printed in the public 2024 Cremation and Burial Report, in a table sourced to the 2023 GPL Study: $2,645 when the family supplies the container and $2,750 when the funeral home does. They are 2023 figures reprinted in 2024, not a separate 2024 price survey.
