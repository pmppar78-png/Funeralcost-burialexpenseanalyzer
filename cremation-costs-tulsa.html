Cremation Costs in Tulsa, Oklahoma (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Oklahoma Cremation Rate: 45%

Tulsa has three long-standing funeral operators handling most local cremations — Moore's Funeral Home, Floral Haven on the city's south side, and Memorial Park Cemetery and Funeral Home. Direct cremation averages $1,470 and full-service cremation $5,250, putting the metro just above the Oklahoma state average. The Oklahoma Funeral Board enforces the state's pre-need rules, which include a strict 100% trust requirement — among the most protective in the country.

National context: the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA 2024 Annual Report put the U.S. cremation rate near 60.5 percent, and Tulsa providers operate under the oversight of the Oklahoma Funeral Board. Families ordering death certificates typically deal with the Tulsa Health Department Vital Records office.

Oklahoma's 100% Pre-Need Trust Requirement

Oklahoma Statutes Title 36 Section 6121 requires 100% of pre-need funeral funds to be deposited into a trust or insurance-backed policy — one of the strongest consumer protections in the country. Moore's, Floral Haven, and Memorial Park all sell pre-need cremation plans under this framework. When prepaying, the law entitles the family to a trust receipt identifying the depository institution and the funds on account. If the funeral home goes out of business, the trust funds move with the contract rather than being lost.

Tulsa's Main Cremation Providers

  • Moore's Funeral Home — Multiple Tulsa locations. On-site crematory on Eastern Avenue. Family-run for four generations.
  • Floral Haven — Broken Arrow. Large cemetery combined with funeral home; in-house crematory and columbarium gardens.
  • Memorial Park Cemetery and Funeral Home — Memorial Park on Peoria. On-site crematory serving Tulsa's south side.
  • Ninde Funeral Directors — Tulsa's Brookside and midtown. Independent operator, competitive direct cremation pricing.
Eastern Oklahoma detail: The Osage, Cherokee, and Muscogee Nations are each sovereign and some members prefer tribal cemetery interment. Several Tulsa funeral homes have long-standing relationships with tribal burial sites and can coordinate between a tribal interment and a funeral-home cremation or service.

Oklahoma Funeral Board Licensing

The Oklahoma Funeral Board licenses every funeral establishment, director, and crematory operator. There's no statutory waiting period before cremation, but medical examiner or physician sign-off on the death certificate is required, and the legal next of kin must sign the cremation authorization. Most Tulsa cremations complete within 5 to 8 days from death. The Board publishes a searchable license roster for Oklahoma consumers.

Scattering in Green Country

Northeastern Oklahoma — "Green Country" — has ample public-land and private-land options. Ouachita National Forest to the southeast permits ash dispersal with a free USFS permit. Oklahoma State Parks including Osage Hills, Keystone, and Robbers Cave allow scattering with ranger approval. Lake Keystone, Grand Lake, and the Arkansas River permit scattering outside recreation zones. Private ranchland — common across the Osage — is the most popular option and needs only the landowner's written permission.

Assistance for Indigent Tulsa Families

Oklahoma Department of Human Services (DHS) administers a modest burial/cremation benefit for indigent decedents; amounts vary but typically run $500 to $800. The funeral home files on the family's behalf. Tulsa County operates a limited coroner-funded cremation for unclaimed remains. Veterans' families should inquire with Fort Gibson National Cemetery about no-cost inurnment for eligible service members and file VA Form 21P-530 for the burial allowance.

Cost-Saving Tip: You do not need to purchase an urn from the cremation provider. The FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to use any urn or container, including ones purchased independently. See our urn buying guide.

What Families Want to Know

How much does cremation cost in Tulsa?

Ninde Funeral Directors and Moore's Southlawn advertise direct cremation packages starting around $1,470 in the Tulsa metro. A traditional funeral service with cremation to follow runs closer to $5,250 at Floral Haven in Broken Arrow or Memorial Park on Peoria. Tulsa pricing tracks the Oklahoma state average of roughly $1,400 for direct cremation. Expect urns from $50 to $3,000, memorial reception fees of $500 to $2,000, and scattering coordination in the $200 to $600 range.

What types of cremation are available in Tulsa?

Tulsa providers offer three main paths. Direct cremation at about $1,470 skips viewing and ceremony and returns cremated remains within 7 to 10 days. Cremation with memorial service — common at Moore's Southlawn and Ninde — holds the gathering after the cremation has taken place. A traditional funeral service at roughly $5,250 puts the casketed viewing and service first, with cremation following. Floral Haven, Memorial Park, Moore's, and Ninde all handle each variant.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Tulsa?

Yes. A $1,470 direct cremation at a Tulsa provider such as Ninde runs roughly $4,935 below a traditional Oklahoma funeral with casketed burial. Even the $5,250 full-service cremation package at Floral Haven or Memorial Park undercuts traditional burial once you add a Rose Hill or Memorial Park cemetery plot ($2,000 to $4,000), a burial vault ($1,400-plus required by most Tulsa cemeteries), and a granite marker.

Can I scatter ashes in Tulsa?

Oklahoma does not restrict ash scattering beyond federal rules. Around Tulsa, Ouachita National Forest permits scattering with a free USFS authorization, Oklahoma State Parks including Keystone and Osage Hills allow it with ranger approval, and scattering on private ranchland — widespread in the Osage — requires only the landowner's written consent. Lake Keystone, Grand Lake, and the Arkansas River are legal for scattering outside posted recreation zones. Offshore scattering must be 3 nautical miles out per EPA, which is irrelevant inland but matters for Gulf trips.
