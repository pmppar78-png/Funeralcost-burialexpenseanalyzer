Burial Costs in Alabama (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 24, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Alabama burial write-up updated around the Board of Funeral Service's cemetery role, county burial duty and veterans cemeteries, and unsourced 30-50% and fee claims removed. Region: South

An Alabama burial costs $14,900 to $19,400 on our estimates once the cemetery's charges are in, starting from a $6,400 funeral service and a $3,200 plot. Since October 2023 one state board, the Alabama Board of Funeral Service, has overseen cemeteries as well as funeral homes. This page covers the costs, a county's duty to pay when there is no estate, the right to visit a family grave on someone else's land, and Alabama's veterans cemeteries.

Paying Less for an Alabama Burial

Ways to bring an Alabama bill down:

  • Get the cemetery's prices on paper. Most cemeteries fall outside the FTC Funeral Rule, so ask each Alabama cemetery for a written sheet; since 2023 the Board of Funeral Service takes complaints about cemeteries too.
  • Ask about church and family cemeteries. Many rural Alabama churches keep their own cemeteries, and the landowner must allow reasonable access to an existing family graveyard on private land (Ala. Code § 35-1-4).
  • Check veterans cemeteries first. Alabama National Cemetery, Fort Mitchell National Cemetery and the state cemetery at Spanish Fort bury eligible veterans with no charge for the plot or marker.

Church, Family and Veterans Cemeteries in Alabama

Opening and closing a grave is often the largest cemetery charge after the plot; we estimate $1,600 in Alabama, but each cemetery sets its own fee, so ask for it in writing. Church cemeteries, family plots and the veterans cemeteries are worth checking before you accept a private cemetery's package. Some Alabama cemeteries also sell mausoleum crypts for above-ground entombment, priced apart from the plot in the table.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a burial in Alabama?

We put Alabama at $14,900 to $19,400: a $6,400 funeral service, a $3,200 plot, a $1,920 casket, a $1,280 vault or liner, $1,600 for opening and closing, and a $500 to $5,000 headstone.

Does Alabama require a vault?

Not because of Alabama law. Whether you need one depends on the cemetery's rules. This page's cost table puts a vault or liner at about $1,280 for an Alabama burial, out of an $800 to $10,000 range, and a grave liner, where the cemetery accepts it, usually costs less than a vault.

Since October 1, 2023, the Alabama Board of Funeral Service has regulated cemeteries as well as funeral homes (Ala. Code §§ 34-13-265 to -277). If an Alabama cemetery's container rule or price is not what it told you in writing, the Board takes complaints at 334-242-4049.

What is the cheapest Alabama burial?

Direct (immediate) burial skips embalming, viewing and a ceremony at the funeral home, so it is usually the lowest-cost burial on an Alabama price list. You may supply your own casket in Alabama; the FTC Funeral Rule forbids a handling fee for it. Eligible veterans in Alabama can be buried in a VA national cemetery at no cost for the grave, opening and closing, liner and marker.

Why do Alabama cemetery prices vary?

Each Alabama cemetery sets its own prices for the plot, opening and closing, and any required vault or liner. The FTC Funeral Rule covers funeral homes, not most cemeteries, so an Alabama cemetery may not be required to give an itemized price list; ask for one anyway. VA national cemeteries in Alabama include Alabama National Cemetery and Fort Mitchell National Cemetery.

What is the full cost of an Alabama burial?

Add service $6,400, plot $3,200, casket $1,920, vault $1,280 and opening $1,600, then a headstone at $500 to $5,000: $14,900 to $19,400 before cash advance items.

Where can I have a green burial in Alabama?

Yes, though dedicated sites are few. The Good Earth Burial Ground in Hazel Green, in Madison County north of Huntsville, describes itself as Alabama's first green burial ground. It does not allow embalming or vaults, so the body goes into the ground in a shroud or a container that breaks down.

Elsewhere in Alabama, ask conventional cemeteries whether they will waive their vault rule for a natural burial. Since 2023 the Alabama Board of Funeral Service oversees most cemeteries, and the FTC Funeral Rule lets you decline embalming and supply your own shroud or casket at any Alabama funeral home without a handling fee.

Visiting a family grave on private land in Alabama

Many older Alabama family graves now sit on land that someone else owns or leases. State law requires that owner or lessee to allow reasonable access to those graves. The right covers family members, friends and descendants of the people buried there, anyone who owns a plot in that cemetery, and people doing genealogical, historical or cultural research, as long as they give the owner or lessee reasonable notice (Ala. Code § 35-1-4; checked October 29, 2026).

  • What the visit can be for: visiting, maintaining the grave or cemetery, research, or, for a plot owner, burying someone in that plot.
  • The landowner's say: the owner can set reasonable hours, how often and how long visits last, and the route in when no traditional path is obvious.
  • Damage: visitors are liable for any harm they cause to the land or graves.

Alabama burial regulators and county help

The Alabama Board of Funeral Services (334-242-4049) licenses funeral homes and, since October 2023, oversees most cemeteries; call it for a license check or a complaint about either.

Alabama requires a licensed funeral establishment to arrange a cremation (Ala. Code § 34-13-121(b)), but it does not make families hire a funeral director for every arrangement, so ask the cemetery what it needs before a burial.

Burial needs a disposition permit. Cremation adds a 24-hour wait (Ala. Code § 34-13-121).

When there is no estate

The county commission must pay necessary burial expenses when a person dies with no estate and no relatives in that county able to pay, and the county of death, not of residence, is charged (Ala. Code § 38-8-2). Call that county's commission office before choosing a plot. The $255 Social Security death payment is limited to a spouse or child who qualifies.

Veterans

Alabama National Cemetery in Montevallo (205-665-9039) and Fort Mitchell National Cemetery (334-855-4731) take burials, as does the Alabama State Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Spanish Fort (251-625-1338). The plot, opening and closing and marker are free for an eligible veteran (VA National Cemetery Administration).

Alabama burial checklist

  1. Price the cemetery on its own, apart from the funeral home.
  2. Find out whether a marker from a monument company is permitted.
  3. With no estate, ask the county of death's commission about an approved burial account.
  4. For a veteran, call Alabama National Cemetery about eligibility.
  5. Report a misleading funeral home or cemetery to the board at 334-242-4049.
