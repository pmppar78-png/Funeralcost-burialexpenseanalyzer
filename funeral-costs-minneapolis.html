Funeral Costs in Minneapolis, Minnesota (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Minnesota

The Twin Cities market behaves unlike anywhere else in the Upper Midwest, largely because of one institution: the Cremation Society of Minnesota, a member-owned cooperative headquartered in Edina that has shifted the regional cremation baseline for four decades. Alongside it, Washburn-McReavy operates the city's largest traditional chain out of Hillside Chapel and eighteen other locations, and Bradshaw runs Stillwater, White Bear Lake, and the Mall of America memorial chapel. Between them, a Minneapolis family can get quoted $1,995 for a direct cremation on one call and $12,000 for a full Lutheran service on the next — same city, same ZIP code.

The Cremation Society of Minnesota Effect

Founded in 1972 and structured as a member cooperative, the Cremation Society of Minnesota sells lifetime memberships for roughly $30 that lock in direct-cremation pricing under $2,000 — a model that has effectively held down the entire Twin Cities cremation floor. Non-members pay slightly more but still price below most traditional providers. The coop's Edina crematory processes a large share of the metro's cremations, and its member-based pricing is one of the clearest examples in the country of a consumer-owned funeral structure lowering regional prices. Families without membership can still request the non-member rate; the GPL will list both.

Minnesota's Preneed Trust Rules and the Washburn-McReavy Model

Minnesota Statutes Chapter 149A governs the funeral industry, with the Department of Health's Mortuary Science Section handling licensing and complaints. Preneed funds are held at 85% in trust under state law, with only 15% available for provider setup costs. Washburn-McReavy — the dominant Minneapolis traditional chain — files all preneed accounts through a trust administrator, and every preneed contract in Minnesota must include a right of cancellation and refund. Ask specifically whether the contract is price-guaranteed or simply prefunded; the distinction can be thousands at payout.

Lakewood, Sunset, and the Minneapolis Cemetery Landscape

Lakewood Cemetery on Bde Maka Ska has been Minneapolis's flagship non-sectarian burial ground since 1871 and is home to Humphrey, Wellstone, and several former governors — standard plots run $3,500 to $7,500 depending on section and tree cover. Sunset Memorial Park in Minneapolis and Resurrection and Saint Mary's Catholic Cemeteries in the metro generally price lower, $2,800 to $5,000. Fort Snelling National Cemetery in South Minneapolis provides no-cost burial for eligible veterans and their dependents and remains one of the most-used national cemeteries in the country.

Minnesota Funeral and Burial Benefit via MFIP and MA

The Minnesota Department of Human Services administers funeral and burial benefits through two channels: Minnesota Family Investment Program Emergency Assistance, and the Medical Assistance funeral benefit that covers up to $2,000 toward burial or cremation costs for MA recipients. Hennepin and Ramsey County Human Services offices process applications — and the application generally must be filed before or at the time services are arranged, with estate-recovery limits on assets above a threshold.

Your Rights in Minneapolis

FTC Funeral Rule Protection Every funeral home in Minneapolis must provide an itemized General Price List, let you choose only the services you want, and accept caskets purchased elsewhere. You are never required to buy a package. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

Winter Burial and Temporary Interment at Minneapolis Cemeteries

Minneapolis winters regularly freeze cemetery ground from late November through mid-April. Lakewood, Sunset, and the Catholic cemeteries all maintain temporary receiving vaults where the casket can be held until spring thaw, typically for $250 to $500. Families insisting on immediate winter burial face a frozen-ground surcharge between $500 and $1,200 depending on depth of frost. Fort Snelling National Cemetery works year-round because of its mechanized opening equipment. Direct cremation bypasses all of this — another driver of Minnesota's 62% cremation rate, one of the highest in the Midwest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are funeral costs in the Minneapolis area?

A traditional Minneapolis funeral averages $8,855, above Minnesota's $7,700 state figure. Direct cremation through the Cremation Society of Minnesota starts near $2,000 for members; non-members and traditional funeral homes quote $2,070 and up. Lakewood Cemetery plots run $3,500 to $7,500, while Catholic cemeteries and Sunset Memorial Park price $2,800 to $5,000. A full Washburn-McReavy traditional funeral with Lakewood burial can reach $15,000.

Do more Minneapolis families choose cremation or burial?

Minnesota's 62% cremation rate is among the highest in the Midwest, and the Cremation Society of Minnesota cooperative — serving the Twin Cities since 1972 — has been the single biggest reason. The $6,000-plus gap between direct cremation and traditional burial with a Lakewood plot is too large for many families to ignore. Lutheran and Catholic traditions still anchor a meaningful share of burial services, particularly in first-ring suburbs.

How do Minneapolis funeral costs compare to the national average?

Minneapolis's $8,855 traditional average runs about 13% above the $7,848 national figure. That premium reflects the metro's cost of living and labor; direct cremation through the Cremation Society cooperative actually prices lower than in comparable-size Midwestern markets thanks to the member-owned model.

What is the best way to compare Minneapolis funeral homes?

Pull GPLs from three distinct provider types — a traditional chain like Washburn-McReavy or Bradshaw, the Cremation Society of Minnesota cooperative, and a small family independent. Minnesota's Mortuary Science Section at the Department of Health handles consumer complaints. The Funeral Consumers Alliance of Minnesota publishes local price comparisons and consumer advocacy resources.

How much is direct cremation in Minneapolis?

Direct cremation in Minneapolis starts near $2,070 at most providers and around $2,000 for Cremation Society of Minnesota members after a roughly $30 lifetime membership. The service covers transport, the cremation itself, and return of the cremated remains. A separate memorial service can be held at a Lutheran or Catholic parish, a lake-district park, or a family home at the family's preferred timing.

Are there affordable funeral options in Minneapolis?

Cremation Society of Minnesota membership is the Twin Cities' sharpest cremation-cost reduction. Eligible veterans should use Fort Snelling National Cemetery for a no-cost grave, vault, and headstone — easily $5,000 to $7,000 in value. Minnesota Medical Assistance provides up to $2,000 toward burial or cremation for qualifying MA recipients, and MFIP Emergency Assistance covers additional categories. Casket purchases from independent online retailers save 50 to 70% under the FTC Funeral Rule.
