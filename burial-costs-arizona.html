Burial Costs in Arizona (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Arizona burial cost range, saving tips and method note reworded. Region: West

Burial in Arizona is almost never a single transaction. In Arizona you pay the funeral home, you pay the cemetery, you pay the monument company, and frequently you pay a handful of administrative fees on top. Across those bills, Arizona families typically see totals from $16,320 to $20,820, with the ceiling often set by the casket and the cemetery choice rather than the ceremony itself.

Ways to Lower a Burial Bill in Arizona

How an Arizona family can spend less on a burial:

  • Compare cemetery prices directly: Cemeteries are not covered by the FTC Funeral Rule but most in Arizona will share a price sheet on request.
  • Look for hybrid or natural burial sections: Several Arizona cemeteries have added green or natural burial sections without advertising them prominently.
  • Consider certified conservation burial grounds: Arizona is one of the more active states for true conservation burial. These grounds protect land in perpetuity and price simply, typically $2,880-$5,040 all-in versus $16,320+ for traditional burial in Arizona.
  • Use the competitive cremation market as leverage: With 68% of Arizona families choosing cremation, burial-focused providers often have pricing flexibility they do not advertise.

Regional Context for Burial in Arizona

Green burial is easier to find in Arizona than in much of the country: certified conservation burial grounds and hybrid cemetery sections have expanded noticeably along the West Coast. A green burial in Arizona typically costs $3,600-$5,040 total, without the vault, embalming, or traditional casket costs. Ask specifically about "hybrid" or "natural" sections; many Arizona cemeteries have them but do not advertise them prominently.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the full price of burial in Arizona?

A full burial in Arizona comes in at about $7,200 for the funeral service, $3,400 for the cemetery plot, $2,160 for a casket, $1,360 for a vault, $1,700 for opening and closing, and $500-$5,000 for a headstone. Arizona totals generally land between $16,320 and $20,820 depending on choices.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Arizona?

No Arizona statute requires a vault, so the answer comes from the cemetery's own rules. Many Arizona cemeteries do ask for an outer container, and a concrete liner usually meets that rule for less money than a sealed vault.

Arizona protects you on the paperwork. Under A.R.S. 32-2194.04, a cemetery contract licensed by the Arizona Department of Real Estate has to say whether a vault or liner bought with the space carries an extra fee, whether you may buy the marker or vault from another seller, and what the cancellation policy is. A contract that leaves those disclosures out cannot be enforced against the buyer.

What are the cheapest burial options in Arizona?

Arizona's cheapest burial line is usually direct (immediate) burial, since no embalming, viewing or funeral-home ceremony is involved, and a casket bought elsewhere cannot draw a handling fee under the FTC Funeral Rule. Qualifying veterans pay nothing at a VA national cemetery for the plot, the opening and closing, or the headstone. If you are offered a bargain casket, know the limits in A.R.S. § 32-1365. A casket that already held a body may not be used for another burial, and a former rental casket may be sold only with written disclosure and a new pillow and mattress.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Arizona?

Green and hybrid burial sections in Arizona cemeteries often sit 30-50% below traditional plots, and conservation burial grounds are more established here than in most regions.

What is an all-in burial estimate for Arizona?

Adding every line item, burial in Arizona usually totals $16,320 to $20,820: funeral service about $7,200, plot about $3,400, casket about $2,160, vault about $1,360, opening-and-closing about $1,700, and headstone $500-$5,000.

Is green burial available in Arizona?

Yes, for veterans in particular. The National Memorial Cemetery of Arizona in Phoenix is one of three VA national cemeteries running a green burial pilot section under 38 U.S.C. § 2404(i), where eligible veterans and family members can be buried without a vault or grave liner, in a biodegradable casket or shroud.

Private Arizona cemeteries are licensed by the Arizona Department of Real Estate. If you are buying a natural burial space from one, the contract still has to state the cancellation policy and whether you may buy the marker from another seller (A.R.S. 32-2194.04), so check those terms as you would for a standard plot.

Where part of an Arizona plot price goes

When an Arizona cemetery calls itself perpetual-care or endowed-care, part of every sale goes into a trust for the grounds. Within 30 days after a purchase contract is paid in full, the cemetery must deposit $2.75 per square foot of each grave, $36 for each niche and $120 for each crypt into its endowed-care trust. It may add up to 15% of the gross sale price on top (A.R.S. § 32-2194.28; checked October 29, 2026).

A standard grave of about 30 square feet works out to roughly $82 going into the trust. The rule applies to any cemetery that markets itself as perpetual- or endowed-care. If a cemetery does not make that claim, ask in writing who will maintain the grounds in the long run.

Burial rules and help in Arizona

Funeral homes handling burials in Arizona are licensed by the Arizona Department of Health Services, Bureau of Licensing for Professions & Occupations (BLPO) (602-364-2079). Cemetery sales in Arizona may fall under a different office, so ask the board who oversees the cemetery you are considering.

Arizona is missing from the Funeral Consumers Alliance count of nine states that insist on a funeral director, which leaves an Arizona family free to arrange a burial on its own once the burial permit is issued, subject to the cemetery's rules.

An Arizona burial needs a disposition permit. If you switch to cremation instead, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (A.R.S. § 32-1365.02).

An Arizona county pays for an indigent burial only when no family member or other authorized person is willing or able to (A.R.S. § 36-831); the board of supervisors funds it and keeps a permanent register of the indigent dead (§ 11-251(27)). (Source: A.R.S. §§ 36-831, 11-251(27).) A surviving spouse or child may claim Social Security's one-time $255 death payment within two years; it is paid to them, not to the Arizona cemetery.

For Arizona veterans, the plot and marker in a national cemetery are free, which can remove thousands from a burial bill. In Arizona, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: National Memorial Cemetery of Arizona (480-513-3600); Prescott National Cemetery (928-445-4860, cremated remains only); state and tribal veterans cemeteries: Arizona Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Camp Navajo (928-214-3474); Arizona Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Marana (520-638-4894); Monte Calvario Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 520-879-6317); San Carlos Apache Tribal Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 928-475-2361); Southern Arizona Veterans Memorial Cemetery (520-458-7144) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps for a burial in Arizona

  1. In Arizona the cemetery prices the plot, opening and marker separately from the funeral home, so collect two or more quotes on each side.
  2. Ask whether the Arizona cemetery lets you buy the headstone elsewhere.
  3. Ask the county public fiduciary whether Arizona's indigent program buries or cremates in your county before you look at plots.
  4. Veterans' families should check with National Memorial Cemetery of Arizona about the records it needs before the service, starting with the DD214.
  5. Pricing complaints about an Arizona funeral home go to the state board, 602-364-2079.
