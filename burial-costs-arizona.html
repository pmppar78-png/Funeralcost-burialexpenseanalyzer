Burial Costs in Arizona (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: West

Arizona's burial economics run on two big levers most families don't think about until they're handed an estimate: the desert-heat calendar that pushes embalming and refrigeration decisions forward, and the sheer size of the Phoenix-Tucson retirement population, which has given the state one of the densest and most competitive funeral markets in the Southwest. Both of those work in a consumer's favor — if the family is willing to shop.

Across every line, a traditional Arizona burial bills out somewhere between $13,660 and $19,220. VA eligibility at the state's national cemeteries can cut this by more than half.

The two Arizona national cemeteries that reshape the math

Arizona has two VA national cemeteries — the National Memorial Cemetery of Arizona in Phoenix (at Cave Creek Road) and Prescott National Cemetery — plus a state veterans' cemetery in Sierra Vista. Eligible veterans and spouses pay nothing for the plot, opening-and-closing, outer burial receptacle, or government-issue marker. Families sometimes don't pursue this path because the funeral home doesn't volunteer it. Ask directly. The wait for a committal service at the Phoenix national cemetery typically runs 3-10 business days, which is comparable to most private cemeteries in the metro.

Notable Arizona cemeteries and how they price

  • Greenwood Memory Lawn Mortuary & Cemetery — Phoenix; one of the oldest continuously operating cemeteries in the metro, with a broad range of plot types and price points.
  • Holy Redeemer Cemetery — Roman Catholic Diocese of Phoenix; typically priced in the mid-market with bundled perpetual care.
  • East Resthaven Park Cemetery — Phoenix; large memorial-park format.
  • Evergreen Cemetery — Tucson's historic municipal cemetery, at the low-to-mid end of Southern Arizona pricing.
  • Bisbee Evergreen Cemetery and similar small-town southern Arizona cemeteries — often significantly cheaper than Phoenix or Tucson.

The Arizona State Board of Funeral Directors and what it actually does

The Arizona State Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers licenses funeral homes and embalmers and handles consumer complaints. Its website publishes disciplinary actions — helpful if you want to check a specific funeral home before committing. Complaints typically involve price-list disputes, improper handling of remains, or failure to return pre-need funds. The board cannot arbitrate a private pricing dispute, but a filed complaint often gets a funeral home to revisit an invoice.

Summer logistics: Phoenix and Tucson routinely see 105-115°F summer highs. Refrigeration is mandatory once the decedent cannot be buried within the first 24 hours, and most Arizona cemeteries will not schedule committals in the afternoon heat during June-August. Morning services are standard. Ask the funeral home to quote the refrigeration charge upfront rather than letting it accumulate.

Maricopa County assistance and other state programs

Maricopa County operates one of the larger county-level indigent burial programs in the Southwest, administered through its Office of the Medical Examiner for unclaimed decedents and through Human Services for families demonstrating financial need. Reimbursement ceilings are modest — typically under $1,500 — and they cover direct burial or direct cremation. Pima County runs a similar program. The Arizona Crime Victim Compensation Program can contribute up to $5,000 toward funeral expenses for qualifying deaths. Social Security's lump-sum death benefit ($255) remains available for eligible survivors, though it covers almost nothing relative to actual burial costs.

Green burial and Arizona's desert approach

Green burial sections are more established in Arizona than the national average suggests. Forever Our Home in the Sedona area has operated as a conservation-style burial ground for years. Several Phoenix and Tucson cemeteries maintain hybrid sections that waive the vault requirement. Pricing for green burial in Arizona typically runs $3,600-$5,040 all-in, without the vault, embalming, or traditional casket costs. Ask specifically about "natural" or "hybrid" sections — many cemeteries have them without prominent signage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the full price of burial in Arizona?

The full ledger looks like this: $7,200 funeral service, $3,400 plot, $2,160 casket, $1,360 vault, $1,700 opening-and-closing, and $1,000-$3,000 for a marker — producing the $13,660-$19,220 all-in range. Eligible veterans using the National Memorial Cemetery of Arizona or Prescott National Cemetery can bring the cemetery side of that bill to zero.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Arizona?

Arizona doesn't require them by statute; individual cemeteries typically do. Greenwood Memory Lawn, Holy Redeemer, and the major Phoenix-Tucson memorial parks all require at least a concrete liner. Green and hybrid sections — where available — skip the requirement. Ask the cemetery for their written vault policy and the exact spec; sometimes the funeral home's default vault product is three tiers above what the cemetery actually requires.

What are the cheapest burial options in Arizona?

VA burial at the Phoenix or Prescott national cemeteries is the cheapest option for eligible veterans, period. Beyond that: direct burial at a Tucson or small-town southern Arizona cemetery runs $3,500-$5,500; online casket purchase saves another $1,500-$3,000; green burial at a hybrid cemetery section avoids the vault. Buying the casket from Costco or Titan Casket and refusing the funeral home's cash-advance markups on death certificates and obituaries adds up quickly.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Arizona?

Call three: one Phoenix-area memorial park, one Catholic cemetery if applicable, and the relevant national or state veterans' cemetery. Ask each for an itemized sheet covering plot, opening-and-closing, vault spec and policy, perpetual care, and any residency restrictions. Arizona cemeteries aren't covered by the FTC Funeral Rule, so they're under no obligation to share — but nearly all will. The Arizona State Board of Funeral Directors' website is worth a quick check on the funeral home you're comparing against.

What is an all-in burial estimate for Arizona?

Adding every line — funeral service ($7,200), plot ($3,400), casket ($2,160), vault ($1,360), opening-and-closing ($1,700), headstone ($1,000-$3,000), and incidentals — produces $13,660-$19,220 for a traditional Arizona burial. Refrigeration charges during peak summer and separately billed monument work can push the upper bound past $20,000. Shopping funeral home and cemetery as separate bills and buying the casket outside the funeral home are the two biggest savings levers.

Is green burial available in Arizona?

Yes, and more readily than in most states. Forever Our Home near Sedona operates as a dedicated natural burial ground; Phoenix and Tucson both have cemeteries with hybrid sections that waive vault and embalming requirements. Pricing usually comes in $3,600-$5,040 total, materially below traditional burial. Ask each cemetery specifically about "natural" or "hybrid" sections — they're often on-site but not prominently advertised, and front-desk staff don't always mention them unless pressed.
