Burial Costs in Georgia (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: South

Georgia is one of the more affordable Southern states for a traditional burial — a 2026 full-service interment totals $12,980 to $18,250 — but the spread between metro Atlanta and the rest of the state is significant. A plot at Oakland Cemetery in Atlanta or Bonaventure in Savannah carries historic-significance pricing, while rural county cemeteries in central and South Georgia still quote plots under $1,500. The Georgia State Board of Funeral Service regulates pricing disclosure at funeral homes; cemeteries sit outside that reach, which makes independent price comparison the single largest lever a family has.

Stacked line by line, a Georgia burial in 2026 totals $12,980 to $18,250. Metro Atlanta and the Savannah coastal market tend toward the top of that band; Middle and South Georgia sit in the lower half.

Georgia's cemeteries tell the state's history — and carry its prices

Oakland Cemetery in downtown Atlanta (founded 1850) and Bonaventure Cemetery in Savannah are two of the most photographed burial grounds in the country. Both are still active, both carry premium plot pricing in their historic sections, and both have newer sections at closer-to-market rates. Oakland runs as a city cemetery with a separately operated historic preservation foundation; Bonaventure is a Savannah city cemetery. Families with ancestral ties often qualify for section pricing unavailable to general buyers — worth asking about directly.

How the Atlanta metro compares to the rest of Georgia

  • Metro Atlanta (Fulton, DeKalb, Cobb, Gwinnett): Service line $7,400-$9,200, plot line $3,800-$5,800.
  • Savannah and the coast: Service line $6,800-$8,400, plot line $3,400-$6,500 depending on proximity to Bonaventure.
  • Columbus, Macon, Augusta: Service line $6,200-$7,600, plot line $2,200-$3,800.
  • Rural South and Middle Georgia: Service line $5,400-$6,800, plot line often under $1,500 at municipal or church-owned cemeteries.

The Georgia State Board of Funeral Service — what it enforces

Funeral directors and establishments in Georgia are licensed by the Georgia State Board of Funeral Service, which handles consumer complaints and disciplinary action. The board enforces compliance with the FTC Funeral Rule, including the General Price List requirement and the no-handling-fee rule on third-party caskets. If a Georgia funeral home will not hand over a GPL or claims embalming is mandatory for a closed-casket service, that is grounds for a complaint to the board — and in practice the threat alone usually resolves it.

County DFCS assistance: Georgia's county Division of Family and Children Services offices administer indigent burial assistance for residents who meet income thresholds. The program varies county by county in dollar amount and documentation requirements. Start with the probate court or county DFCS office in the county of death.

Where Georgia families actually save

  1. Church cemeteries (Baptist, Methodist, Catholic, AME) across the state routinely price plots 40-60% below commercial memorial parks, with lower opening-and-closing fees.
  2. Two VA national cemeteries in Georgia — Georgia National Cemetery in Canton and Marietta National Cemetery — provide plot, vault allowance, opening-and-closing, and marker at no cost to eligible veterans.
  3. Buy the casket outside the funeral home. Savings of $800-$1,800 are routine; Atlanta has more than one independent casket retailer open to the public.
  4. Honeycreek Woodlands in Conyers and a handful of other Georgia cemeteries operate certified green burial sections — no vault, no embalming, lower total.
  5. Ask about grave liners as an alternative to full vaults. Most Georgia cemeteries accept them; it is typically a $600-$1,000 savings.

Green burial in Georgia

Georgia has a small but growing roster of conservation and hybrid cemeteries. Honeycreek Woodlands in Rockdale County is the best-established conservation burial ground, with no vault, no embalming, and land managed as nature preserve. Several traditional cemeteries around Athens and the North Georgia mountains maintain green sections. Green burial consistently lands below a traditional full-service interment. What Families Want to Know

What do Georgia families spend on burial?

A 2026 Georgia burial totals $12,980 to $18,250 once every line is added: funeral service $6,900, plot $3,200, casket $2,070, vault $1,280, opening-and-closing $1,600, marker $1,000-$3,000. Metro Atlanta pushes to the upper half; rural Middle and South Georgia sit comfortably in the lower half, with some church and municipal cemeteries pricing plots under $1,500.

Do I need a vault for burial in Georgia?

Georgia state law does not require a burial vault, but almost every commercial Georgia cemetery does — it is a cemetery policy to prevent grave settling. Many church cemeteries and smaller municipal grounds accept a concrete grave liner in place of a sealed vault, which trims the bill by $600-$1,000. Always ask in writing before committing.

What are the cheapest burial options in Georgia?

The lowest-cost Georgia path is a direct burial through an independent funeral home, paired with a plot at a church, municipal, or family cemetery — easy to find in much of the state. Buying the casket from an online or independent retailer saves $800-$1,800. Eligible veterans should check Georgia National Cemetery in Canton or Marietta National Cemetery, where the core burial costs are covered by the VA. Green burial at Honeycreek Woodlands or a hybrid section removes the vault line entirely.

How do cemetery prices differ across Georgia?

Plot prices in Georgia span a wide range — from $1,200 at a small county cemetery to $6,500 in Oakland Cemetery's or Bonaventure's newer sections. Cemeteries are not covered by the FTC Funeral Rule, so you must ask each cemetery for a written, itemized list covering plot, opening-and-closing, vault policy, perpetual care, and any residency or membership restrictions. Families with ancestral ties to historic Georgia cemeteries sometimes qualify for section pricing unavailable to general buyers.

Once I add every line item, what does burial actually cost in Georgia?

The complete 2026 Georgia burial stack — $6,900 funeral service, $3,200 plot, $2,070 casket, $1,280 vault, $1,600 opening-and-closing, and a $1,000-$3,000 marker — totals $12,980 to $18,250. Shopping the cemetery and funeral home as separate transactions, and buying the casket from an independent retailer, consistently lands Georgia families in the lower half of that band.

Is green burial available in Georgia?

Yes. Honeycreek Woodlands in Rockdale County is the best-known conservation burial ground in the state, with no vault and no embalming required. Several traditional cemeteries around Athens and the North Georgia mountains maintain green burial sections with similar rules. Green burial usually costs less than a traditional full-service interment because the vault and embalming lines disappear. Our green burial guide covers certification.
