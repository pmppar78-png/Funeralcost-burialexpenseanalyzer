Burial Costs in Georgia

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 What changed: Rewrote the page by hand, including the section “Georgia Burial Prices, Charge by Charge”. Region: South

A full burial in Georgia totals about $15,550 to $20,050 in 2026 when this page's line items are added: roughly $6,900 for the funeral service, $3,200 for the plot, $2,070 for a casket, $1,280 for a vault or liner and $1,600 for opening and closing, plus a headstone at $500 to $5,000. Veterans have a strong alternative in Georgia National Cemetery and the two state veterans cemeteries.

The total cost of burial in Georgia typically ranges from $15,550 to $20,050 depending on choices made, and cemetery expenses are separate from funeral home charges and can add thousands to burial costs; using a funeral cost calculator to compare services by state can help put these numbers in context.

The basic services fee at many Georgia funeral homes commonly falls around $1,800 to $3,200 and covers core professional services and use of the funeral home's facilities.

Preparation costs, including embalming, usually fall between $500 and $1,500, and our Georgia estimate for embalming is about $690.

Caskets can cost more than $10,000 depending on materials and customization.

Burial Options and Average Funeral Cost in Georgia

  • A traditional funeral with viewing and burial is the most expensive route in Georgia, at about $6,900 for the funeral home's part before the plot, vault and opening fees in the table above.
  • A Georgia funeral home may insist on embalming as its own policy for a public viewing, but the FTC Funeral Rule bars it from claiming the law requires embalming when it does not.
  • Direct cremation starts around $1,700 in Georgia, and cremation with a full service runs about $5,600; for any cremation, Georgia providers must let you use a simple container instead of a casket.
  • A green burial, without embalming or a vault, can cost less than a conventional burial. Learn about green burial

Cutting the Cost of a Georgia Burial

Arranging things ahead of time is the surest way for Georgia families to save and to know how the bill will be paid.

  • Get cemetery prices in writing: the FTC Funeral Rule does not reach cemeteries, but Georgia cemeteries registered with the Secretary of State will generally give a price sheet when asked.
  • Request the General Price List: Ask each funeral provider for the free GPL, since the federal trade commission Funeral Rule requires itemized price lists from funeral homes.
  • Ask churches and family cemeteries in rural Georgia: many still sell graves, often for 30-50% less than a commercial memorial park.
  • Review charges before you sign: Check every service fee, any add-on charges, and unnecessary services before finalizing funeral arrangements.
  • Ask the county about a veterans' section: some Georgia counties set aside veterans' sections in their own cemeteries, separate from Georgia National Cemetery and the state veterans cemeteries.
  • Ask about financing: Many Georgia funeral homes offer payment plans, and some families may need payment assistance information or other professional guidance to manage costs.
  • Look into benefits and protections: Checking your rights matters in Georgia; eligible families may receive a $255 Social Security death benefit, low-income households may qualify for Georgia DFCS help, and veterans may receive VA burial benefits or cash assistance.

How Georgia Burial Compares With the Region

Georgia families tend to expect the funeral home to take care of everything. In practice costs shift by region, provider and local cemetery market, and the cemetery sends its own bill, usually lower at a church, fraternal or town cemetery than at a commercial memorial park. Georgia funeral homes have to answer price questions over the telephone (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.2(b)(1)), which lets a family compare several of them before visiting any. National averages and the national median cost can be a helpful starting point, but the median cost in Atlanta may not reflect the cost of a funeral in smaller Georgia communities. Local competition and economic conditions also influence pricing from one community to another.

Before choosing a plot in Georgia, ask the cemetery for written prices on four items: the grave itself, the opening and closing, the vault or liner it requires, and the marker setting fee. Those four are billed by the cemetery rather than the funeral home, and together they can cost more than the plot.

Georgia Burial: Questions We Get

What is the usual cost of a Georgia burial?

Adding the typical figures below, with opening and closing at $1,600 and a $500 to $5,000 headstone, a Georgia burial totals about $15,550 to $20,050. The funeral service runs about $6,900, a burial plot may average around $3,200 depending on location, the casket around $2,070, the vault $1,280, and opening and closing fees commonly range from about $500 to $3,000, with weekend and holiday burials usually at the top of a cemetery's fee list. Beyond the main line items, funeral expenses may also include obituary notices, flower arrangements, and certified death certificates; in Georgia, the first certified copy costs $25 and each extra copy ordered at the same time costs $5 (Georgia DPH fees).

Is a burial vault mandatory in Georgia?

Georgia law does not require outer burial containers, though many cemeteries require burial vaults or grave liners by policy to prevent the grave from settling, similar to common practices described in Memphis funeral cost guides. Vault prices in Georgia range from $800 to $10,000 depending on material and brand.

How can a Georgia burial be done for less?

Georgia's lowest-cost burials are a direct burial, with no viewing, service or embalming, so the embalming and facility charges fall away; a natural burial in a biodegradable container where a Georgia cemetery allows one; and a casket bought from an independent seller, which can cut well into the $2,070 casket estimate on this page.

Do Georgia cemetery prices vary much?

A great deal. Two Georgia cemeteries in one city can be thousands of dollars apart on the plot alone.

With all charges added, what does a Georgia burial come to?

A complete burial in Georgia (funeral service ($6,900) + plot ($3,200) + casket ($2,070) + vault ($1,280) + opening-and-closing ($1,600) + headstone ($500-$5,000)) totals $15,550 to $20,050. Headstones often run $500 to $5,000 or more in Georgia and are separate from other cemetery expenses.

Where can you have a green burial in Georgia?

Green burial skips embalming and the vault, and Georgia has at least one dedicated natural burial ground, Honey Creek Woodlands near Conyers, while some conventional cemeteries set aside green sections. Most Georgia cemeteries still have no green section, so call around locally.

Georgia perpetual care rules and death certificates

When a Georgia cemetery sells a grave with perpetual care, part of the price must go into a trust the cemetery cannot spend down. Under O.C.G.A. § 10-14-6, part of the Georgia Cemetery and Funeral Services Act, the cemetery must deposit 15 percent of the sale price of a burial right, with a minimum per grave that is adjusted for inflation every three years, and 7.5 percent of the price of a mausoleum crypt, niche or columbarium space. Deposits are due within 30 days after the end of the month the payment comes in, and a cemetery selling on installments must finish funding the trust within six years. Any trust created since July 1, 2000 must start with $10,000 before the cemetery sells its first grave. The trustee reports to the Georgia Secretary of State every year, and owners who let the fund run short can be held personally liable. A cemetery that sells without perpetual care is a different product, and it is worth asking which one you are buying.

Death certificates from the Georgia State Office of Vital Records cost $25 for the search and first certified copy and $5 for each additional copy bought at the same time; the $25 is not refunded if no record is found (Georgia DPH, checked October 29, 2026). Ordering online through the state's ROVER system adds an $8 processing fee, and expedited shipping is $16, though that does not shorten processing, which can take up to 10 weeks. Five copies ordered together cost $45, so it pays to order all you need in one request rather than coming back for a second $25 search.

Who regulates a Georgia burial, and where to get help

The Georgia State Board of Funeral Service (404-424-9966) licenses the funeral homes that handle burials. Cemetery trusts are reported to the Secretary of State, as explained above, and the board can point you to the right Georgia office for a cemetery complaint.

Georgia is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance's list of nine states that require a funeral director, so a Georgia family can carry out a burial without one, provided it has the disposition permit and keeps to the cemetery's grave rules.

A Georgia burial needs a disposition permit. Cremation has no fixed waiting period, though it too needs a permit (O.C.G.A. § 31-10-20; Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. r. 511-1-3-.23).

When the family cannot pay

Under O.C.G.A. § 36-12-5, the county where the death occurred is responsible for an indigent burial, but each county decides how much it reimburses, up to the actual cost. Social Security's one-time $255 is paid to a surviving spouse or child who claims it within two years, not to the cemetery.

Veterans

An eligible veteran's national cemetery grave, opening and closing, and marker cost nothing. Georgia National Cemetery in Canton, north of Atlanta (770-479-9300), is the state's only VA national cemetery taking burials; the state also runs Georgia Veterans Memorial Cemeteries in Glennville (912-654-5398) and Milledgeville (478-445-3363) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration). The funeral home books a national cemetery grave through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office, 800-535-1117.

Before you sign anything in Georgia

  1. Get prices from two or more cemeteries and two or more funeral homes; the plot, the grave opening and the marker each carry their own charge.
  2. Ask whether the price includes perpetual care, and whether you may buy the headstone elsewhere.
  3. Ask the county of death what it reimburses under § 36-12-5, and get the answer in writing, before choosing a cemetery or signing a funeral contract.
  4. For a veteran, have the DD214 ready and compare Canton with the two state cemeteries.
  5. Funeral home burial prices that prove misleading can be reported to the board at 404-424-9966.
