By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Texas

Arlington sits between Dallas and Fort Worth in the middle of a crowded DFW funeral market. Local providers like Moore Memorial Gardens on Collins Street and Wade Family Funeral Service anchor the city, competing against dozens of Fort Worth and Dallas firms willing to drive to Tarrant County. Average prices — $7,920 traditional, $1,870 direct cremation — sit slightly above the Texas statewide figure, but the competitive density means comparison shopping in Arlington pays off in real dollars.

Texas Chapter 711 and Family Cemeteries in the Arlington Area

Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 711 is one of the friendliest cemetery statutes in the country for families who own land. It permits burial on private property (subject to county approval and proper recording) and specifically allows the continuation of existing family cemeteries. Around the Arlington and greater DFW area, a number of historic family cemeteries still function this way — the plot cost is zero, there's no vault mandate (that's a commercial-cemetery contract item, not state law), and the interment process only requires a licensed funeral director's coordination for transport and permits.

If you own rural land in Parker, Johnson, or Ellis County, talk to the county clerk about recording a family-cemetery designation before you need it. Done upfront, it saves thousands versus a DFW-area commercial cemetery plot.

Your Rights in Arlington

FTC Funeral Rule and Texas Funeral Service Commission Every Arlington funeral home must provide an itemized GPL on request under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), allow you to decline any service, accept a third-party casket or urn with no handling fee, and avoid false legal claims about required services. The Texas Funeral Service Commission in Austin licenses and disciplines individual funeral directors and embalmers — a separate track from FTC complaints. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

The Texas No-Embalming Rule and How It Applies in Arlington

Texas law does not require embalming in standard circumstances. Health and Safety Code Chapter 716 allows refrigeration as an alternative, and burial or cremation can generally proceed within 24 hours without embalming. That matters in Arlington specifically: several providers still default to embalming on first-visit arrangement sheets, particularly for open-casket viewings at church services. You can decline. If you're planning a closed-casket service or cremation, there's no legal reason to pay the $792 embalming charge. If a provider refuses to arrange a service without embalming when no viewing is planned, flag it with the Texas Funeral Service Commission.

What Families Want to Know

What is the typical funeral price in Arlington?

Arlington families are paying $7,920 for a full traditional funeral at a home like Wade Family or Moore Bowen Rose — a few points above Texas's $7,200 statewide figure, reflecting the DFW metroplex's higher overhead. Direct cremation at local providers starts at $1,870; cemetery space at Moore Memorial Gardens or Parkdale averages $3,740; a veteran interment at DFW National Cemetery in Dallas is free of charge for an eligible service member plus spouse.

Is cremation or burial more common in Arlington?

Tarrant County tracks Texas's statewide 48% cremation rate — roughly ten points below the national figure, reflecting stronger burial traditions across the Metroplex's Hispanic-Catholic and evangelical communities. Arlington direct cremation at $1,870 stands against a $7,920 traditional service plus $3,740 plot, so the economic pull toward cremation is real even where tradition runs the other way. Texas's 1-day post-death minimum wait for cremation is shorter than California's 48 hours.

Where does Arlington rank nationally for funeral pricing?

Arlington's $7,920 traditional average sits roughly 1% above the $7,848 US median — modestly expensive for Texas, reflecting DFW metro overhead. The city is cheaper than Dallas proper (where downtown providers often exceed $8,500) but pricier than Fort Worth or Waco. The Texas Funeral Service Commission publishes provider disciplinary records; checking before you commit is worth the ten minutes.

How do I compare funeral quotes in Arlington?

Every Arlington funeral home must hand over a General Price List under the FTC Funeral Rule — and under Texas Occupations Code Chapter 651, the Texas Funeral Service Commission licenses and regulates every Arlington provider. Call Wade Family, Moore Bowen Rose, Max Slayton, and Hawkins Funeral Home to compare across the DFW market. The North Texas chapter of the Funeral Consumers Alliance maintains Arlington-specific pricing surveys.

What is direct cremation in Arlington and how much does it cost?

Direct cremation through an Arlington provider runs $1,870 — it includes removal, the state-required permit, the cremation itself, and return of cremated remains in a basic container. Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 716 governs the process; no embalming is required, and the short waiting period applies. Neptune Society and a handful of DFW direct-cremation specialists publish tiered pricing that sometimes undercuts Arlington traditional homes by several hundred dollars.

How can Arlington families find low-cost funeral options?

Direct cremation at $1,870 is the floor. For burial, DFW National Cemetery gives eligible veterans a free plot plus opening and closing. The Tarrant County indigent program covers qualifying families unable to pay; Social Security's $255 death benefit and VA's $948–$2,473 burial allowance stack on top of either option. Texas Chapter 711 allows family cemeteries on private land in unincorporated Tarrant County — an option for rural families that eliminates most commercial cemetery fees.
