What Drives Funeral Costs in Florida

What you pay for a funeral in Florida depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in Florida, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether Florida state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. South pricing is generally moderate than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in Florida routinely differs by 10-30%.

The state's 62% cremation rate shapes pricing too.

Your Consumer Rights in Florida

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Florida must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Florida has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What's Specific to Florida (South)

Because Florida is in the South and summer heat matters, embalming timelines come up earlier in the arrangement conversation than in cooler regions. Embalming is still not legally required in Florida in most circumstances — refrigeration is an alternative. If you are asked to agree to embalming quickly, that is a signal to slow down and ask whether it is a legal requirement or a provider preference.

Payment Assistance in Florida

  • If the family cannot pay, call the Florida county social services office before signing with a funeral home, since public help may require approval first and is often limited to the lowest-cost option.
  • The $255 Social Security death payment goes only to an eligible surviving spouse or child; in Florida, as elsewhere, they apply by calling 1-800-772-1213 within two years of the death.
  • Whoever paid for a veteran's funeral in Florida can file VA Form 21P-530EZ; for a death on or after October 1, 2026 that was not service-connected, VA pays up to $1,032 toward burial and $1,032 toward a plot (see our veteran burial benefits guide).
  • More options for Florida families, including Medicaid and charity help, are in our funeral payment assistance guide and how to pay for a funeral with no money.

If you are currently arranging a funeral in Florida, here is a step-by-step approach that can save you time, stress, and money:

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in Florida or burial in Florida.

Funeral rules and help in Florida

The Florida Department of Financial Services licenses funeral directors and establishments in Florida; call 850-413-3039 to check a license or file a complaint.

Hiring a funeral home is a choice, not a legal requirement, for most steps in Florida; Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count it among the nine states with funeral-director rules. Florida paperwork and permits still apply.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Florida requires a 48-hour wait before cremation (Fla. Stat. § 872.03; Fla. Stat. § 406.11(1)(c)).

We have not verified a statewide burial-assistance amount for Florida. Ask the Florida county social-services office whether it pays for indigent burial or cremation, and see our guide to public funeral assistance. The federal Social Security lump sum available in Florida is $255, paid only to a qualifying spouse or child.

Eligible Florida veterans get a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. In Florida, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Barrancas National Cemetery (850-453-4108); Bay Pines National Cemetery (727-319-6479, cremated remains only). Source: VA National Cemetery Administration.

Next steps in Florida

  1. Compare at least two Florida providers line by line, using their printed price lists.
  2. Ask the Florida funeral home whether embalming is actually needed for your plans.
  3. If money is short, call the Florida county social-services office before signing a contract.
  4. Veterans' families can schedule through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office; Barrancas National Cemetery is listed above.
  5. Report problems with a provider to the licensing board at 850-413-3039.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Florida?

Traditional funerals in Florida average around $7,800, which lands near the national figure of $8,300. The only reliable way to pin down your number is to pull General Price Lists from two or three Miami-area providers and compare line by line.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in Florida?

Direct cremation in Florida starts around $1,800; a traditional funeral with burial averages $7,800, not counting the cemetery plot or vault. That is a difference of at least $6,000 for most families.

What rights do Florida families have when arranging a funeral?

The federal FTC Funeral Rule gives Florida families the right to an itemized price list, to prices over the phone, and to buy only the goods and services they want. According to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, Florida does not require families to hire a funeral director for every step. The Florida Department of Financial Services, 850-413-3039, licenses Florida funeral homes and takes complaints.

How can I save on funeral costs in Florida?

Start by lining up General Price Lists from two or three Miami-area providers before you commit to anything. Direct cremation at $1,800 remains the lowest-cost disposition in every Florida market.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Florida?

The FTC Funeral Rule bars a Florida funeral home from saying embalming is required by law when it is not, and its price list must state that embalming is not required except in certain special cases. Ask any Florida home that insists on embalming to name the rule in writing.

Is Florida cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Compare the Florida figures in the cost table on this page with the national median of $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, or $6,280 with cremation (NFDA 2023). Individual Florida funeral homes can sit well above or below the state figure, so get several price lists.

What is the cheapest funeral option in Florida?

Direct cremation is the lowest-cost option in Florida, starting at roughly $1,800. Direct burial comes next at around $3,900 and still avoids viewing, ceremony, and embalming.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in Florida?

Ask at least three Florida providers for their General Price Lists and compare direct cremation and immediate burial lines. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Florida home cannot charge a fee for it.
