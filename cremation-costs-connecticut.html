By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Cremation Rate: 52%

What to Know Before Choosing Cremation in Connecticut

When considering cremation in Connecticut, keep these important points in mind:

  • Before any Connecticut cremation, a medical examiner has to complete a cremation certificate after inquiring into the cause and manner of death, and the estate pays $150 for it; that fee is not charged for anyone under 18 (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-323).
  • The town registrar then issues the cremation permit, which costs $5 by statute, and Connecticut bars cremation until 48 hours after death unless the death came from a communicable disease.
  • Before signing, ask the Connecticut provider when the ashes will be ready, how they are returned, and whether a certified copy of the death certificate is in the price.
Connecticut crematories must keep the cremation permit, the signed authorization and the record of who received the ashes for at least three years (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-322), so a family can ask for copies later; urn complaints go to the Department of Public Health.

Regional Context for Cremation in Connecticut

In Connecticut, where winter complicates traditional burial, cremation has a practical scheduling advantage most families underestimate: ashes can be interred or scattered any time of year, which means the service does not have to be planned around frozen-ground delays. This alone pushes some Northeastern families toward cremation, even before considering the $7,800 cost difference versus traditional burial in Connecticut.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Connecticut?

Cremation costs in Connecticut range from $2,400 for direct cremation to $7,600 for a full funeral service followed by cremation. Before those extras, the Connecticut base ranges from $2,400 for direct cremation to $7,600 with a service.

What kinds of cremation services can I choose in Connecticut?

Connecticut families usually pick one of three routes: direct cremation at about $2,400 with no service, cremation followed by a memorial held later wherever the family likes, or a full funeral with viewing before the cremation. Each one can be adjusted to fit the family.

What legal requirements apply to cremation in Connecticut?

The core rule comes from Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-323. Connecticut requires a 48-hour waiting period before cremation unless death was from a communicable disease, plus a medical examiner's certificate.

If a Connecticut death falls under the medical examiner or coroner, that office has to release the body before any cremation. Pacemakers, implanted defibrillators, and certain other implants are removed before a Connecticut cremation; ask the provider whether removal is in its price.

Complaints about how a crematory or funeral home handled these steps go to the Connecticut Department of Public Health (860-509-7603).

Can I scatter ashes in Connecticut?

Connecticut has no statute listing where ashes may or may not be scattered. The state's control comes earlier, in the paperwork: under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-323 the cremation permit records the intended disposition, and the medical examiner's cremation certificate carries a $150 fee. Once the family has the remains, the decision is theirs, with the landowner's permission on private property and the managing agency's rules on public land.

For Long Island Sound or open water, note that the federal burial-at-sea rule applies only to ocean waters at least three nautical miles from shore, with a report to EPA Region 1 within 30 days. The Department of Public Health (860-509-7603) licenses funeral homes and crematories.

What is driving Connecticut's cremation rate?

The cost delta alone explains a lot: direct cremation costs an estimated $7,800 less than a traditional funeral in Connecticut.

What is the timeline for cremation in Connecticut?

The cremation itself runs 2-3 hours in Connecticut. A direct cremation ($2,400) is the fastest end-to-end because nothing but the 48-hour wait Connecticut requires after death (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-323) sits between death and cremation.

Can I hold a funeral service before cremation in Connecticut?

A traditional funeral service followed by cremation costs approximately $7,600 in Connecticut. In Connecticut that price includes visitation, a ceremony, and then cremation instead of burial.

Cremation rules and help in Connecticut

The licensing office for cremation providers in Connecticut is the Connecticut Department of Public Health; its number is 860-509-7603.

For a cremation in Connecticut, state law (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 7-62b, 7-65a, 7-69) reserves filing the death certificate, moving the body, and obtaining the disposition permit for a licensed funeral director, according to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, so even the $2,400 direct cremation in the table runs through a licensee.

Before a cremation can happen: Connecticut requires a 48-hour waiting period before cremation unless death was from a communicable disease, plus a medical examiner's certificate (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-323).

Ask the Connecticut Department of Social Services whether its funeral and burial allowance, up to $1,800 and reduced by contributions over $3,400, applies to the cremation you are planning; the application deadline is one year. Medicaid applicants can also prepay up to $10,000 in an irrevocable funeral contract (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-207), which can be written for a cremation. (Source: Connecticut DSS.)

A Connecticut veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. The one option in Connecticut is Connecticut State Veterans Cemetery (860-616-3688). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Connecticut

  1. Ask whether a Connecticut cremation price includes the death certificates and the permit fee, which some providers bill separately.
  2. Build the 48-hour wait set by Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-323 into any Connecticut travel or memorial plans.
  3. Ask Connecticut DSS how a cremation's cost and any family contributions over $3,400 affect the $1,800 allowance.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 860-509-7603.
