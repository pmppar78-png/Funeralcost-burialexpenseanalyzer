Cremation Costs in Illinois (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Illinois cremation page reorganized around the 24-hour wait, the coroner permit and veterans cemeteries, with unsourced timing and package claims removed. Cremation Rate: 52%

At a 52% cremation rate, Illinois families choose cremation more often than burial, and direct cremation at about $2,000 compares with $8,500 for a full traditional funeral. The Crematory Regulation Act, 410 ILCS 18/35, sets protections worth knowing: no dental gold or valuables may be removed without written authorization, ashes that do not fit the chosen urn come back in a separate container, and shipped remains must travel by a traceable method with a signed receipt. Questions for an Illinois Provider

What to settle before signing in Illinois:

  • Ask an Illinois funeral home for its General Price List before signing. Its direct cremation entry has to spell out what is included, which should be the transfer of the body, the cremation and a simple urn or box for the ashes.
  • A casket is not needed for an Illinois direct cremation: the provider has to accept an unfinished wood box or a cardboard-type container instead (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(b)).
  • Before signing, ask the Illinois provider when the ashes will be ready, how they are returned, and whether a certified copy of the death certificate is in the price.
In Illinois you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Illinois bring a container, and the IL Comptroller Funeral & Cemetery Oversight is who you call if a home in Illinois refuses it.

Lowering an Illinois Cremation Bill

Ways to pay less in Illinois:

  • Get several General Price Lists. Illinois funeral homes must give them on request, so line up the direct cremation entries side by side.
  • Ask for direct cremation alone. A package may add items you do not need; under the FTC Funeral Rule you may pick items one by one, apart from the basic services fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the price of cremation in Illinois?

On our estimates an Illinois cremation runs from about $2,000 for direct cremation to about $6,600 for a full funeral followed by cremation.

What cremation services can I buy in Illinois?

Direct cremation, about $2,000 in Illinois, has no service or viewing: cremation follows soon after death and the family collects the urn. A memorial can follow at any time. Cremation after a full funeral service, about $6,600, costs $4,600 more.

What does Illinois law require before cremation?

Put simply, no Illinois cremation may take place until 24 hours after death, and before it does the crematory has to see the filed death certificate, the coroner's or medical examiner's permit and the authorizing agent's signed form. The section on Illinois rules further down gives the statute, explains why a licensed funeral director is involved, and lists where to complain.

Where may ashes be scattered in Illinois?

On private land, yes, with the owner's permission. Land run by the Illinois Department of Natural Resources is different: 17 Ill. Adm. Code 110.105 requires a scattering permit, applied for at least 14 days in advance. The rule keeps scattering out of sight of public use areas, at least 200 feet from any body of water, and at least 100 feet from archaeological sites, nature preserves and State Historic Sites, and it bars leaving urns, markers or other items behind.

The Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (217-524-1981) licenses crematories and handles complaints about them.

How common is cremation in Illinois?

Not quite. We estimate the Illinois rate at about 52%, compared with NFDA’s 63.4% national projection for 2025. Price is the main pull for families who pick it: direct cremation runs about $2,000 in Illinois, while a funeral service with burial averages near $8,500 before the plot is bought. State law also requires a 24-hour wait and a permit from the county coroner or medical examiner (410 ILCS 18/35).

How long does an Illinois cremation take?

Illinois law sets the first step: no cremation within 24 hours of death, and a coroner's or medical examiner's permit is needed (410 ILCS 18/35). After that, timing depends on the paperwork and the crematory's schedule; in Cook County the permit can be requested online at any hour. Direct cremation, at about $2,000, has no service to schedule around.

Can there be a funeral before an Illinois cremation?

Yes. Expect about $6,600 in Illinois for a service with the body present followed by cremation, within a typical $5,280 to $8,580, against about $2,000 for direct cremation.

Because Illinois bars cremation within 24 hours of death, a wake the evening before and a service the next morning fit the law without delay. An Illinois crematory cannot hold an unembalmed body it cannot cremate within 24 hours unless it has refrigeration below 40°F (410 ILCS 18/35(p)), so ask whether the funeral home will refrigerate or embalm before a later service.

Cook County's $150 cremation permit

If someone dies in Cook County, the funeral director must get a cremation permit from the Medical Examiner's Office before the death certificate can be filed. The county charges $150 for the permit, and funeral directors can request it online at any hour, including weekends and holidays (Cook County Medical Examiner; checked October 29, 2026).

When comparing quotes in the Chicago area, ask whether the $150 Cook County cremation permit is built into the price or will appear later as a cash advance, and whether certified death certificates are counted the same way.

Illinois cremation rules and DHS help

Illinois crematories and funeral homes are licensed by the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (217-524-1981), which also takes complaints.

Illinois is one of the nine states on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list: under 410 ILCS 535 a licensed funeral director files the death certificate, moves the body, attends the cremation and obtains the permits, so even a $2,000 direct cremation runs through a licensee.

Cremation cannot happen within 24 hours of death, and it needs a county coroner's or medical examiner's permit and the authorizing agent's signature (410 ILCS 18/35).

DHS funeral and burial benefit

For an eligible recipient, Illinois DHS pays up to $686 toward the cremation and up to $1,370 toward the funeral home's services, $2,056 in all, if the claim is filed within 180 days of death (Illinois DHS). Check that the crematory's charge is billed under the $686 portion.

Veterans

Illinois veterans who qualify may be inurned at Abraham Lincoln (815-423-9958), Camp Butler (217-492-4070) or Danville (217-554-4550) National Cemetery free of charge; see cem.va.gov.

Illinois to-do list

  1. Get transport, container and crematory charges listed separately.
  2. Build the 24-hour wait into travel and memorial plans.
  3. In Cook County, ask whether the $150 permit is in the price.
  4. Report misstated prices to 217-524-1981.
