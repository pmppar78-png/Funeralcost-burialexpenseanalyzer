What to Know Before Choosing Cremation in Illinois

When considering cremation in Illinois, keep these important points in mind:

  • Get the Illinois provider's General Price List before you agree to anything; the direct cremation line must state what it includes, and the price should cover transport, the cremation itself and a basic container for the ashes.
  • An Illinois provider offering direct cremation must let you use an unfinished wood box or other alternative container instead of a casket (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(b)).
  • Before signing, ask the Illinois provider when the ashes will be ready, how they are returned, and whether a certified copy of the death certificate is in the price.
In Illinois you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Illinois bring a container, and the IL Comptroller Funeral & Cemetery Oversight is who you call if a home in Illinois refuses it.

How to Save on Cremation in Illinois

Cremation costs in Illinois can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Illinois: Request written General Price Lists.
  • Price direct cremation as standalone: Illinois funeral homes often bundle direct cremation into broader packages.

Regional Context for Cremation in Illinois

Cremation adoption in Illinois has crossed 52% and continues to rise, driven largely by cost — direct cremation at $2,000 saves Midwestern families roughly $6,500 compared to traditional burial. Independent cremation societies and direct cremation providers are now common in and around Chicago, and their pricing typically undercuts full-service funeral homes offering the same outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Illinois?

Cremation costs in Illinois range from $2,000 for direct cremation to $6,600 for a full funeral service followed by cremation.

Which cremation options exist in Illinois?

Illinois providers typically offer three tiers. Direct cremation at $2,000 is the simplest Illinois tier: no service or viewing, the body cremated and the ashes returned. Traditional cremation at $6,600 adds a full funeral home service before the cremation, $4,600 more than the direct tier in Illinois.

Does Illinois have specific rules for cremation?

In short, Illinois sets a 24-hour minimum before cremation, and the crematory also needs the death certificate, a cremation permit and a signed authorization from the next of kin or designated agent. For the exact Illinois citation, the funeral-director question and who takes complaints, see the cremation rules and help section lower on this page.

Can I scatter ashes in Illinois?

On private land, yes, with the owner's permission. Land run by the Illinois Department of Natural Resources is different: 17 Ill. Adm. Code 110.105 requires a scattering permit, applied for at least 14 days in advance. The rule keeps scattering out of sight of public use areas, at least 200 feet from any body of water, and at least 100 feet from archaeological sites, nature preserves and State Historic Sites, and it bars leaving urns, markers or other items behind.

The Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (217-524-1981) licenses crematories and handles complaints about them.

Is cremation as common in Illinois as it is nationally?

Not quite. We estimate the Illinois rate at about 52%, compared with NFDA’s 63.4% national projection for 2025. Price is the main pull for families who pick it: direct cremation runs about $2,000 in Illinois, while a funeral service with burial averages near $8,500 before the plot is bought. State law also requires a 24-hour wait and a permit from the county coroner or medical examiner (410 ILCS 18/35).

How quickly can cremation be completed in Illinois?

From start to ashes, most cremations in Illinois take 4 to 10 days. Direct cremation at $2,000 moves fastest: with no viewing or service to schedule, the only wait is the 24-hour wait Illinois requires after death (410 ILCS 18/35).

Can I hold a funeral service before cremation in Illinois?

Yes. Expect about $6,600 in Illinois for a service with the body present followed by cremation, within a typical $5,280 to $8,580, against about $2,000 for direct cremation.

Because Illinois bars cremation within 24 hours of death, a wake the evening before and a service the next morning fit the law without delay. An Illinois crematory cannot hold an unembalmed body it cannot cremate within 24 hours unless it has refrigeration below 40°F (410 ILCS 18/35(p)), so ask whether the funeral home will refrigerate or embalm before a later service.

Cremation rules and help in Illinois

The licensing office for cremation providers in Illinois is the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation; its number is 217-524-1981.

For a cremation in Illinois, state law (410 ILCS 535) reserves filing the death certificate, moving the body, being present at burial or cremation, and obtaining permits for a licensed funeral director, according to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, so even the $2,000 direct cremation in the table runs through a licensee.

What the law says about timing: Illinois bars cremation within 24 hours of death and requires a cremation permit from the county coroner or medical examiner plus a signed authorization form (410 ILCS 18/35).

For an eligible recipient, Illinois DHS puts up to $686 toward the cremation itself and up to $1,370 toward the funeral home's services, $2,056 in all, if the claim is filed within 180 days of death. (Source: Illinois DHS.)

Cremated remains of an eligible Illinois veteran can be placed in a VA national cemetery columbarium or in-ground site at no charge for the space. Options in Illinois include Abraham Lincoln National Cemetery (815-423-9958), Camp Butler National Cemetery (217-492-4070), and Danville National Cemetery (217-554-4550). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Illinois

  1. Ask Illinois cremation providers to itemize transport, the container and the crematory fee before you choose one.
  2. Build the 24-hour wait set by 410 ILCS 18/35 into any Illinois travel or memorial plans.
  3. Make sure the Illinois crematory's charge is billed under the $686 burial portion of the DHS claim.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 217-524-1981.
