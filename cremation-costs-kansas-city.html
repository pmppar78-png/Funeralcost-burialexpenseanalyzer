Cremation Costs in Kansas City, Missouri (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Missouri Cremation Rate: 52%

Kansas City splits the difference on the state line, and that's more than a geographic curiosity: it changes the paperwork. A family living in Overland Park or Prairie Village may use a Missouri funeral home, ship ashes to a Kansas plot, or route the cremation permit through the Jackson County Medical Examiner even though the death occurred in Johnson County, Kansas. Mount Washington Cemetery runs one of the metro's oldest crematories on a 300-acre campus in Independence. D.W. Newcomer's Sons operates the 1893 original funeral home on Main Street, Freeman Mortuary serves the east side, and Passantino Bros. handles much of the Italian-Catholic community in the Northland.

National context: the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA 2024 Annual Report put the U.S. cremation rate near 60.5 percent, and Kansas City providers operate under the oversight of the Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors. Families ordering death certificates typically deal with the Kansas City Missouri Health Department Vital Records office and Jackson County Recorder of Deeds.

Two state lines, two rule books

Kansas City metro families regularly deal with both Missouri and Kansas funeral law on the same arrangement. Missouri requires no statutory waiting period before cremation but does require a coroner or medical examiner permit. Kansas imposes a 48-hour wait under KSA 65-1768 unless the state Board of Mortuary Arts grants a waiver. A death in Overland Park that moves to a Missouri-side funeral home still requires Kansas death-certificate registration; conversely, a Jackson County death routed to a Johnson County crematory must satisfy Kansas's 48-hour rule. Providers licensed in both states — most metro chains are — handle the dual paperwork transparently, but solo-state operators can add days to the timeline for cross-border cases.

Mount Washington, Forest Hill, and historic cremation infrastructure

Mount Washington Cemetery's crematory has operated since 1901 and serves as a contract cremation facility for multiple independent funeral homes in the metro. Forest Hill Calvary Cemetery on Troost Avenue, operated jointly with Resurrection Cemetery in Johnson County, includes a crematory and columbarium complex. Mount Olivet Cemetery and Mausoleum in Kansas City, Missouri, adds another historic option. On the Kansas side, Johnson County Funeral Chapel operates a cremation-dedicated campus in Overland Park. The Kansas City market is unusual in the Midwest for the number of cemetery-affiliated crematories, which can slightly lower pricing for families willing to use the cemetery's paired funeral chapel.

Scattering along the rivers and at Fort Leavenworth National

The Missouri and Kansas rivers border and run through the metro and are commonly used for scattering; neither state requires a specific permit for river scattering, though courtesy suggests well away from water intakes and public docks. Missouri State Parks — Watkins Mill, Fleming Park at Lake Jacomo — require written permission from the park superintendent. Kansas State Parks policy is similar. Fort Leavenworth National Cemetery provides free inurnment for eligible veterans and spouses, and Leavenworth National Cemetery is a common choice for military families across the metro regardless of which side of the state line they live on. EPA ocean-scattering rules do not apply to the region's rivers.

Bistate money-saving tactics

  • Compare prices on both sides of State Line Road. Direct cremation pricing in Overland Park and Leawood often runs $200 to $500 below equivalent Missouri-side providers, despite identical services.
  • Cremation Society of Kansas & Missouri operates a flat-rate direct-cremation package below $1,500 with locations serving both states, a consolidated alternative to negotiating piece-by-piece with full-service chains.
  • The Missouri Funeral Directors and Embalmers Board at pr.mo.gov/embalmers licenses providers; the Kansas State Board of Mortuary Arts at ksbma.ks.gov handles Kansas-side complaints. Both offer free license lookups.
  • Veterans qualify for Leavenworth National Cemetery or the Missouri Veterans Cemetery in Higginsville, both offering free inurnment and a government-issued niche marker.
Cost-Saving Tip: You do not need to purchase an urn from the cremation provider. The FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to use any urn or container, including ones purchased independently. See our urn buying guide.

Questions Worth Asking First

How much does cremation cost in Kansas City?

Direct cremation in the Kansas City metro averages $1,760, about $160 above the Missouri statewide figure of $1,600. Full-service cremation with viewing and ceremony averages $6,050. Kansas-side providers in Overland Park, Leawood, and Prairie Village often price direct cremation several hundred dollars below Missouri-side equivalents. The Cremation Society of Kansas & Missouri publishes flat rates under $1,500 for direct cremation including both states' required permits.

What types of cremation are available in Kansas City?

Kansas City families can choose from: direct cremation ($1,760) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service — a ceremony held after cremation; and traditional cremation ($6,050) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Kansas City?

Yes. Burial at Mount Washington, Mount Olivet, Forest Hill Calvary, or Resurrection Cemetery runs $8,000 to $12,000 once plot, vault, and marker are added. Direct cremation at $1,760 undercuts that by at least $6,000, and cremation-with-service at $6,050 still comes in below a full burial package. Veterans qualify for free interment or inurnment at Fort Leavenworth National Cemetery or the Missouri Veterans Cemetery in Higginsville.

Where can I compare cremation prices in Kansas City?

Call three or four Kansas City cremation providers and ask specifically for their General Price List, which the FTC Funeral Rule entitles you to receive. Look at the direct-cremation figure and the separately listed basic-services fee. Resources like Parting.com and Funeral Consumers Alliance can round out your comparison with reviews and price ranges from other Kansas City families.

Can I scatter ashes in Kansas City?

Private property scattering requires the landowner's consent on either side of the state line. Scattering in the Missouri or Kansas River is not specifically regulated but should be done away from drinking-water intakes and municipal infrastructure. Watkins Mill State Park, Fleming Park, and the Kansas State Parks all require written permission from the park superintendent. Fort Leavenworth National Cemetery columbarium inurnment is free for eligible veterans. EPA ocean-scattering rules do not apply to Missouri-Kansas waterways.
