Funeral Costs in Arkansas: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: South

Arkansas runs among the cheapest funeral markets in the country, with a traditional-service average of $6,100 — roughly 22% below the national figure. The Roller Funeral Homes chain dominates Northwest Arkansas and the Little Rock metro, Ruebel Funeral Home anchors central Little Rock, and a wide scattering of family-owned providers covers the Delta, the Ozarks, and the Ouachitas. The Arkansas Funeral Director Co-Owner Rule

Arkansas is one of a shrinking number of states that requires a licensed funeral director to be a co-owner of any funeral establishment. The Arkansas Code and the rules of the State Board of Embalmers, Funeral Directors, Cemeteries and Burial Services keep corporate ownership at arm's length — a national chain cannot simply buy and operate an Arkansas funeral home without a licensed Arkansas funeral director holding an ownership stake. What that means for your bill:

  • Fewer corporate chain locations. Arkansas has lower concentration of SCI-branded (Dignity Memorial) providers than neighboring states.
  • More independent family-owned homes. Prices are typically more negotiable at independents; package structure is less rigid.
  • Local pricing knowledge matters. A small-town Arkansas funeral home may charge 25-40% under a Little Rock or Fayetteville urban provider for equivalent services.

Ozarks and Delta — Two Different Arkansas Markets

Pricing variance across Arkansas is driven less by metro-rural lines and more by region. The Ozark counties in the northwest (Benton, Washington, Carroll, Madison) and the Delta counties in the east (Phillips, Desha, Chicot) price very differently even for identical services.

Arkansas Invoice Line Items to Question

  • Casket handling fee — Banned under the FTC Funeral Rule since 1994. Arkansas providers know this; watch for it anyway.
  • "Required" embalming — Arkansas does not require it in standard cases. Refrigeration is legal. Know your rights
  • Cemetery perpetual care — Typically $200-$800 at commercial cemeteries; free at most church and family plots.
  • Vault requirement — Not Arkansas state law; a cemetery-contract item.
  • Certified death certificates — $12 per copy in Arkansas through the Department of Health.
  • Weekend or after-hours service — 15-25% surcharge common.
  • Package markup — Itemize. What funeral homes don't tell you

FTC Funeral Rule and the Arkansas State Board

Both federal and state rules protect Arkansas families The FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453, amended 2022) applies in Arkansas as in every state: GPL on demand, no required bundling, third-party casket acceptance with no handling fee, no false legal claims, written itemized statement before work. The Arkansas State Board of Embalmers, Funeral Directors, Cemeteries and Burial Services regulates licensees, cemeteries, and pre-need sales — a broader remit than most state boards.

Pricing complaints go to the FTC. Professional conduct, cemetery, and pre-need complaints go to the Arkansas State Board in Little Rock. Systemic issues can be reported to the Funeral Consumers Alliance.

Key actions most families skip

Families arranging a funeral in Arkansas for the first time often move faster than they need to. The following sequence slows things down just enough to compare options without adding undue delay:

  1. Take a breath. Unless there are legal or medical time constraints, you typically have 24–72 hours before decisions must be finalized.
  2. Request General Price Lists from at least 2–3 funeral homes in your area of Arkansas. They are legally required to provide them. Questions to ask funeral homes
  3. Decide on disposition: cremation in Arkansas or burial in Arkansas. This is the single biggest cost decision.
  4. Choose only the services you need. Embalming, premium caskets, and elaborate arrangements are optional. What funeral homes don't tell you
  5. Explore payment assistance if cost is a concern: Social Security benefits, veteran benefits, Medicaid assistance, or crowdfunding.

A Few Questions We Keep Getting

What is the average price of a funeral in Arkansas right now?

Arkansas traditional funerals average $6,100 — about 22% below the $7,848 national median, making Arkansas one of the cheapest funeral markets in the country. Cremation with service runs $5,000; direct cremation starts at $1,500. A cemetery plot averages $2,800. Providers like Roller Funeral Homes and Ruebel in Little Rock anchor the Pulaski County market; rural Ozark and Delta providers often price 25-40% below that.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Arkansas?

Substantially. Direct cremation at $1,500 versus a $6,100 traditional funeral plus a $2,800 plot is roughly a $7,400 gap. Arkansas's 42% cremation rate is well below the national figure of around 60%, largely because the state's rural-church burial tradition still anchors many arrangements. That leaves room — direct cremation providers in Little Rock and Fayetteville tend to price competitively.

What rights do Arkansas families have when arranging a funeral?

The FTC Funeral Rule gives you an itemized GPL on demand, the right to refuse package bundling, the right to bring your own casket or urn without a handling surcharge, and protection from false legal claims. The Arkansas State Board of Embalmers, Funeral Directors, Cemeteries and Burial Services handles licensee conduct, pre-need sales, and cemetery oversight — a broader state-level remit than most. Arkansas's requirement that a licensed funeral director be a co-owner of any funeral establishment is also an unusual consumer protection.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Arkansas?

Compare GPLs from three Arkansas providers — Roller, Ruebel, and a small-town independent typically span the price range. Consider driving to a neighboring county; Ozark and Delta providers often run $1,000-$2,000 below Little Rock on equivalent services. Choose direct cremation at $1,500 if cost is decisive. Hold the service at your church rather than the funeral home's chapel. Stack VA burial benefits (Little Rock National Cemetery is free for eligible veterans), Social Security's $255, and county indigent programs where applicable.

Does Arkansas require embalming?

No. Arkansas doesn't mandate embalming in standard circumstances; refrigeration is a legal alternative. Some providers default to embalming on arrangement sheets, especially for open-casket viewings, but that's a provider choice. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, no Arkansas funeral home can claim embalming is legally required without citing the specific statute — and there is no such general statute.

Is Arkansas cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Arkansas is firmly in the cheap quartile — 22% below the $7,848 US median at $6,100. Only a handful of Deep South states and some rural Plains states price lower. The state's overall cost of living, high proportion of independent family-owned funeral homes, and active rural-church and family-cemetery traditions all hold pricing down.

Which funeral option costs the least in Arkansas?

Direct cremation at $1,500 is the floor. It covers transport, cremation, and return of the remains — no viewing, no ceremony, no embalming. Direct burial at around $3,050 is next, which skips the ceremony but still includes basic graveside interment.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Arkansas?

Pull GPLs from three Arkansas providers — Roller Chenal or Roller-Drummond in Little Rock, Ruebel, and a small-town independent. Check the Funeral Consumers Alliance of Arkansas for vetted recommendations. Because Arkansas requires funeral-director ownership, many of the cheapest options are independents without chain overhead. Ask every provider for their simplest package; Arkansas funeral homes are generally willing to quote a basic services tier.
