Help With Arkansas Funeral Costs

  • County courts handle pauper burials, and nobody is buried at public expense until next of kin have been searched for and veteran status checked (Ark. Code Ann. § 20-17-702).
  • Social Security's $255 is not automatic: a surviving spouse or eligible child has to ask for it (1-800-772-1213) before two years pass.
  • Paid for an Arkansas veteran's funeral? If the death came on October 1, 2026 or later and was not service-connected, VA Form 21P-530EZ can return as much as $1,032 for the burial and $1,032 more for the plot (eligibility is in our veteran burial benefits guide).
  • Arkansas families short of money can look at Medicaid, church and charity help in our payment assistance guide, and at the cheapest routes in the no-money funeral guide.

Arkansas Consumer Rights

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Arkansas must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. In Arkansas, funeral homes answer to the State Board of Embalmers, Funeral Directors, Cemeteries, and Burial Services inside the Arkansas Insurance Department. Act 788 of 2017 created it by abolishing the old embalmers board, the Burial Association Board and the Arkansas Cemetery Board.

If you believe a funeral home in Arkansas has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. The department's Funeral Services Division also handles prepaid funeral benefits (501-371-2683) and perpetual care cemeteries (501-371-2646), so a question about a preneed contract or a cemetery can go to the same place.

Arkansas Price Differences

A few things drive Arkansas prices.

Low living costs keep Arkansas prices down overall, but competition varies: a town with one funeral home has no price pressure. Burial is still more common than cremation, at a 42% cremation rate, which gives cremation providers less reason to compete on price.

Arkansas Planning Steps

Arkansas families planning a first funeral often hurry more than they need to.

  1. Decide on burial or cremation: cremation in Arkansas or burial in Arkansas.

Arkansas death certificates: $10 and $8

The Arkansas Department of Health sells certified death certificates at $10 for one copy, with each further copy in the same order at $8. If no record is found, the department keeps $10 as a search fee (Arkansas Department of Health, ordering death records; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Online: a $5 processing fee and a $1.85 identity-verification fee are added. Orders usually take 7 to 14 business days, plus shipping.
  • In person: same-day service at 4815 West Markham Street in Little Rock, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays. Bring photo ID, which is required for every order.
  • Filing: the funeral director files the certificate with Vital Records within 10 days of death (Ark. Code Ann. § 20-18-601) and will often order the family's first copies along with it; ask what the home adds for that service.
  • Questions: Arkansas Vital Records answers at 866-209-9482.

Banks, life insurers, pension plans and the revenue office for vehicle titles commonly ask for a certified copy; some will return it or accept a photocopy, so ask before ordering. Five copies in one order cost $42, $10 plus four at $8, so it is cheaper to order enough the first time than to come back for more.

Arkansas's funeral regulator, county burials and veterans cemeteries

The Arkansas Insurance Department's Funeral Services Division licenses funeral homes; call 501-682-0574 about a home that won't show prices or pressures you.

Families in Arkansas may handle a death without a funeral director for most steps, since the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count Arkansas among its nine restrictive states; filing the death certificate and getting a permit are still required. A cremation can proceed once the permit is issued; Arkansas sets no minimum wait (17 CAR § 30-205).

Pauper burials

Arkansas has no statewide funeral payment. Pauper burials are under the county court, headed by the county judge, and before an unclaimed body is buried at public expense, whoever holds it must search for next of kin, including checking whether the person was a veteran (Ark. Code Ann. § 20-17-702). A spouse or child, not the funeral home, claims the $255 Social Security death payment.

The 48-hour rule

Arkansas law does not require embalming, but a body that is not buried within 48 hours of death must be embalmed or refrigerated, and neither is needed when cremation happens within 48 hours (Ark. Code § 20-17-107). Families who want a viewing several days later should expect to pay for one or the other.

Paperwork the Funeral Rule requires

Beyond the General Price List, an Arkansas funeral home has to show separate casket and outer burial container price lists before you choose those items, and once arrangements are made it must give you an itemized statement of the goods and services you selected. A home that sells direct cremation must offer an unfinished wood box or other alternative container, so no casket has to be bought for a cremation.

Veterans

Fayetteville National Cemetery (479-442-2566) and Fort Smith National Cemetery (479-783-5345) take reservations from funeral directors via the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office at 800-535-1117. The state veterans cemeteries at Birdeye (870-588-4608) and North Little Rock (501-683-2259) take requests directly.

Arkansas checklist

  1. Ask for itemized prices by phone or in person; you pay only for what you choose.
  2. Ask whether embalming is really needed; burial or cremation within 48 hours avoids it.
  3. Without money for a funeral, start with the county judge's office.
  4. Veterans may qualify for Fort Smith or Fayetteville national cemeteries at no plot cost.
  5. Price-list disputes go to the Funeral Services Division, 501-682-0574.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an Arkansas funeral cost?

On our 2026 model, a traditional Arkansas funeral costs about $6,100, 27% under the $8,300 national median. A cremation with a service is near $5,000, direct cremation starts at about $1,500, and burial adds roughly $2,800 in cemetery charges.

How much does cremation save in Arkansas?

About $4,600 before cemetery costs. Direct cremation, about $1,500, costs $4,600 less than a $6,100 traditional funeral and roughly $7,400 less once about $2,800 of cemetery costs are added.

What rights do I have with an Arkansas funeral home?

Federal rules require written itemized prices, honest answers to phone price inquiries, and a home must take a casket you supply without charging extra. According to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, Arkansas doesn't require families to hire a funeral director. Complaints go to the Arkansas Insurance Department's Funeral Services Division, 501-682-0574.

How can I lower an Arkansas funeral bill?

Collect several price lists, turn down extras in any package, and if cost decides it, direct cremation runs about $1,500. Burying or cremating within 48 hours also avoids embalming or refrigeration charges.

Is embalming required in Arkansas?

No, not by itself. Ark. Code § 20-17-107 says a body does not have to be embalmed, but one that is not buried within 48 hours of death must be embalmed or refrigerated, and neither is needed if cremation happens within 48 hours. The Funeral Consumers Alliance notes that Arkansas expects embalming when a body is shipped out of state, so ask about this if burial will be elsewhere.

On this page's estimate, embalming in Arkansas costs about $610. A funeral home must get your permission before charging for it (16 CFR 453.5).

Are Arkansas funerals cheaper than average?

Yes, on our figures. A traditional Arkansas funeral is about $6,100, well under the NFDA 2023 national median of $8,300 with viewing and burial, and even a cremation with a service, about $5,000, is below the $6,280 national cremation median. Small-town homes with no nearby competitor can still charge more, so ask for written prices.

What's the cheapest option in Arkansas?

Direct cremation, from about $1,500. Direct burial is the next step up at about $3,050, which is $3,050 below a traditional Arkansas funeral.

Which Arkansas funeral homes charge less?

Phone several. The FTC Funeral Rule makes Arkansas homes give prices by phone, and a casket or urn bought from another seller must be accepted with no added fee.
