Cremation Costs in Memphis, Tennessee (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Tennessee Cremation Rate: 42%

Memphis is a Shelby County cremation market defined by two storied providers and one big underlying dynamic: the Mississippi Delta funeral tradition. R.S. Lewis & Sons, in operation on Vance Avenue since 1937, is the longest-running Black-owned funeral home in the city, and Memorial Park Funeral Home anchors the East Memphis chapel-and-cemetery model. Direct cremation runs about $1,680 — $80 above the Tennessee state median — largely because Memphis providers carry embalming overhead built around traditional service expectations.

Tennessee's Cemetery Act and What It Means for Your Urn

Tennessee's Cemetery Act (Title 46 of the state code) governs how cremated remains are interred at licensed cemeteries — pre-need trust fund requirements, perpetual care obligations, and niche purchase contracts all fall under the Act's scope. Memorial Park in East Memphis and Forest Hill both operate columbaria and scatter gardens compliant with Title 46, meaning families paying for inurnment can verify the perpetual-care funding before signing. This matters more than it sounds: roughly one in eight small cemeteries in Tennessee have faced Department of Commerce and Insurance enforcement action for trust-fund shortfalls, and the Act is the recourse structure.

R.S. Lewis and the Memphis Legacy Funeral Market

R.S. Lewis & Sons — the oldest Black-owned funeral home in Memphis — shaped how Shelby County families experience the full-service cremation format. Their direct cremation price is published on request as required by the Funeral Rule, and it sits near the Memphis median. What Lewis has historically done differently is bundle visitation-and-service around cremation rather than treat cremation as an economy alternative; families wanting a Saturday chapel service followed by cremation land closer to the $5,670 full-service figure than the $1,680 direct price.

TennCare, Shelby County DHS, and Indigent Cremation

Tennessee's Department of Human Services administers a narrow indigent funeral benefit, and Shelby County supplements it case by case. The combined benefit rarely exceeds $1,200 toward direct cremation, meaning Memphis families still carry roughly $500 out of pocket after county assistance is applied. Both R.S. Lewis and Memorial Park accept DHS vouchers and bill directly; several of the smaller North Memphis and Whitehaven houses require family payment up front and wait for reimbursement.

Mississippi River Scattering: What Actually Works

Families often ask about scattering on the Mississippi near Memphis. EPA burial-at-sea rules technically apply only in marine waters, so river scattering falls under Tennessee ordinance and the Mississippi River Commission's navigational authority. In practice, small charter boats operating out of the Memphis riverfront handle family scattering runs without permit friction. Shelby Farms Park and Overton Park both allow scattering with written permission from the park authority.

Common Questions

How much does cremation cost in Memphis?

Memphis direct cremation averages $1,680 and full-service cremation with chapel visitation averages $5,670. The Memphis figure runs $80 above the Tennessee state average because Shelby County providers carry embalming overhead built around traditional service expectations, even on the direct-cremation line. Urn, memorial venue, and scattering fees add separately.

What types of cremation are available in Memphis?

All three standard paths are available through R.S. Lewis & Sons, Memorial Park, Forest Hill, and the smaller Whitehaven and Orange Mound houses. Direct cremation at $1,680 is the simplest. The mid-tier format pairs cremation with a memorial service held at the family's church or a chapel after the retort. Full-service cremation at $5,670 includes visitation, Saturday chapel time, and the ceremonial items most Shelby County families associate with a traditional funeral.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Memphis?

Yes. Direct cremation at $1,680 lands roughly $5,355 below a traditional Memphis funeral with full burial once you count the Memorial Park or Forest Hill plot, vault, and monument. Memphis cemetery plot pricing is not as extreme as the coasts, but the gap still holds — and cremation with a full service at $5,670 sits meaningfully below the traditional burial number.

Where can I compare cremation prices in Memphis?

The FTC Funeral Rule requires every Memphis provider to hand over a General Price List on request, in person or by phone. A useful comparison set for Shelby County includes R.S. Lewis & Sons, Memorial Park, Forest Hill, and one of the independent cremation-only operators. The Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers maintains the licensing roster, and the Funeral Consumers Alliance occasionally publishes member price snapshots.

Can I scatter ashes in Memphis?

Private land with owner permission is straightforward. Mississippi River scattering falls under Tennessee ordinance and the Mississippi River Commission's navigational authority — small charter boats operating out of the Memphis riverfront handle family scattering runs without permit friction. Shelby Farms Park and Overton Park both allow scattering with written permission from the park authority, and Memorial Park operates a dedicated scatter garden for families who prefer a Tennessee Cemetery Act-regulated location.
