Infant and Child Funeral Costs: A Gentle Guide for Families

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 24, 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Government Sources Editorial: Our Standards

If you are here because your baby or child has died, we are deeply sorry. This page is short on purpose. It covers the few things that can protect you from unexpected costs, and where to ask for help, so you can spend your energy on your family.

When you call a funeral home

  • Ask directly whether they reduce or waive fees for infants or children. Some funeral homes reduce or waive their own fee for an infant. We do not have a national count of how many, so ask, and get the answer on the price list.
  • Ask for the general price list. The FTC Funeral Rule gives you the right to itemized prices and to buy only what you choose (Funeral Consumers Alliance: FTC Funeral Rule).
  • You don't have to decide in one call. It's okay to ask a friend or relative to make the calls and compare prices for you.

Rights that matter here

  • You can supply your own casket or urn, and the funeral home cannot charge you a fee for using it (Funeral Consumers Alliance: FTC Funeral Rule).
  • No state law requires a casket for cremation; a funeral home offering cremation must make an alternative container available (Funeral Consumers Alliance: FTC Funeral Rule).
  • You'll receive an itemized written statement before you pay, listing anything required by law or by the cemetery (FTC: Funeral Costs and Pricing Checklist).

Where to ask for help

  • The hospital: social workers and chaplains often know local funeral homes, charities, and options such as hospital-arranged cremation.
  • The TEARS Foundation: a nonprofit that helps families with burial or cremation costs after the loss of a baby; check its application page for current eligibility and amounts.
  • State and county programs: some states and counties help with burial costs for low-income families. Ask your county human services office, or see our payment assistance guide.
  • Family and community: faith communities often help, and a fundraiser can cover gaps (see crowdfunding for funeral costs).

Support for you

Grief after losing a child is its own kind of grief. Our grief resources page lists free support groups and helplines. If you're having thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) any time.

Decisions that can wait, and the ones that should be in writing

If you are reading the infant and child funeral guide after a death, you do not need a long article. The paragraphs above already point you to the Funeral Rule, the hospital, and the TEARS Foundation. What follows is the detail families ask for once the first call is over. None of it is a claim that a certain percentage of funeral homes waive fees. Some homes reduce or waive their professional charge for an infant. We do not have a national count of how many. Ask, and ask for the answer on the price list.

Papers that are easy to forget

A hospital usually starts the fetal death report or the death certificate, depending on the length of the pregnancy and the state's form. The threshold for which form is used is state law, not a single U.S. rule. The funeral director often files it, but you can ask the hospital clerk who has the form today. You will need certified copies later for insurance that actually covered the child, and sometimes for leave from work. Order a few copies from the vital-records office. The fee is a state fee, printed on that office's site, not a funeral charge.

Social Security's $255 lump-sum death payment is for a surviving spouse or for a child who was already receiving benefits on the worker's record. It is not a general infant-funeral benefit. If someone tells you Social Security "pays for the baby," ask them to show the SSA page. The form, when a survivor is eligible, is SSA-8.

What to ask the funeral home, in one conversation

  1. If we choose direct cremation, what is the total, and is an alternative container included? The Funeral Rule requires an alternative container to be available. You do not have to buy a casket.
  2. If we want a tiny casket or an urn we already have, will you accept it with no handling fee? The Rule says yes.
  3. Is any infant fee reduction already on the General Price List, or is it a courtesy you are offering today? If it is a courtesy, ask them to write it on the statement.
  4. May we have a short visitation in a room that is not the main chapel, and what is that room's fee if we want it?
  5. What is due today, and what can a relative pay tomorrow?

People who will make the calls for you

Hand a friend the five questions and the funeral home's phone number. You do not have to be the person who hears the prices. Hospital social workers and chaplains already know which local homes will talk about an infant without a sales pitch. The TEARS Foundation's application page, linked above, is the charity we name because it publishes an application rather than a vague promise. Faith communities and our payment-assistance guide are the other doors. A fundraiser is optional; it is described on the crowdfunding page, and it is not required to have a dignified goodbye.

Keepsakes and photos

Hospitals often offer photographs, footprints, or a lock of hair through their own bereavement program. You can say no. You can also say yes later if they will store them. Independent remembrance-photography charities exist in many cities. The hospital social worker is the person who knows which one actually returns calls locally. We do not rank them.

There is no NFDA median for an infant funeral. The adult medians ($8,300 and $6,280 in the 2023 study) are the wrong comparison, and we will not print them here as if they were a child's bill. Your number is the one on the statement in front of you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do funeral homes charge less for a baby or child?

Some reduce or waive their professional fee for an infant or child. There is no national survey we can cite for how many, so ask directly and get it on the itemized price list.

Can we use our own casket or urn?

Yes. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, a funeral home must accept a casket or urn you buy elsewhere and cannot charge a fee for using it.

Is there financial help for infant funerals?

Possibly. Hospital social workers, the TEARS Foundation, and some state or county programs help with costs. Check each one's current eligibility.
