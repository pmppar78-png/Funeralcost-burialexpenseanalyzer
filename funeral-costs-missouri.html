Funeral Costs in Missouri: What Families Pay in 2026

Reviewed: February 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Missouri Funeral Board Region: Midwest

Missouri is a state of striking contrasts, and its funeral market reflects that duality. Kansas City and St. Louis anchor opposite ends of the state as major metropolitan areas with diverse populations, corporate funeral chain presence, and higher costs. Between them stretches the rural Ozarks and the Missouri River corridor, where small-town funeral homes have served families for generations at a fraction of metro prices. Overseen by the Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors, the state's funeral industry is shaped by a rich tapestry of religious traditions, a significant military community tied to Whiteman Air Force Base and Fort Leonard Wood, and a moderate cremation rate that reflects Missouri's position as a cultural bridge between the Midwest and the South.

Funeral Cost Breakdown in Missouri

Missouri's funeral costs fall below the national median of $7,848, reflecting the state's affordable cost of living outside its two major metro areas. Kansas City and St. Louis both see costs 15-20% above the state average, driven by higher overhead, greater corporate funeral chain presence, and the broader range of premium service options available in urban markets. The Ozarks and rural Missouri, by contrast, feature some of the most affordable funeral services in the country, with many towns served by a single, deeply rooted family funeral home. Missouri's 52% cremation rate, right at the national average, reflects a state evenly divided between traditional and contemporary approaches.

What Drives Funeral Costs in Missouri

Two-metro market structure. Missouri's funeral market is essentially three distinct zones: the Kansas City metro on the western border, the St. Louis metro on the eastern border, and the vast rural expanse between them. Each metro has its own competitive dynamics. Kansas City (straddling the Missouri-Kansas border) and St. Louis both have significant SCI/Dignity Memorial presence, which tends to push up pricing. The rural Ozarks, central Missouri, and the Bootheel region maintain traditional, family-owned funeral homes with considerably lower pricing.

Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors. The Board licenses all funeral professionals and establishments, oversees preneed contract compliance, and investigates consumer complaints. Missouri's preneed regulations require that funds be handled according to state law, providing important consumer protections for families who preplan.

Diverse religious landscape. Missouri's religious communities span a remarkable range. St. Louis has a strong Catholic tradition with elaborate funeral masses and parish-based burial customs. Kansas City's African-American churches maintain vibrant homegoing traditions. The Ozarks are predominantly Baptist and Pentecostal with traditional burial customs. Missouri's Amish and Mennonite communities in the northern part of the state maintain simple funeral traditions. This diversity creates varied demand for different service levels and styles.

Corporate consolidation in metros. Service Corporation International (SCI) and other corporate chains have a significant presence in both Kansas City and St. Louis. These corporate-owned locations typically charge 20-40% more than comparable independent providers. Outside the metro areas, the funeral market remains almost entirely family-owned.

Military community. Missouri has a strong military presence through Whiteman Air Force Base (home to the B-2 Spirit bomber fleet) and Fort Leonard Wood (a major Army training installation). The state's veteran population makes good use of VA burial benefits, and the Missouri Veterans Cemetery system provides burial for eligible veterans at multiple locations.

Missouri's funeral costs are highest in the Kansas City and St. Louis metro areas and lowest in rural communities. Springfield, the largest Ozarks city, offers moderate pricing between the two extremes.

Cremation vs. Burial in Missouri

Missouri's 52% cremation rate mirrors the national average, reflecting the state's position between the cremation-friendly Midwest and the burial-oriented South. Urban Kansas City and St. Louis have higher cremation rates, driven by cost savings and changing attitudes. Rural Missouri and the Ozarks lean heavily toward traditional burial, sustained by Baptist and Pentecostal traditions. Missouri's African-American communities, particularly in St. Louis and Kansas City, maintain strong preferences for traditional burial with full homegoing services. The state's moderate pricing for both options means families are not forced into one choice by financial pressure alone.

Missouri Funeral Regulations

The Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors oversees the state's funeral industry. Key regulations:

  • Licensing — All funeral directors and embalmers must hold active Missouri licenses with education, examination, and continuing education requirements.
  • Preneed protections — Missouri regulates preneed funeral contracts, requiring that funds be handled in compliance with state law. Consumers have protections for prepaid funeral arrangements.
  • No mandatory embalming — Missouri does not require embalming by law. Refrigeration is an accepted alternative.
  • Home funerals — Missouri permits families to care for their own deceased at home, though a funeral director must be involved in filing the death certificate and obtaining necessary permits.
  • Cremation regulation — Written authorization from next of kin is required before cremation. A medical examiner may need to authorize cremation in certain circumstances.

Your Consumer Rights in Missouri

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Missouri must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

Missouri consumers are protected by the State Board and the Missouri Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division. The Board investigates complaints against funeral homes and can impose disciplinary action. The AG's office handles deceptive practices complaints under the Missouri Merchandising Practices Act. File complaints with the Board, the AG's office, or the FTC at ftc.gov. The Funeral Consumers Alliance provides independent guidance.

Payment Assistance in Missouri

Missouri families who need help with funeral costs have these options:

  • Social Security death benefit — A one-time $255 payment for eligible surviving spouses or children. Learn more
  • Veteran burial benefits — Missouri has multiple national cemeteries (Jefferson Barracks in St. Louis, Springfield National Cemetery) and the Missouri Veterans Cemetery system with locations across the state.   • County indigent burial — Missouri counties provide indigent burial assistance. Contact your county's public administrator for information.
  • Military installation support — Whiteman AFB and Fort Leonard Wood both have casualty assistance officers who can help military families navigate burial benefits and funeral arrangements.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Missouri?

The average traditional funeral in Missouri costs approximately $6,800, below the national median. Kansas City and St. Louis are the most expensive markets ($7,400-$7,600), while Ozarks communities and smaller cities like Joplin are significantly more affordable ($5,800-$6,200). Direct cremation averages $1,600 statewide.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Missouri?

Yes. Direct cremation at $1,600 saves families substantially compared to traditional burial. Missouri's 52% cremation rate reflects a state evenly divided, with urban areas trending toward cremation and rural communities maintaining burial traditions. The state's moderate pricing for both options gives families genuine choice without overwhelming financial pressure.

Why is there such a price difference between KC/STL and rural Missouri?

The 15-20% price premium in Kansas City and St. Louis reflects higher real estate and labor costs, the presence of corporate funeral chains (which typically charge 20-40% more than independents), and the broader range of premium services available in urban markets. Rural Missouri funeral homes have lower overhead and virtually no corporate competition, keeping costs more affordable.

Does Missouri require embalming?

No. Missouri does not legally require embalming. Refrigeration is the accepted alternative. Some funeral homes may recommend embalming for open-casket viewings, but it is always the family's choice.

What veteran cemetery options are available in Missouri?

Missouri is well-served by veteran cemeteries. Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery in St. Louis is one of the largest national cemeteries in the country. Springfield National Cemetery and Fort Leavenworth National Cemetery (across the border in Kansas but serving Missouri veterans) are additional options. The Missouri Veterans Cemetery system operates multiple state veterans cemeteries. Contact the Missouri Veterans Commission for eligibility details.

Are there Amish funeral homes in Missouri?

Missouri has Amish and Old Order Mennonite communities, particularly in northern Missouri (Jamesport area, Clark area) and the Ozarks. These communities typically handle funerals within their own traditions: simple wooden coffins, home-based services, and community cemeteries. While not "Amish funeral homes" per se, local funeral homes near Amish settlements are experienced in accommodating these simple, community-directed customs at very affordable costs.
