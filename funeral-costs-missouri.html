Missouri Price Differences

Midwestern funerals generally cost less than the $8,300 US median, but Kansas City and St. Louis homes often charge 10-30% more than those in rural Missouri.

Missouri Funeral Customs

Missouri funerals tend to be practical. Viewings are common but often short, the meal afterwards is usually at church or at home rather than at the funeral home, and cremation followed by a graveside or memorial service is increasingly typical. That helps keep the Missouri average, about $6,800, below the national median.

Money Help for Missouri Funerals

  • Missouri counties pay for a coroner-arranged disposition (RSMo § 58.460) and can cover burial when there is no estate and no one obliged to pay (§ 58.550).
  • A surviving spouse or eligible child can claim Social Security's $255 lump sum on 1-800-772-1213 within two years of the death.
  • For a Missouri veteran, the person who paid files VA Form 21P-530EZ; after October 1, 2026, a death not connected to service brings up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot (eligibility is explained in our veteran burial benefits guide).
  • Medicaid and charity sources are listed in our guide to getting help with funeral bills, and what to do with no money.

Missouri death certificate fees

The Missouri Bureau of Vital Records charges $14 for the first certified copy of a death certificate. That fee covers a five-year search and one copy; each additional copy ordered at the same time is $11 (RSMo 193.265). One dollar of every death-record fee goes to the state's coroners' training fund and another dollar to the endowed care cemetery audit fund (Missouri DHSS, ordering vital records; checked October 29, 2026).

  • County local registrars, usually at the health department, issue death certificates at the same fees and are often the quickest option.
  • Mail-in requests have to be notarized.
  • Four copies ordered together cost $47: $14 for the first and $11 for each of the other three. Ordering a fifth later, separately, costs $14 again, so it pays to order what you need at once.
  • Insurers, banks, pension plans and the Department of Revenue for a vehicle title generally ask for a certified copy each; many smaller accounts accept a photocopy.

Missouri's funeral board and county help

Missouri's State Board of Embalmers & Funeral Directors licenses funeral homes and takes complaints at 573-751-0813, including about a home that won't show prices.

Missouri is absent from the Funeral Consumers Alliance's list of nine states where the law makes a funeral director part of the process, so families can pick and choose services. Cremation needs a permit but has no set waiting period (20 CSR 2120-2.071; RSMo § 194.119).

When a body is unclaimed or there is no money

If no relative or friend claims a body, the coroner arranges burial or cremation and the county commission repays the reasonable cost (RSMo § 58.460). Where there is no estate and nobody is liable to pay, the commission may pay burial costs from the county treasury (§ 58.550; RSMo §§ 58.460, 58.550). The $255 Social Security pays a spouse or child barely dents the $6,800 Missouri average in our table.

Paperwork a Missouri home owes you

Missouri adds its own rules to the federal ones. Before providing services, a funeral home must give you a written statement of charges, signed by both sides, listing the service price you chose, each extra item, every cash advance and how you will pay (RSMo 333.145). Cash advances, such as death certificate fees or a newspaper notice, can be billed only at the net amount the home actually paid, and merchandise on display must carry a price. Embalming needs written authorization (20 CSR 2120-2.070), so a family that did not sign for it should question the charge. Bring these points up if a bill does not match what was agreed. The one exception to written consent is a coroner's order: if no family can be found within six hours, the county coroner can order embalming and the family pays later (RSMo § 58.375).

On the cemetery side, Missouri's township and county cemeteries rarely advertise, yet their plots often cost about half what private cemeteries charge. The clerk in the county where the burial will happen can say which ones still sell graves and what opening the grave costs.

Veterans cemeteries in Missouri

Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery (314-845-8320) and Springfield National Cemetery (417-881-9499, cremated remains only) are reserved by the funeral director, who phones the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). Missouri's five state veterans cemeteries accept requests directly: Bloomfield (573-568-3871), Fort Leonard Wood (573-774-3496), Higginsville (660-362-6111), Jacksonville (660-372-6024) and Springfield (417-823-3944).

What to do first

  1. Have at least two Missouri price lists in hand before anyone signs.
  2. A memorial held after the cremation or burial means no embalming or viewing to pay for.
  3. No funds? The county coroner or medical examiner is the first call, before any home is chosen.
  4. For a veteran, ask about Jefferson Barracks before paying for a private plot.
  5. Keep the board's number, 573-751-0813, for any billing dispute.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a funeral in Missouri?

Our estimates are $6,800 for a traditional funeral, 18% under the $8,300 national median; $5,500 for a cremation with a service; and from $1,600 for direct cremation. Choosing burial adds roughly $3,100 in cemetery costs.

Does cremation save money in Missouri?

Yes. At about $1,600, direct cremation is $5,200 below the $6,800 traditional funeral, and about $8,300 below it once roughly $3,100 of cemetery costs are counted.

What protections do Missouri families have?

Federal law, the FTC Funeral Rule, entitles you to itemized prices, quotes by phone, and the freedom to buy only what you choose. Missouri, the Funeral Consumers Alliance notes, does not make a funeral director mandatory at every step. The State Board of Embalmers & Funeral Directors (573-751-0813) handles complaints.

What saves money on a Missouri funeral?

Look beyond private cemeteries: township and county cemeteries in Missouri rarely show up online but often cost half as much, and the county clerk can tell you about them. Family-owned funeral homes, still the majority in Missouri, usually price more openly than chains.

Is embalming required in Missouri?

No. Under 20 CSR 2120-2.070, a Missouri funeral home may not hold an unembalmed body longer than 24 hours unless it is refrigerated at 40°F or below or sealed in an airtight casket. The same rule requires written authorization to embalm, which the deceased can give in advance, including in a preneed contract.

A county coroner can order embalming when no family can be found within six hours (RSMo § 58.375), and the family pays later. Because Missouri requires written authorization, a charge of about $680 (our Missouri estimate) should not appear on a bill unless someone signed for it or the coroner ordered it.

What must a Missouri funeral home give me in writing?

Missouri requires a signed statement of charges. Under RSMo 333.145, before services are provided the funeral home must give a written statement, signed by both sides, listing the selected service price, each extra item, the cash advances and the payment method. A cash advance may be billed only at the net amount the home paid, and displayed merchandise must be price-marked. Hold quotes against the national median of $8,300 with viewing and burial, or $6,280 with cremation (NFDA 2023).

What is the cheapest Missouri funeral?

Direct cremation, from roughly $1,600. Direct burial is the next step up at about $3,400, which is $3,400 below a traditional Missouri funeral.

Where are the affordable funeral homes in Missouri?

You find them by comparing. Request General Price Lists from three Missouri homes and check direct cremation and immediate burial first. A casket or urn bought online or elsewhere must be accepted at no extra charge.
