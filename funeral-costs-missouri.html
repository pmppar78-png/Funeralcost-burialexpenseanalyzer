Funeral Costs in Missouri: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: Midwest

Missouri's funeral pricing breaks along a St. Louis / Kansas City axis and then fragments by region: Baue Funeral Home in St. Charles and Kutis in South St. Louis County serve the metro's eastern half, Mount Washington and the Muehlebach chain anchor Kansas City, and rural Ozark and northern Missouri counties run on single-provider pricing that varies village to village. Missouri's Office of Funeral Directors and Embalmers, inside the Division of Professional Registration, licenses providers under Chapter 333 of the Missouri Revised Statutes, and the state's 52% cremation rate tracks near the national average.

St. Louis vs. Kansas City Pricing Gap

St. Louis funeral pricing runs slightly above Kansas City on average, largely because St. Louis has more long-tenured traditional chains (Kutis, Schrader, Ortmann, Hoffmann) serving Catholic German, Irish, and Italian parishes that still favor full-service funerals. Kansas City trends more toward cremation-with-memorial and has a more consolidated market where Mount Washington and Muehlebach anchor the metro. Both cities carry a roughly 1.15x multiplier over the Missouri state average, but the composition of that premium differs. Families in the Bootheel, Ozarks, and northern Missouri counties often face single-provider markets where a 40-mile drive to the next county changes the quote significantly.

Chapter 333 and the Missouri Office of Funeral Directors and Embalmers

Missouri regulates funeral service through Chapter 333 of the Revised Statutes, administered by the Office of Funeral Directors and Embalmers within the Division of Professional Registration. The state requires 80% of preneed funds to be deposited in trust or held via an insurance-funded contract, with the cancellation-and-refund right protected by statute. Complaints about billing, refusal of an itemized GPL, or misrepresentation of legal requirements go to the Office in Jefferson City. Missouri also allows home funerals without a licensed funeral director — the family can prepare the body and transport it — though the death certificate must still be filed through a licensed establishment.

Missouri HealthNet Burial Reserve and Title XIX Planning

Missouri Medicaid (HealthNet) permits a designated Irrevocable Burial Reserve of up to $9,999 to be excluded from asset calculations, allowing families to preserve funds specifically for funeral and burial costs without affecting nursing home eligibility. This is one of the more generous Medicaid burial reserve allowances in the country. Funeral homes handle the paperwork through a preneed trust or a funeral-insurance contract; the key is that the reserve must be irrevocable and dedicated to burial expenses, not general savings. This is the most common Missouri planning tool for families with a spouse entering long-term care.

Bellefontaine, Calvary, and the Missouri Historic Cemetery Map

Bellefontaine Cemetery in St. Louis — home to Adolphus Busch, Sara Teasdale, and William Tecumseh Sherman — is the state's flagship non-sectarian historic ground, with plots from $2,500 to $8,500 depending on section. Calvary Cemetery next door serves the Catholic Archdiocese. In Kansas City, Mount Washington and Forest Hill Calvary Cemetery anchor the market. Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery in St. Louis and Missouri Veterans Cemeteries at Higginsville, Bloomfield, Springfield, Jacksonville, and Fort Leonard Wood provide no-cost burial for eligible veterans and spouses.

The Kansas-Missouri State Line Arbitrage and Other Missouri Invoice Surprises

Families in the Kansas City metro have a pricing lever most states don't offer: the Missouri-Kansas state line runs straight through the metropolitan area, and funeral home prices frequently differ by $800–$1,500 for identical services depending on which side holds the provider's license. Kansas-side Johnson County providers generally quote lower, but Missouri cemetery deeds and death-certificate filing rules require coordination when crossing the line. Beyond the state-line calculus, Missouri invoices often slip in a disinterment fee (a specialty mostly at Jefferson Barracks for re-burial petitions), a $200–$500 next-day certified death-certificate rush, and outside-casket handling fees that are illegal under 16 CFR 453 and Chapter 333 alike. Older Catholic cemeteries in Kansas City and St. Louis can also require a specific vault brand that runs $1,200 or more.

Get four quotes in KC: two Missouri-side (Mount Washington and D.W. Newcomer's 1873), one Kansas-side (a Johnson County independent), and one dedicated direct-cremation (Heartland Cremation). The spread across the state line is the fastest negotiating lever in the Midwest.

Heartland Cremation, Direct Burial, and the HealthNet Reserve: Missouri's Sub-$3,500 Options

Missouri's most affordable routes cluster under $3,500. Heartland Cremation in Kansas City and comparable direct-cremation specialists in St. Louis deliver disposition-only services near $1,600 with no viewing or ceremony. Direct burial in a small-town or township cemetery — Cole, Boone, and Greene counties among the most affordable — typically lands near $3,400 including a simple plot. For veterans, Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery in St. Louis (established 1826, one of the oldest national cemeteries) and five Missouri Veterans Cemeteries provide no-cost grave, opening, vault, and marker, leaving only the funeral home services as a family expense. Families facing long-term-care planning can use the $9,999 Missouri HealthNet Irrevocable Burial Reserve to prefund without affecting Medicaid eligibility.

Your Consumer Rights in Missouri

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Missouri must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in Missouri has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Your state attorney general's office can also assist with consumer protection complaints.

Missouri Home Funerals: Legally Permitted, Rarely Used

Missouri is one of a short list of states where a family can legally conduct a home funeral without a licensed funeral director — prepare the body, hold the visitation at home, transport to the cemetery or crematory, and file the death certificate through a cooperating funeral establishment. The practice is uncommon but growing in the Kansas City and Columbia liberal-arts communities, and Funeral Consumers Alliance of Greater Kansas City publishes a home-funeral manual. Expect cost savings of $3,000–$5,000 relative to a full-service funeral, offset by significant family time investment and logistical coordination.

What to Do When Planning a Funeral in Missouri

Families arranging a funeral in Missouri for the first time often move faster than they need to. The following sequence slows things down just enough to compare options without adding undue delay:

  1. Take a breath. Unless there are legal or medical time constraints, you typically have 24–72 hours before decisions must be finalized.
  2. Request General Price Lists from at least 2–3 funeral homes in your area of Missouri. They are legally required to provide them. Questions to ask funeral homes
  3. Decide on disposition: cremation in Missouri or burial in Missouri. This is the single biggest cost decision.
  4. Choose only the services you need. Embalming, premium caskets, and elaborate arrangements are optional. What funeral homes don't tell you
  5. Explore payment assistance if cost is a concern: Social Security benefits, veteran benefits, Medicaid assistance, or crowdfunding.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in Missouri right now?

A traditional Missouri funeral averages $6,800, about 13% below the $7,848 national figure. Cremation with a service is $5,500, and direct cremation starts near $1,600. Bellefontaine Cemetery plots in St. Louis run $2,500 to $8,500; Kansas City's Mount Washington and Forest Hill Calvary price in a similar band. Baue, Kutis, Mount Washington, and Muehlebach will all quote different non-declinable service fees — pull each GPL before choosing.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Missouri?

Yes. Direct cremation near $1,600 versus a traditional funeral at $6,800 plus a plot leaves a gap over $6,000 for most Missouri families. Missouri's 52% cremation rate sits near the national average, with Kansas City running higher adoption than the Catholic-anchored St. Louis parishes. Independent Ozarks and northern Missouri providers often price cremation below the metros.

What rights do Missouri families have when arranging a funeral?

The FTC Funeral Rule applies; Missouri's Chapter 333 adds preneed trust requirements and a licensed-board complaint process through the Office of Funeral Directors and Embalmers in Jefferson City. Missouri is also one of the states that legally permits a family to conduct a home funeral without engaging a licensed funeral director.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Missouri?

Set up a Missouri HealthNet Irrevocable Burial Reserve of up to $9,999 if long-term care planning is on the table. Veterans should use Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery or one of the five Missouri Veterans Cemeteries at Higginsville, Bloomfield, Springfield, Jacksonville, or Fort Leonard Wood. Compare three GPLs — one chain, one independent, one direct-cremation specialist. Rural Missouri families may find the next-county provider less expensive than their local monopoly.

Does Missouri require embalming?

No. Missouri law does not require embalming in ordinary circumstances. Refrigeration is accepted, and any provider claiming embalming is legally required must point to a specific statute. For open-casket public viewings, a funeral home may require it as provider policy — which is different from a legal requirement.

Is Missouri cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Missouri's $6,800 traditional average runs 13% below the $7,848 national median. St. Louis and Kansas City price above the state average, while Springfield, Cape Girardeau, and much of the Ozarks price below. Chapter 333's preneed trust protections give Missouri families stronger consumer standing than most Midwest states.

Which funeral option costs the least in Missouri?

Direct cremation at about $1,600 is the floor. A legal Missouri home funeral (no licensed funeral director) can cut $3,000–$5,000 further, though it requires meaningful family logistics. For veterans, Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery provides no-cost burial — grave, vault, and marker included.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Missouri?

Start with three GPLs covering the metro, a direct-cremation specialist, and a small-town independent. Funeral Consumers Alliance of Greater Kansas City and FCA of Greater St. Louis publish price comparisons. For rural Missouri, reach out to a provider one county over for a comparison quote — single-provider markets sometimes price above the next-town rate.
