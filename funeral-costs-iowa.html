What Drives Funeral Costs in Iowa

What you pay for a funeral in Iowa depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in Iowa, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether Iowa state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. Midwest pricing is generally lower than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in Iowa routinely differs by 10-30%.

At 50%, Iowa's cremation rate is 13.4 points below the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

What's Specific to Iowa (Midwest)

Payment Assistance in Iowa

  • Iowa's state code caps a transient nonresident's burial at $250, but for residents each county board of supervisors sets the amount, such as $1,000 in Linn County (Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27).
  • Funeral homes in Iowa usually report the death to Social Security, but the $255 lump sum still has to be claimed by the spouse or child (1-800-772-1213, within two years).
  • Whoever paid for a veteran's funeral in Iowa can file VA Form 21P-530EZ; for a death on or after October 1, 2026 that was not service-connected, VA pays up to $1,032 toward burial and $1,032 toward a plot (see our veteran burial benefits guide).

Funeral rules and help in Iowa

If a funeral home in Iowa refuses to show prices or pressures you, the licensing body is the Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing, reachable at 515-229-3939.

Iowa is one of nine states where a licensed funeral director must be involved in some steps: arranging a cremation (Iowa Code § 331.805; Iowa Admin. Code 481-900.10(2), as summarized by Funeral Consumers Alliance). An Iowa family can still do the rest of a funeral itself, such as washing and dressing the body or holding the service at home.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Iowa sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (Iowa Code § 331.805(3)(b)).

Iowa leaves funeral help to each county board of supervisors, which sets the form and amount of general assistance (Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27); the state code itself caps burial of a nonresident transient at $250, and counties set their own amounts for residents, such as Linn County's $1,000. (Source: Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27.) The $255 Social Security lump sum goes only to an eligible spouse or child and must be claimed within two years; it does not go to the Iowa funeral home.

An Iowa funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Keokuk National Cemetery (319-524-5193) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemetery, Iowa Veterans Cemetery (515-727-3444), takes requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in Iowa

  1. Request itemized prices from Iowa funeral homes by phone or in person; under the FTC Funeral Rule you only pay for what you choose.
  2. Ask the Iowa funeral home whether embalming is actually needed for your plans.
  3. Apply to the Iowa county general assistance office before signing a funeral contract; amounts vary by county.
  4. If the person served, have the funeral home ask about Keokuk National Cemetery before you buy a private plot.
  5. Report a funeral home that hides or ignores its price list to the board at 515-229-3939.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Iowa?

The only reliable way to pin down your number is to pull General Price Lists from two or three Des Moines-area providers and compare line by line.

Does cremation save money compared with burial in Iowa?

A direct cremation in Iowa runs about $1,500, whereas a traditional funeral with burial averages $6,700 before adding the plot, so the gap can easily exceed $5,200. Iowa's cremation rate of 50% sits below the 63.4% national rate NFDA projected for 2025.

What does the FTC Funeral Rule mean for me in Iowa?

In Iowa, as everywhere in the US, the FTC Funeral Rule requires an itemized General Price List, phone price quotes, and no fee for using a casket bought elsewhere. Iowa law requires a licensed funeral director for arranging a cremation (Iowa Code § 331.805; Iowa Admin. Code 481-900.10(2), per the Funeral Consumers Alliance). The Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing, 515-229-3939, licenses Iowa funeral homes and takes complaints.

How can I save on funeral costs in Iowa?

Start by lining up General Price Lists from two or three Des Moines-area providers before you commit to anything. Township and county cemeteries in Iowa rarely appear in online searches but are often half the price of private cemeteries; call the clerk's office in the county where burial will occur. Independent, family-owned funeral homes still dominate Iowa and tend to price more transparently than chain providers. Direct cremation at $1,500 remains the lowest-cost disposition in every Iowa market.

Is embalming legally required in Iowa?

No, as long as the body is refrigerated or buried in time. Iowa's funeral rules (481 IAC 900.6(3)) let an unembalmed body be held 72 hours after death, or 24 hours after the funeral home takes custody, and refrigeration at 38 to 42°F extends that by another 72 hours.

After that, embalming or disposition is needed. If a viewing is planned past that point, budget about $670, our estimate for Iowa. Iowa's Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (515-229-3939) is the agency to call if a funeral home bills for embalming no one approved.

Where does Iowa rank nationally for funeral costs?

Iowa's $6,700 average for a traditional funeral puts it 19% below the $8,300 national median. Pricing in Iowa is near or slightly below the national average, reflecting moderate costs of living across the region, and the Midwest region as a whole tends to price moderate to lower than other parts of the US.

What is the cheapest funeral option in Iowa?

Direct cremation is the lowest-cost option in Iowa, starting at roughly $1,500. Direct burial comes next at around $3,350 and still avoids viewing, ceremony, and embalming, though the $3,000 Iowa plot estimate still applies.

Where do I look for low-cost funeral providers in Iowa?

Ask at least three Iowa funeral homes for their price lists and compare the direct cremation line, which starts around $1,500 here. Ask for the direct cremation price alone, not a package that adds a service or viewing you did not ask for.

Whole-body donation is another route. The University of Iowa's Deeded Body Program charges nothing for its own services and covers cremation and either the return of the ashes or burial in Oakland Cemetery in Iowa City. The family or estate pays a funeral director's transport and service fees to bring the body to Iowa City, and embalming may be needed if transport is delayed more than eight hours.
