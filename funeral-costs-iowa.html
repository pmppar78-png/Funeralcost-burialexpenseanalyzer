Paying for an Iowa Funeral

  • Iowa's state code caps a transient nonresident's burial at $250, but for residents each county board of supervisors sets the amount, such as $1,000 in Linn County (Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27).
  • Funeral homes in Iowa usually report the death to Social Security, but the $255 lump sum still has to be claimed by the spouse or child (1-800-772-1213, within two years).
  • Whoever paid for a veteran's funeral in Iowa can file VA Form 21P-530EZ; for a death on or after October 1, 2026 that was not service-connected, VA pays up to $1,032 toward burial and $1,032 toward a plot (see our veteran burial benefits guide).
  • Medicaid, charity and other sources are covered in our funeral assistance guide and paying when there is no money.

What's Specific to Iowa (Midwest)

Two Iowa rules matter most for the bill. First, cremation here has to go through a licensed funeral director (Iowa Code § 331.805), so even a family that washes, dresses and sits with the body at home will still pay a director's fee if it chooses cremation; the cheapest Iowa route is to buy only that director's direct cremation line. Second, Iowa's holding-time rule (481 IAC 900.6(3)) allows an unembalmed body to wait 72 hours, or 24 hours after the funeral home takes custody, plus another 72 with refrigeration, which is usually enough time to gather family without paying for embalming. Public help is set county by county: the board of supervisors decides what general assistance pays toward a resident's burial, so Linn County's $1,000 limit says nothing about what Polk or Scott County will pay.

Federal Protections in Iowa

Every Iowa funeral home is bound by the FTC Funeral Rule, which applies on top of Iowa's own licensing rules.

If an Iowa home breaks the federal rule, tell the FTC at ftc.gov; the Funeral Consumers Alliance can also help.

Why Iowa Quotes Differ

Iowa quotes generally come in under the $8,300 national median, with metro and rural homes often 10-30% apart. Before comparing them, settle who can sign. Iowa lets an adult name a designee, and an alternate, in a written declaration; without one, control passes to a spouse who was not legally separated, then a majority of the surviving children, then the parents, then a majority of the grandchildren, then of the siblings, then of the grandparents (Iowa Code § 144C.5).

Half of Iowa families (50%) now choose cremation, 13.4 points under the 63.4% NFDA projected nationally for 2025.

Arranging an Iowa Funeral

In this order, these steps head off the most expensive mistakes in Iowa:

Iowa death certificates: $20 each

Every certified copy of an Iowa death certificate costs $20, from the state or a county recorder, and the fee is kept as a search fee if no record is found (Iowa HHS, how to request a certified record; checked October 29, 2026). There is no discount for extra copies.

  • County recorders: any recorder can issue certified copies for deaths from 1954 on; for earlier deaths, ask the recorder in the county of residence or of death. Iowa rule 641-95.6 sends most of each $20 death-certificate fee, $13, to the State Medical Examiner's office.
  • State office: the Bureau of Health Statistics, 321 E. 12th Street, Des Moines, takes walk-in applications from 7 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. on weekdays (515-281-4944) and does not accept cash. VitalChek handles online orders, with a "Will Call" option for pickup in Des Moines.
  • By mail: the application must be notarized and include a copy of a current government photo ID, with a check or money order. Mailed state orders usually take four to six weeks.
  • Who qualifies: a spouse, child, parent named on the record, grandparent, grandchild, sibling, legal guardian or legal representative, with proof of the link.

At $20 a copy, five certificates cost $100, so ask each bank, insurer and pension office whether it will take a copy before ordering more.

Iowa licensing, the cremation rule, and county burial help

Iowa licenses its funeral directors and funeral homes through the Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (DIAL, mortuary science, 515-229-3939), which also takes complaints about prices and price lists.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance counts Iowa among the nine states that limit home funerals: a licensed funeral director has to arrange any cremation (Iowa Code § 331.805; Iowa Admin. Code 481-900.10(2)). Families may still wash and dress the body or hold a service at home. A permit is needed before cremation, but there is no set waiting period (Iowa Code § 331.805(3)(b)).

Help from the county

Each county board of supervisors decides the form and amount of general assistance for a resident's burial (Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27). The state code itself sets only a $250 limit for a nonresident transient; Linn County, for example, pays up to $1,000 for residents. (Source: Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27.) The $255 Social Security payment is reserved for a surviving spouse or child, who must apply within two years; the funeral home cannot collect it.

Veterans' cemeteries

Keokuk National Cemetery (319-524-5193) is reserved by the funeral home through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office at 800-535-1117. The Iowa Veterans Cemetery near Van Meter (515-727-3444) works with families directly.

Iowa to-do list

  1. Phone two or three Iowa homes for itemized prices before you choose one.
  2. Choose early whether to embalm and hold a viewing; Iowa accepts refrigeration instead.
  3. Ask the county general assistance office what it pays before any contract is signed.
  4. If the person served, ask about the Iowa Veterans Cemetery ahead of any private plot.
  5. Report a missing or ignored price list to DIAL at 515-229-3939.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the price of an Iowa funeral?

The only reliable way to pin down your number is to pull General Price Lists from two or three Des Moines-area providers and compare line by line.

What does cremation save in Iowa?

Usually by more than $5,200. Direct cremation is about $1,500, while a traditional funeral with burial is about $6,700 before the plot. Iowa's 50% cremation rate trails the 63.4% NFDA projected nationally for 2025.

What rights do Iowa families have?

Under federal law an Iowa home must hand you its prices item by item, answer price questions by phone and accept a casket you bought elsewhere at no charge. Iowa adds one requirement: a licensed funeral director must arrange any cremation (Iowa Code § 331.805; Iowa Admin. Code 481-900.10(2), per the Funeral Consumers Alliance). DIAL, 515-229-3939, licenses homes and hears complaints.

How can an Iowa family spend less?

Start by lining up General Price Lists from two or three Des Moines-area providers before you commit to anything. Township and county cemeteries in Iowa rarely appear in online searches but are often half the price of private cemeteries; call the clerk's office in the county where burial will occur. Independent, family-owned funeral homes still dominate Iowa and tend to price more transparently than chain providers. Direct cremation at $1,500 remains the lowest-cost disposition in every Iowa market.

Does Iowa require embalming?

No, as long as the body is refrigerated or buried in time. Iowa's funeral rules (481 IAC 900.6(3)) let an unembalmed body be held 72 hours after death, or 24 hours after the funeral home takes custody, and refrigeration at 38 to 42°F extends that by another 72 hours.

After that, embalming or disposition is needed. If a viewing is planned past that point, budget about $670, our estimate for Iowa. Iowa's Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (515-229-3939) is the agency to call if a funeral home bills for embalming no one approved.

Is Iowa cheaper than the national average?

Cheaper than most. Iowa's $6,700 traditional figure is 19% under the $8,300 national median, much like other Midwest states where living costs are moderate.

What is the lowest-cost Iowa funeral?

Direct cremation is the lowest-cost option in Iowa, starting at roughly $1,500. Direct burial comes next at around $3,350 and still avoids viewing, ceremony, and embalming, though the $3,000 Iowa plot estimate still applies.

How do I find an inexpensive Iowa funeral home?

Get price lists from three or more Iowa homes and compare the direct cremation line, which starts near $1,500. Ask for that price on its own, without a service or viewing added.

Whole-body donation is another route. The University of Iowa's Deeded Body Program charges nothing for its own services and covers cremation and either the return of the ashes or burial in Oakland Cemetery in Iowa City. The family or estate pays a funeral director's transport and service fees to bring the body to Iowa City, and embalming may be needed if transport is delayed more than eight hours.
