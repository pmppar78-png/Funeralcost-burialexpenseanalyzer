Funeral Costs in Iowa: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: Midwest

Iowa is a $6,700-traditional, $1,500-direct-cremation state with a 50% cremation rate and a unique wrinkle in its licensing law: the Iowa Board of Mortuary Science is the only US state board that separately credentials "funeral assistants," a tier below full funeral director that many small-town Iowa homes rely on. Here is what families pay across the state, and where the rules shape the invoice.

Iowa's funeral assistant license — a workforce story

Iowa is alone among US states in offering a distinct "funeral assistant" license under Iowa Code Chapter 156. Full funeral directors complete a two-year mortuary science program plus apprenticeship; funeral assistants meet a shorter training requirement and can perform most arrangement conferences, transport, and service coordination — but not embalming or final signing authority. The practical effect is that rural Iowa funeral homes can staff a second location or run weekend services without keeping two full directors on payroll, and pricing in those markets stays correspondingly lower. The Iowa Board of Mortuary Science publishes the licensee registry online.

Iowa Code 523I and the private-property burial tradition

Iowa Code Chapter 523I — the state's cemetery law — explicitly accommodates private burial grounds on farm property, with county recorder filing and a survey requirement but no minimum acreage. The tradition runs deep in rural Iowa: family plots on homestead corners that have been in continuous use for five generations. For families still operating a working farm, this option can eliminate cemetery costs entirely, provided the land meets setback rules from wells, waterways, and neighboring dwellings.

  • County recorder filing — required, typically $50–$150.
  • Survey — legal description of the burial ground's metes and bounds.
  • Setback rules — vary by county; check the county's zoning ordinance.
  • Deed restriction — private cemetery status generally runs with the land in perpetuity.

Van Meter, Marshalltown, and Glendale — Iowa's veteran burial pathways

The Iowa Veterans Cemetery in Van Meter, just west of Des Moines, is the state's primary veteran burial ground — jointly funded under the VA's State Cemetery Grants Program and offering the standard package (plot, opening/closing, government marker, perpetual care) at no cost to eligible veterans and dependents. The Iowa Veterans Home in Marshalltown, founded 1887, provides end-of-life care and, for residents who pass while in residence, handles final arrangements with state funding. Glendale Cemetery in Des Moines holds a large traditional veteran section for those who prefer a civilian cemetery.

Why Iowa runs so cheap

Three factors keep Iowa pricing among the lowest in America. Labor costs in small-town Iowa sit well below coastal wage ranges, and the funeral assistant license tier reduces staffing overhead further. Real estate for funeral home facilities in county seats is a fraction of urban costs — many Iowa homes operate from converted residential buildings paid off decades ago. And competitive pressure from the direct-cremation segment, particularly providers out of Des Moines and the Quad Cities, keeps traditional-service pricing honest. Families often find the state average of $6,700 high by local standards when they start calling around.

What You're Entitled to Under Federal and State Rules

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Iowa must comply with the FTC Funeral Rule codified at 16 CFR Part 453, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in Iowa has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov, the Iowa Board of Mortuary Science, or the Funeral Consumers Alliance. The Iowa Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division also fields complaints.

Commonly Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Iowa?

Traditional funerals in Iowa average around $6,700, which lands 15% below the national figure of $7,848. Cremation with a service comes in near $5,400, and direct cremation can start as low as $1,500 in Iowa. Expect to budget roughly $3,000 on top of that for a cemetery plot if you choose burial. The only reliable way to pin down your number is to pull General Price Lists from two or three Des Moines-area providers and compare line by line.

What does the FTC Funeral Rule mean for me in Iowa?

In Iowa, the Funeral Rule gives you five practical rights: itemized pricing on demand, no obligation to buy bundled packages, the right to supply your own casket or urn without extra fees, protection from false legal claims about mandatory services, and a written estimate before work begins. Iowa state consumer-protection laws and the state funeral board can add further rights.

How can I save on funeral costs in Iowa?

Start by lining up General Price Lists from two or three Des Moines-area providers before you commit to anything. Township and county cemeteries in Iowa rarely appear in online searches but are often half the price of private cemeteries — call the clerk's office in the county where burial will occur. Independent, family-owned funeral homes still dominate Iowa and tend to price more transparently than chain providers. Direct cremation at $1,500 remains the lowest-cost disposition in every Iowa market. Layer in veteran burial benefits, Medicaid funeral assistance, and the Social Security survivor payment where you qualify.

Is embalming legally required in Iowa?

No — embalming is rarely required by Iowa law (Iowa Code Chapter 156 covers mortuary science practice). Most cases allow refrigeration as an alternative, and the decision is yours. Some Iowa funeral homes have internal rules for open-casket viewings lasting more than 24-48 hours, but that is provider policy rather than state law. The FTC Funeral Rule at 16 CFR § 453.3(d) specifically prohibits providers from falsely claiming embalming is legally mandated.

What is the cheapest funeral option in Iowa?

Direct cremation is the lowest-cost option in Iowa, starting at roughly $1,500. It covers transport, cremation, and return of the ashes — nothing else. Families then hold a memorial on their own schedule, often at a church, park, or home. Direct burial comes next at around $4,020 and still avoids viewing, ceremony, and embalming.

Where do I look for low-cost funeral providers in Iowa?

Start with GPL requests from at least three Iowa funeral homes — that alone exposes the outliers. Reach out to the Funeral Consumers Alliance for Iowa for member-run lists, shortlist dedicated direct cremation providers, ask every funeral home about their simplest no-frills package, and see if a nonprofit or cooperative provider serves your area of Iowa.
