Cremation Costs in Alabama (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Alabama cremation page rewritten, covering the funeral establishment requirement, county help and the 24-hour wait; an unsourced price claim about nonprofit societies was dropped. Cremation Rate: 38%

Our Alabama estimates are about $1,800 for a direct cremation and about $5,200 for a funeral followed by cremation, so the choice of a service is worth about $3,400. Alabama cremates less than most states, 38% by our figure. State law adds a 24-hour wait, requires a licensed funeral establishment, and sets out a tagging system that follows the body through the crematory. Each is covered below.

What Alabama Law Says About the Container and Witnessing

When considering cremation in Alabama, keep these important points in mind:

  • Ask for the Alabama establishment's General Price List first. Its direct cremation line has to list what is covered; look for the transfer, the cremation and a temporary container for the ashes.
  • An Alabama provider offering direct cremation must let you use an unfinished wood box or other alternative container instead of a casket (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(b)). State law spells out what that box must be: Code of Ala. §34-13-121(e) asks only for a combustible, closable, leak-resistant and rigid container labeled with the decedent's name and date of death. The same section lets the family member who signs the authorization witness the transport to the crematory, and a home that bars relatives from viewing the cremation must say so in writing before any contract is signed.
  • Before you sign, find out when the ashes will be ready, whether they are delivered or collected, and whether certified copies of the death certificate are billed separately.
Alabama families may bring their own urn; the FTC Funeral Rule forbids refusing it or charging to accept it, and the Alabama Board of Funeral Service takes complaints if a home does.

Spending Less on an Alabama Cremation

Two ways to pay less:

  • Line up three price lists. Alabama funeral establishments must give out their General Price Lists; compare the direct cremation figure on each.
  • Ask what the direct price includes. Some homes leave the death certificates or the permit out of the headline number, so ask for a full written total before choosing.

Viewing First, Then Cremation, in Alabama

Some Alabama families want cremation and still want a viewing and funeral first. That runs about $5,200 on our estimates, against $1,800 for a direct cremation, so if a visitation matters, ask each home to price the funeral-then-cremation package on its own.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an Alabama cremation cost?

Our Alabama numbers run from about $1,800 for a direct cremation to about $5,200 with a full funeral first.

Which cremation services can I buy in Alabama?

Alabama homes generally sell three: direct cremation at about $1,800 with nothing held first; cremation followed by a memorial whenever and wherever the family likes; and a funeral and viewing before the cremation at about $5,200.

What does Alabama require before a cremation?

Alabama does not allow cremation in the first 24 hours after death unless a medical examiner waives the wait because of infectious disease, and the authorizing agent must sign the consent (Ala. Code § 34-13-121).

The Alabama Board of Funeral Services (334-242-4049) handles complaints about how a crematory or funeral home dealt with any of these steps.

Is scattering ashes allowed in Alabama?

Yes. Alabama has no statute that limits where cremated remains may be scattered, and the funeral services chapter (Ala. Code § 34-13-1) counts scattering as one recognized way of disposing of them. What the law does require is a record: under § 34-13-122 the person who authorizes the cremation signs a statement of ultimate disposition saying what will happen to the remains, and if nobody collects them the funeral home may dispose of them after 60 days.

Private land needs the owner's consent. At Little River Canyon National Preserve and other National Park Service sites, 36 CFR 2.62 allows scattering only under a park permit or in a place the superintendent has designated.

Why are Alabama families turning to cremation?

Price explains much of it: our estimates put direct cremation about $4,600 under a traditional funeral and burial in Alabama.

How quickly can an Alabama cremation happen?

The cremation takes 2 to 3 hours. A direct cremation ($1,800) is the quickest route, since the only fixed delay is the 24 hours Alabama requires after death (Ala. Code § 34-13-121).

Can there be a funeral before an Alabama cremation?

Yes. Our estimate for an Alabama funeral with the body present and cremation afterwards is about $5,200, with most quotes between $4,160 and $6,760. That is about $3,400 above a direct cremation at $1,800, paying for staff, the facility, a casket for the service and usually embalming or refrigeration.

Ask whether the home rents a ceremonial casket, so you are not buying one only to have it cremated. Because the law bars cremation in the first 24 hours (Ala. Code § 34-13-121), a visitation the evening before fits easily.

How Alabama keeps track of identity through cremation

Alabama follows each body through cremation with a paper trail and physical tags. Before cremation, the funeral establishment fills out the Board of Funeral Service's state identification form. That form travels with the remains through transport and cremation and back to the family. Every body to be cremated also wears an ankle bracelet that cannot be removed. When the cremation is finished, the cremationist signs to confirm the identity of the remains and when and where the cremation took place. The family receives a copy of the identification form (Ala. Code § 34-13-121).

  • The metal tag: each crematory assigns a numbered metal tag bearing its name. The tag stays with the body through every stage. Afterward it is fastened to the bag holding the ashes, or placed inside the urn if the ashes go straight into a permanent container (Ala. Admin. Code r. 395-X-6-.13; checked October 29, 2026).
  • The family's own check: the authorizing agent states on the form whether they personally identified the person, so ask about that step if you want to view the body before cremation.

Alabama cremation oversight and help with the cost

The Alabama Board of Funeral Services (334-242-4049) licenses crematories and funeral establishments and takes complaints about them.

Alabama requires every cremation to be arranged through a funeral establishment the Board has licensed (Ala. Code § 34-13-121(b)). The $1,800 direct cremation in our table is therefore bought from a licensed establishment, not from a crematory alone.

Cremation cannot take place within 24 hours of death unless a medical examiner waives the wait for infectious disease, and the authorizing agent must sign the consent (Ala. Code § 34-13-121).

When there is no money

Alabama has no state funeral payment. When a person dies with no estate and no relative in the county able to pay, the necessary disposition costs fall on the county where the death occurred, and the county commission pays once the account is presented (Ala. Code § 38-8-2). Each commission sets its own amount, and many pay less for a cremation than for a burial. Social Security's $255 lump sum goes only to an eligible spouse or child and must be claimed within two years.

A cremated Alabama veteran keeps the right to a national cemetery niche or plot and a marker at no cost: Alabama National Cemetery in Montevallo (205-665-9039), Fort Mitchell National Cemetery (334-855-4731) and the Alabama State Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Spanish Fort (251-625-1338); see cem.va.gov.

Alabama checklist

  1. Ask whether the price covers the death certificates and the permit fee, which some establishments bill on top.
  2. Allow for the 24-hour wait in travel and memorial plans.
  3. If no one can pay, ask the county commission, often through the coroner, what it pays for an indigent cremation.
  4. If you want to watch the transfer to the crematory, say so when you sign the authorization; Code of Ala. §34-13-121(e) lets the signing family member witness it.
  5. Report a provider that misstates its prices to the Board at 334-242-4049.
