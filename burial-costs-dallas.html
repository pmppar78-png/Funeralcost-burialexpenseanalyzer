Burial Costs in Dallas, Texas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Texas

Dallas's burial market reflects the Texas cost-of-living multiplier layered on national averages. A full traditional burial here — funeral service, cemetery plot, vault, casket, headstone — commonly runs $15,709 to $22,103. The funeral home portion ($8,280) and cemetery portion ($3,910 for the plot alone) are the two largest line items.

Dallas burial totals — $15,709 to $22,103 for a full commercial arrangement — cluster around the upper end of the Texas range because of Dallas-Plano-Frisco real estate and the density of corporate-chain cemeteries in the metro. The same burial at a rural Dallas-area church cemetery or at a family cemetery in a smaller county can come in thousands below that floor. And for any family with a veteran connection, the calculus changes dramatically once DFW National Cemetery enters the picture.

Texas Funeral Service Commission Oversight

Dallas funeral homes, directors, and embalmers are licensed by the Texas Funeral Service Commission (TFSC), based in Austin. TFSC investigates consumer complaints, publishes licensee disciplinary history, and enforces both the federal FTC Funeral Rule and a parallel Texas price-disclosure rule. Before signing any Dallas funeral home contract, look up the establishment and the specific staff member on the TFSC public register — it takes about two minutes. Cemeteries in Texas are regulated separately under the Texas Department of Banking's Special Audits Division (for perpetual care cemeteries); consumer complaints about plot sales, perpetual-care funds, or opening-and-closing billing go there, not to TFSC. Church, family, and municipal cemeteries are less tightly regulated, which is part of why their pricing can be so different.

DFW National Cemetery Serves the Whole Metroplex

Dallas-Fort Worth National Cemetery, opened in 2000 and located in southern Dallas County, is one of the largest VA national cemeteries in the country at roughly 638 acres. For eligible veterans and spouses, interment covers the plot, opening and closing, outer burial container, government-furnished headstone, and perpetual care — effectively eliminating the entire cemetery-side cost. DFW National is almost always the largest single cost-reduction lever available to Dallas-area veteran families. Texas also operates Dallas-Fort Worth's Central Texas State Veterans Cemetery network — the nearest state veterans cemetery is in Killeen — but for Dallas metro families, DFW National is much closer and equivalent in benefit. File VA pre-eligibility paperwork early because interment scheduling can occasionally back up.

Cemeteries Dallas Families Actually Use

Restland Memorial Park on Greenville Avenue, at over 200 acres, is one of the largest cemeteries in the United States and a workhorse of the Dallas burial market. Sparkman-Hillcrest Memorial Park in Preston Hollow is the higher-end option — resting place of Tom Landry, Mickey Mantle, H.L. Hunt, and much of Dallas's mid-century establishment. Grove Hill Memorial Park in east Dallas is a long-standing commercial option. Calvary Hill Cemetery is the Catholic cemetery of the Diocese of Dallas. Oakland Cemetery, platted in 1891 in south Dallas, was historically Dallas's major Black cemetery and is now preserved as a historic site with limited ongoing interments. Dallas City Cemetery at Pioneer Park is closed to new interments but is another historic landmark. For Jewish families, Shearith Israel Memorial Park and Temple Emanu-El Cemetery maintain dedicated sections with traditional Jewish interment protocols.

Dallas County Indigent Assistance

Dallas County Health and Human Services administers the county's indigent burial and cremation program for decedents whose families cannot pay and who do not have accessible assets. The benefit generally covers a direct cremation or basic county-plot burial, with limited service, and must be applied for before arrangements are finalized. Texas does not have a statewide funeral benefit comparable to Illinois DHS or California CalWORKs, so county programs are the primary public option. Catholic Charities of Dallas and various congregation-based funds sometimes bridge smaller gaps. Social Security's $255 lump-sum benefit applies for qualifying surviving spouses; the funeral home can file on the family's behalf.

Summer Heat Logistics — Often Overlooked

Dallas summers make prompt disposition and refrigeration materially more important than in cooler climates. Most Dallas funeral homes will accept a deceased without embalming only if immediate refrigeration is available and the interment or cremation is scheduled within 24 to 48 hours. A family choosing a no-embalming Muslim, Jewish, or natural burial should confirm refrigeration capacity and scheduling before committing to a provider. On the cemetery side, July and August opening-and-closing scheduling can be constrained because outdoor crews work limited hours; expect possible morning-only services.

How Dallas Families Actually Save Money

  • File DFW National Cemetery eligibility paperwork for any veteran. This eliminates roughly $6,000 to $7,000 of cemetery-side expenses for eligible veterans and spouses. DFW National's scale means scheduling is usually reasonable.
  • Compare Restland, Sparkman-Hillcrest, and Grove Hill with at least one church cemetery quote. Catholic, Baptist, and independent church cemeteries across outlying Dallas, Collin, Tarrant, and Denton counties routinely price plots 30–50% below the large metro memorial parks.
  • Use independent Dallas funeral homes. Sparkman-Crane, Restland, Hillside Memorial (independent, not the California chain), and others compete with SCI-owned operations; the non-corporate firms typically quote closer to the Texas state average.
  • Consider a rural plot with in-metro services. Transportation from a Dallas funeral home to a rural Texas county cemetery runs $200–$500 and typically saves much more on the plot and opening-and-closing lines.
  • Plan summer logistics around heat. Schedule services early in the morning, confirm refrigeration availability at the funeral home, and ask about summer-specific cemetery operating hours before finalizing a date.
  • Buy the casket online. The FTC Funeral Rule and Texas parallel rules both protect third-party caskets. Dallas funeral homes are used to this. Casket guide | Retailers
  • Price the headstone through an independent monument dealer. Texas monument dealers deliver and install at every major Dallas cemetery; independent pricing is routinely 20–40% below cemetery-supplied markers. Headstone guide
Save on Caskets: Under the FTC Funeral Rule, funeral homes must accept caskets purchased from third-party retailers without charging handling fees. Compare online casket retailers to save significantly.

What Families Want to Know

How much does burial cost in Dallas?

A full-service burial in Dallas runs roughly $8,280 for the funeral home service, $3,910 for a plot at Restland, Sparkman-Hillcrest, or Grove Hill, plus $1,564 vault, $1,955 opening and closing, $2,484 for a mid-range casket, and $1,500–$3,500 for a headstone — $15,709 to $22,103 total. Eligible veterans buried at DFW National Cemetery in southern Dallas County eliminate the cemetery-side costs entirely, which is usually a $6,000–$7,000 saving.

Is a burial vault required in Dallas?

Texas state law does not require a vault. Restland, Sparkman-Hillcrest, and the other major Dallas commercial cemeteries require one as a maintenance policy. DFW National Cemetery includes an outer burial container in its interment package for eligible veterans. Some older church and rural county cemeteries in the Dallas area accept a less expensive concrete grave liner; a few Muslim and orthodox Jewish sections accept neither because faith protocols discourage them. Always verify with the specific cemetery.

What are the cheapest burial options in Dallas?

Veterans and eligible spouses start with DFW National Cemetery, which eliminates most of the cemetery-side expense. Non-veteran families achieve the lowest costs through direct burial at a church or family cemetery in an outlying county (Collin, Denton, Ellis, Kaufman) using a casket ordered online. Dallas County Health and Human Services administers indigent burial/cremation for qualifying decedents, and Catholic Charities of Dallas sometimes bridges smaller gaps. The Social Security $255 lump-sum benefit applies separately.
