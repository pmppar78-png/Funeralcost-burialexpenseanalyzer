Burial Costs in Rhode Island (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Northeast

Rhode Island is the smallest U.S. state and has one of the densest per-square-mile cemetery networks in the country — a legacy of the colonial-era churches, granite-town companies, and 19th-century Catholic immigrant parishes that each built their own grounds. Swan Point in Providence (1846) runs as a nonprofit garden cemetery; North Burial Ground on North Main Street dates to 1700 and is still owned and operated by the City of Providence; St. Francis, Gate of Heaven, and the broader Diocese of Providence network covers Catholic burials statewide. A 2026 traditional Rhode Island burial runs about $9,000 for the funeral service and $4,400 for a plot, with all-in totals between $17,360 and $24,460.

Providence County pricing tends to sit at the top of that band because of metro land values; nonprofit Swan Point, municipal North Burial Ground, and most Diocese of Providence cemeteries sit at the lower end; Aquidneck Island (Newport, Middletown) and rural Washington County grounds fall between.

Rhode Island's Small-State Cemetery Map

Because Rhode Island is geographically small, most families can realistically consider four or five cemeteries within 30 minutes of home. Swan Point in Providence is the state's best-known nonprofit, with published fee schedules, a professional horticultural staff, and a sizable Jewish section (Temple Beth-El). North Burial Ground, owned by the City of Providence, operates on a public-agency fee schedule. The Diocese of Providence's Catholic Cemeteries of the Diocese of Providence runs Gate of Heaven (East Providence), Resurrection (Cumberland), and St. Francis (Pawtucket and other locations) with parishioner pricing that typically undercuts commercial cemeteries. Beth-El and Sons of Jacob serve the state's Jewish community. Rhode Island Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Exeter (state-run, not federal) provides plot, vault, opening-and-closing, and upright marker at no cost to eligible Rhode Island veterans and spouses — the state's only veterans cemetery and one of the highest-use state veterans cemeteries per capita in the country.

Who Regulates the Rhode Island Market

Rhode Island funeral and embalming practice is regulated by the Rhode Island Department of Health's Board of Funeral Directors/Embalmers (Office of Health Professions Regulation), while cemeteries are separately registered with the state. Before signing a pre-need contract, search the director's license on health.ri.gov; Rhode Island also requires pre-need funds to sit in a dedicated escrow with specific cancellation-and-refund rights. Separately, Rhode Island's state burial assistance program (General Public Assistance) provides a modest benefit for the lowest-income families, administered through the Department of Human Services.

Winter Burial, Church Cemeteries, and the Out-of-Town Lever

Rhode Island winters are real: many cemeteries charge a frozen-ground surcharge from approximately mid-December through March, or require winter storage until spring interment. Get the winter number in writing. The state's dense church-cemetery network is the biggest cost lever most Rhode Island families do not use — active church cemeteries in Cranston, Warwick, Pawtucket, Woonsocket, South Kingstown, Charlestown, and Bristol routinely price plots $1,000–$2,500 below the nonprofit Providence average, and most still have available sections. A 20-minute drive can change the bill meaningfully in a state this size.

Rhode Island tip: Because Rhode Island is small enough to drive end-to-end in under an hour, always get written pricing from at least three cemeteries in different cities before deciding — it is a shorter trip than most other states require, and the plot-price spread across 30 miles of Rhode Island can easily exceed $2,000.

Cutting the Rhode Island Bill

  • Request Swan Point's fee schedule first: The nonprofit anchor's published pricing becomes the benchmark for every commercial cemetery quote in the state.
  • Check Rhode Island Veterans Memorial Cemetery eligibility: For qualifying Rhode Island veterans, the state facility in Exeter provides everything plot-side at no cost.
  • Call Diocese of Providence cemeteries for parishioner pricing: Gate of Heaven, Resurrection, and St. Francis typically undercut the private market.
  • Compare prices across the state, not just the county: A 20–30 minute drive between Providence County and South County often saves $1,500+ on the plot.
  • Confirm the winter line item: Ask every cemetery for the written frozen-ground charge in the interment month.
  • Buy the casket independently: FTC Funeral Rule bars a handling fee. Casket buying guide.
  • Apply for General Public Assistance if eligible: Rhode Island DHS administers a modest burial benefit for the lowest-income families — see our payment assistance guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the full price of burial in Rhode Island?

In 2026, a full Rhode Island burial totals $17,360 to $24,460: $9,000 funeral service, $4,400 plot, $2,700 casket, $1,760 vault, $2,200 opening-and-closing, and $1,000–$3,000 for a marker. Providence-metro cemeteries (Swan Point, North Burial Ground, Gate of Heaven) sit toward the low end of the range; Aquidneck Island commercial memorial parks sit higher. Rhode Island Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Exeter provides plot-side items at no cost to eligible veterans.

Is a burial vault required in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island does not require vaults by statute. Most of the state's cemeteries require either a vault or the cheaper grave liner as written policy. Swan Point and a handful of other grounds accept a liner in lieu of a full vault — always ask for the written interment specification before paying a deposit. Rhode Island vault pricing spans $800 to $10,000; a liner typically saves $500–$1,500.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in Rhode Island?

The lowest-cost Rhode Island burials are typically at Rhode Island Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Exeter for eligible veterans (plot, vault, and marker at no cost), a Diocese of Providence parishioner plot (Gate of Heaven, Resurrection, or St. Francis), North Burial Ground on the City of Providence public fee schedule, or a direct burial booked against three written General Price Lists from Rhode Island funeral homes.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Rhode Island?

Because Rhode Island is compact, most families can realistically compare four or five cemeteries within 30 minutes. Request itemized written pricing from Swan Point (nonprofit), North Burial Ground (municipal), at least one Diocese of Providence cemetery (denominational), and at least one commercial memorial park. Ask each for the winter line item and for liner-in-lieu-of-vault policy. Cemeteries are not subject to the FTC Funeral Rule, so you have to request the itemized sheet directly.

What is the total cost of burial in Rhode Island including everything?

Every line item added up, a Rhode Island burial is approximately $9,000 service + $4,400 plot + $2,700 casket + $1,760 vault + $2,200 opening-and-closing + $1,000–$3,000 marker — $17,360 to $24,460. Rhode Island Veterans Memorial Cemetery eligibility takes roughly $8,000 off that figure; parishioner pricing at a Diocese of Providence cemetery typically saves $1,500 or more.

Does Rhode Island have green burial cemeteries?

Yes, availability is expanding. Swan Point has established a natural burial area, and several smaller church and municipal cemeteries around Rhode Island now accept burial without embalming and with biodegradable containers. Full certified conservation burial grounds are not common in Rhode Island itself; some Rhode Island families use grounds in neighboring Massachusetts or Connecticut instead.
