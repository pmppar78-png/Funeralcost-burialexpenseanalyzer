Burial Costs in Rhode Island (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Rhode Island burial page reworded throughout, with GPA-Burial and regulators explained. Region: Northeast

Rhode Island is a small state with high cemetery prices: our estimate for a complete burial is $20,560 to $25,060, with about $9,000 for the funeral home's service and $4,400 for a plot. State law also shapes what you pay, from the share of a plot price that must fund perpetual care to the seven-day burial-transit permit. This page covers each cost and the rules, and where a Rhode Island family can save.

Perpetual Care and Older Rhode Island Cemeteries

Rhode Island law protects the upkeep money built into a plot price. Under R.I. Gen. Laws § 23-18-8.1, perpetual care funds must be kept separate from a cemetery's other money, and at least 20% of the sale price of a lot, crypt or niche sold with perpetual care must go into that fund. Older municipal and religious cemeteries in Rhode Island were often set up on land donated or endowed long ago, and their pricing can still reflect that. A 15-minute longer drive to one of these can cut $1,760 or more off your cemetery bill.

Where Rhode Island Families Can Save

A few ways to keep a Rhode Island burial affordable:

  • Ask for an itemized price sheet. Rhode Island funeral homes must provide a General Price List. Cemeteries are not required to, though most will break out their charges if you ask for that instead of a package.
  • Get the winter charges in writing. Between December and March many Rhode Island cemeteries add a frozen-ground fee or store the casket until a spring burial; ask for that line before you sign.
  • Compare town and church cemeteries. Rhode Island has many nonprofit municipal and religious cemeteries, and their prices are worth checking against commercial ones.
  • Look a little beyond Providence. A cemetery 20 to 30 minutes away, in a county where land costs less, can charge much less for a plot and still be close enough to visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a burial in Rhode Island cost?

Our Rhode Island figures: about $9,000 for the funeral service, $4,400 for a plot, $2,700 for a casket, $1,760 for a vault and $2,200 for opening and closing, plus a headstone at $500 to $5,000. That totals $20,560 to $25,060.

Must a Rhode Island burial have a vault?

Most cemeteries require one by their own policy, not because of state law. A cheaper grave liner may meet the rule. Vaults in Rhode Island cost about $800 to $10,000.

What is the cheapest burial in Rhode Island?

Direct burial costs least, because it leaves out the viewing, the ceremony and embalming. Green or natural burial, with a biodegradable container and no embalming, comes next and is offered at a growing number of Rhode Island cemeteries. A casket bought from an independent seller, which the FTC Funeral Rule allows, often costs less than the funeral home's.

How do I shop for a Rhode Island cemetery?

Ask each cemetery in writing for the price of the plot and the opening and closing, and for its vault rule. The FTC Funeral Rule applies to funeral homes rather than most cemeteries, so a Rhode Island cemetery may not have to give you an itemized list, but asking costs nothing.

Are there costs beyond the Rhode Island estimate?

Yes. Certified death certificates, an obituary and flowers are paid by the funeral home as cash advance items and added to the bill, outside our $20,560 to $25,060 range.

Does Rhode Island offer green burial?

In some places. Rhode Island does not have green burial grounds statewide, and some cemeteries offer a hybrid section instead of a fully natural one. Our green burial guide explains the choices; call nearby cemeteries to ask what they allow now.

Rhode Island's seven-day burial-transit permit

Whoever first takes custody of the body, usually the funeral director, has to prepare a burial-transit permit within seven days of the death and before burial or removal from the state; both the funeral director and the certifying physician sign it. A permit issued by another state is accepted for a body brought into Rhode Island (R.I. Gen. Laws § 23-3-18; checked October 29, 2026).

  • At the cemetery: the sexton signs the permit before the burial and sends the month's permits to the town's local registrar by the fifth of the next month (216-RICR-10-10-1.25).
  • Moving a grave later: disinterment needs its own permit from the local registrar where the grave is.
  • Town rules: city and town councils may ban burials in built-up areas and pass their own burial-ground ordinances (R.I. Gen. Laws § 23-18-10), so a family plot needs the town's approval first.

Rhode Island burial regulators and help with costs

Funeral homes in Rhode Island are licensed through the Rhode Island Department of Health, Board of Funeral Directing/Embalming, which takes license questions and complaints at 401-222-4998. A cemetery may have a different regulator, so ask who oversees the one you choose.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count Rhode Island among the nine states requiring a funeral director, so a family may arrange a burial on its own, provided the burial-transit permit is in hand and the cemetery's rules are met. A 24-hour wait applies only if plans change to cremation (R.I. Gen. Laws § 23-3-18).

GPA-Burial

For a Rhode Island burial, GPA-Burial adds $150 toward a lot, $175 for opening and closing and $125 for a liner the cemetery requires to its $900 funeral payment (R.I. Gen. Laws § 40-6-3.10). (Source: R.I. Gen. Laws § 40-6-3.10; 218-RICR-20-00-3.) Social Security's $255 death payment goes to a surviving spouse or child who claims it within two years, not to the cemetery. Because the GPA amounts are fixed, get the cemetery's lot, opening and liner prices first and see how far they exceed what the program pays.

Rhode Island has no VA national cemetery taking burials, but eligible veterans can be buried at the state's Rhode Island Veterans Memorial Cemetery (401-268-3088), where the plot and marker are free (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps in Rhode Island

  1. Ask the cemetery for its own price sheet, separate from the funeral home's General Price List, and compare at least two.
  2. Find out whether the cemetery requires a liner, because GPA-Burial pays its $125 only then.
  3. For a family plot, check the town's burial-ground ordinance before anything else.
  4. For a veteran, call the Memorial Cemetery with the DD214 discharge record ready.
  5. Price complaints about a funeral home go to the board at 401-222-4998.
