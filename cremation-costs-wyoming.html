Keeping a Cremation in Wyoming Affordable

Cremation costs in Wyoming can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Wyoming: Request written General Price Lists.
  • Account for transport distance: In rural Wyoming, the nearest crematory may be 100+ miles from the funeral home. Ask whether the Wyoming quote includes the transfer from the place of death, and get any mileage rate in writing.

What to Know Before Choosing Cremation in Wyoming

When considering cremation in Wyoming, keep these important points in mind:

  • Get the Wyoming provider's General Price List before you agree to anything; the direct cremation line must state what it includes, and the price should cover transport, the cremation itself and a basic container for the ashes.
  • Wyoming families buying direct cremation do not need a casket; federal rules make the provider offer an unfinished wood box or another alternative container (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(b)). The permit comes first. Under W.S. 35-1-420, whoever first takes custody of the body obtains a burial-transit permit within seventy-two hours of death, and the local registrar may not issue a cremation permit until the death certificate has been filed.
  • Ask how long the Wyoming crematory usually takes to return the ashes once the permit is issued, and whether the quoted price includes delivery or only pickup.
In Wyoming you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Wyoming bring a container, and the WY Board of Embalming is who you call if a home in Wyoming refuses it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does cremation cost in Wyoming in 2026?

Cremation in Wyoming runs from roughly $1,600 for the simplest direct option up to about $5,600 when paired with a viewing or ceremony.

What types of cremation are available in Wyoming?

Most Wyoming providers offer direct cremation (no service) and cremation with a memorial or viewing. Wyoming permits alkaline hydrolysis under the name “chemical disposition,” with its own facility rules (W.S. 33-16-502 and 33-16-530 to -536), though a Wyoming family should ask the provider whether a chemical-disposition facility is actually within reach.

What legal requirements apply to cremation in Wyoming?

The core rule comes from 035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5. Wyoming has no statutory waiting period, but board rules bar cremation within 24 hours of death without the county coroner's written permission.

Complaints about how a crematory or funeral home handled these steps go to the Wyoming State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners (307-777-5403).

Is ash scattering legal in Wyoming?

Yes. Wyoming has no statute on scattering, so the landowner's permission is the main requirement. Wyoming State Parks require a special use permit for organized events, applied for at least 30 days ahead and approved by the park superintendent.

Yellowstone and Grand Teton follow 36 CFR 2.62, which allows scattering only by permit or in a designated area. The State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners (307-777-5403) licenses crematories.

Why is cremation so popular in Wyoming?

In Wyoming, cremation costs an estimated $5,300 less than a traditional burial and leaves the memorial date open; the state's 65% cremation rate is 1.6 points above the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

What is the timeline for cremation in Wyoming?

Wyoming has no statutory waiting period, but the State Board's rules bar cremation within 24 hours of death unless the county coroner gives written permission (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5). The death certificate and the disposition permit have to be done before the crematory starts.

Cremation itself can take under an hour or more than two hours depending on the body (Cremation Association of North America), and the remains then cool and are processed. If the nearest crematory is far away, ask whether transport adds a day.

Do I have to skip the service if I choose cremation in Wyoming?

No. This site's Wyoming estimate for a funeral with the casket present and cremation afterward is about $5,600, typically $4,480 to $7,280, which is $4,000 more than the roughly $1,600 for a direct cremation with no ceremony.

If relatives need time to travel, a memorial with the urn after a direct cremation avoids paying to hold the body. The Board's rules bar cremation within 24 hours of death without the county coroner's written permission (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5).

What a Wyoming coroner case costs the family

In Wyoming, a death that goes to the county coroner does not usually add coroner charges to a cremation bill. Laramie County, which includes Cheyenne, says investigation costs, autopsies, transportation of the deceased and consultations on the results are all provided at no cost to the family (Laramie County Coroner's Office brochure; checked October 29, 2026).

The exception is a private autopsy. If the coroner decides one is not needed, a family can still hire a pathologist of its choice, but at its own expense. The final written autopsy report usually takes 90 to 120 days, and a death certificate may first be filed as "pending investigation" in the meantime.

Cremation rules and help in Wyoming

Crematories and the funeral homes that sell cremation in Wyoming answer to the Wyoming State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners (307-777-5403).

Because Wyoming is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict family-directed care, the family may deal with a Wyoming crematory directly once the cremation permit has been issued.

What the law says about timing: Wyoming has no statutory waiting period, but board rules bar cremation within 24 hours of death without the county coroner's written permission (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5).

Wyoming's Department of Family Services pays up to $1,500 for the cremation of a person on POWER, SSI or Medicaid (W.S. 42-2-103(c)). (Source: W.S. 42-2-103(c); W.S. 18-3-504(c).) The $255 Social Security death payment to a qualifying Wyoming spouse or child would cover only part of the $1,600 direct cremation estimate above.

Cremated remains of an eligible Wyoming veteran can be placed in a VA national cemetery columbarium or in-ground site at no charge for the space. Options in Wyoming include Cheyenne National Cemetery (307-778-7316) and Oregon Trail State Veterans Cemetery (307-235-6673). See cem.va.gov.
