Cremation Costs in Wyoming (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 65%

Wyoming is the least populous state in the country, and its cremation economy reflects that. The 65% cremation rate runs above the national average, but crematory density is low, transport distances are long, and the state regulatory regime sits at the minimalist end. Direct cremation at $1,600 in Wyoming is more sensitive to mileage than to negotiation — and that is the through-line that shapes every number in this guide.

Cost ranges on this page draw on the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report, which documents a national cremation rate of roughly 60.5%. Consumer price disclosures follow the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and Wyoming-specific licensing is administered by the Wyoming Board of Embalming.

Wyoming's rural-transport math: why distance eats your savings

A family in Buffalo, Sundance, or Jackson can sit 150-300 miles from the nearest Wyoming crematory. The $1,600 advertised direct cremation rate almost always assumes a reasonable transport radius, and "reasonable" in Wyoming is generous but not unlimited. Providers like Wiederspahn-Radomsky in Cheyenne and Schrader Funeral Home typically quote a per-mile transport surcharge beyond 50-75 miles, and the real delivered cost for a far-corner family can run $1,900-$2,300 before any other line item. Ask for the transport figure as a separate number before signing; a provider that bundles transport into a flat figure is usually the better value for a remote family.

Minimal state regulation and the WY Funeral Service Practitioners Board

Wyoming's Funeral Service Practitioners Board licenses mortuary staff and crematories but operates with a noticeably lighter regulatory touch than neighboring states — appropriate for a population of fewer than 600,000. Consumer complaints are handled directly by the Board. The lighter regime means Wyoming does not impose additional state-level requirements on top of the federal FTC Funeral Rule, and it does not operate a separate cemetery regulator. Families comparing Wyoming to Colorado or Utah sometimes expect more consumer-protection infrastructure than actually exists; the federal Funeral Rule is the main protection.

Permissive home burial and scattering on BLM land

Wyoming is among the most permissive states in the country for home burial and on-property inurnment of cremated remains. Families with sufficient rural acreage can inter ashes on private land without a mortuary intermediary, subject to county health sign-off that is generally straightforward outside the Jackson area. Scattering on Wyoming's vast BLM land is permitted with a free permit, as are most national forest locations; Yellowstone and Grand Teton require advance coordination with the park. A family doing the scatter themselves pays nothing beyond the cremation; guided scattering services through Wyoming providers run $200-$600.

Mount Olivet Cheyenne and veteran interment at Cheyenne National

Mount Olivet Cemetery in Cheyenne accepts cremation inurnments in ground plots and columbarium niches; pricing runs at the Wyoming median. For eligible veterans, Cheyenne National Cemetery accepts cremated remains at no cost and sits directly on the southeast side of the capital. A Wiederspahn-Radomsky $1,600 direct cremation followed by Cheyenne National inurnment is the benchmark veteran path in the state and lands total out-of-pocket at roughly the cremation price alone.

County coroner indigent cremation in Wyoming

Wyoming handles indigent cases through the county coroner's office in each county rather than a centralized state benefit. When a family genuinely cannot pay for disposition, the county will arrange a basic direct cremation, typically through a contracted local provider. The process varies county by county — Laramie County (Cheyenne) and Natrona County (Casper) have the most established procedures; rural counties handle cases more informally. Families facing this situation should call the county coroner's office before signing anything with a funeral home.

Answers to the Questions We Hear Most

What does cremation cost in Wyoming in 2026?

Cremation in Wyoming runs from roughly $1,600 for the simplest direct option up to about $5,600 when paired with a viewing or ceremony. Urns, memorial services, and interment of ashes are priced separately — typically $50-$3,000 for an urn, $500-$2,000 for a memorial, and $200-$600 for scattering or inurnment. Your final number depends on which tier you pick.

What types of cremation are available in Wyoming?

Families in Wyoming can choose from three main types: direct cremation ($1,600) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service (ceremony held after cremation, often more flexible on timing); and traditional cremation ($5,600) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each option can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget.

What legal requirements apply to cremation in Wyoming?

A 24-48 hour waiting period applies before cremation in Wyoming, along with written authorization from the legal next of kin. Some Wyoming counties require a medical examiner's permit before release. Pacemakers, implanted defibrillators, and certain other implants are removed beforehand. And under the federal Funeral Rule, you do not need to buy a full casket — an alternative cremation container is sufficient.

Is ash scattering legal in Wyoming?

In most cases yes, but the specifics depend on where. In Wyoming you can generally scatter on private land (with owner permission) or at sea beyond 3 nautical miles under EPA rules. National park scattering often requires a free permit. Check Wyoming local ordinances and any park-specific rules before proceeding. Some families prefer columbarium niches, urn burial, or memorial jewelry to avoid the permitting question entirely.

Why is cremation so popular in Wyoming?

The drivers for Wyoming are the usual combination: a meaningful cost delta versus traditional burial, more freedom on the memorial side (when, where, how), and a gradual cultural shift over the past two decades. At 65%, the state sits close to where national demand has been heading.

What is the timeline for cremation in Wyoming?

The cremation itself runs 2-3 hours in Wyoming. Factor in the mandatory 24-48 hour waiting period before cremation, then 3-7 business days for the crematory to process paperwork and prepare the ashes for pickup or delivery. A direct cremation ($1,600) is the fastest end-to-end because no ceremony sits between death and cremation.

Do I have to skip the service if I choose cremation in Wyoming?

Not at all. Wyoming providers offer cremation with a full service — visitation, ceremony, and then cremation instead of burial — for about $5,600. Memorial services after cremation are also common, letting families choose any venue or date. Only direct cremation at $1,600 omits the service entirely.
