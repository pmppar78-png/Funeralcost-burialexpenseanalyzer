Cremation Costs in Wyoming (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Wyoming cremation guide reworded around the 24-hour rule, transport charges and the DFS payment, with an unsupported regional comparison and crematory-distance claim removed. Cremation Rate: 65%

We estimate a Wyoming direct cremation at about $1,600 and a funeral followed by cremation at about $5,600. Cremation is the usual choice here: our 65% rate is a little above the 63.4% NFDA projected nationally for 2025. This page covers what each item costs, the 24-hour rule and the coroner's role, the state's payment of up to $1,500 for people on POWER, SSI or Medicaid, and the veterans cemeteries.

Cutting the Cost of a Wyoming Cremation

Two things to do before you choose:

  • Ask three providers for price lists. Every Wyoming funeral home has to give out its General Price List, and the direct cremation line is the one to compare.
  • Pin down the transfer charge. Ask whether the quote covers moving the body from the place of death to the crematory, and if mileage is extra, get the rate per mile on paper.

Distance and Transport in Wyoming

Wyoming is a big state with few people, so the crematory may be a long drive from the place of death. Some providers include that transfer in the $1,600 direct cremation price and others bill mileage on top. Outside Cheyenne, ask how the transfer is charged before you compare quotes.

Before You Agree to a Wyoming Cremation

Check these with any Wyoming provider:

  • Get the Wyoming provider's General Price List before you agree to anything; the direct cremation line must state what it includes, and the price should cover transport, the cremation itself and a basic container for the ashes.
  • Wyoming families buying direct cremation do not need a casket; federal rules make the provider offer an unfinished wood box or another alternative container (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(b)). The permit comes first. Under W.S. 35-1-420, whoever first takes custody of the body obtains a burial-transit permit within seventy-two hours of death, and the local registrar may not issue a cremation permit until the death certificate has been filed.
  • Ask how soon the ashes come back once the permit is in hand, and whether the price covers delivery, which matters when the crematory is far away.
You may bring your own urn in Wyoming; the FTC Funeral Rule bars a provider from refusing it or adding a handling fee, and the State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners hears complaints if one does.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Wyoming cremation cost?

By our estimates a Wyoming cremation costs about $1,600 with no service and about $5,600 with a viewing or funeral first.

What cremation choices are there in Wyoming?

Wyoming providers sell direct cremation and cremation with a memorial or viewing. State law also allows alkaline hydrolysis, which it calls chemical disposition, under its own facility rules (W.S. 33-16-502 and 33-16-530 to -536); ask whether any such facility is close enough to use.

What has to happen before a Wyoming cremation?

There is no waiting period in the statutes, but the State Board's rules forbid cremation in the first 24 hours after death unless the county coroner gives written permission (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5).

The Wyoming State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners (307-777-5403) takes complaints about how a crematory or funeral home handled these steps.

May ashes be scattered in Wyoming?

Yes. No Wyoming statute governs scattering, so permission from whoever owns the land is what counts. For an organized gathering in a state park, Wyoming State Parks want a special use permit request at least 30 days ahead, approved by the superintendent.

In Yellowstone and Grand Teton, 36 CFR 2.62 allows scattering only with a permit or in a designated area. Crematories are licensed by the State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners (307-777-5403).

Why do most Wyoming families pick cremation?

Our figures put cremation about $5,300 under a traditional burial in Wyoming, and the memorial can be set for whenever family can travel. The 65% rate we use sits 1.6 points over the 63.4% national figure NFDA projected for 2025.

How long does a cremation take in Wyoming?

The first 24 hours are off limits: Board rules bar cremation in that window unless the county coroner agrees in writing (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5). The death certificate and the permit must both be complete before the crematory begins.

According to the Cremation Association of North America the cremation runs from under an hour to more than two, depending on the body, and the remains then cool and are processed. A long drive to the crematory can add a day.

Can a Wyoming family hold a service and still cremate?

No. We estimate a Wyoming funeral with the casket present and cremation afterwards at about $5,600, typically $4,480 to $7,280, about $4,000 above a direct cremation at roughly $1,600.

When relatives live far off, a direct cremation followed by a memorial with the urn saves paying to keep the body while they travel. Remember the 24-hour rule unless the coroner waives it in writing (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5).

What a Wyoming coroner case costs the family

In Wyoming, a death that goes to the county coroner does not usually add coroner charges to a cremation bill. Laramie County, which includes Cheyenne, says investigation costs, autopsies, transportation of the deceased and consultations on the results are all provided at no cost to the family (Laramie County Coroner's Office brochure; checked October 29, 2026).

The exception is a private autopsy. If the coroner decides one is not needed, a family can still hire a pathologist of its choice, but at its own expense. The final written autopsy report usually takes 90 to 120 days, and a death certificate may first be filed as "pending investigation" in the meantime.

Wyoming cremation oversight and help with the cost

Wyoming crematories and the funeral homes that arrange cremations are licensed by the Wyoming State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners (307-777-5403), which also takes complaints.

Wyoming is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as restricting family-directed care, so after the permit is issued a family may arrange things with a crematory directly.

The statutes set no waiting period, but Board rules forbid cremation within 24 hours of death without the county coroner's written permission (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5).

State help with the cost

The Department of Family Services pays up to $1,500 toward the cremation of a person who was on POWER, SSI or Medicaid and left too little to pay, after other resources are used, and up to $500 more to carry a qualifying veteran to the veterans cemetery. County commissioners cover other people without means (W.S. 42-2-103(c); W.S. 18-3-504(c)). A surviving spouse or child who qualifies can also claim Social Security's $255, a small share of the $1,600 we estimate for a direct cremation.

An eligible Wyoming veteran's ashes can be placed at Cheyenne National Cemetery (307-778-7316) or Oregon Trail State Veterans Cemetery in Evansville (307-235-6673) with no charge for the space; see cem.va.gov.

Wyoming checklist

  1. Get the transfer and any mileage charge in writing along with the crematory fee and container.
  2. Plan for the 24-hour rule and the permit when you set a memorial date.
  3. If the person was on POWER, SSI or Medicaid, ask DFS to confirm its payment before you book.
  4. Keep the Board's number, 307-777-5403, for a complaint.
