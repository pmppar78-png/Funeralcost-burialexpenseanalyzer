Funeral Costs in South Carolina: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: South

J. Henry Stuhr Inc. has been burying Charleston families since 1865 — it is one of the oldest continuously-operating funeral homes in the American South — and its presence, along with McAlister-Smith, Dunbar, and a handful of other long-standing firms, defines how the state's funeral market works. South Carolina splits regulation between the South Carolina Board of Funeral Service (inside the SC Department of Labor, Licensing, and Regulation, covering funeral directors and embalmers) and a separate SC Cemetery Board that enforces the Perpetual Care Cemetery Act; both operate alongside the federal FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453). Cost averages cited come from the NFDA 2024 GPL Study and Funeral Consumers Alliance (FCA) survey data. Traditional service pricing averages $6,700, below the national median, and the 45% cremation rate sits on the lower end of the Southern range.

The Perpetual Care Cemetery Act and its enforcement

South Carolina's Perpetual Care Cemetery Act, enforced by the SC Cemetery Board, requires every commercial cemetery to deposit a portion of each plot sale into an irrevocable perpetual-care trust. The Act sets minimum deposit percentages and audits trust balances, which gives South Carolina families more assurance about ongoing cemetery maintenance than families in states with looser trust requirements. When reviewing a cemetery contract, verify that the cemetery's Board registration is current — the SC LLR website publishes active registrations, and an expired or suspended registration is a serious warning sign.

Magnolia Cemetery, Elmwood, and the Lowcountry's historic burial layer

Magnolia Cemetery in Charleston, founded in 1850, and Elmwood Cemetery in Columbia are the two most prominent association cemeteries in the state, both with significant Confederate-era sections and active modern burial areas. Magnolia's nontraditional layout — live oaks, marsh overlooks, and century-old family plots — draws families willing to pay a modest premium for the setting. Bethel Methodist Cemetery, St. Philip's (the "country churchyard" of colonial Charleston), and the Jewish Coming Street Cemetery (one of the oldest Jewish cemeteries in the South, established 1762) each serve specific community traditions.

Florence National, Beaufort National, and the coastal veteran corridor

South Carolina has two active national cemeteries: Florence National Cemetery in the Pee Dee region and Beaufort National Cemetery on the coast. Beaufort is one of the oldest national cemeteries in the country, dating to 1863, and serves both Lowcountry and Georgia-border families. Florence National covers the Pee Dee, Midlands, and much of the Upstate. The state also maintains M.J. "Dolly" Cooper Veterans Cemetery in Anderson, which provides free burial for eligible South Carolina veterans.

DSS burial assistance and the funeral-home cooperation question

The South Carolina Department of Social Services administers limited indigent burial assistance through county offices, typically covering transportation, basic cremation or direct burial, and minimal service fees up to approximately $1,200 to $1,500. Crucially, DSS does not pay the family — the benefit flows to a participating funeral home, and not every SC funeral home accepts DSS cases. Before arranging services, call the county DSS office and ask which specific funeral homes in the county are currently accepting DSS-paid arrangements; the list turns over as providers enter and leave the program.

Charges most families only see once they sign

Many South Carolina families are surprised by charges that appear after the initial quote. Here are the most common hidden funeral fees to watch for:

  • Casket handling fee — Some funeral homes add a surcharge for caskets purchased elsewhere, despite this being illegal under the FTC Funeral Rule
  • Mandatory embalming — Funeral homes may claim embalming is required. In South Carolina, it is almost never legally required. Know your rights
  • Cemetery "perpetual care" fees — An ongoing maintenance charge, typically $200–$1,000, added on top of the plot price
  • Vault/liner surcharge — Cemeteries may require a specific vault brand or type, inflating costs by $500–$2,000+
  • Documentation and filing fees — Death certificates, permits, and filing fees can add $200–$600
  • Weekend/holiday premium — Services held outside business hours may carry a 15–25% surcharge
  • "Package" markups — Bundled packages often include services you don't need. Always compare line-by-line. What funeral homes don't tell you

Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in South Carolina must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in South Carolina has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Your state attorney general's office can also assist with consumer protection complaints.

Gullah, AME, and Black Southern funeral traditions

South Carolina's Black funeral traditions remain unusually strong in the Lowcountry and the Pee Dee, where AME, Baptist, and Gullah Geechee communities have preserved distinct homegoing rituals. Multi-day wakes, large church services, and extensive repast meals are common, and families generally expect the funeral home to coordinate with the church and with community elders. Several South Carolina funeral homes — Leevy's in Columbia, Fielding's in Charleston — have served these communities for more than a century, and they price these traditional bundles at a discount versus what a newer provider would quote.

Questions and Answers

What is the average price of a funeral in South Carolina right now?

Per the NFDA 2024 GPL Study and South Carolina Board of Funeral Service licensure filings, current averages come in at $6,700 for a traditional funeral, $5,400 for cremation with a service, and from $1,600 for direct cremation — with the traditional figure sitting 15% below the $7,848 US median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $3,000 when applicable. Because South Carolina pricing is not standardized, the same service can swing several thousand dollars between neighboring funeral homes, so always request the itemized GPL the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) entitles you to.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in South Carolina?

Yes — substantially. In South Carolina, direct cremation at $1,600 typically saves families roughly $5,100 versus the $6,700 traditional funeral plus cemetery costs. South Carolina's cremation rate sits at 45%, below the national average around 60%. Many Southern families have strong traditions around homegoing services and church-based funerals, which can affect service choices and costs.

What rights do South Carolina families have when arranging a funeral?

Under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), every South Carolina funeral home must give you an itemized price list, allow you to decline any service you do not want, accept caskets or urns brought in from outside providers without surcharges, and avoid false claims about what the law requires. The South Carolina Board of Funeral Service enforces additional state-level protections that apply locally.

How do I keep funeral costs down in South Carolina?

Three moves cut the most: compare at least three South Carolina General Price Lists side by side before choosing a provider, strip packages down to only the services you actually want, and choose direct cremation ($1,600) if cost is the priority. Check with local churches and fraternal organizations — in South Carolina, congregation-owned cemeteries and family sections are often priced well below commercial memorial parks. Veteran burial benefits apply at any national cemetery and some state veterans' cemeteries in South Carolina. Then confirm eligibility for veteran, Medicaid, and Social Security survivor benefits.

Does South Carolina require embalming?

South Carolina law does not require embalming in most situations. It is generally a choice, not a legal obligation — though a funeral home may insist on it as an internal policy for certain open-casket viewings. Refrigeration is almost always a valid substitute. Some Southern funeral homes may emphasize traditional open-casket services, but embalming remains optional by law in most cases. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, no provider may claim embalming is legally required without pointing to a specific statute.

Is South Carolina cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

South Carolina lands 15% below the US median of $7,848 for a traditional funeral, with the state average at $6,700. Costs here are below the national average, reflecting the lower cost of living in the region and the South region trends moderate to lower than the country overall. Which funeral option costs the least in South Carolina?

Direct cremation carries the lowest price tag in South Carolina, from roughly $1,600. You skip the viewing, ceremony, and embalming — transport, cremation, and return of the ashes are all that are included. Direct burial is the next step up at about $4,020 and is still considerably cheaper than a traditional service.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in South Carolina?

Ask three or more South Carolina providers for their General Price Lists and compare line by line; call the local Funeral Consumers Alliance chapter for vetted recommendations; look specifically for direct cremation specialists, which often undercut full-service providers; request the simplest or "basic services only" package; and check for nonprofit and cooperative funeral homes in your part of South Carolina.
