Funeral Costs in South Carolina: What Families Pay in 2026

Reviewed: February 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, South Carolina Funeral Board Region: South

South Carolina's funeral traditions run as deep as the live oak roots that shade its historic cemeteries. From the distinctive Gullah/Geechee burial customs of the Lowcountry to the Baptist homegoing celebrations that mark communities from the Upstate to the coast, the Palmetto State maintains some of the most culturally significant funeral practices in the American South. Funeral costs remain below the national average, and the state's growing population -- fueled by retirees and newcomers from the Northeast and Midwest -- is gradually reshaping the market.

Funeral Cost Breakdown in South Carolina

At $6,700 for a traditional funeral, South Carolina sits well below the national median of $7,848. The state's lower cost of living, strong tradition of family-owned funeral homes, and Southern Baptist cultural influence all help keep prices accessible. However, costs are climbing in the state's rapidly growing metro areas -- Charleston, Columbia, and Greenville -- as population growth drives demand and raises overhead.

What Drives Funeral Costs in South Carolina

The South Carolina Board of Funeral Service regulates all funeral establishments in the state. South Carolina's funeral market remains heavily dominated by independent, family-owned providers, many of which have served their communities for generations. Corporate chain presence is growing in the larger metros but has not reached the saturation seen in some other states.

African-American funeral traditions are particularly significant in South Carolina, where they carry deep cultural and historical weight. The Gullah/Geechee communities of the Sea Islands and Lowcountry maintain distinctive funeral customs that can be traced back to West African traditions, including specific grave decoration practices, "settin' up" (community vigil), and elaborate homegoing celebrations. Historically Black funeral homes in cities like Charleston, Columbia, and Orangeburg serve as vital community institutions.

South Carolina's strong Baptist tradition shapes funeral practices across both Black and white communities, favoring church-based services with hymns, scripture readings, and community meals following burial. The state's growing retiree population -- particularly along the coast and in the Greenville-Spartanburg area -- brings diverse funeral preferences from their states of origin. Charleston's military presence (Joint Base Charleston, The Citadel) creates steady demand for military funeral honors and veteran burial services.

Major Cities in South Carolina

South Carolina's three major metros -- Charleston, Columbia, and Greenville -- represent distinct markets with somewhat higher costs than rural areas. The coastal resort areas (Myrtle Beach, Hilton Head) see seasonal variation. Rural communities in the Pee Dee and Midlands regions offer the most affordable options.

Cremation vs. Burial in South Carolina

South Carolina's 45% cremation rate is below the national average, reflecting the strong influence of Baptist and other Protestant traditions that favor burial. However, the influx of retirees from states with higher cremation rates is gradually shifting the statewide average upward. In Charleston and coastal communities, the cremation rate is somewhat higher than the statewide average, while rural inland areas maintain a stronger preference for traditional burial.

South Carolina Funeral Regulations

The South Carolina Board of Funeral Service oversees licensing and regulation. Key regulations include:

  • No embalming requirement -- South Carolina does not mandate embalming by law.
  • 24-hour cremation waiting period -- South Carolina requires a 24-hour wait before cremation.
  • Preneed protections -- South Carolina provides consumer protections for preneed contracts, including trust requirements.
  • Home funerals permitted with restrictions -- South Carolina allows limited family-directed funeral care.
  • Cemetery regulation -- South Carolina's Perpetual Care Cemetery Act provides oversight for cemetery operations and maintenance funds.

Your Consumer Rights in South Carolina

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in South Carolina must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

South Carolina families can file complaints with the SC Board of Funeral Service, the South Carolina Department of Consumer Affairs, or the FTC. The Funeral Consumers Alliance provides national guidance. South Carolina's community-oriented funeral industry generally provides fair pricing, but comparison shopping remains advisable, particularly in the growing metro areas.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in South Carolina?

The average traditional funeral in South Carolina costs approximately $6,700. A funeral with cremation averages $5,400, while direct cremation typically costs $1,600. Cemetery plots average $3,000 additional. Costs vary by provider and location within the state.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in South Carolina?

Yes. Direct cremation in South Carolina averages $1,600 compared to $6,700 for a traditional burial service. The cremation rate in South Carolina is 45%.

What are my consumer rights at South Carolina funeral homes?

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, all funeral homes in South Carolina must provide an itemized General Price List, allow you to choose only services you want, and accept third-party caskets without penalty. File complaints with the state funeral board or the Attorney General.

How can I save on funeral costs in South Carolina?

Compare prices from at least three providers. Consider direct cremation or direct burial if a full service is not essential. Purchase caskets from third-party retailers. Check veteran benefits, state assistance, and community resources. Always request the General Price List from every provider.

Does South Carolina require embalming?

No. South Carolina does not legally require embalming. Refrigeration is an available alternative at most funeral homes. Some providers may have internal policies for viewings, but these are not state mandates.

What are Gullah/Geechee funeral traditions in South Carolina?

The Gullah/Geechee communities of South Carolina's Sea Islands and Lowcountry maintain funeral traditions with direct connections to West African practices. These may include "settin' up" (an overnight community vigil), specific grave decoration with personal objects, singing of spirituals, and burial in family plots on ancestral land. These traditions are culturally significant and increasingly recognized as important American heritage. Funeral homes serving Lowcountry communities are typically experienced in accommodating these customs.

How is South Carolina's growing retiree population affecting funeral costs?

The influx of retirees, particularly from the Northeast and Midwest, is gradually increasing demand and costs in popular retirement destinations like Charleston, Hilton Head, Myrtle Beach, and the Greenville area. These newcomers often bring expectations shaped by higher-cost markets and may seek services that differ from traditional South Carolina funeral practices. This demographic shift is also contributing to the growing cremation rate, as many retirees come from states where cremation is more common.
