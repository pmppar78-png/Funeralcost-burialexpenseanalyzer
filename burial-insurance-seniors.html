Age-Based Coverage Options

Burial insurance — also called final expense insurance — is designed specifically for seniors who want to ensure their funeral costs are covered. These policies typically offer $5,000 to $25,000 in coverage with simplified underwriting that makes approval easier for older adults.

Ages 50–65

$30 – $80/month
  • Widest range of policy options
  • Best rates available
  • Most carriers offer instant approval
  • Coverage from $5,000 to $25,000
  • No medical exam required

Ages 66–75

$60 – $150/month
  • Simplified issue policies widely available
  • Some health questions required
  • Coverage from $5,000 to $20,000
  • Premiums locked — never increase
  • Many carriers still offer same-day approval

Ages 76–85

$100 – $250/month
  • Guaranteed issue policies available (no health questions)
  • 2-year waiting period on some policies
  • Coverage typically $5,000 to $15,000
  • Higher premiums reflect age risk
  • Valuable for those with health conditions

Guaranteed Issue vs. Simplified Issue

Understanding the difference between these two policy types is crucial for choosing the right burial insurance:

Important: If you can qualify for a simplified issue policy, it is almost always the better choice due to immediate full coverage and lower premiums. Guaranteed issue should be reserved for those who cannot pass health screening questions.

How Much Coverage Do You Need?

The right coverage amount depends on your expected funeral costs and any additional expenses you want covered:

  • $5,000 – $7,500 — Covers direct cremation or a modest burial in most states
  • $10,000 – $15,000 — Covers a traditional funeral with burial in most markets
  • $15,000 – $25,000 — Covers a traditional funeral plus cemetery costs, headstone, and remaining medical bills

Burial insurance after 50: the math, the waiting period, and where this page stops

This page is only about age. The funeral insurance guide explains what these policies are. The comparison page sets final-expense insurance next to preneed contracts. The provider page names carriers. The cremation insurance guide is for people who know they want cremation and a smaller benefit. If you are comparing monthly prices, read the date label on the provider page before you treat any dollar figure as a live quote.

What you are buying

Burial insurance for seniors is a small whole life policy, often sold as final expense insurance. The benefit is cash to a person you name. That person may spend it on a funeral or on anything else. The policy does not hire the funeral home, and the funeral home does not have to accept the face amount as the price. Premiums are level if the policy says they are level. Read that sentence on the specimen policy, not in an ad.

A waiting period changes the product

Simplified-issue policies ask a few health questions and often pay the full benefit from the first day. Guaranteed-issue policies skip the health questions and often pay only a return of premium, sometimes plus a little interest, if death from illness happens in the first two years. Accidental death is often covered in full immediately. If you are 78 and buying because you are ill, a two-year graded benefit may not do the job you think you are paying for. Ask the agent to say, in one sentence, what your family receives if you die in month six from a diagnosed illness. Write it down.

Do the lifetime arithmetic before you buy

This is arithmetic, not a rate quote. A premium of $80 a month is $960 a year. Over ten years that is $9,600, before any cash value. If the face amount is $10,000, you have nearly paid for the funeral yourself, and you still carry the risk that you stop paying and the policy lapses. A 55-year-old paying a lower premium for a longer time can come out differently. Nobody on this page can know your premium. The point of the senior guide is to make you multiply before you sign. Put the premium and the face amount on paper. If the product of premium times the number of years you reasonably expect to pay is close to the face amount, a savings account or a payable-on-death account may be the cleaner tool. It does not lapse, and it does not have a graded period.

How much coverage the funeral medians suggest

The NFDA 2023 study, latest available, puts a viewing and burial at a median of $8,300 before the cemetery and the vault, and direct cremation at $2,645 or $2,750. A $10,000 policy covers the first of those medians with a little room and more than covers direct cremation. It does not cover a cemetery plot in a high-cost city. Look at our state cost pages for the local funeral-home figures we publish, and ask a cemetery for the plot price, which NFDA left out of the $8,300 on purpose.

Free look and lapse

State insurance law gives a free-look window, often 10 to 30 days, during which you can cancel for a refund. The number of days is on the policy cover and in your state's insurance department brochure, not in a national statute we can quote as one figure. If you miss a premium later, ask about the grace period before you assume the policy died. Reinstatement may require new health questions you can no longer pass.

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners publishes consumer guides on life insurance. Start at naic.org/consumer and use your state insurance department's complaint form if a seller will not give you the graded-benefit terms in writing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is burial insurance for seniors?

Burial insurance (final expense insurance) is a small whole life insurance policy designed to cover funeral and end-of-life costs. Policies range from $5,000 to $25,000 and are specifically marketed to seniors ages 50-85. Unlike term life insurance, burial insurance never expires and premiums never increase.

Can you get burial insurance at age 80?

Yes. Many insurance carriers offer burial insurance to adults up to age 85. At age 80, you can typically get $5,000-$15,000 in coverage. Guaranteed issue policies require no health questions, though they may include a 2-3 year waiting period before the full death benefit is available.

How much does burial insurance cost per month?

Monthly premiums depend on age, health, and coverage amount. A healthy 60-year-old might pay $40-$60/month for $10,000 in coverage. A 75-year-old might pay $80-$130 for the same amount. Guaranteed issue policies cost 30-50% more than simplified issue due to the additional risk the insurer assumes.

Is burial insurance worth it for seniors?

Burial insurance can be worth it if you want to ensure your family is not burdened with funeral costs. The average funeral costs $8,300-$10,000+. Without insurance or savings dedicated to this purpose, families may struggle with unexpected expenses during an already difficult time. However, if you have sufficient savings or existing life insurance, a separate burial policy may not be necessary.

What happens to burial insurance money not used for the funeral?

Burial insurance pays a lump sum to your named beneficiary. There is no requirement to use the money specifically for funeral costs — your beneficiary can use the funds for any purpose including outstanding medical bills, debts, or living expenses.
