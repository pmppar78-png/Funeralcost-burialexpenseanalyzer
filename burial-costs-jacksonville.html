Burial Costs in Jacksonville, Florida (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Florida

Jacksonville is Florida's largest city by land area — 874 square miles straddling the St. Johns River and stretching from the Atlantic dunes at Atlantic Beach out to the tidewater flats of the Westside. That geographic spread is the story of burial pricing here. A plot near the river at Evergreen Cemetery or at Oaklawn looks nothing like a plot in a small Duval County church cemetery on the far Westside, and the totals can differ by $7,000 or more. Jacksonville National Cemetery on Lannie Road sits at the northern edge of the city and is free for eligible veterans. The Florida Department of Financial Services, Division of Funeral, Cemetery and Consumer Services is the regulator, and its pre-need burial contract enforcement is active in Duval County.

Traditional burial totals in Jacksonville sit between $15,372 and $21,609. The upper end is driven by premium sections at Oaklawn, by mausoleum entombment at Riverside Memorial Park, and by buying the casket through the funeral home showroom rather than shipping one in.

The Jacksonville cemetery landscape

Evergreen Cemetery, established in 1881 on the west side of Main Street just north of downtown, is the city's oldest active cemetery and still sells single plots in the $2,800–$4,800 range in standard sections. Oaklawn Cemetery (Edgewood Avenue South) sits in a similar tier. Riverside Memorial Park, on the south side of the river, runs more expensive because of its mausoleum inventory and its large Catholic section.

Hardage-Giddens operates several cemeteries and funeral homes across Duval County under a chain umbrella; Dignity Memorial also owns a portfolio in the metro. Independent operators — including Holly Hill Funeral Home on Old Kings Road, Fry Chapel, and several smaller Westside providers — tend to price $1,000–$2,500 below the corporate quotes on identical service packages. The FTC Funeral Rule gives every Jacksonville family the right to request a General Price List, on the phone or in person.

Smaller church cemeteries on the Westside, in Arlington, and in southern Duval near Mandarin continue to sell plots under $1,800 to parishioners or neighborhood residents — information that rarely shows up in a Google search and requires a direct call to the congregation or to St. Johns County clerk's offices for cemeteries straddling the county line.

The St. Johns River and the Jacksonville water table

Duval County's water table runs shallow across most of Jacksonville, particularly east of I-95, along the river corridor, in the Beaches, and through the Southside. Cemeteries in these areas require sealed concrete vaults rather than grave liners — Florida administrative rules effectively force vault choice once the water table sits within a few feet of the surface. This pushes vault pricing in Jacksonville to the higher end of the state range, typically $1,400–$3,200. In drier inland sections (parts of the Northwest and the far Westside), a concrete liner can still be acceptable at $900–$1,200.

Jacksonville National Cemetery. On Lannie Road just off I-295 in northern Duval County, Jacksonville National Cemetery is one of four VA national cemeteries in Florida (the others sit in Bushnell, Lake Worth, and Sarasota). For eligible veterans, it covers the grave, opening-and-closing, liner, and marker at no cost. Spouses are buried alongside at no charge except the plot fee waived under federal benefits. Scheduling is handled through the National Cemetery Scheduling Office at 1-800-535-1117.

Where the spread actually comes from

  1. Cemetery choice. Oaklawn premium versus a church cemetery on the Westside can swing plot and opening-closing by $3,000–$5,000.
  2. Casket sourcing. The Jacksonville independents routinely accept online caskets shipped from third-party retailers; Walmart and Costco ship into Duval County. Savings run $800–$2,500.
  3. Vault type. A sealed vault east of I-95 is rarely optional; a liner may still be acceptable in drier inland sections.
  4. Direct burial versus full visitation. Direct burial strips facility use, staff hours, and embalming. In Jacksonville that's typically a $3,500–$6,000 swing.
  5. Corporate chain versus independent. Two Jacksonville GPLs from a Dignity Memorial location and an independent often sit $1,500–$3,500 apart on identical packages.

Prairie Creek and Florida's natural-burial option

Prairie Creek Conservation Cemetery in Gainesville — about 75 minutes southwest of Jacksonville — is the nearest dedicated green burial ground. No embalming, no vault, biodegradable container, and a plot price typically $2,800–$3,800 plus $800–$1,200 for opening-and-closing. For Jacksonville families willing to make the drive, the total can close under $7,000 with a direct-burial provider handling transport from Duval County.

Common Questions

How much does burial cost in Jacksonville?

Expect $15,372 to $21,609 for a traditional Jacksonville burial in 2026. A direct burial at an independent Westside funeral home paired with a church cemetery plot can close under $8,000. A full-visitation service at Riverside Memorial Park with a premium casket and mausoleum entombment can reach $24,000 or more. Jacksonville National Cemetery removes the plot, marker, and opening-and-closing costs entirely for eligible veterans.

Is a burial vault required in Jacksonville?

Florida statute does not mandate a vault, but almost every Duval County cemetery east of I-95 and along the St. Johns River requires a sealed concrete vault because the water table sits within a few feet of the surface. In drier inland sections a concrete liner can still be acceptable. Evergreen, Oaklawn, Riverside Memorial Park, and Hardage-Giddens chapels all enforce sealed vault policies in their flood-prone sections — confirm in writing with the specific cemetery.

What are the cheapest burial options in Jacksonville?

The lowest-cost routes in Jacksonville: direct burial through an independent Westside provider, plots at church cemeteries in Arlington or southern Duval, and online casket purchase shipped to the funeral home under the FTC Funeral Rule. For eligible veterans, Jacksonville National Cemetery removes most of the bill. Green burial is available at Prairie Creek Conservation Cemetery in Gainesville, 75 minutes southwest, where no embalming, no vault, and a biodegradable container bring totals near $7,000.

How do Jacksonville burial costs compare to Florida?

Jacksonville runs about 5% above the Florida statewide average — funeral service $8,190 vs. $7,800 statewide, plot $3,780 vs. $3,600. Miami is higher than Jacksonville on both lines (funeral $9,750, plot $4,500) largely due to real estate costs and denser urban cemetery inventory. Tampa and Orlando sit roughly at parity with Jacksonville. Duval County's sprawl means families willing to drive to western or southern Duval can recover most of the city premium at small church and community cemeteries.
