Cremation Costs in Las Vegas, Nevada (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Nevada Cremation Rate: 72%

Clark County's 72% cremation rate is the highest of any major metro in this series — higher than Miami, higher than Seattle, higher than the Nevada state figure itself. The transience of the Las Vegas population, a large retiree base that relocated without family plots, and decades of consolidation under Palm Mortuary all push the market toward simple, lower-cost dispositions. Davis Funeral Homes operates multiple Vegas locations under Service Corporation International; Palm Mortuary runs six Clark County campuses combining funeral homes, cemeteries, and crematories; and Simple Tribute Funeral Services and Kraft-Sussman cater to the direct-cremation segment.

National context: the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA 2024 Annual Report put the U.S. cremation rate near 60.5 percent, and Las Vegas providers operate under the oversight of the Nevada State Board of Funeral and Cemetery Services. Families ordering death certificates typically deal with the Clark County Office of Vital Records.

Palm Mortuary, Davis, and the consolidation story

Palm Mortuary is the dominant cemetery-and-funeral-home operator in southern Nevada, with campuses at Eastern Avenue, Cheyenne, Northwest, Henderson, Boulder Highway, and Green Valley — most combining a cemetery, funeral home, and in-house crematory. Davis Funeral Homes operates three locations including the flagship Rancho Drive chapel. Kraft-Sussman Funeral Services on Eastern Avenue serves the Jewish community. Simple Tribute and Affordable Cremation publish flat-rate direct cremation below $1,500. Bunker Family Mortuary in Henderson handles much of the LDS population in the southeast valley. The consolidation under SCI and Palm's regional operator means posted pricing is relatively consistent across the metro, but the direct-cremation-only providers materially undercut both chains.

NRS 451 and the unified Nevada board

Nevada Revised Statutes Chapter 451 governs disposition of human remains. The state requires written authorization from the legal next of kin, removal of pacemakers before cremation, and a minimum 48-hour waiting period unless waived by the Clark County medical examiner. Nevada is unusual in licensing funeral directors, embalmers, crematory operators, and cemeteries under a single Nevada Funeral and Cemetery Services Board (fcsb.nv.gov), rather than separate bodies. Deaths at hotel-casinos on the Strip route through the Clark County Coroner's Office at 1704 Pinto Lane, which handles an unusually high volume of out-of-state deaths — families often coordinate cremation in Las Vegas followed by ash transport home.

Red Rock, Mojave, and the scattering question

Red Rock Canyon National Conservation Area permits scattering with a free written permit from the BLM Southern Nevada District Office — the most popular public-land scattering location in the metro. Lake Mead National Recreation Area allows scattering with National Park Service written authorization. Mount Charleston in the Spring Mountains (Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest) also permits scattering with a ranger-district permit. Valley of Fire State Park requires advance written permission. Private-property scattering in Clark County is permitted with the landowner's consent; HOA-governed communities often restrict or prohibit it. Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City provides free inurnment for eligible veterans and spouses.

Clark County cost levers

  • Direct-cremation-only providers routinely beat chain pricing. Simple Tribute, Affordable Cremation, and Kraft-Sussman's direct option run $1,200 to $1,500 — $500 to $1,000 below Palm Mortuary or Davis for the same outcome.
  • Out-of-state deaths on the Strip add airline-transport fees for ash return; the Clark County coroner handles the paperwork but families should ask the chosen funeral home for a combined-transport quote versus local cremation followed by TSA-screened ash shipment.
  • The Nevada Funeral and Cemetery Services Board at fcsb.nv.gov publishes complaint histories and license status — worth checking before committing on pre-need.
  • Veterans qualify for free inurnment at Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City, avoiding $1,000-$2,500 in niche and opening-closing fees.
Cost-Saving Tip: You do not need to purchase an urn from the cremation provider. The FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to use any urn or container, including ones purchased independently. See our urn buying guide.

What Families Want to Know

How much does cremation cost in Las Vegas?

Direct cremation in Las Vegas averages $1,980 and full-service cremation runs $6,820. The metro has mature direct-cremation competition — Simple Tribute, Affordable Cremation, and Kraft-Sussman's direct option price below $1,500, materially undercutting Palm Mortuary and Davis for families who don't need a ceremony. Out-of-state deaths on the Strip add airline ash-transport fees of $150-$400 on top of the cremation itself.

What types of cremation are available in Las Vegas?

Las Vegas families can choose from: direct cremation ($1,980) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service — a ceremony held after cremation; and traditional cremation ($6,820) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Las Vegas?

Yes — by a wider margin than in most U.S. metros. Burial plots at Palm Mortuary's Las Vegas cemeteries run $3,000 to $8,000 before vault, opening-closing, and marker fees; a complete burial package totals $10,000 to $15,000. Direct cremation at $1,980 saves at least $8,000. Even the full-service cremation figure of $6,820 comes in below burial. Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery provides free interment for eligible veterans.
