Burial Costs in South Dakota (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Midwest

South Dakota is a state of distances. A family in Pierre arranging a burial in Sioux Falls may drive five hours to the cemetery, and funeral homes in Rapid City regularly transport decedents across the Badlands. Those geographies show up on every invoice, in mileage charges and second-day facility fees. Even so, South Dakota remains among the more affordable burial markets in the country, with total costs between $11,720 and $16,440 depending on whether a family uses an urban memorial park or a township cemetery.

A South Dakota burial generally costs between $11,720 and $16,440. Mount Calvary Cemetery in Sioux Falls and Black Hills National Cemetery near Sturgis anchor opposite ends of that range — one is a standard Catholic diocesan cemetery, the other is free for eligible veterans. Eligibility and benefits fall under the VA National Cemetery Administration (38 U.S.C. § 2402) and the VA burial allowance rules at 38 CFR § 3.1600.

Winter ground and the delayed-interment line item

South Dakota's burial economy is shaped first by weather. From roughly late November through March, frozen ground across most of the state forces cemeteries to stop opening graves and defer actual interment until spring thaw. Funeral homes charge a temporary storage fee — often $300 to $900 depending on duration — that simply does not appear on invoices in warmer states. Families choosing a winter funeral should ask in advance whether the cemetery's opening-and-closing fee is charged at time of service or at spring interment, and whether the storage fee is daily or flat. Hills of Rest and Mount Calvary in Sioux Falls both handle this routinely.

The township-cemetery shortcut most families miss

South Dakota still has hundreds of active township, county, and fraternal cemeteries — many with plot fees under $800, no vault requirement, and no online presence. The county clerk's office typically knows which are still accepting new interments and can name the sexton or trustee. For a direct-burial family this is the single largest available cost reduction outside of VA benefits. A Rapid City or Aberdeen family with rural roots should make the county call before the funeral-home call.

Mount Moriah and the historic-cemetery limit

Mount Moriah in Deadwood — where Wild Bill Hickok and Calamity Jane are buried — is essentially at capacity, and its remaining inventory has become a tourist artifact more than a working cemetery. That pattern repeats across several older Black Hills mining-town cemeteries. Families with a sentimental tie should call well before need; walk-in availability is very rare.

FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) — who it covers in SD: Every licensed South Dakota funeral home is bound by the federal rule: written General Price List, no handling surcharge on third-party caskets, no bundled packages that cannot be itemized. Cemeteries are not covered, which is why cemetery pricing in the state varies more than mortuary pricing. Black Hills National and veteran eligibility

Black Hills National Cemetery near Sturgis is one of the more actively used VA cemeteries in the northern Plains. For eligible veterans and qualifying dependents, grave, liner, opening and closing, and marker are all provided at no cost. The Department of Veterans Affairs also provides a modest plot allowance to spouses buried in a non-VA cemetery. The full veteran burial benefits page covers the eligibility thresholds, which are wider than most families realize.

Independent funeral homes and honest shopping

South Dakota still has a healthy supply of family-owned funeral homes — Miller in Sioux Falls, Behrens-Wilson in Rapid City, and dozens of small-town operators — that price closer to the state average than any corporate chain. Two negotiating tactics work especially well in this market: first, ask whether the transport fee can be reduced if the burial is close to the place of death (mileage gets padded aggressively in rural territory); second, request the Casket Price List separately from the package sheet. Most homes will not show it unless asked. A direct-burial path with a green section available at a handful of SD cemeteries is the other way down-market.

Paying for it and where to file complaints

The South Dakota Board of Funeral Service licenses funeral directors and handles consumer complaints against licensed establishments. The Department of Social Services administers a limited burial assistance program for qualifying indigent cases — amounts are modest and forms must be filed promptly. For broader help, the funeral payment assistance guide covers Social Security lump-sum, FEMA reimbursement where applicable, and nonprofit routes. Independent monument work — going directly to a stoneworker rather than through the cemetery's preferred vendor — routinely cuts marker costs 25–35%; the headstone cost guide covers the specifics.

Answers to the Questions We Hear Most

What is the full price of burial in South Dakota?

A typical South Dakota burial runs $6,400 for the funeral service, $2,800 for a cemetery plot, $1,920 for a casket, $1,120 for a vault, $1,400 for opening and closing, and $1,000–$3,000 for a headstone. That adds up to $11,720–$16,440. Township cemetery plots and direct burial can bring the total under $9,000.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in South Dakota?

Black Hills National Cemetery at Sturgis is free for eligible veterans. For civilian families, direct burial at a township cemetery with a third-party casket and no embalming usually produces the lowest bill. Miller Funeral Home, Behrens-Wilson, and other independent providers tend to price closer to the state average than corporate chains. South Dakota Department of Social Services administers a limited burial assistance program for qualifying indigent cases.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in South Dakota?

Call the county clerk first. South Dakota still has hundreds of active township, county, and fraternal cemeteries that are not online and do not advertise. The clerk's office can name which are accepting interments and point you to plot fee schedules. Commercial cemeteries like Hills of Rest will provide itemized pricing if you ask for it specifically — they are not required to under the FTC Funeral Rule, but most will comply.

What is the total cost of burial in South Dakota including everything?

A complete South Dakota burial, including service, plot, casket, vault, opening and closing, and headstone, totals $11,720–$16,440. Sioux Falls and Rapid City metro cemeteries push toward the upper end; rural township and church cemeteries bring the low end down into the high single digits.
