Burial Costs in South Dakota (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: South Dakota burial cost range, family-arranged burial note and method note reworded. Region: Midwest

If you are arranging a burial in South Dakota, the costs can add up faster than most families expect. Between the funeral home bill and the separate cemetery invoice, total burial expenses in South Dakota typically range from $14,140 to well over $18,640. This guide lays out what drives each line item and where South Dakota families have room to save.

South Dakota Burial Laws and Regulations

  • Funeral director licensing: Funeral directors in South Dakota must be licensed by the state, and the state board listed in the oversight section below is the place to check a license or file a complaint.
  • Death certificate and permits: The death certificate must be filed within 5 days of the date of death, and a formal disposition permit is required before burial or cremation of the deceased person's remains, so the funeral director usually handles both filings in the first days after death.
  • Body-holding timeline: Within 24 hours of death, the body must be refrigerated, embalmed, or buried.
  • Veterans' burial allowance: Eligible veterans with qualifying military service who lack funds may receive a state burial allowance, including a $100 burial payment; contact veterans affairs through the SD Department or the Pierre office promptly, because a surviving spouse may need to submit documents.
  • Pre-need trust rules: South Dakota requires 85% of pre-need funeral funds to be placed into a trust, whether that is a revocable trust that can be changed or an irrevocable trust that generally cannot.

Regional Context for Burial and Cremation Services in South Dakota

Rural South Dakota counties still maintain pioneer-era cemeteries with active sections and prices that reflect community upkeep rather than corporate returns. Opening-and-closing fees at a South Dakota township cemetery typically run $400-$900, compared to $1,500-$2,500 at a metro private cemetery. If a rural cemetery away from Sioux Falls is acceptable to your family, the savings compound fast.

Those lower rural prices can matter when weighing overall funeral costs against simpler options like direct cremation, which typically runs $1,450-$2,850 in South Dakota, while cremation costs with a memorial service usually fall between $3,500 and $7,000; using a funeral cost calculator to compare cremation, burial, and service prices by state can also clarify how South Dakota stacks up against other regions.

Our statewide estimate for a traditional funeral service in South Dakota is about $6,400, below NFDA’s $8,300 national median for a funeral with viewing and burial; add the cemetery rows above and a full burial lands near $14,140 to $18,640. How to Reduce the Average Funeral Cost in South Dakota

Ways to keep a South Dakota burial affordable:

  • Price the cemetery and funeral home separately: These are two different bills in South Dakota.
  • Call the township or county clerk: South Dakota has many township, county, and fraternal cemeteries that simply do not appear in Google results.
  • Ask about county indigent burial assistance: Counties in South Dakota may have to cover essential burial expenses when a person dies without family or without sufficient funds available to the estate.
  • Claim the Social Security death benefit: Social Security may provide a $255 death benefit payment to qualifying survivors. How the Social Security death benefit works
  • Tribal burial assistance for Native American families: Native American families may be able to get help with funeral costs through a Burial Assistance program; if that may apply, contact a tribal veteran service officer or another local tribal office.
  • Contact Pennington County early: Pennington County offers economic assistance for qualifying funeral costs, so families should contact the county early when making arrangements.
  • Work with independent funeral homes: South Dakota still has a strong independent, family-owned funeral home market, and these providers generally price closer to the NFDA national averages than corporate-chain locations.
  • Direct burial is widely accepted: Cultural openness to direct burial (no viewing, no ceremony, immediate interment) is stronger in South Dakota than on the coasts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the full price of burial in South Dakota?

A full burial in South Dakota comes in at about $6,400 for the funeral service, $2,800 for the cemetery plot, $1,920 for a casket, $1,120 for a vault, $1,400 for opening and closing, and $500-$5,000 for a headstone. South Dakota totals generally land between $14,140 and $18,640 depending on choices.

Is a burial vault required in South Dakota?

Burial vaults are required by most cemeteries in South Dakota as a matter of cemetery policy (to prevent ground settling), though they are rarely mandated by state law. Vault costs in South Dakota typically range from $800 to $10,000.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in South Dakota?

Direct burial is the lowest-cost path in South Dakota: it skips viewing, ceremony, and embalming. Green or natural burial is the next rung up, available at a growing number of South Dakota cemeteries with biodegradable containers and no embalming.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in South Dakota?

Township, county, and fraternal cemeteries in South Dakota are often only listed through the county clerk and frequently price plots well below commercial memorial parks.

What is the total cost of burial in South Dakota including everything?

The first answer above lists each item; in South Dakota, cash advance items such as certified death certificates, an obituary notice and flowers are added on top of the $14,140 to $18,640 range.

Does South Dakota have green burial cemeteries?

There are green burial options in parts of South Dakota, though coverage is not statewide. Some South Dakota cemeteries have hybrid sections rather than fully dedicated grounds. Check our green burial guide and call local South Dakota cemeteries to confirm what they currently offer.

Moving a grave in South Dakota

South Dakota requires a Department of Health disinterment permit to move a buried body, even to another part of the same cemetery. A licensed funeral service practitioner applies, or a court orders the move. Without a court order, the department needs written consent from the surviving spouse, or from the next of kin if there is no spouse. Moving a child's grave needs both surviving parents' consent (SDCL § 34-25-38.1; checked October 29, 2026).

  • A waiting period: the permit does not take effect for 15 days, unless a court orders otherwise.
  • Who is told: the department notifies the cemetery's board or the county commissioners. For an enrolled tribal member, it notifies the tribal chairperson. Any of them can ask the circuit court to stop the move.

Burial rules and help in South Dakota

For a license check or complaint about a funeral home arranging a burial in South Dakota, call the South Dakota State Board of Funeral Service at 605-642-1600; South Dakota cemeteries can have a separate regulator.

South Dakota is not one of the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring a funeral director, so a family may arrange the burial itself once it holds the burial permit and follows the cemetery's grave rules.

A South Dakota burial needs a disposition permit. If you switch to cremation instead, a 24-hour wait applies (S.D. Codified Laws § 34-26A-13.1).

South Dakota's county of residence pays for an indigent burial, and the county places a lien on the estate, homestead included (SDCL 28-17-1; ch. 28-14). (Source: SDCL 28-17-1; SDCL ch. 28-14.) A surviving spouse or child may claim Social Security's one-time $255 death payment within two years; it is paid to them, not to the South Dakota cemetery.

Eligible South Dakota veterans get a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. In South Dakota, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Black Hills National Cemetery (605-347-3830); state and tribal veterans cemeteries: South Dakota Veterans Cemetery (605-277-8094); Akicita Owicahe Lakota Freedom Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 605-455-1497); Sicangu Akicita Owicahe Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 605-259-3044); Sisseton-Wahpeton Oyate Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 605-698-3388) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration). The Sicangu Akicita Owicahe Veterans Cemetery number comes from the tribe's office directory. The South Dakota Veterans Cemetery was dedicated on Memorial Day 2021 and held its first burials on June 4 that year; to arrange one, the family gives the cemetery the DD214 or enough service details for it to confirm eligibility.

Next steps for a burial in South Dakota

  1. Price the South Dakota cemetery separately: plot, opening and closing, vault or liner, and marker are often sold apart from the funeral home.
  2. Ask the South Dakota cemetery for its rules on grave opening times, vaults and markers.
  3. Consider the South Dakota county lien on a homestead before accepting a county burial.
  4. Black Hills National Cemetery can tell a veteran's family which papers to bring; expect the DD214 discharge record to be first on the list.
  5. If a South Dakota funeral home gets its burial prices wrong, the state board takes complaints at 605-642-1600.
