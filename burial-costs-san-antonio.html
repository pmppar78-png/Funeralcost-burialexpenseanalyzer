Burial Costs in San Antonio, Texas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Texas

San Antonio's burial market is shaped by three unusual facts. First, Catholic and Hispanic funeral traditions dominate the metro more completely than in any other large U.S. city — the Archdiocese of San Antonio's San Fernando Cemeteries I, II, and III and the San Jose Mission parish grounds together handle a large share of Bexar County burials. Second, San Antonio is home to Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery, one of the nation's busiest VA national cemeteries, which dramatically affects the metro's veteran-burial pricing. Third, San Antonio's funeral prices sit below Dallas, Houston, and Austin despite the population size. A 2026 traditional burial runs about $7,560 for the funeral service and $3,570 for a plot, with all-in totals of $14,343 to $20,181.

Line item by line item, a traditional San Antonio burial lands between $14,343 and $20,181 — making San Antonio noticeably cheaper than Dallas, Houston, and Austin despite similar metro size, largely because of lower funeral-home service-fee schedules and the density of lower-priced Catholic and family cemeteries in Bexar County.

San Fernando, Mission Burial Park, and the San Antonio Cemetery Map

San Fernando Cemetery No. 1 on West Commerce Street dates to 1838 and, along with San Fernando No. 2 and No. 3 (south of downtown on Quintana Road), is operated by the Archdiocese of San Antonio — these three grounds handle a substantial share of Catholic burials in Bexar County at parishioner pricing that routinely undercuts the commercial memorial parks. Mission Burial Park North and Mission Burial Park South are the two largest private cemeteries in the metro. Sunset Memorial Park and Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery cover the northeast quadrant. Alamo Heights has its own municipal City Cemetery. For eligible veterans, Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery (inside the city, off Harry Wurzbach Road) provides plot, vault, opening-and-closing, and upright marker at no cost — roughly $6,500 off a San Antonio burial. Texas State Veterans Cemetery Mission (Hidalgo County) and the Central Texas State Veterans Cemetery in Killeen also serve qualifying veterans in the region.

Texas Funeral Service Commission Rules and Pre-Need Protections

The Texas Funeral Service Commission licenses funeral directors, embalmers, funeral establishments, and crematories in the state. Texas requires pre-need funeral contracts to be backed by either a 90 percent trust deposit or a qualifying insurance policy — a lower percentage than some states but still a meaningful protection. Before signing a pre-need contract in San Antonio, search the provider's license on the Commission's website for disciplinary history. Separately, Texas is one of the few states where the Texas Department of Banking regulates the pre-need trust side of funeral contracts, which means two state agencies are involved in enforcement.

Cutting the San Antonio Bill

  • Check Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery eligibility: The national cemetery inside San Antonio provides plot, vault, opening-and-closing, and upright marker at no cost for qualifying veterans and spouses.
  • Ask San Fernando cemeteries about parishioner pricing: The Archdiocese of San Antonio's three San Fernando cemeteries routinely undercut commercial memorial parks by $1,000–$2,500 on the plot.
  • Call parish cemeteries outside downtown: Mission Burial Park, Sunset, and the outlying Bexar County parish grounds all publish rate cards on request.
  • Consider above-ground entombment: Parts of southern Bexar County sit on soils where an above-ground crypt occasionally prices below a plot-plus-vault package — ask both ways.
  • Request three written General Price Lists: San Antonio's funeral-home market is competitive and the spread on identical packages regularly reaches $1,500–$2,500.
  • Buy the casket independently: FTC Funeral Rule bars handling fees. Casket buying guide.
  • Bexar County indigent burial: Bexar County maintains an indigent disposition program through its Medical Examiner's office for decedents without means. See our payment assistance guide.

Common Questions

How much does burial cost in San Antonio?

A traditional burial in San Antonio costs approximately $7,560 for the funeral service plus $3,570 for a cemetery plot. Including vault ($1,428), opening/closing fees ($1,785), casket ($2,268), and headstone ($1,500-$3,000), the total ranges from $14,343 to $20,181.

Is a burial vault required in San Antonio?

Burial vaults are required by most cemeteries in the San Antonio area as a matter of cemetery policy to prevent ground settling. They are rarely required by Texas state law. A grave liner — a less expensive alternative — may meet the cemetery's requirements. Always ask your specific cemetery about their vault policy and whether cheaper alternatives are accepted.

What are the cheapest burial options in San Antonio?

The most affordable burial options in San Antonio include: direct burial (no viewing or ceremony), which can save thousands on embalming and facility fees; green burial using a biodegradable container and no embalming; and purchasing a casket from an independent retailer to save 50-70%. In the South, church and family cemeteries in the San Antonio area are often priced well below commercial memorial parks.

How do San Antonio burial costs compare to Texas?

Burial costs in San Antonio are close to the Texas state average. At $7,560 for a funeral service (vs. $7,200 statewide) and $3,570 for a cemetery plot (vs. $3,400), the San Antonio metro premium reflects higher real estate and operating costs.
