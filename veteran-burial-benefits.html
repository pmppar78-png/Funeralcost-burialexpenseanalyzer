VA Burial Benefits and Cremation Coverage for Veterans (2026)

By Paul Paradis, Founder & Editor

Last updated: November 16, 2026 Last reviewed: November 16, 2026 Sources: VA.gov, VA National Cemetery Administration, eCFR, NFDA Editorial: Our Standards

For a veteran who dies on or after October 1, 2026 from something unrelated to service, the VA lists a burial allowance of up to $1,032 and a separate plot allowance of up to $1,032. When the death was service-connected, the ceiling is $2,000 if it happened on or after September 11, 2001, and $1,500 if it happened earlier (VA burial allowance page, last updated October 30, 2026). On top of the cash, an eligible veteran can be laid to rest in a VA national cemetery with no charge for the grave, the opening and closing, the liner, the headstone, the flag or the memorial certificate (National Cemetery Administration).

How Much Does the VA Pay for a Veteran’s Burial in 2026?

The answer depends on two things: whether the death was connected to military service, and the date of death. The non-service-connected rates move every October 1, so a death on September 30 and one on October 1 can be paid at different amounts. The figures below are the maximums the VA publishes. The VA pays back money that was actually spent and that nobody else is covering, so a family that spent less, or was reimbursed by an employer or another agency, gets less.

VA burial and plot allowance ceilings by date of death
Death and dateVA maximum
Service-connected, on or after Sept. 11, 2001$2,000 burial allowance
Service-connected, before Sept. 11, 2001$1,500 burial allowance
Not service-connected, on or after Oct. 1, 2026$1,032 burial + $1,032 plot
Not service-connected, Oct. 1, 2025 to Sept. 30, 2026$1,002 burial + $1,002 plot
Not service-connected, Oct. 1, 2024 to Sept. 30, 2025$978 burial + $978 plot

Source: VA.gov, Veterans burial allowance and transportation benefits, burial allowance amounts table (page last updated October 30, 2026; checked November 16, 2026).

Three smaller payments sit alongside those ceilings. The same VA page lists a headstone or marker allowance of $420 for deaths on or after October 1, 2026, and $441 for deaths from October 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026. It does not say on that page who receives it, so ask the VA before counting on it. Second, when a family buys its own burial vault for a casket burial in a national cemetery instead of using the government liner, the VA automatically pays the buyer an outer burial receptacle allowance, which is $387 for interments during calendar year 2026 (Federal Register notice, February 11, 2026; 38 CFR 38.629). Third, transportation costs can be paid back in the cases described under transportation reimbursement.

Does the VA Pay for Cremation?

Not directly, but cremation gets the same benefits as a casket burial. The National Cemetery Administration puts it plainly: “The VA does not pay for cremation directly,” and whatever the family buys from a funeral home is at the family’s expense (NCA frequently asked questions). What the VA does pay is the burial allowance, and it covers “all legal burial types, including cremation and burial at sea,” plus donation of the body to a medical school (VA.gov). In practice, the $1,032 or $2,000 reimbursement can go toward the cremation bill.

The plot allowance is not limited to a grave either. Federal rules define a plot as the final resting place of the remains, “whether it is a grave, mausoleum vault, columbarium niche, or other similar place” (38 CFR 3.1707(d)). So a family that buys a niche for the urn in a private cemetery can claim it the same way a casket family claims a grave.

How cremated remains are handled at a national cemetery

  • Same honors. Cremated remains are buried or inurned “in the same manner and with the same honors as casketed remains” (NCA).
  • Marker for the niche. Bronze niche markers exist for columbaria, and an in-ground urn grave gets a headstone or flat marker like any other grave.
  • Flag with the urn. The burial flag can accompany the urn rather than drape a casket (VA burial flags).
  • Ashes scattered? The VA still furnishes a memorial headstone or marker when the cremated remains were scattered (VA headstones and markers).

One choice cannot be undone. For ashes that will never be interred anywhere, the VA offers a commemorative urn or a wall plaque on VA Form 40-1330UP. If the family accepts either one, the law bars the VA from later burying that veteran in a national cemetery or providing a headstone, marker or medallion, and NCA says there is no way to restore those benefits (NCA, urns and plaques). If there is any chance the family will want a niche someday, hold off on that form.

For what the funeral-home side of a cremation runs, see our direct cremation cost guide and cremation costs by state.

Who Qualifies for the VA Burial Allowance?

There are two sets of rules, one for the person filing and one for the veteran. The person filing must have paid the costs without being repaid by anyone else, and must be one of these: the surviving spouse, a surviving legal-union partner, a child, a parent, the executor or administrator of the estate, another relative or friend, or someone from a funeral home, cemetery or other organization.

The veteran must not have a dishonorable discharge, and at least one of these must be true at the time of death:

  • The death resulted from a service-connected disability.
  • The veteran was getting VA care, at a VA facility or one under VA contract.
  • The veteran was traveling, with authorization and at VA expense, to or from an exam or care.
  • An original or reopened claim for compensation or pension was pending and would have been granted.
  • The veteran was receiving VA pension or compensation.
  • The veteran was eligible for compensation or pension but was taking full military retirement or disability pay instead.

No allowance is paid when the person died on active duty, while serving in Congress, or while serving a federal prison sentence. (VA.gov eligibility rules.)

Burial in a national cemetery has its own, broader test. A veteran who did not receive a dishonorable discharge is generally eligible even with no VA pension, no service-connected rating and no claim on file (VA national cemetery eligibility). That is why many families who get no cash allowance still get a free grave.

What a VA National Cemetery Provides at No Cost

NCA lists these items as free to the family for an eligible veteran (NCA burial and memorial benefits):

  • A gravesite in any VA national cemetery that has space
  • Opening and closing of the grave
  • Perpetual care
  • A government headstone, marker or medallion
  • A burial flag
  • Presidential Memorial Certificates
  • A government-furnished graveliner for casket burials, the national cemetery’s version of a vault (38 CFR 38.629)

NCA says it runs 158 VA-managed cemeteries, and the VA’s map also lists 125 VA grant-funded state, territory and tribal cemeteries (cem.va.gov). Not every state has a national cemetery. Arlington National Cemetery is run by the Army, not the VA, and sets its own eligibility (NCA FAQ).

What the family still pays for is everything from the funeral home: transfer of the body, embalming or cremation, the casket or urn, a viewing or service, and transport to the cemetery. A private cemetery plot often runs into the thousands on its own, and our burial plot cost guide shows what that line can look like.

Headstones, Markers, Flags and the Presidential Memorial Certificate

Headstones, markers and medallions

The VA provides a government headstone or marker for the unmarked grave of an eligible veteran in any cemetery in the world, whatever the date of death. If the grave already has a private headstone, the VA can still provide one when the veteran died on or after November 1, 1990 (NCA). Choices are upright granite or marble, flat granite, marble or bronze, and bronze niche markers. Apply on VA Form 40-1330. Veterans who enlisted after September 7, 1980, or served as officers after October 16, 1981, generally need 24 months of continuous active duty unless they died on active duty (VA headstone eligibility).

Instead of a second stone, a family with a private headstone can ask for a bronze medallion reading “VETERAN” on VA Form 40-1330M. It is one or the other, not both. In a private cemetery, the stone is free but the cemetery’s setting fee is usually the family’s cost; our headstone and monument cost guide covers that fee.

Burial flag

A U.S. flag to drape the casket or go with the urn is free. Fill out VA Form 27-2008 and take it to the funeral director, a VA regional office or a post office; call the post office first, since not all of them stock flags. Only one flag is issued, and the VA cannot replace one that is lost or stolen (VA burial flags, last updated November 6, 2026).

Presidential Memorial Certificate

The certificate is an engraved paper document signed by the sitting president. At a national cemetery burial, the VA presents one to the next of kin automatically. If the veteran was eligible for a national cemetery but is buried elsewhere, family or close friends can request copies on VA Form 40-0247, and the VA accepts more than one request (VA Presidential Memorial Certificates).

Military Funeral Honors

On request, every eligible veteran is owed a military funeral honors ceremony. By law the detail is at least two members of the armed forces, one of them from the veteran’s branch, and at minimum they fold and present the flag and play Taps, by a bugler if one is available or on a ceremonial bugle. The funeral director usually makes the request, and the VA suggests contacting the service branch 48 to 72 hours before the service (NCA military funeral honors). Veterans service organizations may add a rifle detail, pallbearers or an escort.

The service branches have provided these ceremonies since January 1, 2000, and Public Law 106-65 requires one for every eligible veteran whose family asks. At a VA national cemetery, the cemetery staff can help set it up. If the funeral home cannot reach the right branch, Military OneSource (800-342-9647) can help (NCA).

Spouses and Dependent Children

A veteran’s spouse or surviving spouse can be buried in a VA national cemetery, even after remarrying following the veteran’s death. So can a minor child, and in some cases an unmarried adult child who depended on the veteran (VA eligibility). The spouse or child is buried with the veteran, the grave gets perpetual care, and their name and dates go on the veteran’s headstone at no cost. They can be buried first if they die before the veteran (NCA).

Two limits trip families up. A former spouse whose marriage to the veteran ended in divorce or annulment is not eligible unless that person is a veteran too. And a spouse buried in a private cemetery does not get a government headstone, though the spouse may be added as an inscription on the veteran’s (VA headstones).

On the money side, a surviving spouse listed on the veteran’s VA profile does not have to file at all. When the VA learns of the death, it automatically pays eligible spouses a set amount for the plot, interment or transportation (VA.gov). Anyone else, including a spouse who was not on the profile, files VA Form 21P-530EZ.

State and Tribal Veterans Cemeteries

States, territories and tribes run their own veterans cemeteries, many built with VA grants. The VA says their eligibility is similar to national cemeteries but may include residency rules and interment fees for spouses, and some charge a fee to set the government headstone (NCA fact sheet on grant-funded cemeteries). Iowa is one example: it does not require Iowa residency, buries eligible veterans for free, and lists a $300 interment fee for an eligible spouse or dependent child (Iowa Department of Veterans Affairs). Other states require residency, so call the cemetery.

A free state plot also changes the plot allowance. When a state or tribal cemetery buries the veteran without charging for the plot, the VA pays the plot or interment allowance to the state or tribe, not to the family (38 CFR 3.1707(b)). The family can still claim the burial allowance for funeral costs. Find state cemeteries on the NCA cemetery map.

Transportation Reimbursement

The VA may pay some or all of that cost in these situations (VA.gov):

  • The death was service-connected and the veteran is buried in a VA national cemetery.
  • The death was not service-connected, but the veteran was hospitalized by the VA or in a VA-contracted nursing home at death, or died traveling to VA-authorized care.
  • The remains were unclaimed and the deceased qualifies; the person or organization that handled the burial can be repaid for moving the remains to a national cemetery.

The VA reimburses costs you paid yourself, so the receipt has to be in your name, and the application should include an itemized receipt for the transport. There is no filing deadline for transportation claims.

How to Apply: VA Form 21P-530EZ and Deadlines

  1. Find the discharge papers. The DD214 or other separation documents covering every period of service. If they are missing, request them from the National Archives before you file.
  2. Get the death certificate with the cause of death listed. For a service-connected claim, medical records help but are optional.
  3. Keep every receipt. Funeral-home and cemetery bills, plus an itemized transport receipt if you are claiming that.
  4. File VA Form 21P-530EZ online at VA.gov, or mail it to Department of Veterans Affairs, Pension Intake Center, PO Box 5365, Janesville, WI 53547-5365.

Questions go to the VA benefits line at 800-827-1000 (TTY: 711), Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. ET.

Plan Ahead: Pre-Need Eligibility Determination

A living veteran, spouse or dependent can ask the VA to confirm burial eligibility now, so the family is not hunting for a DD214 the week of the funeral. Apply online or on VA Form 40-10007 and name a preferred national cemetery. The decision letter does not reserve a specific cemetery or gravesite, and the VA may recheck eligibility at the time of death if laws have changed (VA, schedule a burial). Pre-need through the VA covers national cemeteries only. Arlington and state, territory or tribal cemeteries run their own programs (VA pre-need eligibility).

If the person has already died, skip pre-need. The funeral director or family calls the National Cemetery Scheduling Office at 800-535-1117 (TTY: 711) to check eligibility and book the burial.

Why VA Burial Claims Get Denied

The VA does not publish a list of denial reasons. These are the points in its own rules where claims most plainly fail:

  • Missed the two-year window. For a death that was not service-connected, the burial allowance claim is due within two years of the burial, unless one of the exceptions above applies.
  • No qualifying circumstance. A veteran who was not receiving VA pension or compensation, had no pending claim, did not die in VA care and had no service-connected cause of death does not meet the allowance test, even with an honorable discharge.
  • Someone else already paid. Costs reimbursed by an employer or another agency are not repaid twice.
  • Discharge status. A dishonorable discharge bars the allowance. Other-than-honorable and bad-conduct discharges go to a VA regional office for a character-of-discharge decision.
  • Wrong program. Deaths on active duty, in Congress or in federal prison are excluded from the allowance.
  • Missing paperwork. No death certificate with a cause of death, no receipt in the claimant’s name, or no service records the VA can find.
  • Commemorative urn already issued. Accepting the urn or plaque ends national cemetery burial and government markers for that veteran.

If a claim is turned down, a VA-accredited representative from a veterans service organization can help at no charge with asking for a review.

Worked Example: What a Family Actually Pays

These examples start from the National Funeral Directors Association’s 2023 national medians: $6,280 for a funeral with viewing and cremation, and $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, rising to $9,995 with a vault. Those medians leave out cemetery charges (NFDA 2023 General Price List Study). The VA figures are the ceilings for a death on or after October 1, 2026.

SituationFuneral-home billVA pays up toFamily share
Cremation with viewing, ashes inurned in a national cemetery, death not service-connected$6,280$1,032$5,248
Same cremation, service-connected death after Sept. 11, 2001$6,280$2,000$4,280
Casket funeral, national cemetery with government liner, not service-connected$8,300$1,032$7,268
Casket funeral with vault, private cemetery, not service-connected$9,995$2,064$7,931 plus the plot and opening fees

Take the first row. A widow whose husband was drawing a VA pension chooses a viewing and cremation at the $6,280 median and has his urn placed at the nearest national cemetery. The niche or grave, the opening and closing, the marker, the flag and the certificate cost her nothing. The VA repays up to $1,032 of the funeral bill. No plot allowance applies, because the national cemetery space was free, and the plot rule covers veterans not buried in a national cemetery (38 CFR 3.1707(a)). If she was living with him, Social Security’s lump-sum death payment, which is capped at $255 (SSA history note), brings her share to about $4,993.

The last row shows why a private cemetery costs more even with both allowances. The $2,064 is the $1,032 burial allowance plus the $1,032 plot allowance, but the plot, the opening and closing, and the stone setting are billed by the cemetery on top of the funeral home’s total. For your own state’s figures, use the funeral cost calculator or see burial costs by state.

Other Help That Stacks With VA Benefits

VA money does not reduce other help. The Social Security payment above is separate (see our Social Security death benefit guide). Frequently Asked Questions

Does the VA pay for cremation?

Not directly. The VA does not pay the crematory or funeral home, but it treats cremation like any other burial when it repays the family. For a death on or after October 1, 2026 that was not service-connected, the person who paid can claim up to $1,032 toward the cremation and funeral bill and up to $1,032 for a niche or plot if the ashes go into a private cemetery. For a service-connected death after September 11, 2001, the ceiling is $2,000. An eligible veteran’s urn can also be placed in a VA national cemetery at no charge.

How much is the VA burial allowance in 2026?

For deaths on or after October 1, 2026, the VA lists $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for a plot when the death was not service-connected. A service-connected death on or after September 11, 2001 has a $2,000 ceiling, or $1,500 before that date. Deaths from October 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026 use the earlier $1,002 rate.

Is burial in a VA national cemetery free?

For an eligible veteran, yes: the grave, opening and closing, the liner for a casket, perpetual care, the government headstone or marker, the flag and the memorial certificate cost nothing. Funeral-home charges such as embalming, cremation, the casket and transport are still the family’s to pay.

Can a veteran's spouse be buried in a national cemetery for free?

Yes, if the spouse is eligible. Spouses, surviving spouses who remarried, and minor or certain dependent adult children can be buried with the veteran at no cost, with their name added to the veteran’s headstone. A divorced former spouse is not eligible unless that person also served.

Does the VA pay for a headstone in a private cemetery?

The VA provides the headstone, flat marker or bronze medallion itself at no charge for an eligible veteran, but the private cemetery usually charges the family to set it. Spouses buried in a private cemetery do not get their own government headstone.

How long do I have to file for VA burial benefits?

There is no deadline when the death was service-connected, or when you are claiming only the plot, interment or transportation allowance. For a death that was not service-connected, the burial allowance must be claimed within two years after the burial, unless the veteran died under VA care.

Will the VA pay to move a veteran's body to a national cemetery?

Sometimes. The VA may repay transport to a national cemetery when the death was service-connected, or when a non-service-connected death happened while the veteran was hospitalized by the VA, in a VA-contracted nursing home, or traveling to VA-authorized care. The receipt must be in the name of the person claiming.

Can a family get VA burial benefits and the Social Security death payment?

Yes. They are separate programs, so a spouse who qualifies for Social Security’s lump-sum payment, capped at $255, can receive it in addition to anything the VA pays.
