Burial Costs in Mississippi (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: South

Mississippi offers the most affordable burial costs in the United States, a reflection of the state's low cost of living, abundant rural land, and the deep-rooted tradition of church cemeteries that has served families for generations. From the Delta flatlands to the piney woods of the south and the red clay hills of the northeast, Mississippi's burial landscape is defined by Baptist and Methodist church cemeteries, African-American heritage burial grounds of profound historical significance, and a culture that places burial at the very center of how communities honor their dead. Traditional burial here is not merely a service — it is a gathering of family, faith, and community in a way that few other states still practice with such consistency and devotion.

What Drives Burial Costs in Mississippi

African-American Heritage Cemeteries

Mississippi is home to hundreds of historically significant African-American cemeteries, many dating to the slavery era and Reconstruction. These burial grounds carry profound cultural and historical importance. Preservation efforts across the state work to protect and honor these sacred sites. Burial traditions in African-American communities often feature powerful homegoing celebrations with music, testimony, and extended family gatherings.

Church Cemeteries

Baptist, Methodist, and Pentecostal church cemeteries are the backbone of burial in Mississippi. Many offer free or very low-cost plots for members and their families, making church membership one of the most significant factors in burial affordability.

Plot Prices by Area

Jackson metro: $1,200-$3,500. Gulf Coast (Biloxi, Gulfport): $1,000-$3,000. Hattiesburg: $800-$2,500. Rural Mississippi: $300-$1,000. Many church cemeteries offer free plots to congregation members.

Notable Mississippi Cemeteries

Biloxi National Cemetery serves Gulf Coast veterans at no cost. Natchez City Cemetery, overlooking the Mississippi River, is one of the state's most beautiful and historic. Corinth National Cemetery dates to the Civil War.

Cemetery Types Available in Mississippi

  • Church cemeteries — The most common and affordable option statewide.
  • African-American heritage cemeteries — Historically significant burial grounds across the state.
  • Municipal cemeteries — Available in most Mississippi cities.
  • Veterans cemeteries — Biloxi and Corinth National Cemeteries. Learn about veteran burial benefits
  • Private memorial parks — In the Jackson and Gulf Coast areas.

Casket and Vault Options in Mississippi

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, Mississippi families can buy caskets from any retailer, saving 50-80%. Mississippi has no state law requiring vaults, and many church cemeteries do not require them.

Consumer Rights for Burial in Mississippi

  • FTC Funeral Rule — Itemized pricing required.
  • Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service — Regulates funeral homes.
  • Embalming not required by Mississippi law for prompt burial.
  • Family-directed burial permitted — Mississippi allows families to handle burial arrangements.

Green and Natural Burial in Mississippi

Mississippi's many church cemeteries that have never required vaults are naturally suited to green burial. Families can use biodegradable caskets or shrouds at cemeteries that permit it. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Mississippi?

Mississippi has the lowest burial costs in the nation. A traditional burial costs approximately $5,900 for the funeral service plus $2,600 for a plot. Total costs range from $10,840 to $15,210. Church cemetery burials can be significantly less.

Is a burial vault required in Mississippi?

No state law requires vaults. Many church cemeteries do not require them, making Mississippi one of the easiest states to have an affordable, vault-free burial.

Why is burial so affordable in Mississippi?

Mississippi's low cost of living, abundant rural land, thousands of church-maintained cemeteries offering free or low-cost plots, and a competitive funeral market all contribute to the nation's most affordable burial costs.

What is a homegoing celebration in Mississippi?

A homegoing is a joyful, faith-filled funeral tradition in African-American communities celebrating the deceased's transition to eternal life. Mississippi homegoings typically include gospel music, spoken tributes, and communal meals, reflecting the deep spiritual heritage of the state's Black communities.
