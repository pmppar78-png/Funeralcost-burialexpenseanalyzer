Burial Costs in Mississippi (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: South

Mississippi remains one of the least expensive states in the country to bury someone, and a lot of that comes down to geography. Family cemeteries on rural acreage are still legal and still used; church graveyards in small-town Mississippi frequently sell plots at cost; and commercial memorial parks have to price around those alternatives. A traditional burial here typically lands between $10,840 and $15,210 — meaningfully below the national average, though casket markups and vault requirements can still push a single bill past $18,000 if nobody is watching the invoice.

Running the full arithmetic — $5,900 service + $2,600 plot + $1,770 casket + $1,040 vault + $1,300 opening-and-closing + $1,500 marker — a middle-of-the-road Mississippi burial lands at about $14,110, with the realistic envelope sitting between $10,840 and $15,210. Embalming adds $590 when it happens, and it does not have to happen in Mississippi unless the body is crossing state lines or being held beyond the time limits in the state's mortuary rules.

The Family Cemetery Is Still a Real Option

Mississippi is one of a small number of states where burying a relative on private family land remains a live legal choice, not a historical footnote. Counties still recognize family burial grounds under Mississippi law, and the surveyor's office will typically record the location on the deed once it is established. Families in Neshoba, Oktibbeha, Yazoo, and along much of the Delta still use plots that have been in the family since the 1800s. The practical barriers are zoning in unincorporated areas, setback rules from wells and property lines, and the need to involve a licensed funeral director for the body's preparation and transport — but the plot fee itself can fall to zero.

Where Urban Mississippi Actually Buries

In Jackson, Cedarlawn Cemetery on Raymond Road and Lakewood Memorial Park in Clinton run most of the modern metro burials, with Natchez Trace Memorial Park picking up overflow. Vicksburg's Cedar Hill Cemetery — with its Confederate section and the Vicksburg National Cemetery a short distance away — is where most of Warren County ends up. In Gulfport and Biloxi, high water tables explain why Southern Memorial Park and Floral Hills Memorial Gardens lean heavily on above-ground entombment. In Tupelo and the northeast, Lee Memorial Park and several Baptist and Methodist congregational cemeteries handle the bulk of the work, and the pricing gap between the church yards and the commercial parks routinely exceeds $1,500 on the plot alone.

Regulation: What Actually Binds the Industry Here

The Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service licenses funeral homes, directors, and embalmers out of Jackson, and the board publishes disciplinary actions publicly. The FTC Funeral Rule applies to every Mississippi funeral home without exception: the General Price List must be handed over at the start of any in-person price discussion, casket prices must be available in writing, and outside caskets must be accepted at no extra handling fee. Cemeteries, by contrast, are not covered by the Funeral Rule — which is why almost all of the opaque pricing in a Mississippi burial tends to live on the cemetery invoice, not the mortuary one.

Help Paying the Bill

Mississippi does not run a single statewide funeral assistance program. What does exist: the Mississippi Department of Human Services administers emergency funds through county offices for families on TANF or with demonstrated need, and some counties maintain a modest "pauper burial" line item in the coroner's budget when no family can be located. Veterans benefits are substantial — Biloxi National Cemetery on Pass Road handles most Gulf Coast eligibility; Natchez National Cemetery covers the southwest; and the Mississippi State Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Newton serves the east-central region. Burial in any of the three is at no cost to eligible veterans and at reduced cost for spouses and dependents.

  • Call the congregational cemetery before the memorial park: An active Baptist, Methodist, or AME congregation in the county where burial will happen almost always undercuts the commercial cemetery by $1,000 to $2,000 on the plot, and the opening-and-closing fee tends to follow.

Common Questions We Hear

How much does burial cost in Mississippi?

Adding every line item on a conventional Mississippi burial — $5,900 funeral service, $2,600 cemetery plot, $1,770 casket, $1,040 vault, $1,300 opening-and-closing, and a $1,000-$3,000 marker — the realistic total runs from $10,840 to $15,210. That sits meaningfully below the national average, largely because congregational and family cemeteries hold commercial pricing in check across most of the state.

How can I reduce burial costs in Mississippi?

The three levers with the biggest dollar impact in Mississippi: use a congregational or family cemetery instead of a commercial memorial park (often $1,500+ savings on the plot alone), buy the casket from an outside retailer the funeral home is required to accept under the FTC Funeral Rule (50-70% discount is typical), and choose direct burial if a viewing is not essential (the full all-in can drop under $3,500). Skipping embalming is legal in Mississippi when burial happens promptly.

How do I compare cemetery costs in Mississippi?

Cemeteries are not covered by the FTC Funeral Rule, so nothing forces them to publish prices. Ask in writing for a line-itemed sheet covering the plot, opening-and-closing, vault or liner policy, perpetual care, marker restrictions, and any denominational or residency limits. Compare at least three: a commercial memorial park, a congregational cemetery in the county where the burial will happen, and — if eligible — the veterans cemetery at Biloxi, Natchez, or Newton. The price spread in Mississippi is routinely 3x across those three categories.

Can I bury a family member on private land in Mississippi?

Yes, subject to local zoning and setback rules. Mississippi still recognizes family burial grounds on private property, and the plot can be recorded with the county. A licensed Mississippi funeral director still handles the body's preparation, transportation, and the death certificate, but the plot cost and cemetery fees drop out of the equation entirely. Check with the county planning office about setbacks from wells, streams, and property lines before committing.

What help exists for Mississippi families who cannot afford a burial?

Mississippi does not have a statewide funeral assistance program, but the Mississippi Department of Human Services can release emergency funds through county offices for TANF-eligible or documented low-income families. County coroners in Mississippi also handle indigent burials when no family comes forward. Veterans have the strongest benefits — the Biloxi National Cemetery, Natchez National Cemetery, and Mississippi State Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Newton all offer no-cost burial to eligible veterans and reduced-cost burial for spouses and dependents.
