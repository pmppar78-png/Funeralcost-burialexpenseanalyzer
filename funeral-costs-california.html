Rights Every Funeral Buyer Has in California

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in California must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in California has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

Help Paying for a Funeral in California

  • California has no state funeral payment; indigent burial and cremation are county programs under Health & Safety Code § 7104, so the rules and amounts change from one county to the next.
  • The $255 Social Security death payment goes only to an eligible surviving spouse or child; in California, as elsewhere, they apply by calling 1-800-772-1213 within two years of the death.
  • VA reimburses part of a California veteran's funeral through Form 21P-530EZ, filed by whoever paid; deaths not connected to service on or after October 1, 2026 qualify for up to $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for the plot, as our veteran burial benefits guide explains.
  • More options for California families, including Medicaid and charity help, are in our funeral payment assistance guide and how to pay for a funeral with no money.

What's Specific to California (West)

Western funeral pricing in California is pulled up by real estate and labor costs in the Los Angeles area, but drops noticeably in smaller markets. The $10,600 statewide average hides a real spread; you can find full-service providers in smaller California cities at $8,480 and Los Angeles-area providers at $12,720+ for comparable services. Driving to a lower-priced provider elsewhere in California is often worth it for a family event of this size.

Funeral rules and help in California

If a funeral home in California refuses to show prices or pressures you, the licensing body is the California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau, reachable at 916-574-7870.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list California among the nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so a California family may arrange much of a funeral itself. The California death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

If the family chooses cremation, California sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (Health & Safety Code § 103050).

California counties handle indigent burial or cremation under Health & Safety Code § 7104; the county coroner or public administrator can explain the local process. (Source: Cal. Health & Safety Code § 7104.)

A California funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Bakersfield National Cemetery (661-867-2250), Los Angeles National Cemetery Annex (310-268-4675, cremated remains only), Miramar National Cemetery (858-658-7360), Riverside National Cemetery (951-653-8417), Sacramento Valley National Cemetery (707-693-2460), or San Joaquin Valley National Cemetery (209-854-1040) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state and tribal veterans cemeteries, Big Sandy Rancheria Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 559-374-0066), California Central Coast Veterans Cemetery (831-393-9371), Northern California Veterans Cemetery (866-777-4533), or Yurok Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 707-482-1350), take requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in California

  1. Ask every California funeral home for its General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule says it must give you one when you ask in person.
  2. Ask the California funeral home whether embalming is actually needed for your plans.
  3. If no one can pay for a California funeral, call the county coroner or public administrator in the county of death before a funeral home is hired.
  4. If the person served, have the funeral home ask about Riverside National Cemetery before you buy a private plot.
  5. Report a funeral home that hides or ignores its price list to the board at 916-574-7870.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in California right now?

Current averages in California come in at $10,600 for a traditional funeral, $7,800 for cremation with a service, and from $2,200 for direct cremation, with the traditional figure sitting 28% above the $8,300 US median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $5,200 when a California family chooses burial.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in California?

In California, direct cremation at $2,200 costs about $8,400 less than the $10,600 traditional funeral, and roughly $13,600 less once about $5,200 in cemetery costs is added.

What rights do California families have when arranging a funeral?

The federal FTC Funeral Rule gives California families the right to an itemized price list, to prices over the phone, and to buy only the goods and services they want. California is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. The California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau, 916-574-7870, licenses California funeral homes and takes complaints.

How do I keep funeral costs down in California?

Three moves cut the most: compare at least three California General Price Lists side by side before choosing a provider, strip packages down to only the services you actually want, and choose direct cremation ($2,200) if cost is the priority. Conservation burial grounds and hybrid cemetery sections in California typically cost 30-50% less than traditional plots in the same cemetery.

Does California require embalming?

Not for a funeral or burial in California. The main requirement is for shipping: under Health and Safety Code § 7355, a common carrier may accept a body only if it has been embalmed and placed in a sound casket and shipping case, or, if embalming is not possible, sealed in an airtight metal casket or case.

If the body stays in California and is refrigerated, embalming is your choice. It costs about $1,060 here on this page's estimate, above the national median of $845 in NFDA's 2023 survey. The Cemetery and Funeral Bureau (916-574-7870) takes complaints about charges you did not approve.

Is California cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Compare the California figures in the cost table on this page with the national median of $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, or $6,280 with cremation (NFDA 2023). Prices inside California vary by provider, so the General Price Lists you collect matter more than any average.

Which funeral option costs the least in California?

Direct cremation carries the lowest price tag in California, from roughly $2,200. Direct burial is the next step up at about $5,300, which is $5,300 below a traditional California funeral.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in California?

Call several California funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the California home cannot charge a fee for it.
