Burial Costs in Virginia (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Virginia burial guide rewritten around the state's cemetery price-list rules and local help for unclaimed burials. Region: South

Virginia burial costs come from several places at once: the funeral home, the cemetery, the vault maker and the monument company. Our 2026 estimate puts the total at $18,760 to $23,260, with about $8,200 for the funeral service and $4,000 for the plot. Virginia is also unusual in requiring licensed cemetery companies to hand over written price lists, which makes comparing them easier; this page explains how.

Family and Church Cemeteries in Virginia

Family burial grounds and church cemeteries are still in use across much of rural Virginia, next to the commercial cemeteries. Congregations often price their sections for members rather than for profit, so a church or family plot may save more than $1,600 against a private cemetery (our estimate). These cemeteries are usually exempt from the state's cemetery licensing, so ask them for prices directly.

How Virginia Families Spend Less on Burial

Where Virginia families usually find savings:

  • Use the price lists Virginia requires. Every funeral home must give you its General Price List, and a licensed Virginia cemetery company must give you a written price list too when you ask in person.
  • Ask churches and family cemeteries. Plots in congregational and family burial grounds are often cheaper than in a commercial cemetery.
  • Check veterans cemeteries. For a veteran, Virginia's three state veterans cemeteries and its VA national cemeteries are worth calling before you buy a private plot.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cost of a burial in Virginia?

We estimate about $8,200 for the funeral service and $4,000 for the plot. With a vault ($1,600), opening and closing ($2,000), a casket ($2,460) and a headstone ($500-$5,000), a Virginia burial usually comes to $18,760 to $23,260.

Is a vault required for a Virginia burial?

No. State law does not require one, though a Virginia cemetery's rules may. If yours does, its price list should already show what the containers cost, because the Cemetery Board's regulation (18VAC47-20-220) requires a licensed cemetery company to disclose prices for burial vaults and other receptacles, which it may list as a range.

Compare a liner against a vault using that range before you sign. If a licensed Virginia cemetery company does not follow the rules, complain to the Cemetery Board at the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation; church and family cemeteries are usually not licensed.

What Virginia rules help keep burial costs down?

Virginia regulates cemetery pricing directly. Under Va. Code §54.1-2327, a licensed cemetery company must hand anyone who asks in person a written general price list, then an itemized statement covering vaults, facilities and services. §54.1-2319 sends at least 10% of each grave, crypt or niche sale into a perpetual care trust within 30 days, which is part of what a plot price pays for. Direct burial, without embalming or a viewing, keeps the funeral home side low, and you may bring your own casket without a handling fee. Eligible veterans can be buried in a VA national cemetery at no cost.

How can I compare Virginia cemetery prices?

Ask for the paperwork Virginia requires. A licensed cemetery company must give you its General Price List as soon as you start discussing arrangements, and an itemized statement before you sign (18VAC47-20-220). Put the lists side by side to compare the burial right, opening and closing, vault or liner, and marker setting.

Those companies are licensed by the Cemetery Board at the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation, which can discipline one that does not provide the price list (Va. Code § 54.1-2314). Church and family cemeteries are generally outside that system.

What does a Virginia burial cost all told?

Counting everything, a Virginia burial is usually $18,760 to $23,260: about $8,200 for the funeral service, $4,000 for the plot, $2,460 for the casket, $1,600 for the vault, $2,000 for opening and closing, and $500-$5,000 for the headstone.

Can I have a green burial in Virginia?

Yes, at cemeteries with a green burial section. These require a biodegradable container and allow no embalming or vault, and the total is usually lower than for a conventional burial. If none is near you, a Virginia family can buy a standard plot and simply decline embalming.

Virginia's interment record and the rule for moving a grave

Virginia has no separate burial permit for an ordinary in-state burial; the filed death certificate does that job. What the state does require is a lasting record at the cemetery. Every regulated cemetery must keep a permanent record of each interment showing the date, the name of the person buried and the specific location of the grave (18VAC47-20-180; checked October 29, 2026). If a family ever needs to prove where someone lies, that record is the place to start.

Moving a body later is controlled by the registrar. Unless a court orders it, a body cannot be disinterred for removal or transportation until an application has been filed with the city or county registrar or the State Registrar (12VAC5-550-430).

Virginia's boards, local burial help and veterans cemeteries

The Virginia Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers (804-367-4479) licenses the funeral homes that handle burials. Licensed cemetery companies come under the Cemetery Board instead, and church and family cemeteries usually under neither.

No funeral director is needed for a Virginia burial; the state is missing from the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine that demand one. That lets a family deal with a Virginia cemetery directly about the plot, opening the grave and which container it accepts.

Burial has no waiting period. If the family decides on cremation instead, Virginia sets no fixed wait either, but a permit is needed (Va. Code 54.1-2818.1).

Help with the cost

When a resident's body is unclaimed, the Virginia city or county pays the reasonable cost of burial, with a court's approval (Va. Code § 32.1-309.2). (Source: Va. Code §§ 32.1-309.1, 32.1-309.2.) Social Security's $255 for a surviving spouse or child is a small part of the $4,000 plot estimate on this page.

Veterans

For a qualifying veteran, the grave, opening and closing, liner and headstone in a national cemetery are free. Virginia's VA national cemeteries taking burials are Culpeper (540-825-0027), Danville (704-636-2661, cremated remains only) and the National Memorial Cemetery at Quantico (703-221-2183). The state also runs the Albert G. Horton, Jr. Memorial Veterans Cemetery, the Southwest Virginia Veterans Cemetery and the Virginia Veterans Cemetery in Amelia, all reached at 855-482-8387 (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps in Virginia

  1. Ask the cemetery for its General Price List; a licensed Virginia cemetery company has to provide one.
  2. Find out the cemetery's rules on opening times, containers and markers.
  3. For an unclaimed body, ask the city or county whether a court has approved the burial before buying any plot.
  4. For a veteran, call Quantico about eligibility before paying for a private grave.
  5. Report a funeral home that misleads you about prices by calling 804-367-4479.
