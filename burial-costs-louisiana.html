Spending Less on Burial in Louisiana

Burial costs in Louisiana can be managed with smart planning:

  • Request itemized pricing from every provider: Funeral homes in Louisiana must provide a General Price List. Cemeteries are not required to, but most in the Louisiana area will share one if you ask specifically for the itemized price sheet rather than a "package."
  • Check church and family cemeteries: Active church and family-owned cemeteries are still common in rural Louisiana and routinely price plots 30-50% below commercial memorial parks.
  • County veterans' sections: Many Louisiana counties maintain dedicated veterans' sections separate from national cemeteries.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do Louisiana families spend on burial?

Typical Louisiana burials total between $15,290 and $19,790. Of that, the Louisiana funeral service runs about $6,700, the cemetery plot $3,200, the casket around $2,010, the vault $1,280, opening-and-closing $1,600, and a headstone $500-$5,000.

Is a burial vault required in Louisiana?

Burial vaults are required by most cemeteries in Louisiana as a matter of cemetery policy (to prevent ground settling), though they are rarely mandated by state law. Vault costs in Louisiana typically range from $800 to $10,000.

How can I reduce burial costs in Louisiana?

Direct (immediate) burial skips embalming, viewing and a ceremony at the funeral home, so it is usually the lowest-cost burial on a Louisiana price list. You may supply your own casket in Louisiana; the FTC Funeral Rule forbids a handling fee for it. Eligible veterans in Louisiana can be buried in a VA national cemetery at no cost for the grave, opening and closing, liner and marker.

How do cemetery prices differ across Louisiana?

Each Louisiana cemetery sets its own prices for the plot, opening and closing, and any required vault or liner. The FTC Funeral Rule covers funeral homes, not most cemeteries, so a Louisiana cemetery may not be required to give an itemized price list; ask for one anyway. VA national cemeteries in Louisiana include Louisiana National Cemetery and Port Hudson National Cemetery.

What is the total cost of burial in Louisiana including everything?

The first answer above lists each item; in Louisiana, cash advance items such as certified death certificates, an obituary notice and flowers are added on top of the $15,290 to $19,790 range.

Can I choose a natural or green burial in Louisiana?

A growing number of Louisiana cemeteries now offer green burial sections (biodegradable container, no embalming, no vault), and the total cost is usually below a traditional burial. If a dedicated green burial ground is not nearby, some families in Louisiana use a hybrid approach (traditional plot without embalming).

Louisiana's burial-transit permit and moving a grave

No Louisiana death can end in a burial, a vault or tomb interment, or removal from the state until the local registrar issues a burial-transit permit (La. R.S. 40:52). The death certificate itself must be filed with a registrar within five days of the death and in every case before the burial (La. R.S. 40:47). State law also limits where a body may rest: outside of cremated remains, it must be in a cemetery or a building dedicated to that purpose (La. R.S. 8:652).

Moving a body to another space or another cemetery later takes the consent of the cemetery authority plus the written consent of the surviving spouse, or if none, a majority of the adult children, then grandchildren, parents and siblings, in that order. Without that consent the family needs a final judgment from the district court of the parish where the cemetery lies (La. R.S. 8:659; checked October 29, 2026).

Burial rules and help in Louisiana

Funeral homes handling burials in Louisiana are licensed by the Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors (504-838-5109). Cemeteries have their own regulator: the Louisiana Cemetery Board, created by Act 417 of 1974, licenses cemetery authorities and cemetery sales organizations, audits perpetual care and merchandise trust funds, and mediates consumer complaints.

A burial in Louisiana cannot be handled entirely by the family: La. R.S. 37:848 requires a licensed funeral director for filing the death certificate, moving the body, and being present at the final disposition, as the Funeral Consumers Alliance summarizes it, while the Louisiana cemetery itself opens and closes the grave.

A burial in Louisiana goes ahead once the permit is issued; for cremation, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (La. R.S. 37:876-877; 13:5716).

A Louisiana pauper burial is billed to the parish or municipality where the person lived, not necessarily where the death happened, and patients of state-run facilities are paid for by the state (La. R.S. 13:5715). (Source: La. R.S. 13:5715(A)(2).) Social Security's $255 payment to a surviving spouse or child is well short of the $3,200 Louisiana plot estimate on this page.

A Louisiana veteran's grave in a national cemetery costs the family nothing for the plot, opening and closing, or marker. In Louisiana, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Louisiana National Cemetery (225-654-1988); Port Hudson National Cemetery (225-654-1988, cremated remains only); state veterans cemeteries: Central Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (337-238-6405); Northeast Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (318-728-4346); Northwest Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (318-925-0612); Southeast Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (985-646-6458); Southwest Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (337-246-7094) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps for a burial in Louisiana

  1. Price the Louisiana cemetery separately: plot, opening and closing, vault or liner, and marker are often sold apart from the funeral home.
  2. Ask whether the Louisiana cemetery lets you buy the headstone elsewhere.
  3. If the person was in a Louisiana state-run facility, ask whether the state pays the burial instead of the parish.
  4. For a veteran, ask Louisiana National Cemetery about eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. A complaint call to 504-838-5109 is free if a funeral home misleads you about burial costs.
