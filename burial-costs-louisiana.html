Burial Costs in Louisiana (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Louisiana burial costs rewritten by hand, explaining pauper burial billing, the Cemetery Board and the state veterans cemeteries. Region: South

Louisiana burials have their own customs, from family tombs above ground to parish cemeteries in the countryside, but the bills follow a familiar pattern. Our 2026 estimate is about $6,700 for the funeral service and $3,200 for a plot, and $15,290 to $19,790 once the casket, vault, grave opening and headstone are added. Below are the item prices, the Louisiana Cemetery Board's role, and who pays when a family cannot.

Lowering Louisiana Burial Costs

Useful ways to lower a Louisiana burial bill:

  • Ask for prices one line at a time. Funeral homes in Louisiana must give you their General Price List. A cemetery is not bound by that rule, but most Louisiana cemeteries will provide an itemized price sheet if you ask for one instead of a package.
  • Try parish and family cemeteries. Church-run and family burial grounds are still found throughout rural Louisiana, and their plots often cost less than a memorial park's.
  • Call the veterans cemeteries. Louisiana has five state veterans cemeteries as well as Louisiana National Cemetery, so check them before buying a private plot for a veteran.

Church and Family Cemeteries in Louisiana

Church and family cemeteries remain common in rural Louisiana alongside commercial ones, and parishes often price their sections well below market for members. A church or family plot could save more than $1,280 compared with a private cemetery (our estimate), so ask the parish office as well as the large cemeteries.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Louisiana burial cost?

Our estimate is $15,290 to $19,790 for a Louisiana burial, made up of about $6,700 for the funeral service, $3,200 for the plot, $2,010 for a casket, $1,280 for a vault, $1,600 for opening and closing, and a headstone at $500-$5,000.

Do Louisiana cemeteries require vaults?

The state does not require one, but most Louisiana cemeteries do, to keep graves from sinking. Expect to pay $800 to $10,000 for a vault, and ask whether the cemetery will take a lower-cost liner instead.

What is the least expensive burial in Louisiana?

Direct burial is usually the cheapest burial on a Louisiana price list, because there is no embalming, no viewing and no ceremony at the funeral home. You can buy the casket elsewhere, and the FTC Funeral Rule forbids a handling fee for it. At Louisiana National Cemetery in Zachary, a qualifying veteran's grave, liner, opening and marker are all provided without charge.

How do I compare cemetery prices in Louisiana?

Prices for the plot, grave opening and any required vault or liner are set by each Louisiana cemetery, so collect them in writing. Most cemeteries fall outside the FTC Funeral Rule and need not itemize; ask anyway, and the Louisiana Cemetery Board can help if a licensed cemetery will not cooperate. Louisiana National Cemetery and Port Hudson National Cemetery are the VA national cemeteries in the state.

What is added on top of a Louisiana burial estimate?

Cash advance items are extra: certified copies of the death certificate, an obituary notice and flowers are added to the $15,290 to $19,790 estimate.

Is green burial an option in Louisiana?

Some cemeteries in Louisiana now have green burial sections, where the body is buried in a container that breaks down, without embalming or a vault, and the bill is usually smaller. Without one nearby, a family can still choose an ordinary plot and skip embalming.

Louisiana's burial-transit permit and moving a grave

No Louisiana death can end in a burial, a vault or tomb interment, or removal from the state until the local registrar issues a burial-transit permit (La. R.S. 40:52). The death certificate itself must be filed with a registrar within five days of the death and in every case before the burial (La. R.S. 40:47). State law also limits where a body may rest: outside of cremated remains, it must be in a cemetery or a building dedicated to that purpose (La. R.S. 8:652).

Moving a body to another space or another cemetery later takes the consent of the cemetery authority plus the written consent of the surviving spouse, or if none, a majority of the adult children, then grandchildren, parents and siblings, in that order. Without that consent the family needs a final judgment from the district court of the parish where the cemetery lies (La. R.S. 8:659; checked October 29, 2026).

Louisiana's two boards, pauper burials and veterans cemeteries

Funeral homes handling burials in Louisiana are licensed by the Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors (504-838-5109). Cemeteries have their own regulator: the Louisiana Cemetery Board, created by Act 417 of 1974, licenses cemetery authorities and cemetery sales organizations, audits perpetual care and merchandise trust funds, and mediates consumer complaints.

A Louisiana family cannot carry out a burial entirely on its own, because La. R.S. 37:848 reserves three jobs for a licensed funeral director: filing the death certificate, transporting the body and attending the final disposition, as summarized by the Funeral Consumers Alliance. Opening and closing the grave is left to the cemetery.

A burial can proceed once the permit is issued. Cremation has no fixed waiting period in Louisiana, though it too needs a permit (La. R.S. 37:876-877; 13:5716).

When the family cannot pay

The cost of a pauper burial in Louisiana falls on the parish or municipality where the person lived, which may not be where they died. For a patient of a state-run facility, the state pays instead (La. R.S. 13:5715). (Source: La. R.S. 13:5715(A)(2).) The $255 Social Security payment to a surviving spouse or child covers only a small share of the $3,200 Louisiana plot estimate on this page.

Veterans

For a qualifying veteran, the family pays nothing for a national cemetery grave, its opening and closing, or the marker. Louisiana National Cemetery (225-654-1988) takes burials, and Port Hudson National Cemetery (225-654-1988) takes cremated remains only. The state runs five veterans cemeteries: Central Louisiana (337-238-6405), Northeast Louisiana (318-728-4346), Northwest Louisiana (318-925-0612), Southeast Louisiana (985-646-6458) and Southwest Louisiana (337-246-7094) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Louisiana checklist

  1. Remember that a Louisiana cemetery bills separately from the funeral home, so get its prices for the plot, tomb or grave opening, container and marker.
  2. Ask whether the cemetery allows a headstone bought from another seller.
  3. If the person lived in a state-run facility, ask whether the state rather than the parish pays.
  4. For a veteran, ask Louisiana National Cemetery about eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. Report misleading burial prices from a funeral home to the board at 504-838-5109.
