Burial Costs in Louisiana (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: South

Louisiana's burial tradition is unlike any other state's — above-ground tombs, family vaults, and walled cemeteries are the cultural norm in the New Orleans region because much of south Louisiana sits at or below sea level. St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 (founded 1789), Lafayette Cemetery No. 1, Metairie Cemetery, and Greenwood Cemetery shaped the Louisiana cemetery landscape long before the rest of the country standardized on below-ground burial. The Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors licenses funeral homes; the Louisiana Office of Financial Institutions regulates pre-need burial and funeral contracts. Statewide totals run $12,780 to $17,990 in 2026, with New Orleans-area above-ground tomb burials priced differently from north Louisiana traditional ground burial.

Louisiana totals sit between $12,780 and $17,990. North Louisiana (Shreveport, Monroe, Ruston) typically runs closer to the floor; the New Orleans and Baton Rouge metros run above the ceiling once above-ground tomb construction, maintenance fees, and mausoleum vault pricing are included.

The above-ground burial tradition and why it costs what it does

Below sea level and below the water table, south Louisiana cemeteries — St. Louis No. 1, No. 2, and No. 3 in New Orleans, Lafayette Cemetery No. 1 and No. 2 in the Garden District, Metairie Cemetery, Greenwood Cemetery, and dozens of church and parish cemeteries across the region — developed around above-ground tombs rather than in-ground graves. A family tomb (often a multi-generation structure accommodating successive interments after remains decompose and are "cremated by time") is the norm. New tomb construction runs $8,000–$25,000+ depending on size, materials, and cemetery. A single vault in a wall of vaults (a "wall vault" or "oven vault") typically prices $2,500–$6,000. Rental of a society tomb or mausoleum crypt — common in older New Orleans cemeteries — prices $900–$2,500.

This makes the Louisiana burial math fundamentally different from the rest of the country. A "traditional burial" in New Orleans rarely involves a plot, vault, and opening-closing in the sense used in Kansas or Iowa. Instead, it involves opening an existing family tomb or wall vault and interring remains in a casket that will eventually be replaced by the next family member's interment after statutory waiting periods (Louisiana Revised Statutes Title 8 governs tomb reuse). The opening-and-closing fee is lower per interment but is paid repeatedly across generations.

North Louisiana and the traditional ground-burial market

Outside the Coastal Zone — Shreveport, Bossier City, Monroe, Alexandria, Ruston, Natchitoches — Louisiana cemeteries operate on the same ground-burial model used in the rest of the South. Forest Park Cemetery in Shreveport (west side), Memory Gardens of Shreveport, and Hill Crest Memorial Park in Haughton quote standard plots $2,400–$3,800. North Louisiana Catholic cemeteries — administered by the Diocese of Shreveport and the Diocese of Alexandria — typically price $2,000–$3,200 for practicing Catholics.

The Louisiana National Cemetery system includes Alexandria National Cemetery, Baton Rouge National Cemetery, and Port Hudson National Cemetery (near Zachary). All three cover grave, vault, marker, and opening-and-closing at no cost for eligible veterans. The Louisiana Veterans Cemetery system — with locations in Keithville (Caddo Parish), Jennings (Jefferson Davis Parish), Rayville (Richland Parish), and Leesville (Vernon Parish) — serves families outside the VA national cemetery catchment.

The Louisiana Cemetery Board. The Louisiana Cemetery Board, established under LSA-R.S. 8:67 et seq., regulates perpetual care cemeteries and requires cemetery care funds to be maintained in trust. Combined with the Office of Financial Institutions' oversight of pre-need contracts, Louisiana has one of the most active state-level cemetery regulatory structures in the Gulf South. A family signing a pre-need contract should request the cemetery's perpetual-care trust balance and confirm compliance with the state's 50% trust requirement on burial space sales.

Parish assistance and indigent burial

  1. Parish coroner indigent programs. Orleans Parish Coroner's Office, Caddo Parish Coroner, East Baton Rouge Parish Coroner, and other parish coroners administer indigent disposition programs. Cremation is typical for unclaimed or indigent decedents at no cost to the family.
  2. Louisiana Crime Victims Reparations Board. Up to $5,000 toward funeral expenses for families of violent-crime victims, administered through the Louisiana Commission on Law Enforcement.
  3. Social Security lump-sum. $255 to surviving spouse or dependent child.
  4. FEMA COVID-19 funeral assistance. Up to $9,000 for COVID-related deaths on or after January 20, 2020.
  5. Louisiana VA state burial benefit. Veterans buried at one of the four Louisiana state veterans cemeteries receive free grave, vault, marker, and opening-closing; spouses at $300.

Hurricane and flood-zone cemetery logistics

After Hurricane Katrina (2005), Hurricane Ida (2021), and repeated flooding events, Louisiana cemetery operators across Jefferson, Orleans, St. Bernard, Plaquemines, and Terrebonne parishes developed stricter tomb sealing and above-ground structural requirements to reduce casket displacement during storm surge. Pre-need contracts in south Louisiana now routinely include language about tomb reconstruction obligations if flood damage occurs. The Louisiana Cemetery Board has specific guidance on post-hurricane remains identification and reinterment — a consideration worth reviewing before signing.

Questions and Answers

What do Louisiana families spend on burial?

A 2026 Louisiana traditional burial runs $12,780 to $17,990. North Louisiana families using ground burial at a Shreveport, Monroe, or Alexandria cemetery typically close near the floor. New Orleans-area families using an existing family tomb can pay under $7,000 for an interment; those constructing a new family tomb can exceed $30,000. Port Hudson, Alexandria, and Baton Rouge National Cemeteries, plus the four Louisiana state veterans cemeteries, eliminate most of the bill for eligible veterans.

Is a burial vault required in Louisiana?

In north Louisiana traditional ground burial, yes — most commercial cemeteries require a sealed vault or concrete liner. In New Orleans-area above-ground tomb burial, no — the tomb itself serves the structural function and no additional vault is required. South Louisiana wall vaults and family tombs follow different Louisiana Cemetery Board rules; the Louisiana Office of Financial Institutions enforces pre-need trust requirements on both. Confirm in writing with the specific cemetery before ordering.

How do cemetery prices differ across Louisiana?

The north-south split is the defining variable. North Louisiana ground burial at a Forest Park, Memory Gardens, or Hill Crest plot runs $2,400–$3,800 on the plot line. Above-ground tomb burial in New Orleans ranges from $2,500 for a wall vault to $25,000+ for new family tomb construction. Historic cemeteries — St. Louis No. 1, Lafayette No. 1, Metairie, Greenwood — carry premium pricing on their limited available inventory. Rural parish church cemeteries across Acadiana and the Cenla region still list plots under $1,500 for parishioners.

What is the total cost of burial in Louisiana including everything?

Louisiana 2026 traditional burial totals $12,780 to $17,990: funeral service $6,700, plot or tomb placement $3,200, casket $2,010, vault $1,280, opening-closing $1,600, headstone $1,000–$3,000. New Orleans tomb burial in an existing family structure can close under $7,000. New family tomb construction in a south Louisiana cemetery can add $8,000–$25,000+ on top of funeral services. The Louisiana state veterans cemetery system removes plot, vault, marker, and opening-closing for qualifying veterans.
