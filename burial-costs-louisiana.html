Burial Costs in Louisiana (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: South

Louisiana's burial traditions are unlike anything else in the United States. The state's high water table — particularly in the New Orleans area and along the Gulf Coast — has given rise to the iconic above-ground burial tradition that defines Louisiana's famous "Cities of the Dead." New Orleans' St. Louis Cemetery No. 1, Lafayette Cemetery No. 1, and Metairie Cemetery draw visitors from around the world with their striking above-ground tombs, vaults, and mausoleums. Beyond the spectacle, Louisiana's burial culture is shaped by deep Catholic traditions, the state's unique French and Spanish colonial heritage, African-American jazz funeral customs, and Cajun and Creole burial practices that blend European and African influences into something uniquely Louisianan.

What Drives Burial Costs in Louisiana

Above-Ground Burial Tradition

Louisiana's high water table means that in many areas — especially greater New Orleans — in-ground burial has historically been impractical. Coffins placed in the ground could resurface during heavy rains or flooding. This gave rise to the tradition of above-ground tombs, vaults, and wall ovens (tiered burial structures). Above-ground entombment is more expensive than in-ground burial, adding significantly to costs in the New Orleans area.

Jazz Funerals

The New Orleans jazz funeral is one of the most distinctive burial traditions in America. A brass band accompanies the procession from the church to the cemetery, transitioning from solemn dirges to joyful, upbeat music in the "second line." While deeply meaningful, jazz funerals add $1,000 to $3,000 to funeral costs for the band and related arrangements.

Catholic Cemetery System

Louisiana's strong Catholic heritage means the Archdiocese of New Orleans and other dioceses operate extensive cemetery systems. Catholic cemeteries are often more affordable than private alternatives and offer a range of above-ground and in-ground options.

Notable Louisiana Cemeteries

St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 (1789) is the oldest cemetery in New Orleans and a National Historic Landmark. Lafayette Cemetery No. 1 in the Garden District is one of the most photographed. Metairie Cemetery features some of the most elaborate above-ground tombs in the nation.

Cemetery Types Available in Louisiana

  • Catholic cemeteries — Extensive network, especially in the New Orleans area and Acadiana.
  • Above-ground cemeteries — The distinctive "Cities of the Dead" found primarily in New Orleans and surrounding parishes.
  • Municipal cemeteries — Available across the state, with both above-ground and in-ground options.
  • Veterans cemeteries — Baton Rouge National Cemetery, Port Hudson National Cemetery, and others. Learn about veteran burial benefits
  • Church cemeteries — Baptist and other Protestant church cemeteries, particularly in North Louisiana.

Casket and Vault Options in Louisiana

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, Louisiana families can buy caskets from any retailer, saving 50-80%. In New Orleans, above-ground entombment may require specific vault types — consult with the cemetery before purchasing.

Consumer Rights for Burial in Louisiana

  • FTC Funeral Rule — Itemized pricing required.
  • Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors — Regulates funeral homes.
  • Louisiana Cemetery Board — Separately regulates cemetery operations and consumer protections.
  • Embalming not required by Louisiana law for prompt burial.

Green and Natural Burial in Louisiana

Green burial faces unique challenges in Louisiana due to the high water table. In-ground green burial may be impractical in low-lying areas. However, North Louisiana and higher-ground areas can accommodate natural burial. Some cemeteries are beginning to offer green burial sections. Learn more about green burial options

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Louisiana?

A traditional burial costs approximately $6,700 for the funeral service plus $3,200 for a plot. Total costs range from $12,780 to $17,990. New Orleans above-ground entombment can significantly exceed these figures.

Why are New Orleans cemeteries above ground?

New Orleans sits below sea level with a very high water table. Historically, in-ground burials were impractical as coffins could resurface during flooding. Above-ground tombs, vaults, and wall ovens became the standard, creating the famous "Cities of the Dead."

What is a jazz funeral?

A jazz funeral is a New Orleans tradition where a brass band accompanies the funeral procession. The music begins with solemn hymns and transitions to joyful, celebratory music in the "second line." This tradition adds approximately $1,000 to $3,000 to funeral costs.

Is a burial vault required in Louisiana?

No state law requires vaults. However, in areas with a high water table, cemeteries may require sealed vaults or above-ground entombment. In North Louisiana, requirements are more relaxed.
