What Drives Funeral Costs in Utah

What you pay for a funeral in Utah depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in Utah, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether Utah state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. Mountain pricing is generally lower than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in Utah routinely differs by 10-30%.

At 42%, Utah's cremation rate is 21.4 points below the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025. Direct cremation providers tend to post flat rates; in Utah the table puts that rate near $1,600.

Hidden Funeral Fees in Utah — What to Watch For

Quoted prices in Utah rarely include every charge.

  • Be wary of an embalming charge presented as required: in Utah it almost never is.
  • Cemetery "perpetual care" fees — An ongoing maintenance charge, typically $200–$1,000, added on top of the $3,200 Utah plot price.
  • Documentation and filing fees — Utah death certificates, the disposition permit, and filing fees can add $200–$600.
Protect Yourself: Request a General Price List (GPL) from every Utah provider, and report one that refuses to the Utah Board of Funeral Service (801-530-6621). Compare at least 3 funeral homes in Utah before committing.

Your Consumer Rights in Utah

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Utah must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Utah has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What's Specific to Utah (Mountain)

Sparse population in much of Utah means fewer funeral homes per capita, which can narrow choice but also keeps many providers focused on community reputation. The Salt Lake City metro has the most provider density; outside of it, getting a GPL from 2-3 providers may require driving across county lines. It is still worth the effort — price variation in Utah is wider than the statewide $6,900 average suggests.

Payment Assistance in Utah

  • Social Security death benefit — A one-time $255 payment for an eligible Utah spouse or child, claimed within two years of the death.

What to Do When Planning a Funeral in Utah

Families arranging a funeral in Utah for the first time often move faster than they need to.

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in Utah or burial in Utah.
  2. Explore payment assistance if cost is a concern: Social Security benefits, veteran benefits, Medicaid assistance, or crowdfunding; in Utah, also ask the county indigent burial program (such as Salt Lake County Vital Records).

Funeral rules and help in Utah

If a funeral home in Utah refuses to show prices or pressures you, the licensing body is the Utah Board of Funeral Service, reachable at 801-530-6621.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Utah among the nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so a Utah family may arrange much of a funeral itself. The Utah death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

Cremation timing: Utah sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (Utah Code 58-9-610, 26B-8-230).

Utah requires the county to give an unclaimed body found there a decent burial at county expense unless the University of Utah School of Medicine requests it (Utah Code § 26B-8-225), and lets counties pay to cremate indigent people (§ 17-53-221); Salt Lake County, for example, runs its own indigent program. (Source: Utah Code §§ 26B-8-225, 17-53-221.) The $255 Social Security lump sum goes only to an eligible spouse or child and must be claimed within two years; it does not go to the Utah funeral home.

A Utah funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Southern Utah National Cemetery (435-267-2710) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemetery, Utah Veterans Cemetery and Memorial Park (801-254-9036), takes requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in Utah

  1. Request itemized prices from Utah funeral homes by phone or in person; under the FTC Funeral Rule you only pay for what you choose.
  2. Ask the Utah funeral home whether embalming is actually needed for your plans.
  3. Ask the Utah county's indigent program, such as Salt Lake County Vital Records, before a funeral is arranged.
  4. If the person served, have the funeral home ask about Southern Utah National Cemetery before you buy a private plot.
  5. Report a funeral home that hides or ignores its price list to the board at 801-530-6621.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Utah?

The only reliable way to pin down your number is to pull General Price Lists from two or three Salt Lake City-area providers and compare line by line.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in Utah?

Direct cremation in Utah starts around $1,600; a traditional funeral with burial averages $6,900, not counting the cemetery plot or vault. For most Utah families, that is a difference of at least $5,300.

What rights do Utah families have when arranging a funeral?

The federal FTC Funeral Rule gives Utah families the right to an itemized price list, to prices over the phone, and to buy only the goods and services they want. Utah is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. The Utah Board of Funeral Service, 801-530-6621, licenses Utah funeral homes and takes complaints.

How can I save on funeral costs in Utah?

Start by lining up General Price Lists from two or three Salt Lake City-area providers before you commit to anything. Home and family burial is legal in parts of Utah with permits and can eliminate cemetery fees entirely. Direct cremation at $1,600 remains the lowest-cost disposition in every Utah market.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Utah?

The FTC Funeral Rule bars a Utah funeral home from saying embalming is required by law when it is not, and its price list must state that embalming is not required except in certain special cases. If a Utah provider says embalming is needed, ask which law or cemetery rule it is citing.

Is Utah cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Utah's $6,900 traditional-funeral average is 17% below the $8,300 national median (NFDA 2023).

What is the cheapest funeral option in Utah?

Direct cremation is the lowest-cost option in Utah, starting at roughly $1,600. In Utah that price covers transport, cremation, and return of the ashes — nothing else. Direct burial comes next at around $3,450 and still avoids viewing, ceremony, and embalming, though the $3,200 Utah plot estimate still applies.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in Utah?

Call several Utah funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Utah home cannot charge a fee for it.
