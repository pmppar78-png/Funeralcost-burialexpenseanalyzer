Funeral Payment Plans: What Families Need to Know in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 22, 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Government Sources Editorial: Our Standards

Explore funeral payment plans, financing options, and assistance programs for managing funeral costs. A funeral can be paid over time in three main ways: an installment plan offered by the funeral home, a loan or card from a third-party lender, or a prepaid plan bought years ahead. Each has a different cost and a different set of rules.

Understanding Funeral Payment Plans

The FTC Funeral Rule governs how funeral homes disclose prices, but it says nothing about interest rates or payment terms. Credit is covered by the federal Truth in Lending Act. Under Regulation Z (12 CFR Part 1026), a lender or a funeral home extending consumer credit with a finance charge has to disclose the annual percentage rate, the finance charge and the total of payments in writing before you sign.

A prepaid, or preneed, plan is different again. The money is paid before death and held in a trust or an insurance policy under state law, and states differ on how much must be held in trust and whether you can cancel. An at-need installment plan, signed after a death, is usually a contract with the funeral home or a finance company it works with.

What You Need to Know

Before agreeing to pay for a funeral over time:

  • Get the price first. Compare General Price Lists from two or three homes before talking about financing, so the financing is not part of the price negotiation.
  • Ask for the Truth in Lending disclosure. If the plan has a finance charge, the APR and total of payments must be in writing.
  • A "zero-interest" offer from a lender may carry deferred interest that is charged back to the start if the balance is not paid off in time; read the terms.
  • Many funeral homes ask for a deposit or full payment before the service. Ask whether they accept an insurance assignment instead. Life insurance and funeral costs
  • A prepaid plan locks in some prices, but cash-advance items such as death certificates, the obituary and cemetery fees are often not guaranteed. Prepaid funeral plans
  • Crowdfunding and family contributions can cover a deposit without interest. Explore payment assistance
  • Whoever signs the funeral contract is personally responsible for the bill, even if the estate later reimburses them.
  • A credit card can spread the cost, but interest usually runs higher than a personal loan; compare the APR on each disclosure.
  • A home equity loan or a 401(k) loan puts the house or retirement savings on the line for a one-time bill, so treat either as a last resort.

Costs and Pricing

Interest adds to the price of the funeral itself, so the smaller the amount financed, the better.

The NFDA 2023 General Price List Study puts the national median for a funeral with viewing and burial at $8,300, and $6,280 for a funeral with viewing and cremation, before cemetery charges. Financing the full burial median for two years at a double-digit APR would add hundreds of dollars or more to that figure.

Before you finance, check whether a lower-cost arrangement fits the family's wishes: direct cremation's NFDA median is about a third of the $8,300 median for a viewing and burial, and a memorial service can be held later at a church or home. Put the cash price and the financed total side by side on the funeral cost comparison worksheet. Even a short plan adds up: on a $6,000 balance, each percentage point of APR adds up to about $60 a year in interest, less as the balance is paid down.

Your Consumer Rights

Your protections when a funeral is paid over time:

  • You must receive the General Price List and an itemized statement of the goods and services you selected. Full FTC guide
  • A funeral home cannot make you buy items you do not want in order to get the ones you do, financed or not. How to compare prices
  • Truth in Lending requires written credit terms before you sign a consumer credit agreement.
  • For a prepaid plan, state law sets trust requirements and cancellation rights; ask for a copy of the contract and the trust or policy details.
  • The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau takes complaints about lenders, including funeral financing companies.

Pricing complaints go to the state funeral board or the Federal Trade Commission; prepaid plan complaints usually go to the state agency that licenses preneed sellers.

Practical Steps You Can Take

  1. Decide on the arrangements and get the itemized price before discussing payment.
  2. Ask the funeral home what it requires up front and whether it offers its own installment plan.
  3. If it works with a lender, ask for the Truth in Lending disclosure and compare the APR with a credit union loan.
  4. Check for an insurance policy that could be assigned to the funeral home instead.
  5. Ask about any Social Security, VA or state burial benefit that would reduce the amount financed.
  6. Read the contract for late fees, prepayment penalties and who is liable if payments stop.
  7. Pay cash-advance items such as death certificates directly if that avoids a markup.
  8. Keep every receipt; if the estate has money, the person who paid may be reimbursed as a priority claim in many states.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do funeral homes offer payment plans?

Some do, either in-house or through a finance company. Many still ask for a deposit or full payment before the service, so ask early.

What disclosures must a funeral loan include?

If there is a finance charge, the Truth in Lending Act and Regulation Z require written disclosure of the APR, the finance charge and the total of payments before you sign.

Is a prepaid plan the same as a payment plan?

No. A prepaid plan is bought before death and held in a trust or insurance policy under state law. A payment plan finances a funeral that has already happened.

Who is responsible if the payments stop?

The person who signed the contract. Family members are not liable for a funeral bill they did not sign for.

Can life insurance replace a payment plan?

Often. Many funeral homes accept an assignment of policy benefits and wait for the claim to be paid.

Does the Funeral Rule regulate interest rates?

No. It covers price disclosure and sales practices. Credit terms fall under the Truth in Lending Act and state law.

Where can I complain about a funeral lender?

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau at consumerfinance.gov and your state attorney general take complaints about lenders.

Does a funeral payment plan affect my credit?

It can. A loan or credit line from a lender may involve a credit check, and late payments can be reported; an in-house plan may not be reported at all. Ask before signing.
