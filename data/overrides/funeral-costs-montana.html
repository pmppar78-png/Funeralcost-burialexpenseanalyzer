Montana funeral licensing, county help and veterans cemeteries

In Montana, funeral homes are licensed by the Department of Labor and Industry, which answers at 406-841-2359. It handles complaints about pricing, misleading sales and homes that will not hand over a price list, and it can confirm a license before you commit.

Montana law does not require a funeral director, and the Funeral Consumers Alliance leaves Montana off its list of nine states with that rule. A family can therefore take on parts of the work, such as a home wake or a service at the ranch or church, and pay a home only for what it chooses. The death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

The 24-hour cremation rule

A cremation cannot take place within 24 hours of death, and the crematory needs a signed cremation authorization (MCA 37-19-705). In winter, when travel to a crematory can be slow, ask the home whether its quote includes transport and any days of refrigeration.

County indigent funerals are optional here

Unlike some states, Montana does not make counties pay. A county may provide indigent burial, entombment or cremation help, and "indigent" means the person could not pay the amount the county negotiated with the funeral home (Mont. Code Ann. § 53-3-116). The county can seek repayment from the estate and draw on the state indigent-funeral account (§ 7-4-2924). Ask the county commissioners whether their county takes part before choosing a home. Social Security's $255 goes only to an eligible spouse or child, must be claimed within two years, and is not paid to the funeral home.

Montana's veterans cemeteries

Yellowstone National Cemetery near Laurel (406-647-2746) is booked by the funeral home through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state runs three more: Eastern Montana State Veterans Cemetery in Miles City (406-218-2945), Montana State Veterans Cemetery at Fort Harrison (406-324-4793) and Western Montana State Veterans Cemetery in Missoula (406-721-2995). The tribal Apsaalooke Veterans Cemetery answers at 406-620-3504. Ask any of them what an eligible veteran's family would pay before buying a private plot, which averages $3,200 in the table.

A Montana to-do list

  1. Get each home's General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule requires it when you ask in person.
  2. Price a memorial gathering after cremation or burial instead of embalming and a viewing.
  3. Ask the county commissioners whether indigent funeral help is offered.
  4. For a veteran, ask about Yellowstone National Cemetery before buying a private plot.
  5. Complaints about a hidden or ignored price list go to 406-841-2359.
