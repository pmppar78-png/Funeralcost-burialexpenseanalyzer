Nebraska cremation licensing and county help

Funeral homes and crematories in Nebraska are licensed through the Nebraska Board of Funeral Directing and Embalming; call 402-471-2117 to check a license or complain.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance counts Nebraska among nine states where a licensed funeral director must file the death certificate, move the body, get permits and arrange a cremation (Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 38-1400s, 71-605), so even a $1,500 direct cremation goes through one.

There is no statutory waiting period; the county attorney signs the permit and the next of kin authorizes the cremation (Neb. Rev. Stat. 71-605).

County general assistance

Each county's general assistance standards must include burial expenses (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 68-133), and the county of legal settlement decides how a cremation is treated (Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 68-131, 68-133, 68-146). Social Security adds $255 only for an eligible spouse or child who claims within two years.

Veterans

Omaha National Cemetery (402-253-3949), Fort McPherson National Cemetery (308-582-4433) and the Nebraska Veterans Cemetery at Alliance (308-763-2958) accept urns, and an eligible veteran's niche or grave and marker are free; see cem.va.gov.

Nebraska checklist

  1. Compare at least two written direct cremation prices and what each covers.
  2. Ask when the county attorney is likely to sign, before fixing a memorial date.
  3. Ask the county assistance office what its standards pay toward cremation.
  4. Report misquoted prices to 402-471-2117.
