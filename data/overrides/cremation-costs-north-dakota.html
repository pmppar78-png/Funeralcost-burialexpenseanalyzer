North Dakota cremation oversight and public help

North Dakota imposes no waiting period before cremation. What it does require is a final disposition-transit permit and a cremation authorization signed by the next of kin (N.D.C.C. § 23-06-07). With that permit in hand, a family can work directly with a crematory.

Crematories and funeral homes are licensed by the North Dakota State Board of Funeral Service.

When there is no money

If a death leaves too little to pay and no responsible relative, the human service zone must arrange the disposition within seven days of the application, with a $3,500 payment; it is a cremation unless a burial is requested for religious reasons (N.D.C.C. § 23-06-03(6)). A surviving spouse or child may also claim Social Security's $255 lump sum, far short of the $1,500 estimate.

Veterans

A cremated veteran can still have a space and marker at no cost at Fargo National Cemetery (701-451-4650) or the North Dakota Veterans Cemetery (701-667-1418), and the tribal All Nations Veterans Cemetery at Fort Yates (701-854-8689) is also open (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

North Dakota to-do list

  1. Ask every provider to list the transfer, container and crematory fee.
  2. Find out whether a coroner or medical examiner must release the body first.
  3. If the family cannot pay, apply to the human service zone.
  4. If the person wanted cremation, look for a signed and dated written instruction; a wish only spoken to relatives does not carry the same weight (N.D.C.C. § 23-06-03).
  5. When the ashes will be mailed to relatives out of state, ask what insurance amount the crematory will use and who pays for it.
  6. Write down the Board's number, 701-776-6222.
