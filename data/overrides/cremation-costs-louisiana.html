Louisiana's board, pauper dispositions and veterans cemeteries

Louisiana does not let a family arrange a cremation without a licensee. La. R.S. 37:848 reserves filing the death certificate, moving the body and attending the final disposition for a licensed funeral director, so even a direct cremation runs through one. There is no waiting period; the director acts on the next of kin's signed authorization, and the coroner issues the permit (La. R.S. 37:876-877; 13:5716).

Crematory authorities and retort operators, as well as funeral homes, are licensed by the Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors, which was formed in 1914 under La. R.S. 37:831.

Pauper dispositions

When a coroner arranges a pauper's disposition, the parish or municipality where the person lived pays, and the cost may not exceed the actual price of the service (La. R.S. 13:5715). Ask the coroner whether that disposition is a cremation. (Source: La. R.S. 13:5715(A)(2).) A qualifying spouse or child can claim Social Security's $255 lump sum within two years.

Choosing cremation costs a Louisiana veteran nothing at a national cemetery: space and headstone remain free. Louisiana's options include Louisiana National Cemetery (225-654-1988), Port Hudson National Cemetery (225-654-1988, cremated remains only) and Central Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (337-238-6405); see cem.va.gov.

Louisiana checklist

  1. Ask each provider to itemize transport, the container and the crematory fee.
  2. Find out when the coroner usually signs the permit, so the memorial date is realistic.
  3. For a pauper's disposition, ask the parish coroner whether it is a cremation and the most it pays.
  4. Report a provider that misstates prices to the board at 504-838-5109.
  5. If the ashes will go into a family tomb or church niche, ask its keeper about urn size before buying one.
