Burial rules and help in California

The California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau (916-574-7870) licenses the funeral homes that handle California burials, and it also licenses many private cemeteries. Religious, public and district cemeteries are often outside its reach, so before you buy a plot, ask the cemetery who regulates it and where a complaint would go.

California is not one of the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring a funeral director, so a family can arrange a burial directly with the cemetery. The cemetery still sets its own rules for the grave and the container, and a burial permit has to be issued first (Health & Safety Code § 103050). There is no fixed waiting period for cremation either, though a permit is required.

When the family cannot pay

If the estate cannot pay and no relative in the state can be found to take charge, the coroner of the county where the person lived takes the remains and buries or cremates them as the county does for its indigent dead, under Cal. Health & Safety Code § 7104. Some counties cremate rather than bury, so ask early if burial matters to the family.

Veterans

For an eligible veteran, a California national cemetery provides the grave, opening and closing, liner and headstone at no cost. National cemeteries accepting burials in the state are Bakersfield (661-867-2250), Los Angeles National Cemetery Annex (310-268-4675, cremated remains only), Miramar in San Diego (858-658-7360), Riverside (951-653-8417), Sacramento Valley in Dixon (707-693-2460) and San Joaquin Valley near Gustine (209-854-1040). State and tribal veterans cemeteries are the California Central Coast Veterans Cemetery (831-393-9371), the Northern California Veterans Cemetery (866-777-4533), Big Sandy Rancheria Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 559-374-0066) and Yurok Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 707-482-1350). (Source: VA National Cemetery Administration.)

Before you buy a California plot

  1. Price the cemetery on its own: plot, endowment care, opening and closing, vault or liner, and marker setting are usually separate from the funeral home bill.
  2. Ask the cemetery for its written rules on vaults, opening times and the markers it allows.
  3. If the county may be paying, ask the county coroner's office whether burial is possible or only cremation before you buy a plot.
  4. For a veteran, call Riverside or the nearest national cemetery about eligibility; the DD214 discharge record is usually the first thing they ask for.
  5. Report a funeral home or licensed cemetery that misprices a burial to the bureau at 916-574-7870.
