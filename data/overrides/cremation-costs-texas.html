Cremation rules and help in Texas

Texas requires a 48-hour waiting period before cremation unless it is waived in writing by a justice of the peace, a medical examiner or a court order (Tex. Health & Safety Code § 716.004). The crematory also needs the death certificate, the cremation permit and a signed authorization. Once those conditions are met, a family may deal with a crematory itself. The Texas Funeral Service Commission licenses crematories and also regulates certain human anatomical donation programs, including willed-body programs.

When a Texas family cannot pay, the county commissioners court is responsible for disposing of the body (Tex. Health & Safety Code § 694.002); ask the county indigent-services office whether it uses cremation and what the family must sign. (Source: Tex. Health & Safety Code ch. 694.)

A Texas veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. Options in Texas include Dallas-Fort Worth National Cemetery (214-467-3374), Fort Bliss National Cemetery (915-564-0201), and Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery (210-820-3891). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Texas

  1. Get written cremation prices from at least two Texas providers and compare what each direct cremation price includes.
  2. Build the 48-hour wait set by Tex. Health & Safety Code 716.004 into any Texas travel or memorial plans.
  3. Ask the Texas county whether its indigent program cremates and whether the family can receive the ashes.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 512-936-2474.
