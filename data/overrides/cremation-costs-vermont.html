Who oversees Vermont cremation, and where to get help

A Vermont cremation cannot begin until 24 hours after death, and the crematory needs the required permit and authorization (18 V.S.A. § 5201). Families may work with a crematory themselves once those conditions are met. Every funeral establishment and disposition facility, crematories included, must be inspected at least once every two years (26 V.S.A. § 1213). Vermont has no separate funeral board; licenses come from the Office of Professional Regulation.

Help with the cost

Vermont DCF General Assistance can pay up to $1,100 toward a cremation, minus whatever the person who died and their spouse had; money from other relatives does not reduce it. (Source: Vermont DCF General Assistance burial rules; 33 V.S.A. § 2301.) Social Security's one-time $255 goes only to an eligible spouse or child, and must be claimed within two years.

A veteran's ashes can go into a columbarium or in-ground site at no charge for the space. Vermont's one option is Vermont Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Randolph Center (802-828-3379); see cem.va.gov.

What to do next in Vermont

  1. Check whether the quoted price includes death certificates and the $25 permit charge.
  2. Leave room for the 24-hour wait and the provider's own turnaround before setting a memorial date.
  3. When applying to DCF, have the spouse's resources listed, since they reduce the $1,100.
  4. Report a provider that misstates prices to the Office of Professional Regulation at 802-828-1188.
