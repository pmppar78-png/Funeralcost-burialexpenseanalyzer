Wyoming cremation oversight and help with the cost

Wyoming crematories and the funeral homes that arrange cremations are licensed by the Wyoming State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners (307-777-5403), which also takes complaints.

Wyoming is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as restricting family-directed care, so after the permit is issued a family may arrange things with a crematory directly.

The statutes set no waiting period, but Board rules forbid cremation within 24 hours of death without the county coroner's written permission (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5).

State help with the cost

The Department of Family Services pays up to $1,500 toward the cremation of a person who was on POWER, SSI or Medicaid and left too little to pay, after other resources are used, and up to $500 more to carry a qualifying veteran to the veterans cemetery. County commissioners cover other people without means (W.S. 42-2-103(c); W.S. 18-3-504(c)). A surviving spouse or child who qualifies can also claim Social Security's $255, a small share of the $1,600 we estimate for a direct cremation.

An eligible Wyoming veteran's ashes can be placed at Cheyenne National Cemetery (307-778-7316) or Oregon Trail State Veterans Cemetery in Evansville (307-235-6673) with no charge for the space; see cem.va.gov.

Wyoming checklist

  1. Get the transfer and any mileage charge in writing along with the crematory fee and container.
  2. Plan for the 24-hour rule and the permit when you set a memorial date.
  3. If the person was on POWER, SSI or Medicaid, ask DFS to confirm its payment before you book.
  4. Keep the Board's number, 307-777-5403, for a complaint.
