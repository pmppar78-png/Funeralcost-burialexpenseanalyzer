Arkansas burial oversight and where to get help

An Arkansas family may arrange a burial without a funeral director once it has the permit and meets the cemetery's rules. If nobody claims a body, whoever holds it must look for relatives and check for military service, and the county court decides on a pauper burial (Ark. Code Ann. § 20-17-702). Funeral homes and perpetual care cemeteries are both overseen by the Arkansas Insurance Department's Funeral Services Division.

When no one can pay

If nobody claims a body in Arkansas, whoever holds it must look for relatives and check for military service, and the county court decides on a pauper burial (Ark. Code Ann. § 20-17-702). (Source: Ark. Code Ann. § 20-17-702.) Social Security's one-time $255 death payment can be claimed within two years by a surviving spouse or child; it goes to them, not to the cemetery.

Eligible veterans pay nothing for a grave, its opening or a government headstone at a national cemetery. Arkansas has Fayetteville National Cemetery (479-442-2566) and Fort Smith National Cemetery (479-783-5345), plus state veterans cemeteries at Birdeye (870-588-4608) and North Little Rock (501-683-2259) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps in Arkansas

  1. Treat the cemetery as a second purchase and get its fees on paper.
  2. Confirm the cemetery is registered with the state, since Arkansas burials must take place in one.
  3. If the family cannot pay, speak to the county judge's office first.
  4. Veterans' families can call Fort Smith or Fayetteville with the DD214 in hand.
  5. Pricing problems with a funeral home go to the Insurance Department's division at 501-682-0574.
