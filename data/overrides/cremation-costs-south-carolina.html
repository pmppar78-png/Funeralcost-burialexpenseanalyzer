Who regulates South Carolina crematories, and paying for one

A South Carolina cremation must wait 24 hours after death, and the crematory will not start without the death certificate, the next of kin's signed authorization and a coroner's cremation permit (S.C. Code § 32-8-340). Relatives may work with the crematory themselves once those documents are in place.

Crematories and funeral homes hold licenses from the South Carolina Board of Funeral Service.

When the estate cannot pay

South Carolina has no state funeral payment. An unclaimed body goes to the county coroner if the anatomical board declines it, the estate pays first, and the county is charged only after that; county councils may also fund indigent burial or cremation as a public purpose (S.C. Code § 17-5-570; S.C. Code § 4-9-130). Social Security's $255 lump sum goes only to an eligible spouse or child and has to be claimed within two years.

A South Carolina veteran who is cremated can still be placed in a national cemetery at no charge: Fort Jackson National Cemetery (803-699-2246), Beaufort National Cemetery (843-524-3925) and Florence National Cemetery Annex on Stockade Drive (843-669-8783), which is the Florence site taking new interments. The state's M.J. Dolly Cooper Veterans Cemetery near Anderson is at 864-332-8022; see cem.va.gov.

South Carolina checklist

  1. Ask each provider to list the transfer, the container, the crematory fee and the coroner's permit fee.
  2. Allow for the 24-hour wait when setting travel or memorial dates.
  3. If the estate cannot pay, ask the county council whether it funds indigent cremation.
  4. Report a provider that misstates prices to the Board at 803-896-4429.
