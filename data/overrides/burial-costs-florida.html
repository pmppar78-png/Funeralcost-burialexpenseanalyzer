Who regulates a Florida burial, and where to get help

In Florida one agency covers both sides of a burial. The Florida Department of Financial Services licenses the funeral home and, through its Division of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services, licensed cemeteries too, so a complaint about a plot sale or about a funeral home's bill starts at the same number: 850-413-3039. Some church, family and municipal cemeteries fall outside that licensing, so ask a cemetery whether it is state-licensed before you buy.

No Florida law makes the family hire a funeral director for a burial. The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not put Florida among its nine restrictive states, so a family can deal with the cemetery directly over the plot, the opening of the grave and which container it will accept, and pay a funeral home only for what it actually does.

The 48-hour wait in Fla. Stat. § 872.03 and § 406.11(1)(c) applies only to cremation; a burial can go ahead as soon as the burial-transit permit is issued. It matters only if the family switches plans.

Unclaimed remains and limited money

Florida has no state burial payment. When a body is unclaimed, whoever has charge of it must make a reasonable effort to find relatives and to check whether the person qualifies for a national cemetery before it is buried or cremated, and the county commission can then arrange the burial (Fla. Stat. §§ 406.50, 406.53). Social Security's one-time $255 goes only to a surviving spouse or eligible child and covers a small fraction of the $3,600 Florida plot estimate used on this page.

Veterans

Florida has eight VA national cemeteries open for burials, more than most states, and an eligible veteran's grave, opening and closing, liner and headstone in any of them cost the family nothing: Barrancas in Pensacola (850-453-4108), Bay Pines in St. Petersburg (727-319-6479, cremated remains only), Cape Canaveral (321-383-2638), Florida National in Bushnell (352-793-7740), Jacksonville (904-766-5222), Sarasota (877-861-9840), South Florida in Lake Worth (561-649-6489) and Tallahassee (850-402-8941) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration). The funeral home books the grave through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office, 800-535-1117.

Before you sign anything in Florida

  1. Get the cemetery's own price sheet for the plot, opening and closing, vault or liner and marker, since these are usually billed apart from the funeral home.
  2. Ask the cemetery about its rules on opening times, outer containers and markers before you buy any of them.
  3. If the person served, say so early; § 406.50 requires whoever has charge of unclaimed remains to check national-cemetery eligibility.
  4. Compare the nearest of the eight national cemeteries with the cost of a private Florida plot.
  5. If a funeral home or licensed cemetery misstates a price, call 850-413-3039; the call costs nothing.
