Who regulates a Minnesota burial, and where help comes from

Minnesota requires burial or another final disposition within 14 days of death, and embalming, refrigeration or dry ice if burial will not happen within 72 hours (Minn. Stat. § 149A.94). A family may handle a burial itself once it has the disposition permit. Funeral homes are licensed by the Minnesota Department of Health, Health Regulation Division. A cemetery may answer to a different office, so ask the department which one covers yours.

The timing rules come from Minn. Stat. § 149A.94: burial or another disposition within 14 days, and embalming, refrigeration or dry ice if burial will not happen within 72 hours. Should the family choose cremation instead, there is no waiting period, only the permit (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95, subd. 6a).

County help with the cost

When someone dies without the means to pay, the county first checks for a prepaid funeral contract. If none is found and no spouse can pay, the county's default is to pay for cremation and for burial or interment of the ashes, unless the decedent's known wishes or faith tradition, or the spouse or next of kin, require a burial and funeral (Minn. Stat. § 261.035; official text checked November 12, 2026). Each county sets its own amount and approved providers, so call the human-services office before buying a plot. Social Security's $255 death payment goes to a qualifying spouse or child who applies within two years, not to the funeral home.

Veterans

A qualifying veteran's grave, opening and closing, liner and headstone cost nothing in a national cemetery. Fort Snelling National Cemetery (612-726-1127) is the only national cemetery in Minnesota. The state runs four veterans cemeteries of its own, at Duluth (218-520-0120), Little Falls (320-616-2527), Preston (507-765-7320) and Redwood Falls (507-709-4390), and the Leech Lake Band operates a tribal veterans cemetery at Cass Lake (218-335-4463) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Minnesota burial steps

  1. Ask the cemetery to price the grave, its opening, the liner and the marker; a Minnesota funeral home's quote rarely covers them.
  2. Ask about its rules on grave opening times, vaults and markers.
  3. If no one can pay, ask the county human-services office for its burial amount first.
  4. For a veteran, have the DD214 discharge record ready and ask Fort Snelling what else it needs.
  5. Report a funeral home that misstates burial prices to the Department of Health at 651-201-4200.
