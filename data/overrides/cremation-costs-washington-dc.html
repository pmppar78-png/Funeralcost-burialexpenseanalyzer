Cremation rules and help in Washington, DC

A District of Columbia cremation may begin once next-of-kin authorization, a filed death certificate, a Registrar's permit and medical examiner approval are in hand; the District sets no fixed waiting period (D.C. Code § 7-231.17). A family may deal with a crematory itself after those papers are complete. Cremation providers are licensed through the DC Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection.

Since DC Burial Assistance ended on September 30, 2025, there is no District payment for a cremation; a family that was on DC benefits can call the Economic Security Administration at 202-727-5355 about what is left. (Source: DC DHS Burial Assistance notice.) The $255 Social Security death payment to a qualifying DC spouse or child would cover only part of the $1,945 direct cremation estimate above.

Cremated remains of an eligible DC veteran can be placed in a VA national cemetery columbarium or in-ground site at no charge for the space. See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in DC

  1. Ask whether a DC cremation price includes the death certificates and the permit fee, which some providers bill separately.
  2. Ask which office signs DC cremation permits where the death occurred, and how long it takes.
  3. Plan a DC cremation budget without District aid, which ended in September 2025; the Economic Security Administration (202-727-5355) can review other benefits.
  4. Note the board's number, 202-671-4500, before you pay a cremation provider.
