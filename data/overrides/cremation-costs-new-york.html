New York cremation regulators and HRA help

New York sets no statutory waiting period before cremation. What it does require is a disposition permit and authorization from the next of kin (Pub. Health Law 4145; N-PCL 1517). A licensed funeral director must still file the death certificate, move the body and supervise the cremation (N.Y. Pub. Health Law § 4140). The Bureau of Funeral Directing licenses the funeral homes that sell cremation; crematories also answer to the state Division of Cemeteries.

Help with the cost

New York City's HRA can pay up to $1,700 toward a cremation for a low-income City resident. If the funeral bill exceeds $3,400, the cremation charge is deducted from that total; if the remainder is less than $3,400, an allowance can still be approved. Direct cremation is still the arrangement most likely to fit. Apply within 120 days of the death, and apply before paying if you can. Outside the city, each county social-services district sets its own rules (NYC HRA, Burial Assistance; checked November 12, 2026).

Veterans

A cremated veteran keeps the burial benefit, and the niche or plot and marker are free at Calverton (631-727-5410), Bath (607-664-4853) or Gerald B. H. Solomon Saratoga (518-581-9128) National Cemetery; see cem.va.gov.
