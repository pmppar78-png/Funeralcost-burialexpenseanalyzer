Who oversees Texas funeral homes and cemeteries

The Texas Funeral Service Commission (512-936-2474) licenses the funeral homes that arrange Texas burials. It does not regulate cemeteries, but it can point you to the right office: perpetual care cemeteries answer to the Texas Department of Banking, and church, small family and public cemeteries are exempt from that law.

Texas is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring a funeral director, so a family can work out the plot, the grave opening and the container directly with the cemetery. A disposition permit is still needed. If plans switch to cremation, Texas requires a 48-hour wait (Tex. Health & Safety Code § 716.004).

If no one can pay for the burial

Each county's commissioners court must arrange the disposition of a person whose family cannot pay (Tex. Health & Safety Code § 694.002). Policies differ widely; call the county indigent-services office to ask whether it provides a burial in a county cemetery or only cremation.

Veterans

For an eligible Texas veteran, a national cemetery supplies the plot, opens and closes the grave and sets the marker without charge. National cemeteries accepting burials in Texas are Dallas-Fort Worth (214-467-3374), Fort Bliss in El Paso (915-564-0201), Fort Sam Houston in San Antonio (210-820-3891), Houston (281-447-8686) and San Antonio National Cemetery (210-820-3891, cremated remains only). The Texas Veterans Land Board at the General Land Office maintains five state veterans cemeteries: Central Texas in Killeen (254-616-1770), Coastal Bend in Corpus Christi (361-248-4830), Rio Grande Valley in Mission (956-583-7227), the Texas State Veterans Cemetery in Abilene (325-673-4446) and West Texas in Lubbock (806-392-9927). National cemetery details: VA National Cemetery Administration.

Before you buy a Texas plot

  1. Get the funeral home's General Price List and the cemetery's own price sheet before agreeing to a burial.
  2. Ask whether the cemetery requires an outer burial container, and whether a plain liner satisfies the rule.
  3. If the county may be paying, ask the commissioners court whether it has a county cemetery for indigent burials.
  4. Veterans' families should confirm eligibility with Dallas-Fort Worth or the closest national cemetery before paying for a private plot.
  5. In a perpetual care cemetery, ask to see the trust deposit on the contract and the fund's latest annual statement.
  6. Report misleading burial prices from a funeral home to the commission at 512-936-2474; calling costs nothing.
