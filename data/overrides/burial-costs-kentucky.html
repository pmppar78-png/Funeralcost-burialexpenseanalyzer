Kentucky's regulators, veterans help and county aid

Funeral homes handling burials in Kentucky are licensed by the Kentucky Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors (502-426-4589). Cemeteries answer to a different office: under KRS 367.946, every Kentucky cemetery company and anyone selling preneed cemetery merchandise must register with the Attorney General, and KRS 367.954 requires 40% of the payments for merchandise not installed within six months to be held in trust, unless the seller posts a bond instead.

Kentucky does not appear on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that require a funeral director, so a family may arrange a burial on its own, provided it has the burial permit and follows the cemetery's rules for the grave.

Every Kentucky burial needs a disposition permit. Cremation has no set waiting period in Kentucky, though it also needs a permit (KRS 213.081; KRS 213.076(7)).

Paying for the burial

For an indigent veteran, the Kentucky Department of Veterans Affairs reimburses up to $1,000 of the burial (17 KAR 4:040). For anyone else, it is up to the county fiscal court whether the county pays (KRS 72.450). (Source: KRS 72.450; 17 KAR 4:040.) Social Security pays a one-time $255 to a qualifying spouse or child who applies within two years, and it is paid to them rather than to the cemetery.

Veterans cemeteries in Kentucky

In a national cemetery, a qualifying veteran's grave, opening and closing, liner and headstone are free. Kentucky has three VA national cemeteries taking burials: Camp Nelson (859-885-5727), Lebanon (270-692-3390) and Mill Springs (859-885-5727). The state runs five more: Kentucky Veterans Cemetery Central (270-351-5115), North (859-823-0720), Northeast (606-929-5354), Southeast (606-672-2750) and West (270-889-6106) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Kentucky next steps

  1. Ask the cemetery for its own prices; in Kentucky the plot, grave opening, container and marker rarely appear on the funeral home's bill.
  2. Ask the cemetery when it can open the grave and what it requires for vaults and markers.
  3. For a veteran, ask a state or national veterans cemetery first, since the KDVA program favors them.
  4. Have the DD214 discharge record ready when you call Camp Nelson National Cemetery.
  5. If a funeral home misprices a burial, call the board at 502-426-4589.
