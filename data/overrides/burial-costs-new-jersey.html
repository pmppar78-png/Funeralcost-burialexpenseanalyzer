New Jersey burial rules, regulators and benefits

The New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs (973-504-6425) licenses the state's funeral homes. Cemetery sales can fall under a different office, so ask who regulates the particular cemetery you are considering before you buy a plot.

Unlike most states, New Jersey does not let a family handle a burial entirely on its own. N.J. Stat. § 26:6 requires a licensed funeral director to file the death certificate, move the body and attend the final disposition, as the Funeral Consumers Alliance explains; the cemetery itself opens and closes the grave. Every burial needs a disposition permit, and if the plan changes to cremation, a 24-hour wait applies (N.J.S.A. 26:7-18.1).

Public help with the cost

New Jersey pays its two burial allowances separately: up to $714 to the cemetery, which it claims on its own form, and up to $3,056 to the funeral home. Up to $1,570 contributed by family is not counted against them (Source: N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5).

For an eligible veteran, a national cemetery provides the grave, the opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost. Finn's Point National Cemetery in Pennsville (215-222-7510) now accepts cremated remains only, so a casket burial for a New Jersey veteran usually goes to the state's Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Wrightstown (609-738-2400) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

What to do next in New Jersey

  1. Get prices from at least two cemeteries and two funeral homes, since the plot, grave opening and marker are each priced separately.
  2. Before buying an outer burial container, confirm whether the cemetery requires one.
  3. If the county is paying, make sure the cemetery files its own WFNJ-11C claim with the county welfare agency.
  4. For a veteran, ask the Doyle cemetery about eligibility before paying for a private plot.
  5. If a funeral home misleads you about burial prices, a call to 973-504-6425 is free.
