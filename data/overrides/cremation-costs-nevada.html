Cremation rules and help in Nevada

A Nevada cremation can begin as soon as the crematory has a signed death certificate and written authorization from the authorizing agent; there is no statutory waiting period (NRS 451.660). Families may buy only a direct cremation if that is what they want. Crematories and the funeral homes that sell cremation answer to the Nevada Funeral and Cemetery Services Board, which also licenses cemeteries.

Nevada counties must arrange a burial or cremation for an eligible indigent person (NRS 428.090); Washoe County's Human Services Agency runs a burial and cremation assistance program with residency and income rules. (Source: NRS 428.090.) Social Security's $255 lump sum is paid only to an eligible Nevada spouse or child, and only if claimed within two years of the death.

Choosing cremation does not give up a Nevada veteran's cemetery benefit; urns can be interred in national cemeteries free of charge. Options in Nevada include Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery (702-486-5920) and Northern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery (775-575-4441). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Nevada

  1. Ask whether a Nevada cremation price includes the death certificates and the permit fee, which some providers bill separately.
  2. Confirm who signs the Nevada cremation permit and when, so the memorial date is realistic.
  3. Ask the Nevada county human services agency about indigent cremation before choosing a crematory.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 775-825-5535.
