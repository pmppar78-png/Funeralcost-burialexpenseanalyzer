Cremation rules and help in Indiana

A cremation in Indiana cannot take place less than 48 hours after death unless the city or county health officer waives the wait in writing, and an authorizing agent must sign (IC 23-14-31-36). Indiana law also reserves removing and transporting the body for a licensed funeral director (Ind. Code § 16-37), so even a direct cremation runs through a licensee.

Crematories and the funeral homes that sell cremation are licensed through the Indiana Professional Licensing Agency.

Indiana FSSA's help for Medicaid recipients is split into up to $1,200 for the funeral home and $800 for the cemetery, and the claim has to be filed within 90 days (Ind. Code § 12-14-17); ask the local FSSA office how those amounts apply to a cremation. (Source: Ind. Code § 12-14-17.)

Choosing cremation does not give up an Indiana veteran's cemetery benefit; urns can be interred in national cemeteries free of charge. Options in Indiana include Marion National Cemetery (765-674-0284), Crown Hill National Cemetery Annex (317-916-5460, cremated remains only), and New Albany National Cemetery (502-893-3852, cremated remains only). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Indiana

  1. Get written cremation prices from at least two Indiana providers and compare what each direct cremation price includes.
  2. Allow at least 48 hours after death in Indiana, plus time for the permit, before the cremation date.
  3. Ask Indiana DFR how the $1,200 funeral and $800 cemetery split applies to a cremation before the 90 days run out.
  4. Note the board's number, 317-234-3031, before you pay a cremation provider.
