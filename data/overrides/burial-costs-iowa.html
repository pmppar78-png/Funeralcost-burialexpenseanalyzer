Iowa burial regulators and county assistance

The Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (515-229-3939) licenses funeral homes and handles complaints. A cemetery can have a different regulator, so ask the cemetery.

The funeral-director requirement the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists for Iowa (Iowa Code § 331.805; Iowa Admin. Code 481-900.10(2)) applies to arranging a cremation, not a burial, so for a burial ask the cemetery what it needs from the family.

The burial needs a disposition permit. Cremation instead has no fixed waiting period, but a medical examiner must first issue a cremation permit (Iowa Code § 331.805(3)(b)).

County assistance

Each county board of supervisors decides what burial help it gives; the state code caps it at $250 only for a nonresident transient, and counties set their own amounts for residents, such as Linn County's $1,000 (Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27). Call the county's general assistance office before choosing a cemetery. Social Security's $255 is paid to a qualifying spouse or child within two years, not to the cemetery.

Veterans

Keokuk National Cemetery (319-524-5193) and the Iowa Veterans Cemetery (515-727-3444) give eligible veterans a grave, opening and marker at no cost (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Iowa burial steps

  1. Ask each cemetery for its § 523I.301 disclosure of opening and interment fees.
  2. Find out whether the cemetery wants an outer container before buying one.
  3. Ask the county about its general assistance burial amount.
  4. For a veteran, call Keokuk or the Iowa Veterans Cemetery with the DD214.
  5. Complaints about a funeral home's prices go to 515-229-3939.
