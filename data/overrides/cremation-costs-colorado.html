Cremation rules and help in Colorado

Colorado sets no waiting period before cremation. The crematory needs written authorization and a permit (C.R.S. § 12-135-307), and a family can arrange the cremation without a funeral director. Funeral homes and crematories already register with the Department of Regulatory Agencies. Under Senate Bill 24-173 (C.R.S. 12-135-501), the person running a Colorado cremation will also need an individual license beginning January 1, 2027, with a provisional license for current practitioners.

A Colorado cremation can qualify for state Burial Assistance of up to $1,500 if, at death, the person was enrolled in a Health First Medicaid program (including Medicare Savings Programs) or in Adult Financial assistance, and only if the provider's total charge stays at or under $2,500. Life insurance and voluntary contributions from a spouse or parent reduce the state payment (Colorado DHS Adult Financial Reference Guide, Burial Assistance Program, December 2025, Version 11.0; checked November 12, 2026). Social Security's $255 lump sum is paid only to an eligible Colorado spouse or child, and only if claimed within two years of the death.

A Colorado veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. Options in Colorado include Fort Logan National Cemetery (303-761-0117), Fort Lyon National Cemetery (719-391-6920), and Pikes Peak National Cemetery (719-391-6920). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Colorado

  1. Get written cremation prices from at least two Colorado providers and compare what each direct cremation price includes.
  2. Confirm who signs the Colorado cremation permit and when, so the memorial date is realistic.
  3. Keep a Colorado cremation quote under $2,500 if you want county human services to approve the $1,500 Burial Assistance payment.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 303-894-7800.
