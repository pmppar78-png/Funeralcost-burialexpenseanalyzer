Utah's cremation regulator and county help

Utah's Division of Professional Licensing staffs the Utah Board of Funeral Service (801-530-6621); call it to confirm a provider's license or report a problem.

Utah does not appear on the Funeral Consumers Alliance roster of states that require a funeral director, which means a Utah family holding the permit can arrange the cremation directly.

Utah sets no waiting period; filing the death certificate and getting the medical examiner's permit come first (Utah Code 58-9-610, 26B-8-230).

County help

A Utah county can cover cremation for someone who died without means (Utah Code § 17-53-221). Some, such as Salt Lake County, set income limits and leave out memorial and transport costs, so ask what is excluded before booking (Utah Code §§ 26B-8-225, 17-53-221). Social Security's $255 death payment to a widow, widower or eligible child covers only part of a $1,600 direct cremation.

Veterans

Choosing cremation keeps a veteran's cemetery benefit. Southern Utah National Cemetery (435-267-2710) and the state's Utah Veterans Cemetery and Memorial Park (801-254-9036) inter urns without a fee for the niche or grave; see cem.va.gov.

Utah to-do list

  1. Compare what two providers include in direct cremation.
  2. Ask how long the medical examiner review is running and whether refrigeration is billed while you wait.
  3. For county help, ask which costs are excluded.
  4. Keep the board's number, 801-530-6621, before you pay.
