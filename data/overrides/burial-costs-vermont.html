Who to call about a Vermont burial

A Vermont family may arrange a burial without a funeral director once the death certificate is filed and a disposition permit is issued. The 24-hour wait in 18 V.S.A. § 5201 applies to cremation, not burial. Funeral homes are licensed by the Office of Professional Regulation. Cemetery records of where remains are permanently placed must be open to the public at all reasonable times (18 V.S.A. § 5313), and a natural burial ground may locate graves by mapping, surveying or GPS instead of a standard plat (§ 5323). Because Vermont has no county government, help with an unpaid burial bill is a state General Assistance question.

Help with the cost

Vermont's General Assistance program, run by DCF, pays up to $1,100 toward a burial once the resources of the person who died and of a spouse are counted, and relatives can contribute on top without the payment being cut. (Source: Vermont DCF General Assistance burial rules; 33 V.S.A. § 2301.) The $255 Social Security payment, which goes only to a surviving spouse or child, would not cover even the $4,200 plot we estimate for Vermont.

A veteran can be buried with no charge for the plot or the marker in a national cemetery. In Vermont that usually means the Vermont Veterans Memorial Cemetery (802-828-3379), the state-run veterans ground (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Before you sign anything in Vermont

  1. Get the cemetery's charges (plot, opening and closing, vault rule, marker rules) separately from the funeral home's price list.
  2. Ask whether the cemetery will accept a headstone bought from an outside monument dealer.
  3. Have DCF confirm the $1,100 General Assistance amount before anyone buys a plot.
  4. For a veteran, check eligibility with the Vermont Veterans Memorial Cemetery before paying for a private grave.
  5. If a funeral home misstates its burial prices, report it to the Office of Professional Regulation at 802-828-1188; the call costs nothing.
