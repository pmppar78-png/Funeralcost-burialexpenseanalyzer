Iowa licensing, the cremation rule, and county burial help

Iowa licenses its funeral directors and funeral homes through the Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (DIAL, mortuary science, 515-229-3939), which also takes complaints about prices and price lists.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance counts Iowa among the nine states that limit home funerals: a licensed funeral director has to arrange any cremation (Iowa Code § 331.805; Iowa Admin. Code 481-900.10(2)). Families may still wash and dress the body or hold a service at home. A permit is needed before cremation, but there is no set waiting period (Iowa Code § 331.805(3)(b)).

Help from the county

Each county board of supervisors decides the form and amount of general assistance for a resident's burial (Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27). The state code itself sets only a $250 limit for a nonresident transient; Linn County, for example, pays up to $1,000 for residents. (Source: Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27.) The $255 Social Security payment is reserved for a surviving spouse or child, who must apply within two years; the funeral home cannot collect it.

Veterans' cemeteries

Keokuk National Cemetery (319-524-5193) is reserved by the funeral home through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office at 800-535-1117. The Iowa Veterans Cemetery near Van Meter (515-727-3444) works with families directly.

Iowa to-do list

  1. Phone two or three Iowa homes for itemized prices before you choose one.
  2. Choose early whether to embalm and hold a viewing; Iowa accepts refrigeration instead.
  3. Ask the county general assistance office what it pays before any contract is signed.
  4. If the person served, ask about the Iowa Veterans Cemetery ahead of any private plot.
  5. Report a missing or ignored price list to DIAL at 515-229-3939.
