The Kansas board, coroner burials and veterans cemeteries

Kansas sets no mandatory waiting period before cremation, but a crematory still needs a signed authorization and a coroner's permit to cremate (K.S.A. 65-1762; 65-2426a). Hiring a funeral home is optional for most other steps, so a family can pay only for the services it actually wants.

Funeral homes are licensed by the Kansas State Board of Mortuary Arts, which also takes complaints about prices or a home that will not show its general price list.

Unclaimed bodies

When no family claims a body, the Kansas coroner arranges burial or cremation (K.S.A. 22a-215). Payment comes in order: property found with the person, then the state assistance program if the person qualified under K.S.A. 39-713d, and last the county general fund. (Source: K.S.A. 22a-215; K.S.A. 39-713d.) Social Security adds only a one-time $255, and only for a qualifying spouse or child, which will not go far against a $6,600 Kansas funeral.

Kansas veterans cemeteries

Leavenworth National Cemetery (913-727-1376), Fort Scott National Cemetery (913-212-4612) and Fort Leavenworth National Cemetery (913-684-4348, cremated remains only) are reserved by the funeral home through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). Kansas's four state veterans cemeteries take requests directly: Fort Dodge (620-682-7564), Fort Riley / Manhattan (785-717-3050), WaKeeney (785-743-5685) and Winfield (620-705-6300).

Kansas steps

  1. Lay two Kansas funeral homes' printed price lists on the table and go through them line by line.
  2. Plan a Kansas memorial for after the burial or cremation to avoid paying for embalming and a viewing.
  3. When the person had no means, ask the county coroner whether K.S.A. 22a-215 applies before anyone signs.
  4. For a veteran, get the Leavenworth National Cemetery option before pricing a private Kansas plot.
  5. The board's number, 785-296-3980, is the one to call if a home will not stick to its price list.
