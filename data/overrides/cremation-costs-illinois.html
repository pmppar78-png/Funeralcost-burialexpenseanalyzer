Illinois cremation rules and DHS help

An Illinois cremation cannot happen within 24 hours of death. It also needs a permit from the county coroner or medical examiner and a signed authorization (410 ILCS 18/35). Under 410 ILCS 535, a licensed funeral director files the death certificate, moves the body, attends the cremation and obtains the permits, so even a direct cremation runs through a licensee.

Crematories and funeral homes are licensed by the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation.

DHS funeral and burial benefit

For an eligible recipient, Illinois DHS pays up to $686 toward the cremation and up to $1,370 toward the funeral home's services, $2,056 in all, if the claim is filed within 180 days of death (Illinois DHS; checked November 12, 2026). Check that the crematory's charge is billed under the $686 portion.

Veterans

Illinois veterans who qualify may be inurned at Abraham Lincoln (815-423-9958), Camp Butler (217-492-4070) or Danville (217-554-4550) National Cemetery free of charge; see cem.va.gov.

Illinois to-do list

  1. Get transport, container and crematory charges listed separately.
  2. Build the 24-hour wait into travel and memorial plans.
  3. In Cook County, ask whether the $150 permit is in the price.
  4. Report misstated prices to 217-524-1981.
