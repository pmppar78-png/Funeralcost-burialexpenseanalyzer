Who regulates a Georgia burial, and where to get help

A Georgia family may arrange a burial without a funeral director once the death certificate is filed and a disposition permit is issued. Cremation has no fixed waiting period either, though it too needs a permit (O.C.G.A. § 31-10-20; Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. r. 511-1-3-.23). Funeral homes are licensed by the Georgia State Board of Funeral Service.

When the family cannot pay

Under O.C.G.A. § 36-12-5, the county where the death occurred is responsible for an indigent burial, but each county decides how much it reimburses, up to the actual cost. Social Security's one-time $255 is paid to a surviving spouse or child who claims it within two years, not to the cemetery.

Veterans

An eligible veteran's national cemetery grave, opening and closing, and marker cost nothing. Georgia National Cemetery in Canton, north of Atlanta (770-479-9300), is the state's only VA national cemetery taking burials; the state also runs Georgia Veterans Memorial Cemeteries in Glennville (912-654-5398) and Milledgeville (478-445-3363) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration). The funeral home books a national cemetery grave through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office, 800-535-1117.

Before you sign anything in Georgia

  1. Get prices from two or more cemeteries and two or more funeral homes; the plot, the grave opening and the marker each carry their own charge.
  2. Ask whether the price includes perpetual care, and whether you may buy the headstone elsewhere.
  3. Ask the county of death what it reimburses under § 36-12-5, and get the answer in writing, before choosing a cemetery or signing a funeral contract.
  4. For a veteran, have the DD214 ready and compare Canton with the two state cemeteries.
  5. Funeral home burial prices that prove misleading can be reported to the board at 404-424-9966.
