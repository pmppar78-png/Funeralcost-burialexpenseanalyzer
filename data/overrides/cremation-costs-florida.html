Cremation rules and help in Florida

Florida law bars cremation until 48 hours after death, and the district medical examiner must also approve it first (Fla. Stat. § 872.03; Fla. Stat. § 406.11(1)(c)). The crematory still needs the death certificate, the cremation permit and a signed authorization. At the arrangement, Fla. Stat. § 497.607 requires a signed declaration of intent for the remains, has the cremation done within 48 hours of a time agreed in writing, and treats cremated remains as not property, so dividing them needs the legally authorized person's consent. Cremation providers are licensed by the Florida Department of Financial Services.

A Florida county can authorize cremation of unclaimed remains after the anatomical board declines them (Fla. Stat. § 406.50), and § 406.53 lets the county skip that board notice in some indigent cases, such as when a relative claims the remains. (Source: Fla. Stat. §§ 406.50, 406.53.) Social Security's $255 lump sum is paid only to an eligible Florida spouse or child, and only if claimed within two years of the death.

A Florida veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. Options in Florida include Florida National Cemetery (352-793-7740), Barrancas National Cemetery (850-453-4108), and Bay Pines National Cemetery (727-319-6479, cremated remains only). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Florida

  1. Ask whether a Florida cremation price includes the death certificates and the permit fee, which some providers bill separately.
  2. Build the 48-hour wait set by Fla. Stat. § 872.03; Fla. Stat. § 406.11(1)(c) into any Florida travel or memorial plans.
  3. Call the county indigent burial program in Florida before a crematory is booked; each county sets its own policy by resolution or ordinance.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 850-413-3039.
