Pennsylvania cremation licensing and DHS help

A Pennsylvania cremation may not begin until 24 hours after death, and the crematory also needs the coroner's authorization and the next of kin's consent (49 Pa. Code § 13.212). Once those conditions are met, a family holding the permit may make the cremation arrangements itself. Funeral homes that sell cremation are licensed by the Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors.

DHS payment

Social Security's one-time $255 is paid only to a surviving spouse or eligible child who applies within two years. For an eligible person, DHS can pay up to $750 toward cremation through an enrolled funeral director; an estate with $1,500 or more in resources does not qualify (55 Pa. Code ch. 283; Pennsylvania DHS).

Veterans

Washington Crossing (215-222-7510), Indiantown Gap (717-865-5254) and the National Cemetery of the Alleghenies (724-746-4363) accept an eligible veteran's urn in a columbarium or in-ground site for free; see cem.va.gov.

Pennsylvania checklist

  1. Have transport, the container and the crematory fee priced separately.
  2. Plan for at least 24 hours plus the coroner's review.
  3. Confirm the provider is enrolled with DHS before counting on $750.
  4. If the coroner takes the case, ask whether the family will want the autopsy report, which costs $500, or the toxicology or coroner's report at $100 each (16 Pa.C.S. § 13952).
  5. Keep 1-833-367-2762 for complaints.
