Cremation rules and help in Michigan

Michigan law reserves filing the death certificate, moving the body, supervising the disposition and obtaining the permits to a licensed funeral director (Mich. Comp. Laws § 700.3206). The state sets no waiting period before cremation, but the medical examiner of the county where the death occurred must sign the authorization (Mich. Comp. Laws § 333.2848). Cremation providers are licensed by the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs.

For a cremation, Michigan State Emergency Relief pays $640 in 2026 when there is a memorial service and $390 when there is not, compared with $875 and $610 for a burial (Michigan MDHHS; checked November 12, 2026). The application is due no later than 20 business days after the cremation, and only if the remains are in Michigan; MDHHS will not pay transportation to bring someone back to the state.

A Michigan veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. Options in Michigan include Great Lakes National Cemetery (248-328-0386) and Fort Custer National Cemetery (269-731-4164). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Michigan

  1. Ask whether a Michigan cremation price includes the death certificates and the permit fee, which some providers bill separately.
  2. Ask which office signs Michigan cremation permits where the death occurred, and how long it takes.
  3. Tell Michigan MDHHS whether a memorial service is planned; the 2026 SER cremation payment is $640 with one and $390 without.
  4. Note the board's number, 517-241-9221, before you pay a cremation provider.
