Cremation rules and help in Alaska

An Alaska cremation can begin as soon as the permit required by AS 13.75.020 has been issued; the state sets no waiting period. A family may deal with a crematory itself once it has the permit. Cremation providers are licensed through the mortuary science program of the Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing.

An Alaska cremation for a person with no means can be paid through General Relief Assistance: the funeral home's basic services are capped at $1,250, and the crematory charge is one of the additional necessary costs the Division of Public Assistance may pay (7 AAC 47.130). (Source: 7 AAC 47.130.) Social Security's $255 lump sum is paid only to an eligible Alaska spouse or child, and only if claimed within two years of the death.

Choosing cremation does not give up an Alaska veteran's cemetery benefit; urns can be interred in national cemeteries free of charge. Options in Alaska include Fort Richardson National Cemetery (907-384-7075), Sitka National Cemetery (907-384-7075), and Metlakatla Veterans Memorial Cemetery (tribal, 907-886-1121). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Alaska

  1. Get written cremation prices from at least two Alaska providers and compare what each direct cremation price includes.
  2. Confirm who signs the Alaska cremation permit and when, so the memorial date is realistic.
  3. Ask the Alaska funeral home to request General Relief pre-authorization (form GEN-61) before the cremation is scheduled.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 907-465-2250.
