Delaware funeral oversight, DSS funeral help and veterans' cemeteries

Delaware funeral homes are licensed through the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation, which you can reach at 302-744-4500. Use that number if a home will not show its prices, piles on charges you did not ask for, or pushes you to commit on the first visit. The division can also confirm that a funeral director's license is active.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not include Delaware among the nine states that require a funeral director for after-death care, so the family may buy only the services it actually wants. Hiring a home is a choice for most steps, not a legal duty.

The medical examiner's permit

Delaware has no waiting period before cremation, but no cremation may go ahead without a permit signed by the chief medical examiner and an authorization from the next of kin (16 Del. C. § 3159). Ask the home how long the permit is taking, since that sets the earliest date for a memorial held after cremation.

What the Division of Social Services pays

Two forms of help exist. First, DSS pays up to $100 toward funeral expenses when the person was on TANF, General Assistance or Medicaid at death (16 Del. Admin. Code 2000-2020). The next of kin sends a written request with the death certificate and proof of the bill, and the money goes to the funeral home. Second, DSS runs an indigent burial service that buries indigent or unclaimed people at state expense. Neither comes near the $8,600 average Delaware funeral in the table, and Social Security's $255, paid only to a qualifying spouse or child, does not change that much.

Veterans

The state operates Delaware Veterans Memorial Cemetery in New Castle County (302-834-8046) and in Sussex County (302-934-5653). Both take burial requests directly, and the VA provides the headstone or marker at no charge. Ask the cemetery about any fee before paying for a private plot, which averages $4,200 in the table.

Steps for a Delaware family

  1. Collect written price lists from at least two homes before agreeing to anything.
  2. Decide early about embalming and a viewing; leaving them out is often the biggest saving.
  3. If the person was on TANF, General Assistance or Medicaid, send DSS the request, the death certificate and the bill.
  4. For a veteran, compare the New Castle County veterans cemetery with a private plot.
  5. Keep 302-744-4500 in case a home does not honor its price list.
