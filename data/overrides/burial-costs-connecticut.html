Burial rules and help in Connecticut

Connecticut requires a licensed funeral director to file the death certificate, remove the body and obtain the disposition permit before a burial can proceed (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 7-62b, 7-65a and 7-69). The grave itself is opened and closed by the Connecticut cemetery. The 48-hour clock in Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-323 applies to cremation, not burial. Funeral homes are licensed by the Department of Public Health.

For a Connecticut burial, the DSS funeral and burial allowance is capped at $1,800 and falls as contributions pass $3,400; the claim has to be filed within a year, and Connecticut has no county government to ask instead. (Source: Connecticut DSS.)

For Connecticut veterans, the plot and marker in a national cemetery are free, which can remove thousands from a burial bill. In Connecticut, state veterans cemetery: Connecticut State Veterans Cemetery (860-616-3688) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration). VA runs no national cemetery in Connecticut, which leaves the Middletown state cemetery as the in-state choice for an eligible veteran.

Next steps for a burial in Connecticut

  1. Get the funeral home's General Price List and the Connecticut cemetery's own price sheet before you agree to a burial.
  2. Ask the Connecticut cemetery for its rules on grave opening times, vaults and markers.
  3. Ask Connecticut DSS how much of the $1,800 remains for the cemetery once family contributions are counted.
  4. For a veteran, ask Connecticut State Veterans Cemetery about eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. If a Connecticut funeral home gives you misleading burial prices, you can complain to the state at 860-509-7603 at no cost.
