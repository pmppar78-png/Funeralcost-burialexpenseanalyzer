Who regulates burial in Hawaii, and what help exists

Hawaii's Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs licenses both cemeteries and prepaid funeral plan sellers, so one agency covers a license question about either. Its Regulated Industries Complaints Office investigates complaints. A family does not need a funeral director for a Hawaii burial and can settle the plot, the opening and the container with the cemetery directly.

A burial still needs a disposition permit. Cremation has no fixed waiting period, but a health-department permit cannot be issued until a death certificate is filed (HRS §§ 338-23, 338-25).

State assistance

Hawaii's program does not pay for a plot. An unclaimed body is cremated with up to $1,600 paid to the provider, and the family of an eligible assistance recipient can apply within 60 days for a lump sum equal to Social Security's (Haw. Rev. Stat. § 346-15; HAR ch. 17-1745). Social Security's own death payment is $255, and only a qualifying spouse or child receives it.

Veterans

The National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (808-532-3720) takes cremated remains only. The state veterans cemeteries take burials: Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery on Oahu (808-369-3575), Kauai (808-241-4460), Lanai (808-565-7086), East and West Hawaii (808-961-8311), Maui (808-876-4534) and Molokai (808-553-3222). The plot and marker are free for an eligible veteran (VA National Cemetery Administration).

Hawaii burial checklist

  1. Get written prices from two cemeteries and two mortuaries.
  2. Ask the cemetery whether it requires an outer container.
  3. If the person received state assistance, apply to DHS within 60 days.
  4. For a veteran, call the island's state veterans cemetery first.
  5. Report a misleading cemetery or pre-need seller to 808-586-2670.
