Montana cremation oversight and help with costs

A Montana cremation cannot begin within 24 hours of death, and the crematory needs a signed cremation authorization (MCA 37-19-705). After the 24-hour wait and the signed authorization, a Montana family may make the cremation arrangements itself. Crematories and funeral homes are licensed by the Department of Labor and Industry, Board of Funeral Service.

When the family cannot pay

A Montana county can help pay for an indigent cremation through the state's indigent-funeral account, even if the county runs no general indigent program (Source: Mont. Code Ann. §§ 53-3-116, 7-4-2924). The $255 Social Security death payment, which only a qualifying spouse or child receives, covers part of the $1,600 direct cremation estimate on this page.

An eligible veteran's cremated remains can go into a VA national cemetery columbarium or an in-ground site with no charge for the space. In Montana the options include Yellowstone National Cemetery (406-647-2746), the tribal Apsaalooke Veterans Cemetery (406-620-3504) and the Eastern Montana State Veterans Cemetery in Miles City (406-218-2945). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps in Montana

  1. Ask two or more Montana providers for written direct cremation prices and check what each price covers, mileage included.
  2. Build in at least 24 hours after the death, plus time for the coroner's sign-off and the permit, when setting a date.
  3. If the county may pay, ask about its negotiated cremation rate under § 53-3-116.
  4. Keep the Department of Labor and Industry's number, 406-841-2359, handy before paying any provider.
