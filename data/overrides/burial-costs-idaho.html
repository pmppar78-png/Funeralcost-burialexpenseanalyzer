Who regulates Idaho burials, and county help

An Idaho family may arrange a burial without a funeral director once it has the permit and meets the cemetery's rules. If a person who dies in the county is indigent, the board of county commissioners must see that the body is buried or cremated, paying no more than the county's established or negotiated rate; if no one claims the body within 14 days, the coroner makes the arrangements (Idaho Code § 31-3412). Funeral homes are licensed by the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses through the State Board of Morticians.

The burial can go ahead once the permit is issued. For cremation, Idaho has no waiting period, but the county coroner must authorize it in writing (IDAPA 24.08.01.100; Idaho Code § 39-268).

County help

The county commissioners must provide a burial or cremation for an indigent person who dies in the county, paid only up to the county's set or negotiated rate; if nobody claims the body within 14 days, the coroner makes the arrangements (Idaho Code § 31-3412). Ask what the rate pays for before you buy a plot. Social Security's $255 goes to a qualifying spouse or child and does not come close to the $3,000 plot estimate.

Veterans

Snake River Canyon National Cemetery (208-732-7499) and the state veterans cemeteries at Blackfoot (208-701-7161) and Boise (208-780-1340) bury eligible veterans with the grave, opening, liner and headstone at no charge (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Idaho burial steps

  1. Get fee sheets from two cemeteries and price lists from two funeral homes.
  2. Ask whether you may buy the headstone from a monument dealer.
  3. If the county may pay, ask the commissioners whether their rate includes the opening.
  4. For a veteran, check eligibility with Snake River Canyon or a state cemetery before buying a private plot.
  5. Report misleading prices to the Division at 208-334-3233; the call costs nothing.
