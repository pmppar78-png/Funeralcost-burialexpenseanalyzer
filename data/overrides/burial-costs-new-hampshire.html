New Hampshire burial oversight and town help

A New Hampshire family may arrange a burial without a funeral director once the death certificate is filed and a disposition permit is issued. The 48-hour clock in RSA 325-A:18 applies to cremation, not burial. Funeral homes are licensed by the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification. Help with an unpaid burial bill is a town welfare question under RSA 165:3, not a state funeral program.

Town help

When a person receiving local assistance dies, the town or city welfare officer must provide a decent burial or cremation at public expense, and the town may recover the cost from the estate or liable relatives (RSA 165:3). Ask the town welfare office before buying a lot. Social Security pays $255 once, to an eligible spouse or child, not to the cemetery, and must be claimed within two years.

Veterans

New Hampshire has no national cemetery. The New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery in Boscawen, north of Concord (603-796-2026), buries eligible veterans, with the grave, opening and closing and marker at no cost to the family (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

New Hampshire burial steps

  1. Get written prices from two cemeteries as well as two funeral homes; the plot, opening and marker are billed apart from the service.
  2. Ask whether the cemetery opens graves in winter, and what holding the body until spring costs.
  3. If the person was on town assistance, speak to the welfare officer before buying anything.
  4. For a veteran, ask the Boscawen cemetery what it needs, usually starting with the DD214.
  5. Complain to the licensing office at 603-271-2152 if a funeral home's prices were misstated.
