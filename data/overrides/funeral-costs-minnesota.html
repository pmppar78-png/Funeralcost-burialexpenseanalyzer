Who licenses Minnesota funeral homes, and county and veterans help

When a person dies in Minnesota without apparent means to pay for a funeral or final disposition, the county board must first look for any prepaid funeral contract. If there is not enough money, and no spouse who can pay, the county pays for cremation and for burial or interment of the ashes unless the decedent's known wishes or faith tradition, or the spouse or next of kin, call for a burial and funeral instead (Minn. Stat. § 261.035). If those wishes are unknown and the county has no information about next of kin, it may provide cremation. Each county sets its own dollar amount; ask the human-services office for the figure in writing before a funeral home sets its price. Minnesota funeral homes are licensed by the Department of Health's Health Regulation Division, not a separate board.

Veterans' cemeteries

Burial at Fort Snelling National Cemetery in Minneapolis (612-726-1127) is booked by the funeral director, who calls the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office on 800-535-1117. Families deal directly with the state cemeteries at Duluth (218-520-0120), Little Falls (320-616-2527), Preston (507-765-7320) and Redwood Falls (507-709-4390), and with the tribal Leech Lake Veterans Cemetery at Cass Lake (218-335-4463).

Minnesota steps

  1. Request each Minnesota home's printed price list; federal law says you get one on the spot.
  2. Decide on embalming and a viewing early, since dropping them saves the most.
  3. Ring the county human-services office before signing to learn its § 261.035 amount.
  4. For a veteran, have the home check Fort Snelling or the nearest state cemetery first.
  5. Report price-list problems to the Health Regulation Division, 651-201-4200.
