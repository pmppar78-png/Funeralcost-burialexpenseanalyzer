Cremation rules and help in Ohio

Ohio bars cremation until 24 hours after death and requires a complete death certificate, a burial-transit permit and the next of kin's written authorization (Ohio Rev. Code § 4717.23). Once those conditions are met, a family may deal with a crematory itself. Crematories and funeral homes are licensed by the Ohio Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors.

An Ohio township or city pays for cremation, as well as burial, when the body is unclaimed or the person claiming it earns no more than 150% of the poverty line (Ohio Rev. Code § 9.15); the state board can reimburse it up to $1,000 for an adult and $750 for a child 11 or younger while funds last. (Source: Ohio Rev. Code § 9.15; Ohio Admin. Code 4717-17-06.)

An Ohio veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. Options in Ohio include Dayton National Cemetery (937-268-2221), Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery (330-335-3069), and Ohio Veterans Home Cemetery (419-625-2454). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Ohio

  1. Ask whether an Ohio cremation price includes the death certificates and the permit fee, which some providers bill separately.
  2. Build the 24-hour wait set by Ohio Rev. Code § 4717.23 into any Ohio travel or memorial plans.
  3. Ask the Ohio township or city whether it pays for cremation, and bring proof of income at or below 150% of poverty.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 614-466-4252.
