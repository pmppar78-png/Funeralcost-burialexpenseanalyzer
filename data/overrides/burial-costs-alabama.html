Alabama burial regulators and county help

The Alabama Board of Funeral Services (334-242-4049) licenses funeral homes and, since October 2023, oversees most cemeteries; call it for a license check or a complaint about either.

Alabama requires a licensed funeral establishment to arrange a cremation (Ala. Code § 34-13-121(b)), but it does not make families hire a funeral director for every arrangement, so ask the cemetery what it needs before a burial.

Burial needs a disposition permit. Cremation adds a 24-hour wait (Ala. Code § 34-13-121).

When there is no estate

The county commission must pay necessary burial expenses when a person dies with no estate and no relatives in that county able to pay, and the county of death, not of residence, is charged (Ala. Code § 38-8-2). Call that county's commission office before choosing a plot. The $255 Social Security death payment is limited to a spouse or child who qualifies.

Veterans

Alabama National Cemetery in Montevallo (205-665-9039) and Fort Mitchell National Cemetery (334-855-4731) take burials, as does the Alabama State Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Spanish Fort (251-625-1338). The plot, opening and closing and marker are free for an eligible veteran (VA National Cemetery Administration).

Alabama burial checklist

  1. Price the cemetery on its own, apart from the funeral home.
  2. Find out whether a marker from a monument company is permitted.
  3. With no estate, ask the county of death's commission about an approved burial account.
  4. For a veteran, call Alabama National Cemetery about eligibility.
  5. Report a misleading funeral home or cemetery to the board at 334-242-4049.
