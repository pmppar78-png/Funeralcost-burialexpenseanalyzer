Cremation rules and help in Oklahoma

Oklahoma sets no waiting period before cremation, but the medical examiner must issue a cremation permit and the next of kin must authorize the cremation in writing. After that permit is issued, a family may deal with a crematory itself. Crematories and funeral homes are licensed by the Oklahoma Funeral Board.

Oklahoma's county overseers of the indigent must arrange disposition for a person who dies without means (56 O.S. § 54); ask the county commissioners whether they use cremation. (Source: 56 O.S. § 54.) The $255 Social Security death payment to a qualifying Oklahoma spouse or child would cover only part of the $1,400 direct cremation estimate above.

An Oklahoma veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. Options in Oklahoma include Fort Gibson National Cemetery (918-478-2334), Fort Sill National Cemetery (580-492-3200), and Seaman First Class Billy Turner Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 580-490-6489). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Oklahoma

  1. Ask whether an Oklahoma cremation price includes the death certificates and the permit fee, which some providers bill separately.
  2. Ask which office signs Oklahoma cremation permits where the death occurred, and how long it takes.
  3. Ask the Oklahoma county commissioners whether their indigent program offers cremation.
  4. Note the board's number, 405-522-1790, before you pay a cremation provider.
