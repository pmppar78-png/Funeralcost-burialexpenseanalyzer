New Jersey's regulator, welfare allowances and veterans cemeteries

The New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs (973-504-6425) is the office to call to check a cremation provider's license or to report a problem.

New Jersey is one of the nine states on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list that restrict family-directed care. Under N.J. Stat. § 26:6, only a licensed funeral director may file the death certificate, transport the body and be present at the final disposition, which is why the $2,400 direct cremation in the table is always arranged by a licensee.

At least 24 hours must pass after death before cremation, and cremating without a final disposition permit is a crime (N.J.S.A. 26:7-18.1).

WorkFirst and General Assistance

For a cremation paid through WorkFirst New Jersey or General Assistance, the funeral home's services and the urn come out of the $3,056 funeral allowance. The crematory's own charge, and any burial of the urn, come out of the separate $714 cemetery allowance. Family contributions of up to $1,570 do not reduce either amount (N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5). (Source: N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5.)

A qualifying veteran's ashes can go into a national or state veterans cemetery columbarium or in-ground site with no charge for the space. New Jersey's options include the Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery (609-738-2400) and Finn's Point National Cemetery (215-222-7510, cremated remains only); see cem.va.gov.

New Jersey checklist

  1. Get written prices from at least two providers and compare what each direct cremation includes.
  2. Allow for the 24-hour wait under N.J.S.A. 26:7-18.1 in any travel or memorial plans.
  3. If the county welfare agency is paying, have the crematory bill it on the cemetery form, since its charge falls under the $714 allowance.
  4. Report misstated prices to the Division of Consumer Affairs at 973-504-6425.
