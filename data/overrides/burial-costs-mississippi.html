Who oversees Mississippi burials, and where to find help

Mississippi splits burial oversight. Funeral homes are licensed by the Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service. Perpetual-care cemeteries answer to the Secretary of State's Regulation and Enforcement Division instead. No funeral director is required, so a family may arrange the grave, its opening and the burial container with the cemetery. Every burial needs a disposition permit. If the family switches to cremation, there is no fixed wait, but the death certificate must be completed and filed with Vital Records first.

County burial for those who cannot pay

Mississippi law makes the county board of supervisors responsible for burying paupers who die in the county, and the board can find a person a pauper from public-assistance records even after death (Miss. Code Ann. § 43-31-29). (Source: Miss. Code Ann. § 43-31-29.) The $255 Social Security payment to a spouse or child falls far short of the $2,600 plot estimate on this page.

Veterans can be buried free in a national cemetery, with the plot and marker included. Mississippi has Biloxi National Cemetery (228-388-6668), Corinth National Cemetery (901-386-8311) and Natchez National Cemetery (601-445-4981), and state veterans cemeteries at Kilmichael (662-262-4232) and Newton (601-683-2959) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

What to do next in Mississippi

  1. Get the funeral home's price list and the cemetery's price sheet before agreeing to anything.
  2. If the service is more than two days away, ask whether embalming or refrigeration will be used and what each costs per day.
  3. For a county burial, bring proof of public assistance to the board of supervisors.
  4. Ask Biloxi National Cemetery about a veteran's eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. Call the Board at 601-932-1973 if a funeral home misleads you about burial prices.
