Montana's licensing office, county help and veterans cemeteries

A Montana burial can take place once the disposition permit is issued. Cremation, by contrast, must wait 24 hours after death (MCA 37-19-705). Families may arrange a burial themselves, with the permit in hand and within the cemetery's rules.

Funeral homes are licensed through the Montana Department of Labor and Industry. Private, for-profit cemeteries also come under the Board of Funeral Service, while town, county and church cemeteries set their own rules.

County help

When a Montana county helps with an indigent burial, it pays the amount it has negotiated with the funeral home, and it may later seek repayment from the estate (Mont. Code Ann. § 53-3-116). (Source: Mont. Code Ann. §§ 53-3-116, 7-4-2924.) A qualifying spouse or child can also claim Social Security's $255 death payment within two years; it is paid to them, not to the cemetery.

Veterans

In a national cemetery, the family of a qualifying veteran pays nothing for the plot, opening and closing, or marker. Yellowstone National Cemetery (406-647-2746) is Montana's VA national cemetery. The state runs veterans cemeteries at Fort Harrison (406-324-4793), Miles City (406-218-2945) and Missoula (406-721-2995), and the Apsaalooke Veterans Cemetery (406-620-3504) is tribal (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Montana next steps

  1. Get the funeral home's General Price List and the cemetery's own price sheet before agreeing to a burial.
  2. Ask whether the cemetery requires an outer container, and whether it accepts burials in winter.
  3. If the county may help, ask what it has negotiated with local funeral homes.
  4. For a veteran, have the DD214 ready and call Yellowstone National Cemetery.
  5. Report a funeral home that misprices a burial at 406-841-2359.
