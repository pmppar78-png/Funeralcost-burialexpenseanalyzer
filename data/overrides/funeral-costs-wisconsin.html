Wisconsin funeral licensing, WFCAP aid and veterans burial options

In Wisconsin, funeral directors and funeral establishments hold licenses from the Department of Safety and Professional Services. The department's number, 608-266-2112, is where to check a credential or complain about a home that will not give you its price list or adds charges you did not choose. State law also limits sales pressure: Wis. Stat. §445.12 bars a funeral director from soliciting a funeral, or paying someone else to steer one, whether before or after a death.

A Wisconsin family does not have to hand every task to a funeral home. The Funeral Consumers Alliance leaves Wisconsin off its list of nine states with funeral-director requirements, which means you can buy only the services you actually want and handle the rest yourselves.

Cremation has to wait at least 48 hours after death under Wis. Stat. 979.10, so a cremation booked for the next morning is not possible.

Wisconsin is one of the few states with a dedicated funeral aid program. The Wisconsin Funeral and Cemetery Aids Program, run with county and tribal agencies, pays up to $1,500 toward funeral costs and up to $1,000 toward cemetery or crematory costs, and it caps the size of the total bill it will help with (Wisconsin DHS). Because of that cap, raise WFCAP with the county or tribal agency before the arrangements grow past its limits.

Veterans have four cemeteries to consider. Northwoods National Cemetery (906-396-9276) is booked by the funeral home through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office at 800-535-1117. The three state cemeteries take requests directly: Central Wisconsin Veterans Memorial Cemetery (715-256-5000), Northern Wisconsin Veterans Memorial Cemetery (715-635-5360) and Southern Wisconsin Veterans Memorial Cemetery (262-878-5660). Wood National Cemetery in Milwaukee no longer accepts new burials, so Milwaukee-area families usually turn to the southern cemetery at Union Grove.

What to do next in Wisconsin

  1. Collect General Price Lists from two or more Wisconsin homes; on our figures direct cremation starts near $1,700 and a traditional service averages $7,200.
  2. If money is short, ask the county or tribal agency about WFCAP before signing a contract.
  3. Leave room for the 48-hour wait if you choose cremation.
  4. For a veteran, compare Northwoods and the state cemeteries with the cost of a private plot, about $3,400 here.
  5. Report solicitation or a hidden price list to the DSPS at 608-266-2112.
