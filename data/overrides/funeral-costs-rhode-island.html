Rhode Island's funeral board, state burial aid and the veterans cemetery

No Rhode Island cremation may begin until 24 hours after death, and the medical examiner must first issue a cremation certificate (R.I. Gen. Laws § 23-3-18). A family may handle much of the other after-death care itself, as long as someone files the death certificate and obtains the disposition permit. The Board of Funeral Directing/Embalming at the Rhode Island Department of Health licenses funeral homes and directors.

GPA-Burial from the state

With no counties, the safety net is the Department of Human Services General Public Assistance burial program. It pays up to $900 toward the funeral, plus fixed amounts of $150 for a lot, $175 for opening the grave, $125 for a required liner and $150 for a crematory fee, if an application is filed within 30 days of the death. Relatives may contribute up to $1,600 for additional funeral-home services and merchandise without affecting the $900 basic allowance (R.I. Gen. Laws § 40-6-3.10; 218-RICR-20-00-3; checked November 12, 2026). Social Security's $255 death payment is a separate matter: a surviving spouse or child applies for it within two years, and it is never paid to the home.

Rhode Island Veterans Memorial Cemetery

Rhode Island has no national cemetery, so veterans are usually buried at the state's Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Exeter (401-268-3088). Families apply there directly, and the VA provides the headstone or marker free.

Rhode Island steps

  1. Get each Rhode Island home's General Price List in hand; it must give one when you visit.
  2. Think about a memorial after cremation or burial instead of a viewing, which drops embalming.
  3. Apply for GPA-Burial within 30 days; DHS pays the providers directly.
  4. For a veteran, call the Exeter cemetery first; a grave there can make a private plot unnecessary.
  5. Take any price-list complaint to the board at 401-222-4998.
