New Mexico burial rules and where to find help

The New Mexico Board of Funeral Services licenses funeral homes and answers license questions and complaints at 505-476-4622. Cemetery sales can fall under another office, so ask the board who oversees the cemetery you have in mind.

A funeral director is not required in New Mexico; the Funeral Consumers Alliance leaves it off its list of nine states that require one. A family handling a burial itself needs the burial-transit permit described above, and the cemetery's rules still apply. If plans change to cremation, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (NMSA 61-32-19).

County help

When a person leaves no estate, a New Mexico county can pay up to $1,000 toward an indigent burial. The county of residence pays, or the county where the body was found if residence is unknown (NMSA § 24-13-3). (Source: NMSA §§ 24-13-1, 24-13-3.) Social Security's one-time $255 is paid to a surviving spouse or child who claims it within two years, not to the cemetery, so it rarely changes what the cemetery is owed. Ask the county before signing a contract with a funeral home, so you know whether its help applies.

For New Mexico veterans, the plot and marker in a national cemetery are free, which can remove thousands from a burial bill. In New Mexico, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Fort Bayard National Cemetery (505-988-6400); Santa Fe National Cemetery (505-988-6400); state veterans cemeteries: Angel Fire State Veterans Cemetery (505-225-4341); Fort Stanton State Veterans Cemetery (505-205-6707); Gallup State Veterans Cemetery (575-921-3494) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration). The New Mexico Department of Veterans Services, which runs these cemeteries, also operates a fourth, Taos State Veterans Cemetery in Ranchos de Taos (505-470-3065), and lists its own numbers for Fort Stanton (505-383-4381) and Gallup (505-905-9770).

What to do next in New Mexico

  1. In a rural county, get the cemetery fee schedule from the county clerk early.
  2. Ask whether the cemetery closes for winter and what storage until spring would cost.
  3. If there is no estate, contact the county of residence before buying a plot.
  4. For a veteran, Santa Fe National Cemetery will ask for the DD214 discharge record.
  5. Call the Board of Funeral Services at 505-476-4622 about a funeral home's pricing.
