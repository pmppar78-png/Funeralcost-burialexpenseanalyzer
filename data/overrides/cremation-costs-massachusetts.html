Massachusetts's board, state help and veterans cemeteries

Funeral homes and the crematories they use in Massachusetts are overseen by the Board of Embalming and Funeral Directing (617-701-8628), which takes license questions and complaints.

Massachusetts is not one of the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as restricting family-directed care, so once the permit is issued a family can deal with a crematory itself.

Before any cremation, Massachusetts requires a 48-hour wait after death, unless a contagious disease caused it, and a medical examiner's view of the body and authorization (M.G.L. ch. 114, § 44).

Help with the cost

The Department of Transitional Assistance pays up to $1,100 toward a funeral for an eligible person, but only when the whole bill is $3,500 or less, so a direct cremation at about $2,500 is one of the few arrangements likely to qualify. (Source: Massachusetts DTA.) A qualifying spouse or child can separately claim Social Security's $255 death payment within two years.

A Massachusetts veteran's cemetery benefit does not end with cremation: the urn can be interred in a national cemetery free of charge. Massachusetts National Cemetery in Bourne (508-563-7113) serves the state, and Massachusetts runs veterans cemeteries in Agawam (413-821-9500) and Winchendon (978-297-9501); see cem.va.gov.

Massachusetts checklist

  1. Get written prices from at least two providers and compare what each direct cremation includes.
  2. Allow at least 48 hours after death, plus time for the medical examiner's view, when setting a date.
  3. Before applying to DTA, add up the whole bill and confirm it stays at or under $3,500.
  4. Collect the ashes promptly; unclaimed remains may be interred in a common grave after 12 months (M.G.L. c. 114, § 43M).
  5. Note the board's number, 617-701-8628, before you pay.
