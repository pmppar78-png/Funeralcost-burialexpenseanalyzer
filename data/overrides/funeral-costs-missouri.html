Missouri's funeral board and county help

Missouri's State Board of Embalmers & Funeral Directors licenses funeral homes and takes complaints at 573-751-0813, including about a home that won't show prices.

Missouri is absent from the Funeral Consumers Alliance's list of nine states where the law makes a funeral director part of the process, so families can pick and choose services. Cremation needs a permit but has no set waiting period (20 CSR 2120-2.071; RSMo § 194.119).

When a body is unclaimed or there is no money

If no relative or friend claims a body, the coroner arranges burial or cremation and the county commission repays the reasonable cost (RSMo § 58.460). Where there is no estate and nobody is liable to pay, the commission may pay burial costs from the county treasury (§ 58.550; RSMo §§ 58.460, 58.550). The $255 Social Security pays a spouse or child barely dents the $6,800 Missouri average in our table.

Paperwork a Missouri home owes you

Missouri adds its own rules to the federal ones. Before providing services, a funeral home must give you a written statement of charges, signed by both sides, listing the service price you chose, each extra item, every cash advance and how you will pay (RSMo 333.145). Cash advances, such as death certificate fees or a newspaper notice, can be billed only at the net amount the home actually paid, and merchandise on display must carry a price. Embalming needs written authorization (20 CSR 2120-2.070), so a family that did not sign for it should question the charge. Bring these points up if a bill does not match what was agreed. The one exception to written consent is a coroner's order: if no family can be found within six hours, the county coroner can order embalming and the family pays later (RSMo § 58.375).

On the cemetery side, Missouri's township and county cemeteries rarely advertise, yet their plots often cost about half what private cemeteries charge. The clerk in the county where the burial will happen can say which ones still sell graves and what opening the grave costs.

Veterans cemeteries in Missouri

Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery (314-845-8320) and Springfield National Cemetery (417-881-9499, cremated remains only) are reserved by the funeral director, who phones the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). Missouri's five state veterans cemeteries accept requests directly: Bloomfield (573-568-3871), Fort Leonard Wood (573-774-3496), Higginsville (660-362-6111), Jacksonville (660-372-6024) and Springfield (417-823-3944).

What to do first

  1. Have at least two Missouri price lists in hand before anyone signs.
  2. A memorial held after the cremation or burial means no embalming or viewing to pay for.
  3. No funds? The county coroner or medical examiner is the first call, before any home is chosen.
  4. For a veteran, ask about Jefferson Barracks before paying for a private plot.
  5. Keep the board's number, 573-751-0813, for any billing dispute.
