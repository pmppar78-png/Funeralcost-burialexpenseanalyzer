Minnesota's regulator, county cremation and veterans cemeteries

A Minnesota crematory must cremate remains within 24 hours of taking custody, and it needs written authorization from whoever controls disposition (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95, subd. 6a). The state sets no waiting period after death. Once the permit is issued, a family can work with a crematory itself. Crematories and the funeral homes that sell cremation are licensed by the Department of Health's Health Regulation Division.

If no one can pay

If a person dies without the means to pay, and no prepaid contract and no spouse who can pay are found, the county pays for the cremation and, if the family does not want to keep the ashes, for their burial or interment. The county must instead provide a burial and funeral when that is what the decedent's known wishes or faith tradition, or the spouse or next of kin, require (Minn. Stat. § 261.035; official text checked November 12, 2026). Amounts and approved providers differ by county, so speak to the county before choosing a crematory. Social Security's $255 lump sum is paid only to an eligible spouse or child who claims it within two years.

Cremation does not take away a Minnesota veteran's cemetery benefit: in a national cemetery the niche or plot and the marker are free. Choices in the state are Fort Snelling National Cemetery (612-726-1127), Leech Lake Veterans Cemetery - Cass Lake (tribal, 218-335-4463) and Minnesota State Veterans Cemetery - Duluth (218-520-0120); see cem.va.gov.

Before you book a Minnesota crematory

  1. Check that the quote includes death certificates and the permit fee, since some providers add them later.
  2. Find out which office approves cremations in the county of death and how long it usually takes.
  3. For a county-paid cremation, ask the county which crematory it uses.
  4. Keep the Health Regulation Division's number, 651-201-4200, in case a bill does not match the price list.
