Minnesota's regulator, county cremation and veterans cemeteries

In Minnesota, the Department of Health's Health Regulation Division licenses crematories and the funeral homes that sell cremation, and it is the office to call with a complaint (651-201-4200).

Minnesota does not appear on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict family-directed care, so once the cremation permit is issued a family can work with a crematory itself.

There is no waiting period. The crematory needs written authorization from whoever controls disposition, and it must cremate within 24 hours of taking custody (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95, subd. 6a).

If no one can pay

The county must pay to cremate a person who died without the means to cover it (Minn. Stat. § 261.035). Amounts and approved providers differ from county to county, so speak to the county before choosing a crematory. (Source: Minn. Stat. § 261.035.) Social Security's $255 lump sum is paid only to an eligible spouse or child who claims it within two years.

Cremation does not take away a Minnesota veteran's cemetery benefit: in a national cemetery the niche or plot and the marker are free. Choices in the state are Fort Snelling National Cemetery (612-726-1127), Leech Lake Veterans Cemetery - Cass Lake (tribal, 218-335-4463) and Minnesota State Veterans Cemetery - Duluth (218-520-0120); see cem.va.gov.

Before you book a Minnesota crematory

  1. Check that the quote includes death certificates and the permit fee, since some providers add them later.
  2. Find out which office approves cremations in the county of death and how long it usually takes.
  3. For a county-paid cremation, ask the county which crematory it uses.
  4. Keep the Health Regulation Division's number, 651-201-4200, in case a bill does not match the price list.
