New Hampshire cremation oversight and town help

The New Hampshire Office of Professional Licensure and Certification (603-271-2152) licenses every crematory in the state and takes complaints. Check the license before you sign.

New Hampshire does not appear on the Funeral Consumers Alliance's list of nine states that restrict family-directed care, so once the permit is issued a family can work with the crematory without hiring a funeral home. Every crematory in the state must hold a license from the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification, and its operator must have finished a training course from the Cremation Association of North America or the maker of the cremation chamber (RSA 325-A:2, 325-A:4).

Cremation must wait 48 hours after death, unless death was from a contagious disease, and needs a medical examiner's certificate (RSA 325-A:18).

Town welfare help

When a person receiving town assistance dies, the town or city welfare office must pay for a decent cremation (RSA 165:3); for a death in a county nursing home, the town where the person lived on admission pays. Social Security's $255, paid to an eligible spouse or child, covers only a slice of a $2,100 direct cremation.

Veterans

New Hampshire has no national cemetery. A cremated veteran can be placed at the New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery (603-796-2026), where the niche or plot and marker cost nothing; see cem.va.gov.

New Hampshire to-do list

  1. Have each provider list transport, the container, the crematory fee and the $60 medical examiner fee (RSA 325-A:18).
  2. Set the date at least 48 hours after death, with time for the medical examiner.
  3. For a death in a county nursing home, call the welfare office of the town of residence at admission.
  4. Save the licensing office's phone number, 603-271-2152.
