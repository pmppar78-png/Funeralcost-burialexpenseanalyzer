California's bureau, county help and veterans cemeteries

California sets no waiting period before cremation. The crematory still needs a filed death certificate, a permit and a signed authorization, and a family that has those papers may deal with a crematory itself. Crematories and funeral homes are licensed by the California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau.

When the family cannot pay

California has no state funeral payment. Indigent burial and cremation are county programs under Health & Safety Code § 7104, so the process differs by county (Source: Cal. Health & Safety Code § 7104); the county coroner or public administrator where the death occurred can explain it and say whether the family may receive the ashes. A qualifying spouse or child can also claim Social Security's $255 death payment within two years.

A California veteran keeps the national cemetery benefit after cremation, with the niche or grave and the marker provided free. National cemeteries in the state include Riverside (951-653-8417), Bakersfield (661-867-2250), Miramar (858-658-7360), Sacramento Valley (707-693-2460), San Joaquin Valley (209-854-1040) and the Los Angeles National Cemetery Annex (310-268-4675, cremated remains only); see cem.va.gov.

California checklist

  1. Ask each provider to itemize transport, the container, the crematory fee and the permit.
  2. Find out which registrar issues the permit where the death occurred and what it charges.
  3. If scattering, have the location named on the permit and return the signed copy within 10 days (§ 103060).
  4. If no one can pay, call the county coroner or public administrator before hiring anyone.
  5. Keep the bureau's number, 916-574-7870, in case of a problem.
