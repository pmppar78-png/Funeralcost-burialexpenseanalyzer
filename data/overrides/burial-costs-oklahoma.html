Oklahoma's Funeral Board, county help and veterans cemeteries

Oklahoma law lets a family arrange a burial without hiring a funeral director. The family still needs the burial permit and has to meet the cemetery's rules for the grave. There is no waiting period for burial. If plans change to cremation, Oklahoma sets no fixed wait, but the medical examiner must issue a cremation permit and the next of kin must authorize the cremation in writing (63 O.S. § 1-329.1).

Funeral homes are licensed by the Oklahoma Funeral Board. Cemeteries are handled elsewhere: perpetual care trust funds by the Insurance Department, and upkeep complaints by the Attorney General.

County help

When a person dies in an Oklahoma county without the money or means to pay for a funeral, the overseers of the poor (the county commissioners) must arrange the burial. In counties of 300,000 or more people, public cemeteries that sell plots to the general public must provide a plot at no cost and must open and close the grave; the county treasurer pays the necessary and reasonable opening-and-closing expense the overseers set (56 O.S. § 54, official OSCN text, checked November 12, 2026). Separately, Social Security pays a one-time $255 to a qualifying spouse or child who claims it within two years; the money goes to them, not to the cemetery.

Veterans

A qualifying veteran receives a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. Oklahoma's two VA national cemeteries are Fort Gibson (918-478-2334) and Fort Sill (580-492-3200). Tribal veterans cemeteries include the Seaman First Class Billy Turner Veterans Cemetery (580-490-6489), the Seminole Nation and Veterans Memorial Cemetery (405-295-4549) and White Eagle Cemetery (580-762-8104) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Oklahoma next steps

  1. Have both price lists in hand, the funeral home's and the cemetery's, before you sign for an Oklahoma burial.
  2. Ask the cemetery whether it requires an outer burial container, and whether a liner will do, before you buy one.
  3. For an indigent burial, ask the county commissioners about the grave-opening cost, which the county treasurer pays on their order.
  4. For a veteran, have the DD214 ready and call Fort Gibson National Cemetery.
  5. If a funeral home misprices a burial, call the board at 405-522-1790.
