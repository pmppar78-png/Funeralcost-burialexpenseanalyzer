Alaska's mortuary licensing, General Relief burials and veterans cemeteries

Alaska licenses funeral homes through the mortuary science program of the Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing, reachable at 907-465-2250. That is the office to call about a misleading sale, a pricing dispute or a home that will not produce its General Price List.

The state places few legal demands on families. The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count Alaska among the nine states with funeral-director requirements, so hiring a funeral home is optional for most steps and you can buy only the parts you need.

Unlike many states, Alaska has no set waiting period before cremation. A cremation permit is still required first under AS 13.75.020.

Help with costs works differently here than in the lower 48. Most of Alaska lies in the Unorganized Borough, where there is no borough government to ask for help, so the state's General Relief Assistance program is the usual source of aid. Under 7 AAC 47.130 it can pay a funeral home up to $1,250 for basic services, meaning preparation, a qualifying casket, one chapel service and the hearse, with the least-cost plot, grave opening, cremation and needed transport paid on top. The claim has to be approved in advance: the funeral home sends the GEN-61 pre-authorization form to the Division of Public Assistance before services take place. The $255 Social Security death payment goes only to a qualifying spouse or child and does little against the $9,800 average traditional funeral shown in this page's table.

For a veteran, the funeral home can book a grave at Fort Richardson National Cemetery or Sitka National Cemetery (both 907-384-7075) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). Metlakatla Veterans Memorial Cemetery, run by the tribe (907-886-1121), takes requests directly.

Steps for an Alaska family

  1. Compare General Price Lists from at least two homes; direct cremation starts around $2,800 on our estimates.
  2. If General Relief may help, make sure the GEN-61 form is sent and approved before any service.
  3. Ask whether refrigeration is available so embalming and viewing stay optional.
  4. For a veteran, weigh Fort Richardson or Sitka against a private plot, estimated at $4,500 here.
  5. Keep 907-465-2250 handy in case a home will not honor its price list.
