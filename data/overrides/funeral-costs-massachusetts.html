The Massachusetts board, state aid and veterans' graves

Massachusetts does not require a family to hire a funeral director for every step of a funeral, so there is room to do a good deal of the work yourself. The death certificate and a disposition permit are still required. A cremation, unlike a burial, must wait 48 hours after death unless a contagious disease caused it, and a medical examiner must view the body and authorize the cremation (M.G.L. ch. 114, § 44).

Funeral directors are licensed by the Massachusetts Board of Embalming and Funeral Directing.

State help with the bill

The Department of Transitional Assistance pays up to $1,100 toward the funeral of an eligible person, but only if the whole funeral costs $3,500 or less (Massachusetts DTA). With a $10,400 average traditional funeral in our table, that cap rules out most full services.

Burial for veterans

Massachusetts National Cemetery is in Bourne, on Cape Cod (508-563-7113); to reserve a grave, the funeral home calls the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The two state veterans cemeteries, in Agawam (413-821-9500) and Winchendon (978-297-9501), take requests directly.

Before you sign in Massachusetts

  1. Get the General Price List from each home you talk to; the FTC Funeral Rule requires one when you visit in person.
  2. Ask what happens to an unembalmed body after 50 hours; the answer decides whether embalming is needed.
  3. Call the DTA before costs pass $3,500, not after.
  4. For a veteran, ask about Bourne, Agawam or Winchendon before buying a private plot.
  5. If a home will not show its prices, report it to the board at 617-701-8628.
