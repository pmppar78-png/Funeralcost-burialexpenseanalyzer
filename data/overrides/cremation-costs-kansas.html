Kansas's board, unclaimed bodies and veterans cemeteries

A Kansas crematory can proceed as soon as it has a signed authorization form and a coroner's permit to cremate; the state does not impose a waiting period (K.S.A. 65-1762; 65-2426a). It will also want the death certificate. Relatives may arrange the cremation themselves once those documents are in place.

Crematories and funeral homes are licensed by the Kansas State Board of Mortuary Arts.

When no one claims the body

A Kansas coroner may cremate an unclaimed body under K.S.A. 22a-215. The state assistance program pays if the person qualified under K.S.A. 39-713d; otherwise the county general fund pays. (Source: K.S.A. 22a-215; K.S.A. 39-713d.) Social Security's separate $255 payment goes to a surviving spouse or child who applies within two years.

Burial in a national cemetery stays open to a cremated Kansas veteran at no charge for the space or the headstone. Kansas options include Leavenworth National Cemetery (913-727-1376), Fort Leavenworth National Cemetery (913-684-4348, cremated remains only) and Fort Scott National Cemetery (913-212-4612); see cem.va.gov.

Kansas checklist

  1. Find out if certified copies of the death certificate and the state permit are priced separately.
  2. Ask how quickly the coroner in the county of death usually issues the permit.
  3. If the person received public assistance, ask the coroner whether the K.S.A. 39-713d program will pay.
  4. Report misstated prices to the board at 785-296-3980.
