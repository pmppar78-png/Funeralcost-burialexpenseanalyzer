Nevada's funeral board, county burials and the two veterans cemeteries

One board covers both sides of the business in Nevada: the Nevada Funeral and Cemetery Services Board licenses funeral homes, crematories and cemeteries. Its office, 775-825-5535, takes complaints about refused price lists, pressure to buy, and bills that do not match the quote, and it can tell you whether a license is in good standing.

A Nevada family is not legally bound to hire a funeral home for most steps, and the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Nevada among the nine states with funeral-director rules. That leaves you free to buy only what you want, for example a direct cremation from one provider and a gathering you host yourselves.

Cremation rules

No statutory waiting period applies before cremation, but the crematory must have a signed death certificate and written authorization from the authorizing agent (NRS 451.660). Embalming is not required for cremation, burial or transport except where the State Board of Health orders it (NRS 451.065).

County help with an indigent funeral

Nevada places the duty on county commissioners: when an eligible indigent person dies, the board of county commissioners must provide or order a decent burial or cremation and may pay its reasonable cost (NRS 428.090). Each county runs its own program, and Washoe County's Human Services Agency, for one, applies residency and income tests, so call the county social services office before signing with a home. Social Security's one-time $255 payment, limited to a qualifying spouse or child, covers little of the $8,000 average Nevada funeral shown in the table.

Veterans

Nevada has no open national cemetery; Elko National Cemetery has not opened yet. Burials instead go to the state-run cemeteries, Northern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Fernley (775-575-4441) and Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City (702-486-5920), which take requests directly. The VA supplies the headstone or marker there at no charge. Ask the cemetery what it charges, if anything, before paying the $3,800 average private plot shown in the table.

Nevada steps in order

  1. Lay two homes' printed price lists side by side and compare them line by line.
  2. Ask what a memorial service after cremation would cost, without embalming or a viewing.
  3. Apply to the county social services agency before arrangements are made; residency and income rules apply.
  4. For a veteran in the south, price Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery against a private plot.
  5. Keep 775-825-5535 handy in case a home will not honor its price list.
