South Carolina burial regulators and county help

The South Carolina Board of Funeral Service (803-896-4429) issues funeral home licenses and investigates complaints. Perpetual care cemeteries answer to the Perpetual Care Cemetery Board, and church or family cemeteries set their own rules.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance names nine states where a funeral director must handle the arrangements, and South Carolina is not one of them, so a family can deal directly with the cemetery about the plot, the opening and the container.

The burial goes ahead once the permit is issued. If the family decides on cremation instead, South Carolina requires a 24-hour wait, next-of-kin authorization and a coroner's permit (S.C. Code § 32-8-340).

County help

South Carolina has no state funeral payment. An unclaimed body goes to the county coroner if the anatomical board declines it; the estate pays first and the county only after that, and county councils may fund indigent burial as a public purpose (S.C. Code § 17-5-570; S.C. Code § 4-9-130). Social Security's $255 lump sum, paid to an eligible spouse or child, is a small fraction of the $3,000 plot estimate.

Veterans

Beaufort National Cemetery (843-524-3925), Florence National Cemetery Annex (843-669-8783), Fort Jackson National Cemetery in Columbia (803-699-2246) and the M.J. Dolly Cooper Veterans Cemetery in Anderson (864-332-8022) bury eligible veterans with the plot and marker free (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

South Carolina burial steps

  1. Get the cemetery's written list of charges as well as the funeral home's price list.
  2. Ask whether you can bring a headstone or vault from another seller; § 40-8-100(F) says the cemetery cannot block one that meets its rules.
  3. If no one can pay, ask the county coroner what the county covers.
  4. For a veteran, ask Fort Jackson or another veterans cemetery about eligibility before buying a plot.
  5. Complaints about a funeral home go to the Board at 803-896-4429.
