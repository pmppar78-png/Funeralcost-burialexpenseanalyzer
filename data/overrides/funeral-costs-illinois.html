Licensing, state aid and veterans' graves in Illinois

In Illinois, a licensed funeral director has to file the death certificate, move the body, be present at burial or cremation, and obtain the permits (410 ILCS 535). Washing and dressing the body, or holding the service at home, can still be done by the family. Cremation may not happen sooner than 24 hours after death (410 ILCS 18/35).

Funeral directors and embalmers are licensed by the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation.

The DHS funeral and burial payment

For eligible people, the Illinois Department of Human Services pays up to $1,370 for funeral services and up to $686 for burial or cremation, a combined $2,056. The claim has to be filed within 180 days of the death (Illinois DHS; checked November 12, 2026).

Veterans cemeteries

Illinois has six VA national cemeteries: Abraham Lincoln in Elwood, southwest of Chicago (815-423-9958); Camp Butler (217-492-4070); Danville (217-554-4550); Fort Sheridan (224-610-7296, cremated remains only); Mound City (618-694-1391); and Rock Island (309-784-0000). Graves are booked by the funeral home through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). Sunset Veterans Cemetery, run by the state, takes requests directly at 217-222-8641.

An Illinois checklist

  1. Ask two or more Illinois homes for written prices before agreeing to anything.
  2. Decide early about embalming and a viewing; leaving them out is often the biggest saving.
  3. Have the funeral home file the DHS claim, both parts, well inside 180 days.
  4. For a veteran, compare Abraham Lincoln National Cemetery with a private plot.
  5. Keep IDFPR's number, 217-524-1981, handy if a home strays from its price list.
