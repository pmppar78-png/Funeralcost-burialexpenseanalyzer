Georgia's funeral board and county cremation help

Crematories and funeral homes in Georgia are licensed by the Georgia State Board of Funeral Service (404-424-9966), the place to check a license or complain.

Georgia is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict family-directed care, so a family can go to a crematory directly after the local registrar issues the disposition permit.

No waiting period applies, but the local registrar's disposition permit is required (O.C.G.A. § 31-10-20; Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. r. 511-1-3-.23).

County help when the family is indigent

The county where a person dies must provide or reimburse a cremation when the person and close family are all indigent, at an amount the county sets and never above the actual cost (O.C.G.A. § 36-12-5). Whitfield County, for one, caps cremation at $800 and burial at $2,100, and Fulton County screens requests through DFCS. The $255 Social Security pays a qualifying widow, widower or child leaves most of a $1,700 direct cremation unpaid.

A cremated Georgia veteran keeps the right to a national or state veterans cemetery space at no charge: Georgia National Cemetery in Canton (770-479-9300) and the state's Georgia Veterans Memorial Cemeteries at Glennville (912-654-5398) and Milledgeville (478-445-3363); see cem.va.gov.

Georgia checklist

  1. Ask every Georgia provider for an itemized quote covering transport, the urn or container and the crematory's own fee.
  2. Confirm who holds the right to authorize under O.C.G.A. § 31-21-7, and bring any disposition affidavit.
  3. If the family is indigent, ask the county what it pays toward a cremation; amounts differ by county.
  4. Keep the Board's number, 404-424-9966, for a complaint.
