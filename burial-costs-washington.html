Burial Costs in Washington (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 What changed: Who must consent to moving a grave in Washington (new section) Region: West

On our 2026 estimates a full Washington burial costs $19,920 to $24,420 once the funeral, plot, casket, vault, grave opening and marker are all counted. The casket and the choice of cemetery are where most families can save, and Washington offers more natural and hybrid burial sections than most states.

Cutting the Cost of a Washington Burial

Where a Washington family can save:

  • Get itemized prices everywhere: funeral homes must hand over a General Price List; cemeteries need not, but most will give you an item-by-item price sheet if you ask for that rather than a package.
  • Ask about natural sections: several Washington cemeteries now have them, skip the vault and embalming, and often price plots 30-50% below their standard sections.
  • Look at conservation burial grounds: Washington has more of them than most states. They protect the land permanently and price simply, typically $3,520-$6,160 all in, against $19,920 or more for a traditional Washington burial.
  • Mention cremation: with 76% of Washington families cremating, burial providers often have room on price, and asking about direct cremation can bring a better burial quote.
  • Check our $2,640 Washington casket estimate against the casket buying guide and these online casket retailers.
  • Liners: far cheaper than the $1,680 vault in our Washington table, and many cemeteries accept one.

Natural Burial in Washington

Natural burial is easier to arrange in Washington than in most of the country, as conservation grounds and hybrid sections have spread along the West Coast. With no vault, no embalming and a simple casket or shroud, a green burial here usually totals $4,400-$6,160. Many cemeteries have a "natural" or "hybrid" section they do not advertise, so ask.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a Washington burial cost in all?

About $8,800 for the funeral and $4,200 for a plot, then a vault ($1,680), grave opening and closing ($2,100), a casket ($2,640) and a headstone ($500-$5,000). Altogether a Washington burial typically runs $19,920 to $24,420.

Does a Washington grave need a vault?

Most Washington cemeteries ask for a vault or a cheaper liner to keep the ground level, and the Washington table puts that cost at $1,680. Get the cemetery's written policy, since a liner often satisfies it. Nationally, vaults cost about $800 to $10,000, against the $1,680 Washington estimate above.

How can I reduce burial costs in Washington?

Washington lists four lawful outcomes in RCW 68.50.110. Remains must be buried or reduced by cremation, alkaline hydrolysis or natural organic reduction within a reasonable time. Under RCW 68.50.185, only one person's remains may be processed at a time unless written permission is given after disclosure, and a violation is a gross misdemeanor. Direct burial skips embalming and a viewing, so it usually prices lowest, and you can supply your own casket with no handling fee. Veterans who qualify can be laid to rest in a VA national cemetery at no charge.

How should I price Washington cemeteries?

Prices differ even between cemeteries in the same city. Ask each for an itemized sheet covering the plot, opening and closing, vault rules, endowment care and any residency or religious limits. Natural and hybrid sections often cost 30-50% less, and conservation burial grounds are better established here than in most regions.

What is the full figure for a Washington burial?

Usually $19,920 to $24,420: funeral about $8,800, plot about $4,200, casket about $2,640, vault about $1,680, opening and closing about $2,100 and a headstone $500-$5,000.

Is green burial available in Washington?

Yes. More Washington cemeteries now have green sections (biodegradable container, no embalming, no vault), and the total is usually below a traditional burial. Where none is near, some families use a standard plot and simply skip embalming.

How Washington regulates cemeteries and prepaid plots

Washington's Funeral and Cemetery Board licenses cemeteries as well as funeral homes. A cemetery company needs a certificate of authority from the board under RCW 68.05, unless it falls within the exemptions in RCW 68.05.400, and before it may sell a single plot it must put $25,000 into an endowment care fund (RCW 68.40.010). That fund is kept apart from the cemetery's other money, and only its income may be spent on upkeep (RCW 68.44.020).

Buying cemetery goods or services ahead of time is covered by chapter 68.46 RCW (checked October 29, 2026). For each prearrangement contract the cemetery must deposit into a trust the greater of half the contract price or the wholesale cost of merchandise, and the greater of half the price or the direct cost of services. Payments are due in the trust by the 20th of the following month, and the contract must itemize each item, each service such as marker setting, and the endowment care charge on its own line (WAC 308-51B).

When you buy a plot, ask whether the cemetery holds a certificate of authority, how much of your price goes to endowment care, and whether opening and closing, the liner and marker setting are prepaid or still owed at the time of death.

Who must consent to moving a grave in Washington

Washington ties disinterment to family consent, not to a health permit. Under RCW 68.50.200–.220 (checked October 29, 2026), remains can be taken out of a cemetery plot when the cemetery authority agrees and one relative gives written consent, in this order: the surviving spouse or registered domestic partner, then the children, then the parents, then the siblings. If that consent cannot be obtained, the superior court in the cemetery's county can give permission instead, as long as that permission does not override a written contract or the regulations the cemetery authority has adopted.

A court application has its own notice rule. The cemetery and every relative who has not consented must be told at least 10 days ahead if served in person, or 15 days ahead if notice goes by mail. None of this applies when remains move to another plot within the same cemetery, when a cemetery clears a plot whose price was never paid, or when a court or coroner orders the disinterment, although the cemetery must still let the next of kin know first. Disinterring remains without legal authority is a class C felony.

Washington burial oversight, county help and veterans' graves

The Washington State Department of Licensing (360-664-1555) licenses the funeral homes that arrange burials, and its Funeral and Cemetery Board also oversees most cemeteries, as described above.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Washington among its nine states that require a funeral director, so a family can arrange a burial itself, provided the burial permit is in hand and the cemetery's own rules for the grave are met. Burial follows once the permit is issued; cremation also needs a permit but has no waiting period (RCW 70.58A.210).

County payment

Counties pay for the disposition of indigent veterans (RCW 73.08.070) and of other indigent people who die there and are not claimed (RCW 36.39.030). (Source: RCW 36.39.030; RCW 73.08.070.) Social Security's one-time $255 is paid to a surviving spouse or child who claims within two years, never to the cemetery.

Veterans

A national cemetery gives an eligible veteran the grave, its opening and closing, a liner and a headstone free. Washington options: Tahoma National Cemetery in Kent (425-413-9614), which takes casket burials; Vancouver Barracks National Cemetery (503-273-5250), cremated remains only; and the state-run Washington State Veterans Cemetery at Medical Lake, west of Spokane (509-299-6280) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Before you buy a Washington grave

  1. Price two cemeteries and two funeral homes; plot, opening and marker are billed apart from the funeral.
  2. Ask whether you may buy the headstone from an outside seller.
  3. For a veteran with no money left, ask the county about RCW 73.08.070.
  4. Tahoma will want the DD214 discharge record first; find it early.
  5. Report a home that misprices a burial to the board at 360-664-1555.
