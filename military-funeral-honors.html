Military Funeral Honors: What Families Need to Know in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 22, 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Government Sources Editorial: Our Standards

Complete guide to military funeral honors including eligibility, types of honors, and VA benefits. Honors are free, but they have to be requested, and the request usually goes through the funeral director in the first day or two after a death. This page covers what the law guarantees, what costs money, and what the family has to supply.

Understanding Military Funeral Honors

Under 10 U.S.C. § 1491, the Secretary of Defense must provide a funeral honors detail, on request, for the funeral of any eligible veteran. The minimum ceremony is set in the statute: the folding of a United States flag, presentation of that flag to the family, and the playing of Taps. If no bugler is available, the detail plays a recorded Taps on equipment it brings.

The detail must have at least two people, and at least two of them must be uniformed members of the armed forces who are not retired. Veterans organizations approved by the Defense Department can fill out the rest of the detail. The statute's definition of a veteran covers anyone discharged from active service under conditions other than dishonorable, and members or former members of the Selected Reserve.

What You Need to Know

The points families most often miss when a veteran dies:

  • Honors are requested, not automatic. Tell the funeral director that the person served, and have the discharge papers ready, because the request to the military service usually goes through the funeral home.
  • The DD-214 or another discharge document is the proof of eligibility. If it cannot be found, the family can request a copy from the National Archives' National Personnel Records Center; ask the funeral director to start that request early.
  • The honors ceremony itself costs nothing. The funeral home's own charges for the service, the casket or urn, and the cemetery are separate and are priced on its General Price List like any other funeral. Read our FTC guide
  • Burial in a VA national cemetery is free for an eligible veteran: the gravesite, opening and closing, a grave liner, a government headstone or marker, and perpetual care. Spouses and dependent children can also be eligible.
  • A burial flag is available with VA Form 27-2008, from a VA regional office or most post offices; the funeral director usually handles it.
  • Families can request a Presidential Memorial Certificate, a signed certificate honoring the veteran's service, with VA Form 40-0247. There is no charge.
  • Some veterans' families qualify for a VA burial allowance under 38 CFR § 3.1600. For a death connected to military service after September 11, 2001, the allowance is up to $2,000. Explore payment assistance

Costs and Pricing

The honors detail, the flag and the national cemetery gravesite are paid for by the government. What a veteran's family still pays depends on the choices made with the funeral home.

For comparison, the NFDA 2023 General Price List Study puts the national median for a funeral with viewing and burial at $8,300, before cemetery costs, and the median for direct cremation at $2,645 when the family supplies the container. A national cemetery burial removes the plot, vault-type liner and marker from the bill, but not the funeral home's charges.

Ask the funeral home for its General Price List and compare the lines you will actually use: transfer of remains, the basic services fee, and either a casket or a cremation container. If the veteran is being cremated, a national cemetery will inter the urn, and the honors ceremony can still take place at the committal or at a memorial service. Your Consumer Rights

The federal Funeral Rule applies to a veteran's funeral exactly as it does to any other:

  • The funeral home must give you an itemized General Price List and must quote prices over the phone. Full FTC guide
  • You can decline any item you do not want; a package that includes services you would not otherwise buy is optional. How to compare prices
  • A funeral home cannot charge a fee for handling a casket or urn bought elsewhere.
  • A provider may not say that embalming is required for a military funeral when no law requires it.
  • Ask in writing what the funeral home charges to coordinate the honors request and the national cemetery paperwork, since that work is part of its professional services.

Problems with a funeral home can be reported to the Federal Trade Commission or the state funeral board. Problems with the honors detail or a national cemetery go to the military service or to the VA's National Cemetery Administration.

Practical Steps You Can Take

  1. Find the DD-214 or other discharge papers, and the veteran's full name, service number and dates of service.
  2. Tell the funeral director that the person served and that the family wants military funeral honors.
  3. If burial or inurnment in a national cemetery is wanted, ask the funeral director to contact the National Cemetery Administration's scheduling office before setting a date.
  4. Decide whether the honors will be at the graveside, at a chapel, or at a memorial service after cremation.
  5. Name the family member who will receive the folded flag.
  6. Ask whether a local veterans organization can add a rifle volley or other honors beyond the two-person minimum.
  7. Request a Presidential Memorial Certificate, and apply for any VA burial allowance with the funeral receipts.
  8. Get the funeral home's itemized statement before you sign, and check it against the General Price List.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is eligible for military funeral honors?

Under 10 U.S.C. § 1491, a veteran who served on active duty and left under conditions other than dishonorable, and a member or former member of the Selected Reserve. Honors are barred for some people convicted of certain capital crimes, under 10 U.S.C. § 985.

What does the honors ceremony include?

At a minimum, two uniformed service members fold the flag, present it to the family, and play Taps, live or recorded. A rifle detail or larger honors depend on the service and the veteran's grade and are not guaranteed.

Do military funeral honors cost anything?

No. The detail, the flag and Taps are provided by the military. The funeral home's own charges, and any private cemetery costs, are still billed to the family.

Is burial in a national cemetery free?

For an eligible veteran, yes: the gravesite, opening and closing, a liner, the headstone or marker, and perpetual care. The family pays the funeral home for preparation, transport and the casket or urn.

Can a veteran who is cremated still receive honors?

Yes. The honors can be performed at the inurnment or at a memorial service, and an urn can be placed in a national cemetery columbarium or in-ground site.

How do we request the honors?

Most families ask the funeral director, who contacts the military service and arranges the date. Have the DD-214 ready, because eligibility has to be confirmed first.

Can the family get help with the rest of the funeral bill?

Possibly. The VA burial allowance under 38 CFR § 3.1600 pays up to $2,000 for a service-connected death after September 11, 2001; other allowances depend on the circumstances. Social Security's $255 lump-sum payment can also apply.
