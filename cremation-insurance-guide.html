What Is Cremation Insurance?

Cremation insurance is a type of final expense insurance specifically designed to cover the cost of cremation services. While any life insurance policy can technically pay for cremation, cremation-specific plans are tailored for smaller coverage amounts that match typical cremation costs of $1,000 to $7,000.

Cremation Insurance vs. Prepaid Cremation Plans

Families often confuse cremation insurance with prepaid cremation plans. Here is how they differ:

Cremation Insurance vs. Prepaid Plans
FeatureCremation InsurancePrepaid Cremation Plan
What it isLife insurance policyContract with a specific provider
PaymentMonthly premiumsLump sum or installments to provider
FlexibilityBeneficiary chooses any providerLocked to one provider
PortabilityWorks anywhere in the USMay not transfer if you move

Recommendation: Cremation insurance generally offers more flexibility and security than prepaid plans. Your beneficiary can choose any provider and keep any excess funds. Prepaid plans can be risky if the provider goes out of business or you relocate. See our prepaid plans comparison for more details.

How Much Cremation Insurance Do You Need?

The right coverage amount depends on the type of cremation you prefer and your location:

  • $3,000 – $5,000: Covers direct cremation in most states with some margin for incidental costs
  • $5,000 – $10,000: Covers cremation with a memorial service, urn, and other expenses
  • $10,000 – $15,000: Covers a full cremation funeral plus memorial, travel, or other final expenses

Cremation insurance is a small life policy with a marketing name

Insurers use "cremation insurance" to mean a final-expense policy sized to a cremation. There is no separate federal product. This page is that sizing question. For the policy types, read the funeral insurance guide. For carriers, read best burial insurance. For age bands, read burial insurance for seniors. For a contract with one crematory, read prepaid plans compared.

Size the benefit to a sourced cremation price

The NFDA 2023 General Price List Study, latest available, reports these cremation medians from member funeral homes: direct cremation $2,645 if the family provides the container and $2,750 if the funeral home provides it; a funeral with viewing and cremation $6,280. An urn's median in that study was $295. A policy of $5,000 covers a direct cremation near the median and leaves room for an urn, a death-certificate fee, and a simple gathering. A policy of $10,000 is in the range of a viewing plus cremation. It is more than a direct cremation requires. Buying $25,000 because the advertisement said "cremation insurance" is how people pay for a burial-sized policy they did not mean to buy.

Insurance versus a preneed cremation contract

A preneed contract names a funeral home, lists the services, and takes your money now, usually into a trust or an insurance policy the home controls. If you move, you are negotiating a transfer. If the home closes, you are dealing with whatever your state preneed law provides. The state board in our regulations directory is where those rules live. A cremation insurance policy pays cash to a person. That person can hire any crematory, in any state, and keep what is left. The tradeoff is that the cash may not track a future price increase, while a guaranteed preneed contract might. "Might" is doing work in that sentence. Read whether the guarantee covers cash-advance items such as the death certificate and the medical examiner fee. Many do not.

A payable-on-death account as the alternative

If you can set aside $3,000 to $6,000 now, a payable-on-death savings account pays the person you name when they bring a death certificate. There is no health question and no lapse. The money is yours while you are alive. That is often the better tool for a healthy person who wants only a direct cremation. It is a poor tool if you cannot leave the money untouched. Insurance exists for the second case, and it costs more over a long life, which the senior page asks you to multiply out.

Questions that belong on a cremation policy application

  1. Is the full benefit paid in month six if death is from illness, or only a return of premium?
  2. Is the premium guaranteed not to rise?
  3. What is the free-look period in my state?
  4. Does the application ask health questions I must answer myself, or is someone filling it in for me? You are responsible for the answers.
  5. Is this a policy I own, or a preneed contract with a named crematory?

Frequently Asked Questions

What does cremation insurance cover?

Cremation insurance pays a cash benefit to your beneficiary upon your death. While intended for cremation costs, the funds can be used for any purpose including the cremation itself, a memorial service, an urn, scattering arrangements, outstanding bills, or family expenses. The policy does not restrict how the money is spent.

How much does cremation insurance cost?

Cremation insurance typically costs $15-$60 per month depending on age, health, and coverage amount. A 60-year-old in good health might pay $20-$35/month for $5,000-$7,000 in coverage. Premiums are locked and never increase. No medical exam is required for most policies.

Is cremation insurance the same as burial insurance?

Cremation insurance and burial insurance are both types of final expense insurance. The only difference is marketing; cremation insurance is typically sold at lower coverage amounts ($3,000-$10,000) matching cremation costs, while burial insurance covers the full range up to $25,000. The actual policies work the same way.

Can I get cremation insurance with no health questions?

Yes. Guaranteed issue cremation insurance policies are available with no health questions for ages 50-85. However, these policies typically include a 2-3 year waiting period before the full benefit is payable. If you can answer health questions favorably, simplified issue policies offer immediate coverage at lower rates.

At what age should I buy cremation insurance?

The younger you purchase cremation insurance, the lower your locked-in premium will be. A 55-year-old will pay significantly less per month than a 75-year-old for the same coverage. However, cremation insurance is available and worthwhile at any age up to 85. The key factor is whether the total premiums you will pay are reasonable compared to the coverage amount.
