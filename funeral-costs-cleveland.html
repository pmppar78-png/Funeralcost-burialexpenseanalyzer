Funeral Costs in Cleveland, Ohio (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Ohio

Cleveland is the most affordable of Ohio's three major metros for funeral services — its $7,455 traditional average actually runs $355 below Columbus and Cincinnati, and the city's deep ethnic funeral home tradition keeps pricing competitive in a way the coastal cities lost long ago. Brown-Forward, Berkowitz-Kumin-Bookatz, E.F. Boyd & Son, Corrigan Craciun, Dworkin & Bernstein, and Calhoun — these aren't interchangeable chains but distinct neighborhood institutions serving Slovenian, Jewish, Black, Irish, Polish, and East Side Jewish communities, and pricing varies along community lines.

Lake View, Riverside, and the Cleveland Cemetery Landscape

Lake View Cemetery in East Cleveland — the 285-acre Victorian landmark where Presidents Garfield and Rockefeller are buried — prices standard plots at $4,200–$6,500 depending on section, well above the Cleveland average of $3,570. Riverside Cemetery on the West Side runs closer to average, and the various Catholic cemeteries (Calvary, St. Joseph's in Avon, Holy Cross in Brookpark) serve the city's large Catholic population at market rates. Ohio's cemetery tax district law lets several suburban townships in Cuyahoga and Lake counties operate municipal cemeteries at $1,200–$2,000 for residents — significantly cheaper than commercial alternatives.

Cleveland's Ethnic Funeral Home Network

Few US cities maintain the distinct ethnic funeral home tradition Cleveland still has. Brown-Forward (founded 1825, now on Mayfield Road) serves University Circle and Cleveland Heights' old-money community. E.F. Boyd & Son is the city's longest-operating Black funeral home, anchoring Cleveland's historic African-American funeral tradition at 65th and Cedar. Berkowitz-Kumin-Bookatz on South Green Road and Berkowitz-Kumin on Mayfield serve the East Side Jewish community under traditional halacha. Corrigan Craciun in Parma handles much of the West Side Slovak and Slovenian Catholic community. Milanich and Dworkin handle Southeast Side and Polish populations. These homes rarely advertise competing pricing, but the spread on the same traditional service package across communities runs $800–$1,500.

Your Rights in Cleveland

FTC Funeral Rule Protection Every Cleveland funeral home must provide an itemized General Price List, allow you to decline services, and accept third-party caskets without surcharge. The Ohio Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors enforces state licensing standards under ORC Chapter 4717. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery

Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery in Rittman, about 40 miles south of downtown Cleveland, provides free interment for eligible veterans and spouse — no plot, no opening and closing, government-issued headstone included. For Cleveland families with military service, the savings versus Lake View plus plot exceed $5,000. Stack VA benefits ($948 plot allowance, $948–$2,473 burial allowance) with Social Security's $255 death benefit.

Cleveland Invoice Lines to Question

  • Casket handling fee — Prohibited under the FTC Funeral Rule. Cleveland providers have been fined for this.
  • Mandatory embalming claims — Ohio requires embalming or refrigeration if disposition is delayed beyond 24–48 hours. Refrigeration always substitutes. Know your consumer rights
  • Lake View premium — The cemetery is historically significant but pricing runs 20-40% above alternatives. Riverside and suburban Catholic cemeteries offer the same basic service for meaningfully less.
  • Vault requirements — Cleveland Catholic and commercial cemeteries universally require vaults. Basic liner to bronze-upsell is a $2,000+ swing.

  • Winter burial surcharges — Cleveland cemeteries charge additional fees ($200–$500) for frozen-ground opening between December and March.

Answers to Common Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Cleveland?

Cleveland's 2026 traditional-funeral average is $7,455 — roughly 5% above the $7,100 Ohio state figure but $355 below Columbus and Cincinnati, making it the cheapest of Ohio's big three metros. Brown-Forward, E.F. Boyd & Son, Berkowitz-Kumin-Bookatz, and Corrigan Craciun anchor the full-service market across distinct communities. Direct cremation starts at $1,785. Lake View plots range from $4,200–$6,500; Riverside and suburban Catholic cemeteries average $3,570.

What is Cleveland's cremation-versus-burial split?

Cuyahoga County tracks Ohio's 55% cremation rate, a few points below the national 60%. Cleveland's Catholic tradition (West Side Polish, Slovenian, Italian parishes), the East Side Jewish community's halachic burial preference, and the city's Black Baptist funeral tradition all keep burial rates elevated. Still, the economic pull — $1,785 direct cremation versus $11,025 for a full Lake View burial — is steadily shifting choices toward cremation with memorial services.

Is Cleveland more or less expensive than the US average for funerals?

Cleveland runs about 5% below the $7,848 national median at $7,455 — moderately affordable by US standards. It is the cheapest of Ohio's big three metros thanks to weaker downtown commercial pressure and the density of independent ethnic funeral homes that still compete against each other. Shopping across Brown-Forward, Corrigan Craciun, and a direct-cremation specialist typically reveals $1,200+ in variable lines.

Where can I compare funeral home prices in Cleveland?

The Ohio Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors (based in Columbus) licenses every Cleveland provider under ORC Chapter 4717 and publishes disciplinary records. Request GPLs across communities: Brown-Forward (University Circle/East Side), Corrigan Craciun (West Side Catholic), E.F. Boyd & Son (historic African-American), Berkowitz-Kumin-Bookatz (Jewish), and at least one direct-cremation specialist. The Funeral Consumers Alliance of Northeast Ohio publishes annual Cuyahoga County price surveys.

What does direct cremation include in Cleveland?

Direct cremation in Cleveland is $1,785 — covers removal, the Ohio death certificate and burial-transit permit, the cremation itself, and return of cremated remains in a basic container. Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4717 governs cremation practice, and no embalming or viewing is included. Cleveland Cremation Services and Neptune Society publish competitive pricing at the $1,500–$1,700 tier.

What are the cheapest funeral choices in Cleveland?

Direct cremation at $1,500–$1,785 is the floor. Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery in Rittman (40 minutes south) gives eligible veterans free interment plus spouse. Cuyahoga County Division of Senior and Adult Services supports qualifying low-income families with indigent burial. Township-operated cemeteries in suburban Cuyahoga and Lake counties offer plots at half Lake View pricing. Stack Social Security's $255, VA benefits ($948–$2,473), third-party casket purchases from Costco (saves $1,400+ on the $2,237 casket line), and church-hosted services.
