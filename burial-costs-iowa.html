Burial Costs in Iowa (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Midwest

Burial in Iowa in 2026 is still, by national standards, an affordable undertaking — but the pricing is spread across at least three separate invoices (funeral home, cemetery, monument company), and the gap between the lowest and highest figures in a single county can easily hit $7,000. The Iowa Board of Mortuary Science at the Department of Public Health licenses funeral directors and sets the state-level consumer protections; cemeteries operate under a separate body of law and are regulated primarily by their nonprofit or municipal operators. Across the state, expected totals sit between $12,400 and $17,410.

Expect totals between $12,400 and $17,410. Rural Iowa counties can come in below that floor; Polk, Linn, Scott, and Black Hawk counties tend to trend higher.

Des Moines, Cedar Rapids, and the urban-rural pricing gap

Glendale Cemetery in Des Moines — the city's historic park cemetery, opened in 1870 and now operated by Des Moines Parks and Recreation — routinely quotes resident plots in the low $2,000s, including perpetual care. Resurrection Cemetery and Mount Calvary, run by the Catholic Diocese of Des Moines, stay close behind for practicing Catholics. Cedar Memorial in Cedar Rapids and Oakland Cemetery in Iowa City are the other long-established urban grounds, and their pricing climbs well above the $3,000 state average in premium sections. Mount Mercy in Dubuque, Fairview in Council Bluffs, and Oakdale in Davenport round out the urban tier.

Drop out of the metro areas and the price curve bends sharply downward. Madison, Warren, Jasper, and Dallas counties still list township and small-city plots between $500 and $1,200 — often without vault requirements — at grounds maintained by cemetery associations or township trustees. This is the quiet Iowa advantage: a 25-minute drive from Des Moines can save $2,500–$4,000 on plot and opening-closing fees combined.

Winter opening-and-closing in Iowa

Frost penetration in Iowa averages 30–48 inches from late December through March, deeper in the northern tier counties (Howard, Winneshiek, Allamakee, Lyon, Osceola, Dickinson, Emmet, Kossuth). Nearly every Iowa cemetery can still open a winter grave — grounds crews use frost rippers or propane thaw blankets — but the surcharge is real, typically $200–$600 on top of the standard opening-and-closing fee. Some rural cemetery associations decline winter burials entirely and hold remains in a receiving vault until spring with no charge, which can actually be the cheaper path when the family is willing to wait.

Iowa veterans' benefits stack well. The Iowa Veterans Cemetery in Adel, operated by the Iowa Department of Veterans Affairs, provides no-cost burial for eligible Iowa-resident veterans, including the grave, opening-and-closing, liner, and marker. Combined with the federal VA's $893 plot allowance (where applicable) and the Iowa Veterans Trust Fund for emergency burial assistance, a qualifying family's out-of-pocket can fall well under $3,000 total.

Where Iowa families actually cut the bill

  1. Use the county recorder. Iowa counties keep surprisingly current lists of active cemeteries, including township, pioneer, and fraternal grounds. The recorder's office or the county auditor can point you at plots that never surface in a web search.
  2. Ask the funeral home for its GPL. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, every Iowa funeral home must hand you the General Price List on arrival or over the phone. Two GPLs side-by-side almost always reveal $1,000–$2,500 of negotiable package structure.
  3. Buy the casket yourself. Iowa funeral homes cannot refuse a third-party casket and cannot charge a handling fee. Online retailers shipping to Iowa save families $800–$2,500 on identical caskets.
  4. Consider a concrete grave liner instead of a vault. Most Iowa cemeteries that require an outer enclosure accept a liner at roughly 40% of a sealed vault's cost.
  5. Compare independents with corporate chains. Iowa's independent funeral home network is unusually strong — Hamilton's, Iles, O'Keefe Mortuary, Stevens Memorial Chapel, and hundreds of small-town operators. Corporate chains (Dignity Memorial, Legacy) operate alongside them; pricing differences of $1,500–$3,500 on identical service packages are routine.

Natural burial grounds in Iowa

Iowa's green burial footprint is small but growing. Cedar Memorial in Cedar Rapids operates a dedicated natural-burial section. Prairie Wind Memorial in the Des Moines metro has offered hybrid green burial options. Private land burial is legal in Iowa under Iowa Code 523I, subject to county setbacks and recording requirements — a practice most common on multi-generation farms in the western and southern tiers of the state. County recorders can walk families through the filing requirements.

Answers to the Questions We Hear Most

What do Iowa families spend on burial?

Iowa totals for 2026 run $12,400–$17,410 for a traditional burial. Direct burial at an independent funeral home using a township cemetery plot can close below $8,500. The upper end comes from a Des Moines or Cedar Rapids metro cemetery, a premium casket purchased in-showroom, and a full visitation with rental casket pass-through — that package can push $19,000–$22,000.

Is a burial vault required in Iowa?

Iowa state statute does not mandate a burial vault. The requirement comes from individual cemetery bylaws. Glendale in Des Moines, Cedar Memorial in Cedar Rapids, Oakdale in Davenport, and essentially all active private cemeteries require either a concrete vault or a liner. Township and pioneer cemeteries across rural Iowa are more flexible — some still permit direct-in-ground interment without any outer enclosure. Confirm policy in writing with the specific cemetery before ordering.

How can I reduce burial costs in Iowa?

The largest savings in Iowa come from three moves: first, call the county recorder or auditor to identify township and pioneer cemeteries (plots often $500–$1,200 versus $3,000+ at urban memorial parks); second, shop 2–3 funeral home GPLs side-by-side, including at least one independent — the Iowa independent market is robust and pricing routinely swings $1,500–$3,500; third, purchase the casket from an online retailer under the FTC Funeral Rule, which Iowa funeral homes must accept without a handling fee.

How do cemetery prices differ across Iowa?

The spread is dramatic. A premium section at Glendale in Des Moines or Cedar Memorial can exceed $6,000. A standard plot at the same cemeteries runs $2,500–$3,500. A rural township plot in Warren, Jasper, Dallas, or Madison County is routinely $500–$1,200. Catholic diocesan cemeteries — Resurrection and Mount Calvary in Des Moines among them — sit in the middle of the range and bundle standard burial services for practicing Catholics.

What is the total cost of burial in Iowa including everything?

Adding funeral service ($6,700), plot ($3,000), casket ($2,010), vault ($1,200), opening-and-closing ($1,500), and headstone ($1,000–$3,000), the 2026 Iowa total lands at $12,400 to $17,410. Winter surcharges of $200–$600 apply at most cemeteries from December through March. Veterans burying at the Iowa Veterans Cemetery in Adel pay nothing for the grave, opening-and-closing, liner, or marker.

Can I choose a natural or green burial in Iowa?

Yes. Cedar Memorial in Cedar Rapids operates a dedicated natural-burial section. A handful of hybrid-allowing cemeteries exist in the Des Moines metro. Private-land burial is permitted under Iowa Code 523I provided the county setback and recording requirements are met — an option most practical on multi-generation farmland. The county recorder's office walks families through the filing. Natural burial totals in Iowa, without embalming or a vault, typically run $2,500–$5,500 all-in.
