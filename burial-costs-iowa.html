Burial Costs in Iowa (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Iowa burial text now explains the cemetery price disclosures in chapter 523I and county assistance; unsourced township fee ranges were removed. Region: Midwest

Expect an Iowa burial to cost about $14,910 to $19,410, cemetery fees included, on our estimates. The funeral home's service is about $6,700 of that; the plot, liner and grave opening together add about $5,700. Iowa's cemetery law makes cemeteries disclose their opening and closing prices, and this page covers that, the strict disinterment rules, county burial help and burial for veterans at Keokuk.

Cutting Iowa Burial Costs

Where Iowa families find savings:

  • Ask the township. Townships and counties run many Iowa cemeteries, and their trustees or the county auditor can tell you who sells lots and at what price.
  • Work with independent funeral homes: many Iowa homes are still family-owned, and their prices usually track the NFDA national averages more closely than chain-owned locations. On the cemetery side, Iowa Code §523I.301 requires a cemetery to say before a sale whether opening and closing is included, to disclose current opening and closing prices if it is not, and to list every fee for interment or disinterment. Section 523I.303 bars a cemetery from shutting out the licensed funeral director you hired.
  • Consider direct burial. Leaving out the viewing, embalming and ceremony takes those charges off the funeral home's bill, and a memorial can be held later.

Township and County Cemeteries in Iowa

Cemetery prices are not set by the state, so the township, county, church and private cemeteries near you may each charge differently for a lot and an opening. Before a sale, Iowa Code § 523I.301 makes the cemetery say whether opening and closing is included and list its interment fees, so ask each one for that disclosure and compare.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a burial in Iowa?

On this site's estimates, a complete Iowa burial costs $14,910 to $19,410. The largest single item is the funeral home's service, about $6,700, and the cemetery charges together (plot about $3,000, vault about $1,200, opening and closing about $1,500) add roughly $5,700. A casket bought from the funeral home adds about $2,010, and a headstone $500 to $5,000.

Two choices move the total most: direct burial, which drops the viewing and embalming, and buying the casket from an outside seller, which the FTC Funeral Rule lets you do without a handling fee. Complaints about Iowa funeral homes go to the Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (515-229-3939).

Do Iowa cemeteries make you buy a vault?

No Iowa statute requires a vault; the requirement, where there is one, is the cemetery's own. Ask before you shop for a container, since a basic liner may be all it asks for.

How do Iowa families save on burial?

The cheapest burial on most Iowa price lists is direct burial, with no embalming, viewing or service. Bringing your own casket is allowed without a handling fee under the FTC Funeral Rule, and a veteran's grave at Keokuk National Cemetery costs the family nothing.

What must an Iowa cemetery tell me about prices?

The cemetery must tell you before the sale whether opening and closing is included, give its current opening and closing prices if not, and list every interment and disinterment fee (Iowa Code § 523I.301). It also cannot bar the funeral director you hired (§ 523I.303).

Adding it all up, what does Iowa burial cost?

Line by line we use $6,700 for the service, $3,000 for the plot, $2,010 for the casket, $1,200 for the vault, $1,500 for the opening and $500 to $5,000 for a headstone, so $14,910 to $19,410, with the funeral home's cash advance items extra.

Are green burials offered in Iowa?

Some cemeteries may accept a burial with no embalming, no vault and a biodegradable container, so ask each Iowa cemetery directly; if none near you does, a family can still use a traditional plot and skip embalming. Our green burial guide lists what to ask.

Iowa's limits on disinterment

Iowa is stricter than most states about opening a grave. Without a court order, a body may be disinterred only for reburial or an autopsy, and only with a funeral director supervising. The state registrar will not issue the permit unless the person authorized to control the remains under Iowa Code § 144C.5 consents (Iowa Code § 144.34; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Cremated remains: these may be disinterred without a court order, still under a funeral director's supervision.
  • Disinterment for cremation: this needs a court order. The body can be cremated afterward only if the state or county medical examiner finds the death was from natural causes.

Iowa burial regulators and county assistance

The Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (515-229-3939) licenses funeral homes and handles complaints. A cemetery can have a different regulator, so ask the cemetery.

The funeral-director requirement the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists for Iowa (Iowa Code § 331.805; Iowa Admin. Code 481-900.10(2)) applies to arranging a cremation, not a burial, so for a burial ask the cemetery what it needs from the family.

The burial needs a disposition permit. Cremation instead has no fixed waiting period, but a medical examiner must first issue a cremation permit (Iowa Code § 331.805(3)(b)).

County assistance

Each county board of supervisors decides what burial help it gives; the state code caps it at $250 only for a nonresident transient, and counties set their own amounts for residents, such as Linn County's $1,000 (Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27). Call the county's general assistance office before choosing a cemetery. Social Security's $255 is paid to a qualifying spouse or child within two years, not to the cemetery.

Veterans

Keokuk National Cemetery (319-524-5193) and the Iowa Veterans Cemetery (515-727-3444) give eligible veterans a grave, opening and marker at no cost (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Iowa burial steps

  1. Ask each cemetery for its § 523I.301 disclosure of opening and interment fees.
  2. Find out whether the cemetery wants an outer container before buying one.
  3. Ask the county about its general assistance burial amount.
  4. For a veteran, call Keokuk or the Iowa Veterans Cemetery with the DD214.
  5. Complaints about a funeral home's prices go to 515-229-3939.
