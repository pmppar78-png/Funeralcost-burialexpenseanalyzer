Cremation Costs in Minnesota (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Minnesota cremation guide rewritten by hand, with a new section on county cremation and the regulator. Cremation Rate: 62%

About 62% of Minnesota deaths now end in cremation, by the rate we use, and the price gap explains much of it: direct cremation runs about $1,800 here, against about $6,000 for cremation with a full service. Minnesota's law works through deadlines rather than a waiting period, and every cremation needs sign-off from a county coroner or medical examiner. Both are explained below, along with what a county pays when a family cannot.

Choosing a Kind of Cremation in Minnesota

Direct Cremation

$1,800
  • The cheapest way to arrange a Minnesota cremation
  • No service is held first
  • The body goes from the place of death straight to the crematory
  • The family receives the ashes when it is done
  • Any memorial can wait until the family is ready

Cremation with Service

$6,000
  • A full funeral takes place, then the cremation
  • Visitation with the body is an option
  • The service can be in a church or a funeral home
  • A plain urn usually comes with the package
  • Feels much like a conventional funeral

One Point to Settle Before a Minnesota Cremation

Ask about this before choosing a Minnesota provider:

  • Ashes: expect 3 to 7 pounds of cremated remains, returned in whatever urn or container you choose. If the person was an eligible veteran, the ashes can be placed at Fort Snelling National Cemetery at no charge.
In Minnesota you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Minnesota bring a container, and the MN Dept. of Health is who you call if a home in Minnesota refuses it.

Cutting the Cost of a Minnesota Cremation

Three ways Minnesota families keep the bill down:

  • Ask several homes for prices. Every Minnesota funeral home must give you its General Price List, and putting three side by side shows quickly where each falls in the $1,260 to $2,520 range for direct cremation.
  • Ask for direct cremation on its own. Some Minnesota funeral homes fold it into a larger package. The price list still has to show the direct cremation price by itself, so compare that line rather than the bundle.
  • Look for help with the bill. Veteran benefits, Medicaid assistance arranged through the county human-services office, and other programs can pay some or all of a Minnesota cremation.

Cremation Societies Around the Twin Cities

Around Minneapolis and St. Paul, independent cremation societies and direct cremation providers compete with full-service funeral homes, and for the same end result their prices are often lower. That competition matters in a state where about 62% of families choose cremation. By our estimate, direct cremation at $1,800 costs a Minnesota family roughly $5,900 less than a traditional burial, so it is worth calling at least one cremation society as well as the local funeral home.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a Minnesota cremation usually cost?

Our model puts a Minnesota direct cremation, covering transport, the cremation and return of the ashes, at about $1,800. Add a ceremony and a full-service cremation comes to about $6,000.

Which kinds of cremation can a Minnesota family pick?

Three arrangements are common: direct cremation, about $1,800 on our estimate; cremation followed by a memorial; and a funeral with viewing before cremation, about $6,000. Minnesota also permits alkaline hydrolysis, sometimes called water cremation, and regulates it together with flame cremation under Minnesota Statutes chapter 149A. Not every provider offers it, so ask each one which methods it uses.

Does Minnesota have specific rules for cremation?

Minnesota sets deadlines rather than a wait. A body has to reach final disposition within 14 calendar days of death or release by the coroner or medical examiner, and if that will take more than 72 hours it must be embalmed, refrigerated or packed in dry ice; refrigeration is capped at 14 days and dry ice at four (Minn. Stat. § 149A.94). Once a crematory accepts custody, it has 24 hours to carry out the cremation (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95). The statute behind the Minnesota timing rule, whether a funeral director must be involved, and the licensing board's phone number are listed in the cremation rules section further down this page.

Where can ashes go in Minnesota?

Location decides it. Ask the owner before scattering on private land, and check with whoever manages a park or other public land. Many Minnesota families choose instead to place the urn in a columbarium niche, bury it, or keep a small portion of the ashes in keepsake jewelry.

Why do most Minnesota families choose cremation?

Cost is a large part of it. By our estimate cremation is about $5,900 cheaper than a traditional burial in Minnesota, and it lets the family set the memorial for a later date. At 62%, Minnesota sits 1.4 points under the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025 in its Cremation & Burial Report, which also expects the national share to keep climbing over the next twenty years.

How many days does a Minnesota cremation take?

Plan on 4 to 10 days from death to ashes for most Minnesota cremations. Direct cremation ($1,800) is quickest because there is no viewing or service to schedule; the cremation permit is the main wait, as Minnesota sets no fixed waiting period (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95, subd. 6a).

Can a Minnesota family hold the funeral first and cremate afterwards?

Yes. Many Minnesota providers rent a casket for a viewing or funeral held before the cremation. The law sets no waiting period, but it does require written authorization from the person who controls disposition, and once a crematory takes custody it has 24 hours to cremate (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95, subd. 6a), so settle the service date before the body goes to the crematory.

Minnesota's coroner or medical examiner approval

Every Minnesota cremation needs approval from the county coroner or medical examiner, given after looking into the death and recorded either on a cremation authorization form or electronically in the state's death record system. Performing a cremation without that approval is a misdemeanor (Minn. Stat. § 390.152; checked October 29, 2026).

  • What gets reviewed: the Minnesota Department of Health says the coroner or medical examiner reviews the cause-of-death statement before authorizing final disposition whenever the body will be cremated.
  • The family's form: separately, the crematory needs a written authorization from whoever has the legal right to control disposition, which must confirm there is no pacemaker or similar device in the body (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95, subd. 4).

Minnesota's regulator, county cremation and veterans cemeteries

In Minnesota, the Department of Health's Health Regulation Division licenses crematories and the funeral homes that sell cremation, and it is the office to call with a complaint (651-201-4200).

Minnesota does not appear on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict family-directed care, so once the cremation permit is issued a family can work with a crematory itself.

There is no waiting period. The crematory needs written authorization from whoever controls disposition, and it must cremate within 24 hours of taking custody (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95, subd. 6a).

If no one can pay

The county must pay to cremate a person who died without the means to cover it (Minn. Stat. § 261.035). Amounts and approved providers differ from county to county, so speak to the county before choosing a crematory. (Source: Minn. Stat. § 261.035.) Social Security's $255 lump sum is paid only to an eligible spouse or child who claims it within two years.

Cremation does not take away a Minnesota veteran's cemetery benefit: in a national cemetery the niche or plot and the marker are free. Choices in the state are Fort Snelling National Cemetery (612-726-1127), Leech Lake Veterans Cemetery - Cass Lake (tribal, 218-335-4463) and Minnesota State Veterans Cemetery - Duluth (218-520-0120); see cem.va.gov.

Before you book a Minnesota crematory

  1. Check that the quote includes death certificates and the permit fee, since some providers add them later.
  2. Find out which office approves cremations in the county of death and how long it usually takes.
  3. For a county-paid cremation, ask the county which crematory it uses.
  4. Keep the Health Regulation Division's number, 651-201-4200, in case a bill does not match the price list.
