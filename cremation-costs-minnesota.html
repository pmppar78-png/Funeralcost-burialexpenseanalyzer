Cremation Costs in Minnesota (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 62%

Minnesota's 62% cremation rate runs several points above the national average, and the reason is not hard to find: Cremation Society of Minnesota, a member-owned cooperative founded in the 1970s, offers direct cremation at $795 to its members — a figure that reshaped the Twin Cities market and pulled statewide pricing down with it. The statewide direct-cremation average sits at $1,800 through traditional full-service houses, but the co-op effectively sets the floor. Cost ranges on this page are drawn from the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report, which recorded a U.S. cremation rate of roughly 60.5 percent — the benchmark against which Minnesota's state rate is compared below. Pricing rules follow the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and licensing in Minnesota is administered by the Minnesota Department of Health Mortuary Science Program.

Minnesota's direct-cremation range — $1,260 at the low end to $2,520 at the top — captures one of the widest cooperative-to-full-service spreads in the country. Cremation Society of Minnesota's $795 member pricing sits below the statewide low figure; full-service houses like Bradshaw and Washburn-McReavy occupy the upper half of the range.

What to Know Before Choosing Cremation in Minnesota

Minnesota regulates funeral directing and cremation through the Department of Health's Mortuary Science Section. Key points:

  • Mortuary Science Section oversight: Licensure, cremation authorization, and identification chain-of-custody all fall under the Section's jurisdiction.
  • 24-hour minimum wait: Cremation cannot proceed within 24 hours of death absent medical examiner waiver.
  • Home burial on private land: Minnesota uniquely permits home burial in a family cemetery on private land, subject to zoning and local ordinance — an option most other states restrict or prohibit.
  • Pacemaker removal: Standard implanted device removal before cremation.
  • Alternative container: FTC Funeral Rule allows a rigid alternative in place of a casket.
Cost-Saving Tip: You do not need to purchase an urn from the cremation provider. Urns can be purchased independently for significantly less. The FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to use a container purchased elsewhere.

Cremation Society of Minnesota's Cooperative Model

The Cremation Society of Minnesota is member-owned, not investor-owned. Families pay a modest one-time membership, and the co-op's direct-cremation pricing — currently $795 — reflects the absence of equity returns. This figure has defined the low end of the Minnesota market for four decades, pulling competitive full-service pricing down as a result. The Society's model has been imitated in Colorado, Washington, and Oregon, but nowhere else has the co-op captured the market share it holds in Minnesota, where it handles a substantial portion of all direct cremations in the Twin Cities, Rochester, and Duluth combined.

Minnesota's Home Burial Allowance and the Cremation Alternative

Minnesota permits home burial in a family cemetery on private land — a provision few other states allow and one that occasionally shapes disposition decisions in rural counties. Families with intergenerational farms or cabin property in Itasca, Otter Tail, or Crow Wing counties sometimes choose cremation plus home burial of the urn rather than scatter, cemetery inurnment, or traditional full-body burial. The Minnesota Department of Health publishes guidelines covering zoning setbacks, water-table distance, and deed annotation.

County-Level Funeral Assistance

Minnesota administers funeral assistance at the county rather than state level. Hennepin and Ramsey both operate active programs paying up to $2,000 toward direct cremation — enough to cover Cremation Society membership and the direct cremation fee combined. Olmsted County (Rochester) and St. Louis County (Duluth) run smaller programs with benefit caps near $1,200. Applications go through county human services offices, and Cremation Society of Minnesota accepts assignment from all participating counties.

Scattering in the North Woods

The Boundary Waters Canoe Area allows scattering with a dispersal permit — the only wilderness permit category specifically contemplating scattering. Itasca State Park's Mississippi headwaters accommodate scattering with DNR authorization. Voyageurs National Park requires a Superintendent's permit. Private lakeshore land with owner permission remains the simplest scattering path for families, and Minnesota's 10,000-lake geography means that path is available to most families.

Answers to Common Questions

What is the typical price of cremation in Minnesota?

Minnesota cremation pricing lives in two tiers: direct cremation — transport, cremation, and return of ashes — starting around $1,800, and full-service cremation with a ceremony landing near $6,000. Urns ($50-$3,000), memorial venues ($500-$2,000), and scattering or inurnment fees ($200-$600) are separate line items most providers will quote on request. Sources: CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report and NFDA 2024 data, with regulatory detail from the Minnesota Department of Health Mortuary Science Program and the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

What types of cremation are available in Minnesota?

Families in Minnesota can choose from three main types: direct cremation ($1,800) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service (ceremony held after cremation, often more flexible on timing); and traditional cremation ($6,000) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each option can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget. Sources: CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report and NFDA 2024 data, with regulatory detail from the Minnesota Department of Health Mortuary Science Program and the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

Does Minnesota have specific rules for cremation?

Yes. Minnesota requires written authorization from the legal next of kin, a standard 24-48 hour waiting period, and in some counties a permit from the medical examiner before cremation can proceed. Pacemakers and certain implants must be removed first. The FTC Funeral Rule (federal) separately guarantees you do not need a casket for cremation — a basic cremation container suffices. Sources: CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report and NFDA 2024 data, with regulatory detail from the Minnesota Department of Health Mortuary Science Program and the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

Where are ashes allowed to be scattered in Minnesota?

Minnesota scattering rules depend on the specific location. Private land is generally fine with the owner's permission; at sea requires being at least 3 nautical miles offshore per EPA rules; many state and national parks need a permit (and in some cases prohibit it entirely). Check the local ordinance before choosing a spot. Families in Minnesota also often opt for columbarium niches, burial of the urn, or keepsake jewelry in place of scattering. Sources: CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report and NFDA 2024 data, with regulatory detail from the Minnesota Department of Health Mortuary Science Program and the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

Why is cremation so popular in Minnesota?

The 62% figure for Minnesota lines up with what most states are seeing: affordability first, a more flexible memorial window second, and slow cultural normalization of cremation as a standard choice rather than a departure. Sources: CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report and NFDA 2024 data, with regulatory detail from the Minnesota Department of Health Mortuary Science Program and the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

How quickly can cremation be completed in Minnesota?

From start to ashes, most cremations in Minnesota take 4 to 10 days. The cremation itself is 2-3 hours, but the mandatory 24-48 hour pre-cremation waiting period and the 3-7 business day processing window dominate the timeline. Direct cremation at $1,800 moves fastest since there is no viewing or service to schedule beforehand. Sources: CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report and NFDA 2024 data, with regulatory detail from the Minnesota Department of Health Mortuary Science Program and the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Minnesota?

Yes. Minnesota funeral homes regularly arrange visitation and a ceremony before cremation takes place — the full-service option typically runs around $6,000. If the ceremony timing is flexible, a memorial service after cremation (at any venue, anytime) is another option and usually costs less than the full traditional route. Sources: CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report and NFDA 2024 data, with regulatory detail from the Minnesota Department of Health Mortuary Science Program and the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).
