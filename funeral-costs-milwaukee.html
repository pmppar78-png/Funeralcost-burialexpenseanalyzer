Funeral Costs in Milwaukee, Wisconsin (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Wisconsin

Milwaukee's funeral market is still shaped by the city's deep Catholic and Lutheran traditions — Prasser-Kleczka on the south side, Becker Ritter in Brookfield and Menomonee Falls, and Krause serving the north and west suburbs have operated for generations serving German, Polish, and Irish parishes that still prefer full-service traditional burial over cremation. Wisconsin's Funeral Directors Examining Board licenses the state's funeral homes, and the state's cremation rate of roughly 58% runs close to the national figure. For Milwaukee families, the real price variance shows up between chain-owned providers and the family-run south-side operations that anchor Catholic parish burials.

Milwaukee's German Catholic Funeral Tradition Still Drives Price

A full Milwaukee Catholic funeral — vigil service at the funeral home, morning Mass at the parish, burial at Holy Cross, Calvary, or Mount Olivet Cemetery — remains the dominant arrangement on the south and northwest sides. That full-traditional pattern anchors Milwaukee's average around $7,920 even as cremation climbs nationally. Families planning a Mass-then-burial should book the parish before the funeral home sets the service timing; Milwaukee Archdiocese parishes limit funeral Mass slots to specific morning windows, and a misaligned schedule can trigger weekend-rate surcharges on the funeral home side.

Wisconsin Funeral Directors Examining Board and Your Consumer Recourse

The Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services houses the Funeral Directors Examining Board, which licenses providers and investigates complaints. Wisconsin also requires 40% of preneed funeral trust principal to be held in trust and permits cancellation with a right to the trust balance — weaker than Michigan's 100% rule but stronger than several neighboring states. If a Milwaukee funeral home refuses an itemized GPL, demands embalming for a direct cremation, or disputes a preneed refund, the Examining Board is the first place to file. Wisconsin also recognizes the Funeral Consumers Alliance of Southern Wisconsin as an active consumer group that publishes local price comparisons.

Holy Cross, Calvary, and the Milwaukee Cemetery Premium

The Catholic Cemeteries of the Archdiocese of Milwaukee operate Holy Cross on West Nash, Calvary on the west side, Mount Olivet, and Resurrection in Mequon. Archdiocese plots generally price between $1,500 and $4,500 depending on section and proximity to a mausoleum, with a perpetual-care contribution folded in. Forest Home Cemetery, the city's historic Protestant and non-sectarian burial ground since 1850, prices similarly but can run higher for heritage sections near Beer Baron Row. Wisconsin Memorial Park in Brookfield, which many northern suburbs default to, sits slightly below the Archdiocese range.

Wisconsin Funeral and Cemetery Aids Program

Milwaukee County low-income residents can apply for the state's Wisconsin Funeral and Cemetery Aids Program (WFCAP), administered locally through the Milwaukee County Department of Health and Human Services. The benefit — up to $1,500 toward funeral costs and $1,000 toward cemetery costs — requires application before services are finalized and has a strict estate-recovery provision. County social workers at the Marcia P. Coggs Center can confirm eligibility before a provider is engaged.

Your Rights in Milwaukee

FTC Funeral Rule Protection Every funeral home in Milwaukee must provide an itemized General Price List, let you choose only the services you want, and accept caskets purchased elsewhere. You are never required to buy a package. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

Lake-Effect Winter Burial Logistics in Milwaukee

Milwaukee's lake-effect winters routinely freeze cemetery ground from December through early April. Most Archdiocese cemeteries and Forest Home will hold the casket in a receiving vault and schedule the committal for spring — a service Wisconsin law permits and that adds roughly $200–$500. Families who want an immediate winter burial should ask specifically whether the cemetery charges a frozen-ground surcharge ($400–$900 at some private grounds). This is also why direct cremation demand spikes in Milwaukee during winter months — the timeline compresses and cremation sidesteps the weather entirely.

Questions and Answers

What are funeral costs in the Milwaukee area?

Milwaukee's traditional funeral averages $7,920, slightly above Wisconsin's $7,200 state figure. Direct cremation starts near $1,870, and Archdiocese or Forest Home cemetery plots run $1,500 to $4,500 depending on section. A full Catholic funeral with burial at Holy Cross or Calvary can reach $13,000 once the vault, headstone, and parish fees are included.

Is cremation or burial more common in Milwaukee?

Wisconsin's cremation rate sits around 58%, but Milwaukee's Catholic and Lutheran parish tradition keeps burial more common on the south and northwest sides than in the suburbs. Cremation demand climbs sharply from December through March when frozen-ground surcharges make immediate burial expensive. Direct cremation at $1,870 versus a $7,920 traditional service leaves a gap that drives a meaningful share of Milwaukee families toward cremation regardless of season.

What is the best way to compare Milwaukee funeral homes?

Request GPLs from two or three providers — ideally mixing a south-side independent, a west-suburban family firm, and a dedicated direct-cremation operator. Wisconsin's Funeral Directors Examining Board handles complaints if a provider refuses the itemization the FTC Funeral Rule requires. The Funeral Consumers Alliance of Southern Wisconsin publishes local member-submitted price comparisons.

What is direct cremation in Milwaukee and how much does it cost?

Direct cremation in Milwaukee starts around $1,870 and covers transport from place of death, the cremation itself, and return of the cremated remains — no embalming, viewing, or ceremony. Families typically hold a Mass or memorial service on their own schedule, often weeks or months later. Ask any Milwaukee provider for their dedicated direct-cremation package rather than the traditional cremation service line.

What are the cheapest funeral choices in Milwaukee?

Direct cremation at $1,870 is the lowest-cost disposition in Milwaukee. For burial-preferring families, Wood National Cemetery on the Milwaukee VA grounds provides veterans and qualifying family members with a free grave, headstone, and opening/closing — which alone can save $6,000. Wisconsin Funeral and Cemetery Aids Program (WFCAP) provides up to $1,500 in funeral costs and $1,000 in cemetery costs for qualifying low-income Milwaukee County residents. Buying a casket through an outside retailer remains legal under the FTC Funeral Rule and cuts casket cost by roughly half.
