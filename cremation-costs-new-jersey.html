Cremation Costs in New Jersey (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 24, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: New Jersey cremation page reworded, with the welfare allowance split and unsupported claims about the market and crematory delays removed. Cremation Rate: 48%

Nearly half of New Jersey families, about 48% on the figure we use, now choose cremation, which is 15.4 points below the 63.4% national rate NFDA projected for 2025. Prices here run higher than in most states: our estimate is about $2,400 for direct cremation and about $7,600 for cremation with a ceremony. New Jersey also requires a licensed funeral director for every cremation and a 24-hour wait, and its welfare program splits the cost between two allowances. All of that is explained below.

Ways to Lower a New Jersey Cremation Bill

Two ways to keep a New Jersey cremation affordable:

  • Compare written prices. Ask at least three New Jersey funeral homes for their General Price Lists and set the direct cremation lines side by side.
  • Ask how long it will take. Turnaround differs between New Jersey providers and crematories, so ask each for a realistic date before you plan a memorial.

Questions for a New Jersey Funeral Director

Ask a New Jersey funeral director about these points:

  • The direct cremation line. Check that the price list spells out transport, the crematory's fee and a temporary container for the ashes as part of that one price.
  • The container. You do not need a casket. Federal rules (16 CFR 453.3(b)) let you pick a simple wooden box or cardboard container for a New Jersey direct cremation.
  • The date. Ask when the ashes will be ready, allowing for the 24-hour wait, how they will be returned, and whether a certified death certificate is included.
An urn bought somewhere else is fine in New Jersey. The FTC Funeral Rule allows a family to supply the container, and the State Board of Mortuary Science, part of the Division of Consumer Affairs, hears complaints when a funeral home says no.

Catholic Families and Church Columbaria in New Jersey

About 48% of New Jersey families choose cremation. For Catholic families, the Church has allowed cremation since 1963 provided the ashes are buried or placed in a niche rather than scattered, so a niche or plot belongs in the budget along with the $2,400 direct cremation. Some New Jersey parishes have columbaria, which can keep the total under $6,080.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in New Jersey?

Our estimate for New Jersey is about $2,400 for direct cremation, including transport, the cremation and return of the ashes, and about $7,600 for cremation with a ceremony.

What kinds of cremation are available in New Jersey?

Two arrangements cover most New Jersey cremations: a direct cremation with nothing beforehand, or a cremation paired with a viewing or a later memorial. Alkaline hydrolysis is not authorized here: the state's cemetery law defines cremation as reduction by heat (N.J.S.A. 45:27-2), so water cremation for a New Jersey death has to be arranged in another state through a New Jersey funeral director. At least 24 hours must pass after death before cremation, and cremating without a final disposition permit is a crime (N.J.S.A. 26:7-18.1).

What rules govern cremation in New Jersey?

New Jersey requires at least 24 hours between death and cremation. Before starting, the crematory must have the death certificate, the permit and written consent from the next of kin or the agent the person named. For the statutes and the office that takes complaints, see the New Jersey rules section further down.

Where can I scatter ashes in New Jersey?

New Jersey has no general scattering statute, so ask the landowner, or the agency running a park or beach, before you go. Many New Jersey families instead place the urn in a columbarium niche, bury it, or keep part of the ashes in jewelry.

Why are more New Jersey families choosing cremation?

Cost is a big factor: by our estimate cremation is about $7,800 cheaper than a traditional burial in New Jersey, and the memorial can be held on any date. New Jersey is still well behind the national trend, though: NFDA projected 63.4% nationally for 2025.

How soon can a New Jersey cremation happen?

Plan on 4 to 10 days for most New Jersey cremations. Direct cremation ($2,400) is quickest, because the only fixed wait is the 24 hours New Jersey requires after death (N.J.S.A. 26:7-18.1).

Can a New Jersey family hold a funeral before cremation?

Yes. New Jersey funeral homes regularly arrange a visitation and ceremony before the cremation, for about $7,600 on our estimate. A memorial after a direct cremation is the lower-cost alternative.

New Jersey's 24-hour death certification

New Jersey's cremation timeline starts with the death certificate. The attending, covering or resident physician, an attending advanced practice nurse, or the county medical examiner must execute the death certification within a reasonable time, no more than 24 hours after death is pronounced, and it is completed through the state's electronic death registration system, NJ-EDRS (N.J.S.A. 26:6-8, as amended by P.L. 2015, c. 38; checked October 29, 2026).

If the doctor who last treated the person is unavailable, the covering physician, or failing that any physician, may examine the body and sign once satisfied the death was not from unlawful means (N.J.S.A. 26:6-10). When neither is available, the county medical examiner supplies the medical particulars. Families can ask the funeral home which of these is signing, because that is often what sets the cremation date.

New Jersey's regulator, welfare allowances and veterans cemeteries

The New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs (973-504-6425) is the office to call to check a cremation provider's license or to report a problem.

New Jersey is one of the nine states on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list that restrict family-directed care. Under N.J. Stat. § 26:6, only a licensed funeral director may file the death certificate, transport the body and be present at the final disposition, which is why the $2,400 direct cremation in the table is always arranged by a licensee.

WorkFirst and General Assistance

For a cremation paid through WorkFirst New Jersey or General Assistance, the funeral home's services and the urn come out of the $3,056 funeral allowance. The crematory's own charge, and any burial of the urn, come out of the separate $714 cemetery allowance. Family contributions of up to $1,570 do not reduce either amount (N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5). (Source: N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5.)

A qualifying veteran's ashes can go into a national or state veterans cemetery columbarium or in-ground site with no charge for the space. New Jersey's options include the Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery (609-738-2400) and Finn's Point National Cemetery (215-222-7510, cremated remains only); see cem.va.gov.

New Jersey checklist

  1. Get written prices from at least two providers and compare what each direct cremation includes.
  2. Allow for the 24-hour wait under N.J.S.A. 26:7-18.1 in any travel or memorial plans.
  3. If the county welfare agency is paying, have the crematory bill it on the cemetery form, since its charge falls under the $714 allowance.
  4. Report misstated prices to the Division of Consumer Affairs at 973-504-6425.
