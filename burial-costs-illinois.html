Burial Costs in Illinois (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: Midwest

Illinois presents one of the starkest contrasts in burial costs of any state in the nation. The Chicago metropolitan area — with its dense population, diverse cultural communities, and premium real estate — drives burial costs well above the national average, while downstate communities from Springfield to Carbondale offer significantly more affordable options. The state's burial landscape is shaped by its extraordinary ethnic and religious diversity: Polish, Italian, Irish, and German Catholic cemeteries in the Chicago area; historic African-American burial grounds; Jewish cemeteries; and the quiet municipal graveyards that dot the farming communities of central and southern Illinois. Abraham Lincoln National Cemetery in Elwood, one of the nation's busiest, serves thousands of veterans each year.

What Drives Burial Costs in Illinois

Chicago vs. Downstate

Burial in Cook County and the Chicago suburbs can cost $20,000-$30,000+ for a traditional funeral and burial. Cemetery plots in desirable Chicago-area cemeteries range from $3,000 to $10,000+. In contrast, downstate communities like Springfield, Peoria, Champaign, and Carbondale offer plots for $800 to $3,000, and total burial costs often fall below $12,000.

Diverse Cultural Cemetery Traditions

Chicago's cemetery system reflects its immigrant heritage. Polish Catholic cemeteries like St. Adalbert and Resurrection dominate parts of the southwest side. Italian Catholic cemeteries, Irish Catholic cemeteries, Jewish cemeteries in the north suburbs, and African-American cemeteries on the south side each serve distinct communities with specialized traditions and pricing.

Strong Perpetual Care Laws

Illinois has robust perpetual care requirements for licensed cemeteries, which provides assurance that cemetery grounds will be maintained but adds to plot costs. The Illinois Cemetery Oversight Act provides one of the nation's strongest regulatory frameworks for cemetery operations.

Notable Illinois Cemeteries

Abraham Lincoln National Cemetery in Elwood is one of the busiest national cemeteries, serving veterans across the Chicago region. Graceland Cemetery in Chicago features stunning architecture. Rosehill Cemetery is the largest in Chicago. Oak Ridge Cemetery in Springfield is the final resting place of President Abraham Lincoln.

Cemetery Types Available in Illinois

  • Catholic cemeteries — Extensive network through the Archdiocese of Chicago and other dioceses. Major presence in the Chicago area.
  • Municipal cemeteries — Available throughout the state, typically more affordable.
  • Private memorial parks — Large commercial operations in the Chicago suburbs.
  • Veterans cemeteries — Abraham Lincoln National Cemetery (Elwood), Rock Island National Cemetery, and others. Learn about veteran burial benefits
  • Ethnic and cultural cemeteries — Polish, Italian, Irish, Jewish, and other community cemeteries in the Chicago area.

Casket and Vault Options in Illinois

Illinois's large funeral market is competitive. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, families can buy caskets from any retailer. Chicago-area families especially benefit from online casket shopping, saving 50-80% compared to funeral home prices in the state's most expensive market.

Consumer Rights for Burial in Illinois

  • FTC Funeral Rule — Itemized pricing required.
  • Illinois Cemetery Oversight Act — One of the strongest cemetery regulatory frameworks in the nation, providing consumer protections for cemetery purchases.
  • Illinois Comptroller's Office — Oversees cemetery regulation and consumer complaints.
  • Embalming not required by Illinois law.

Green and Natural Burial in Illinois

Green burial is growing in Illinois, with some cemeteries offering natural burial sections. Windridge Memorial Park and Nature Sanctuary in Cary is one of the Chicago area's green burial options. Burial Options in Illinois

  • Green burial — Growing availability. Learn about green burial

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Illinois?

A traditional burial in Illinois costs approximately $8,500 for the funeral service plus $4,200 for a plot. Total costs range from $16,480 to $23,230. Chicago-area costs are 40-50% higher than downstate.

Is a burial vault required in Illinois?

No state law requires vaults. Most cemeteries require them, but some green burial sections allow biodegradable containers without vaults.

What are the cheapest burial options in Illinois?

Direct burial at a downstate municipal cemetery can cost $7,000-$10,000. Buying caskets online saves $1,500-$5,000. Veterans receive free burial at Abraham Lincoln National Cemetery and other facilities.

Can I visit Abraham Lincoln's tomb?

Yes. The Lincoln Tomb at Oak Ridge Cemetery in Springfield is a state historic site open to visitors. It is the final resting place of President Abraham Lincoln and his family.
