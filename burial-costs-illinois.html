Burial Costs in Illinois (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Illinois independent-home tip rewritten: the unsourced family-owned pricing claim is gone and readers are told to compare General Price Lists. Region: Midwest

An Illinois burial means three separate bills: the funeral home's service, around $8,500 on our estimate; the cemetery's plot and interment, from about $4,200; and a headstone or marker at $500 to $5,000. Illinois also has its own rules on licensed funeral directors, on what cemeteries must disclose, and on state help with the cost, all covered below.

Lowering the Cost of an Illinois Burial

Ways to keep an Illinois burial affordable:

  • Price cemeteries separately. Cemeteries fall outside the FTC Funeral Rule, though most Illinois cemeteries will hand over a price sheet if you ask.
  • Ask the township or county clerk. Many Illinois township, county and fraternal cemeteries have no website, and the clerk can tell you which of them still sell plots.
  • Work with independent funeral homes: an Illinois home's ownership says little about its prices, so request the General Price List from each one you are considering and compare the same lines. Cemeteries carry duties of their own. 225 ILCS 411/20-5 requires a cemetery authority to publish its rules online (or send a copy within 5 days of a request) and to show and give you its current prices for interment, inurnment, entombment and disinterment, and it makes the cemetery responsible for opening and closing graves on its property.
  • Consider direct burial. Burial soon after death, with no viewing or ceremony first, is the lowest-cost burial most Illinois funeral homes offer.

Rural Illinois Cemeteries and Their Fees

Away from Chicago, many rural Illinois counties still have old community cemeteries with open sections, priced to pay for upkeep. Opening and closing a grave at an Illinois township cemetery typically costs $400 to $900, against $1,500 to $2,500 at a private cemetery in a metro area, so a rural cemetery can save a good deal if the location suits the family.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an Illinois burial cost?

Our estimate is about $8,500 for the funeral service and $4,200 for the plot. Add a vault ($1,680), opening and closing ($2,100), a casket ($2,550) and a headstone ($500-$5,000), and the full Illinois cost is usually $19,530 to $24,030.

Must an Illinois burial have a vault?

Illinois law does not require one, but many Illinois cemeteries do, to keep the ground level. Illinois also protects your right to shop: under the Cemetery Oversight Act (225 ILCS 411/35-10) you may buy cemetery merchandise from another seller without a penalty or extra charge, provided it meets the cemetery's published rules.

Most Illinois cemeteries are licensed by the Department of Financial and Professional Regulation, which takes complaints on its hotline at 1-888-756-8331 (1-888-RLOVED1). Some small, religious and family cemeteries are exempt from the Act, so check that yours is licensed before counting on these rights.

How can an Illinois family spend less on burial?

Choose direct, or immediate, burial: with no embalming, viewing or funeral home ceremony, it is normally the cheapest burial an Illinois funeral home lists. The casket can come from somewhere else, too, and the FTC Funeral Rule bans a handling fee for that. An eligible veteran can be buried in a VA national cemetery with the grave, opening and closing, liner and marker provided free.

How should I compare Illinois cemeteries?

Prices can differ widely inside one city. Township, county and fraternal cemeteries are often listed only with the county clerk, and their plots frequently cost well under those at commercial memorial parks, so call the clerk as well as the larger cemeteries.

What does an Illinois burial cost with everything included?

All in, an Illinois burial usually comes to $19,530 to $24,030: about $8,500 for the funeral service, $4,200 for the plot, $2,550 for the casket, $1,680 for the vault, $2,100 to open and close the grave, and $500-$5,000 for a headstone.

Is green burial available in Illinois?

Some Illinois cemeteries have green burial sections, which use a biodegradable container with no embalming and no vault and usually cost less in total than a traditional burial. If no green burial ground is close, a family can ask for an ordinary plot and simply skip embalming.

Illinois: when the 24-hour report doubles as the burial permit

Illinois keeps paperwork light for an in-state burial. The funeral director, or a family member acting as one, who first takes custody of the body files a written report with the local registrar within 24 hours. For burial or entombment inside Illinois, that report serves as the permit, provided the funeral director certifies that the treating doctor or other health care professional has agreed to sign the death certificate (410 ILCS 535/21; checked October 29, 2026).

  • When a full permit is needed: cremation, moving the body out of Illinois and any other method of disposition require a permit for disposition from the registrar, issued only after a completed death certificate is on file.
  • Weekend burials: registrars must expedite permits for Sunday or holiday burials when the time of death and the family's religious beliefs call for them.
  • Cook County: in a county over 3 million people, a funeral director who buries without that certification pays the medical examiner's cost of disinterring and reburying the body.

Illinois burial rules, DHS help and veterans cemeteries

The Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation licenses funeral homes; call 217-524-1981 to check a license or complain about one arranging a burial. Cemeteries in Illinois can have a different regulator.

In Illinois a family cannot do everything itself. Under 410 ILCS 535 a licensed funeral director must file the death certificate, move the body, be present at the burial or cremation, and obtain the permits, according to the Funeral Consumers Alliance summary. Opening and closing the grave is the cemetery's job.

A burial has no waiting period; a cremation would need 24 hours (410 ILCS 18/35).

State help with the cost

For an eligible recipient, Illinois DHS can pay up to $1,370 for funeral services and up to $686 for the cemetery or burial, $2,056 together, provided the claim is filed within 180 days of the death. (Source: Illinois DHS.) Social Security adds a one-time $255 for an eligible spouse or child who claims it within two years.

Veterans cemeteries in Illinois

For a veteran, a national cemetery grave costs the family nothing for the plot, opening and closing or marker. Illinois has six VA national cemeteries: Abraham Lincoln National Cemetery (815-423-9958), Camp Butler National Cemetery (217-492-4070), Danville National Cemetery (217-554-4550), Fort Sheridan National Cemetery (224-610-7296), Mound City National Cemetery (618-694-1391) and Rock Island National Cemetery (309-784-0000). Fort Sheridan, on the North Shore, takes cremated remains only, while the other five accept casket burials. Sunset Veterans Cemetery (217-222-8641) is run by the state (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

What to do next in Illinois

  1. Collect the funeral home's General Price List and the cemetery's separate price sheet before agreeing to anything.
  2. Ask the cemetery about its rules for opening times, vaults and markers.
  3. File the DHS claim, with the cemetery's invoice, inside the 180-day limit.
  4. For a veteran, check eligibility with Abraham Lincoln National Cemetery before buying a private plot.
  5. If a funeral home misleads you about burial costs, complain to the department at 217-524-1981.
