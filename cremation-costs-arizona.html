Cremation Costs in Arizona (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Arizona page rewritten by hand; the urn tip now names ADHS's licensing bureau, which replaced the funeral board in 2023. Cremation Rate: 68%

With 68% of Arizona deaths ending in cremation, providers here compete hard, and direct cremation sits near $1,600 while a cremation with a funeral ceremony first runs about $5,800. Before any Arizona cremation, a medical examiner reviews the death certificate. This page covers the costs, that review and the other rules, and the questions to put to a provider before you pay.

Direct Cremation

  • No viewing or service takes place first

  • The family can hold a memorial whenever it chooses

Before You Sign for Cremation in Arizona

Before you commit to an Arizona provider, check these points:

  • Ask for the General Price List first and read the direct cremation line closely. It has to say what is included, and in Arizona that price should take in transport, the cremation and a basic container for the ashes.
  • You never need a casket for a direct cremation. Under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR 453.3(b)), any Arizona provider selling one has to accept an unfinished wood box or another alternative container.
  • Find out how many days the crematory normally needs, after the medical examiner's review and the permit, to return the ashes, and whether the price covers delivery to the family or only pickup.
An urn from the crematory is optional. The FTC Funeral Rule allows an Arizona family to bring its own container for the ashes, and if a funeral home turns it away, the office to call is the Arizona Department of Health Services' Bureau of Licensing for Professions & Occupations, which took over the former funeral board's duties on April 1, 2023.

How to Save on Cremation in Arizona

Ways to keep an Arizona cremation affordable:

  • Collect three price lists. Ask at least three Arizona providers for written General Price Lists and compare the direct cremation lines side by side.
  • Consider a flat-rate provider. Arizona's direct cremation market is large and competitive, and flat-rate providers around Phoenix post their prices openly. There is little room to bargain, so choose on turnaround time and reputation.
  • Plan an outdoor scattering. Ashes can be scattered on private land with the owner's permission, in national forests with a free permit, and in several Arizona state parks.

Scattering Ashes in Arizona

Because Arizona is landlocked, a water ceremony usually means a lake or a river. Check first with the agency that manages that water, since state and local rules govern inland scattering and the EPA's ocean rule does not apply. Freedom to scatter in the desert, forests and parks is one reason so many Arizona families choose cremation. A scattering service here usually costs $200 to $800, depending on the place and whether family members attend.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a cremation cost in Arizona?

Arizona prices fall into two groups. Direct cremation, which covers transport, the cremation and return of the ashes, starts around $1,600; cremation with a funeral ceremony first comes to about $5,800.

Which types of cremation can an Arizona family choose?

Most Arizona providers sell three options: (1) direct cremation for about $1,600, with no service; (2) cremation first and a memorial later, wherever the family wants it, priced between the two; and (3) a full funeral with viewing followed by cremation, about $5,800.

What rules apply to cremation in Arizona?

Arizona sets no minimum wait before cremation, but the crematory will not start until it holds three things: the death certificate, the cremation permit, and written authorization signed by the next of kin or the person chosen as agent. The rules section further down gives the statute, whether a funeral director is needed and the licensing office's number.

Where can ashes be scattered in Arizona?

That depends on the place: private land needs the owner's permission, national forests issue a free permit, and several state parks allow it. Other Arizona families choose a columbarium niche, bury the urn, or keep a portion of the ashes in jewelry.

Why is cremation so common in Arizona?

Price explains much of it. By our estimate, a direct cremation in Arizona costs about $5,600 less than a traditional funeral.

How long does an Arizona cremation take?

The cremation takes 2 to 3 hours. Direct cremation ($1,600) is fastest because there is no viewing or service to schedule; since Arizona has no minimum wait, the permit is the main step (A.R.S. § 32-1365.02).

Can there be a funeral before an Arizona cremation?

Yes. An Arizona family can hold a viewing or service before cremation, and many providers rent a casket for it. Plan the date around the law: Arizona has no waiting period before cremation. The authorizing agent must give prior written consent and the medical examiner reviews the death certificate (A.R.S. § 32-1365.02). The authorizing agent is chosen by the priority order in A.R.S. § 36-831. When several people share the same rank, any one of them may sign unless another objects, and then a majority of those reasonably available decides; if no one is willing or able to pay, the county public fiduciary may order the cremation (A.R.S. § 32-1365.02).

The medical examiner's review in Arizona

No Arizona cremation goes ahead until the county medical examiner has looked at the death certificate. State law makes the funeral director, embalmer or alkaline hydrolysis operator notify the county medical examiner, who certifies the cremation only after finding no evidence of foul play or violence (A.R.S. § 11-599; checked October 29, 2026).

In practice that review touches almost every death in the Phoenix area. Maricopa County's Office of the Medical Examiner reviewed 34,289 death certificates for cremation in 2025, about 82% of all deaths in the county, and an on-call forensic pathologist signs off on them each day (Maricopa County OME, 2025 annual report). A provider's quote should say whether this county step is included in the direct cremation price.

Who regulates Arizona cremation, and where to find help

Arizona licenses cremation providers through the Arizona Department of Health Services, Bureau of Licensing for Professions & Occupations (BLPO), which can be reached at 602-364-2079 for license checks and complaints.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Arizona among the nine states that restrict family-directed care, so an Arizona family may deal with a crematory directly after the cremation permit is issued. There is no waiting period before cremation in Arizona.

If the family cannot pay

Each Arizona county has an indigent program, usually run by the public fiduciary, that handles the disposition only after family members decline or cannot pay (A.R.S. § 36-831); several counties, including Yavapai, provide it as a cremation. (Source: A.R.S. §§ 36-831, 11-251(27).) The $255 Social Security death payment goes only to a qualifying spouse or child and covers part of the $1,600 direct cremation estimate above.

A cremated Arizona veteran has the same burial benefit as one who is buried: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery are free. Arizona options include the National Memorial Cemetery of Arizona (480-513-3600), Prescott National Cemetery (928-445-4860, cremated remains only) and the Arizona Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Camp Navajo (928-214-3474). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps in Arizona

  1. Get written direct cremation prices from two or more Arizona providers and compare exactly what each includes.
  2. Find out which office signs cremation permits where the death occurred, and how long its review usually takes.
  3. If the public fiduciary may be involved, ask what financial records it wants, such as bank statements, before arranging the cremation.
  4. Write down BLPO's number, 602-364-2079, before paying any cremation provider.
