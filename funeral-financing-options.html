Overview of Funeral Financing

When funeral costs exceed your immediate resources, several financing options can help bridge the gap. Understanding all available options — from insurance assignments to government programs — ensures you find the most appropriate solution for your situation.

Government Assistance Programs

Several government programs can help with funeral costs:

  • Social Security death benefit — $255 one-time payment for eligible survivors. Learn how to apply
  • Veteran burial benefits — Up to $2,000+ in burial allowances, free cemetery plots at national cemeteries, and headstones/markers. See eligibility requirements
  • Medicaid funeral assistance — Most states offer $1,000-$5,000 toward funeral costs for Medicaid recipients. Check your state's program
  • FEMA funeral assistance — Available when death is related to a federally declared disaster
  • County indigent burial programs — Most counties provide basic burial or cremation for those who cannot afford it

Loans and payment plans, kept separate from aid and from having nothing

This financing page is about borrowing and about paying over time: an assignment of life insurance, a funeral-home payment plan, a personal loan, or a credit card. It is not the directory of grants. That directory is funeral payment assistance. It is not the page for a family that cannot borrow at all. That page is how to pay for a funeral with no money. Mixing the three is how people take a loan they did not need to take.

Try price before you try credit

The NFDA 2023 median of $8,300 is a viewing and burial. Direct cremation's median is $2,645 or $2,750. The gap between those two sourced numbers is larger than the interest on a small loan. Before anyone finances a traditional funeral, ask for the direct-cremation price on the same General Price List. The Funeral Rule requires the home to offer the items separately. A payment plan on an $8,300 package is a worse product than paying cash for a $2,700 direct cremation if the family would have accepted cremation. Have that conversation first.

Assignment of an existing life policy

If the person who died already had life insurance, the beneficiary can often assign part of the benefit so the funeral home is paid by the insurer. The home may ask for a small deposit while the claim is pending. You are not buying a new policy. You are directing money that already exists. Ask the insurer, not only the funeral director, whether an assignment form is accepted and how long the claim takes. Get the assignment amount in writing so the rest of the benefit still reaches the family.

Payment plans and loans

Some funeral homes will divide their own bill over months. The interest rate, or the fact that there is no interest, has to be on the paper. A personal loan from a bank or a credit union is a different contract, with your credit, and it survives even if you later dispute the funeral. Credit cards are the expensive version of the same idea unless a promotional rate is real and you will pay it off inside the promotional window. We do not publish a national average APR for funeral loans. Any percentage on a lender's site today is that lender's number. Compare at least the funeral home's plan and one credit-union quote, and choose the smaller total you will pay, not the smaller monthly coupon.

What is not financing

Social Security's $255, a VA burial allowance, a county indigent burial, and Medicaid burial help are aid. They are described on the payment-assistance page and, for the case where nothing else is available, on the zero-budget page. Do not sign a loan "in the meantime" if an aid program will pay the crematory directly and the home is willing to wait. Ask. FEMA funeral help exists only for deaths tied to a declared disaster, under that disaster's rules. It is not a standing funeral grant.

Crowdfunding, covered on its own page, is also not a loan. Do not promise a lender you will repay from donations that have not arrived.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you make payments on a funeral?

Yes. Many funeral homes offer payment plans ranging from 12 to 60 months. Some charge no interest while others charge 10-18% APR. Always ask about payment options before signing any agreement, and get the terms in writing. Not all funeral homes offer financing, so call ahead if this is important to your planning.

What happens if you cannot afford a funeral?

If you cannot afford a funeral, several options exist: county indigent burial programs provide basic disposition at no cost; Medicaid may cover funeral expenses for eligible recipients; veteran benefits cover burial costs for qualifying veterans; crowdfunding platforms like GoFundMe can rally community support; and choosing direct cremation, which averages about $1,400 to $3,000 depending on the state, is the most affordable option. See our complete guide to paying for a funeral with no money.

Are funeral loans a good idea?

Funeral loans can help when immediate funds are unavailable, but they come with interest charges that increase the total cost. Before borrowing, explore alternatives: insurance assignment, government programs, crowdfunding, payment plans from the funeral home, or reducing costs through simpler service options. If you do borrow, compare rates from multiple lenders and choose the shortest repayment term you can afford.

Can life insurance pay for a funeral directly?

Yes, through a process called insurance assignment. You can assign your life insurance policy to a funeral home, which receives payment directly from the insurer. This allows the funeral to proceed before the full claim is processed. Most funeral homes accept insurance assignments, though they may require partial upfront payment. The process typically takes 3-10 business days.

How much does the government pay for funerals?

Government funeral assistance varies: Social Security provides a $255 death benefit; VA burial allowances range from $1,032 (plus a $1,032 plot allowance) for non-service-connected deaths to $2,000 for service-connected deaths; Medicaid burial allowances range from $1,000 to $5,000 by state; and FEMA may help with funeral costs when a death is caused by a federally declared disaster (the now-closed COVID-19 program paid up to $9,000 per deceased person). These programs have specific eligibility requirements — see our payment assistance guide for details.

Where can I complain about a funeral loan or payment plan?

It depends on who extended the credit. A personal installment loan from a bank, credit union or online lender can be reported to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau at consumerfinance.gov/complaint or (855) 411-2372; the CFPB sends the complaint to the lender, which generally responds within 15 days. A payment plan run by the funeral home itself is a matter for your state funeral board and the state attorney general. Either way, if the plan carries a finance charge or is payable in more than four installments, federal Truth in Lending rules (Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026) require the creditor to give you the APR and total finance charge in writing before you sign, so ask for that disclosure and keep a copy.
