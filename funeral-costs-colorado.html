What Drives Funeral Costs in Colorado

What you pay for a funeral in Colorado depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in Colorado, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether Colorado state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. Mountain pricing is generally moderate than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in Colorado routinely differs by 10-30%.

At 72%, Colorado's cremation rate is 8.6 points above the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

Your Consumer Rights in Colorado

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Colorado must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Colorado has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What's Specific to Colorado (Mountain)

Mountain-state funeral pricing in Colorado (avg $8,000) is usually moderate, but the Denver area can run noticeably higher than rural parts of the state. Fuel and transportation appear on GPLs in Colorado more often than in denser regions; if the funeral home is more than 30 miles from the residence or cemetery, mileage charges can add $100-$400 to the final bill.

Payment Assistance in Colorado

  • Colorado's $1,500 Burial Assistance payment is lost entirely if the bill goes over $2,500, and the estate and any voluntary family money reduce what the state pays.
  • The $255 Social Security death payment goes only to an eligible surviving spouse or child, who applies by calling 1-800-772-1213 within two years of the death. The funeral bill itself is governed by C.R.S. 12-135-107: a Colorado establishment must hand over a written, itemized list of every service and item before you choose the funeral, cannot make you buy anything on it that the law does not require, and may label a charge a cash advance only when it is the same amount the family would pay the outside vendor directly.
  • VA reimburses part of a Colorado veteran's funeral through Form 21P-530EZ, filed by whoever paid; deaths not connected to service on or after October 1, 2026 qualify for up to $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for the plot, as our veteran burial benefits guide explains.
  • More options for Colorado families, including Medicaid and charity help, are in our funeral payment assistance guide and how to pay for a funeral with no money.

What to Do When Planning a Funeral in Colorado

If you are currently arranging a funeral in Colorado, here is a step-by-step approach that can save you time, stress, and money:

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in Colorado or burial in Colorado.

Funeral rules and help in Colorado

The Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies registers funeral homes and crematories in Colorado; call 303-894-7800 to check a registration or file a complaint. Individual funeral directors, embalmers and cremationists have not needed a Colorado license so far; Senate Bill 24-173 (C.R.S. 12-135-501) requires one starting January 1, 2027, and practitioners without the new qualifications can apply for a provisional license before then.

Hiring a funeral home is a choice, not a legal requirement, for most steps of a funeral in Colorado; the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count Colorado among the nine states with funeral-director rules, and you can buy only the services you want.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Colorado sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (C.R.S. § 12-135-307).

Colorado's Burial Assistance program pays up to $1,500 toward a funeral, burial or cremation when the person was receiving Old Age Pension, Aid to the Needy Disabled, Aid to the Blind or a similar program, but nothing is paid if the total bill is over $2,500; the estate and voluntary family contributions reduce the payment, and the money goes straight to the provider. (Source: Colorado DHS Adult Financial Reference Guide (Burial Assistance).) Social Security's one-time $255 death payment, paid only to a qualifying spouse or child, is small next to the $8,000 average Colorado funeral in the table.

A Colorado funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Fort Logan National Cemetery (303-761-0117), Fort Lyon National Cemetery (719-391-6920), or Pikes Peak National Cemetery (719-391-6920) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemetery, Veterans Memorial Cemetery of Western Colorado (970-263-8986), takes requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in Colorado

  1. Compare at least two Colorado funeral homes line by line, using their printed price lists.
  2. Consider a Colorado memorial service after cremation or burial, which avoids embalming and viewing fees.
  3. Ask the Colorado county human services office to confirm Burial Assistance eligibility while the total is still under the $2,500 limit; the state pays the provider, not the family.
  4. For a veteran, price Fort Logan National Cemetery against a private Colorado plot before choosing.
  5. Keep the board's number, 303-894-7800, in case a funeral home will not honor its price list.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Colorado?

Traditional funerals in Colorado average around $8,000, which lands near the national figure of $8,300. The only reliable way to pin down your number is to pull General Price Lists from two or three Denver-area providers and compare line by line.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in Colorado?

Direct cremation in Colorado starts around $1,800; a traditional funeral with burial averages $8,000, not counting the cemetery plot or vault. For most Colorado families, that is a difference of at least $6,200.

What rights do Colorado families have when arranging a funeral?

In Colorado, as everywhere in the US, the FTC Funeral Rule requires an itemized General Price List, phone price quotes, and no fee for using a casket bought elsewhere. According to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, Colorado does not require families to hire a funeral director for every step. You can confirm a Colorado funeral home's registration or file a complaint with the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies at 303-894-7800. Individual funeral directors are a newer matter: under Senate Bill 24-173 (C.R.S. 12-135-501), they must hold a state license starting January 1, 2027.

How can I save on funeral costs in Colorado?

Start by lining up General Price Lists from two or three Denver-area providers before you commit to anything. Home and family burial is legal in parts of Colorado with permits and can eliminate cemetery fees entirely. Direct cremation at $1,800 remains the lowest-cost disposition in every Colorado market. Layer in veteran burial benefits (a free grave at Fort Logan National Cemetery), the county department of human services, and the $255 Social Security payment where you qualify.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Colorado?

No. Colorado law gives a funeral home three choices once it takes custody of a body: it must embalm, refrigerate, cremate, bury or entomb the remains within 24 hours (C.R.S. 12-135-106). Refrigeration meets that rule, so embalming is a choice for a viewing, not a legal requirement.

Under the FTC Funeral Rule a Colorado funeral home may not charge for embalming you did not approve, unless state law required it (16 CFR 453.5). Colorado also lets a family with authority over the remains file the death certificate and disposition permit itself, without hiring a funeral director (Colorado death certificate completion guide). Questions about a provider go to the Division of Professions and Occupations (303-894-7800).

Is Colorado cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Colorado's $8,000 traditional-funeral average is 4% below the $8,300 national median (NFDA 2023).

What is the cheapest funeral option in Colorado?

Direct cremation is the lowest-cost option in Colorado, starting at roughly $1,800. Direct burial comes next at around $4,000 and still avoids viewing, ceremony, and embalming, though the $3,800 Colorado plot estimate still applies.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in Colorado?

Ask at least three Colorado providers for their General Price Lists and compare direct cremation and immediate burial lines. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Colorado home cannot charge a fee for it.
