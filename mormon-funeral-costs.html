LDS / Mormon Funeral Requirements and Traditions

Church guidance asks for a simple funeral held as a sacrament-meeting-style service in the ward meetinghouse, with temple clothing for endowed members and a grave dedication. Because the Church does not charge for the meetinghouse and ward members often prepare the luncheon, most of what a family spends goes to the funeral home, the casket and the cemetery. Here is what each of those means for cost:

  • Burial is preferred, though cremation is now accepted by Church leadership
  • Endowed members are dressed in temple clothing by authorized individuals
  • Funeral services are typically held at the local meetinghouse
  • A priesthood holder dedicates the grave at the cemetery
  • The bishop presides over the funeral service
  • Services emphasize the plan of salvation and the hope of resurrection

For a Latter-day Saint funeral we use a planning range of $6,000 to $14,000. It is our own estimate, not an official statistic, and not drawn from the medians in the NFDA 2023 General Price List Study, which remains the latest and was still shown on NFDA's statistics page on September 29, 2025. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints does not publish a national funeral median that this LDS page cites. The LDS bracket sits around the NFDA median of $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, which excludes the plot, vault, and monument, plus the LDS casket, cemetery plot, vault, and marker rows in the table. Meetinghouse use is listed at $0 on this LDS page, and temple clothing is listed at $50 to $200, so those two LDS items are not what sets the width of the range. The religion overview carries the same $6,000 to $14,000 figure. Families seldom need every line below, and some overlap, so the rows are not meant to add up to the range; each is an estimate. Last checked October 24, 2026.

Ways to Save on a LDS / Mormon Funeral

More ways to cut costs are in our guides to affordable funeral options and payment assistance programs. Ask each funeral home for its General Price List and compare the dressing, viewing and transfer lines, since a viewing before the service is common.

Know Your Rights: Whatever the ward provides, the funeral home is bound by the FTC Funeral Rule: it must give you an itemized price list, let you turn down items you do not want, and accept a casket you bought elsewhere at no added fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a LDS / Mormon funeral cost?

Budget roughly $6,000 to $14,000; free use of the meetinghouse keeps the low end down, while the casket and cemetery set the top. LDS funerals benefit from the Church providing meetinghouse facilities at no charge, which eliminates one of the significant costs of traditional funerals.

Does the LDS / Mormon tradition require burial or cremation?

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS/Mormon) has specific funeral traditions including dressing the deceased in temple clothing (for endowed members), holding the funeral service at a meetinghouse, and dedicating the grave. Burial is generally preferred over cremation, though cremation is not prohibited. The requirements section above covers temple clothing, the meetinghouse service and the dedication of the grave.

How can I save on a LDS / Mormon funeral?

Ward members often provide food for the post-funeral luncheon. The Church's welfare program and fast offering fund may assist families in financial need. Bishop's storehouse resources may also be available to support bereaved families.

Are there financial assistance programs for LDS / Mormon funerals?

Start with the ward bishop and the Relief Society. Both of them can help organize the service and say whether the ward can assist with costs. Outside the Church, Social Security's $255 lump-sum death payment, VA burial benefits and state Medicaid-related burial programs are available to Latter-day Saint families like anyone else, as our payment assistance guide describes.

What makes a LDS / Mormon funeral different from a secular funeral?

A Latter-day Saint funeral is usually a church meeting held in the ward meetinghouse and conducted under the direction of the bishop, and the Church does not charge for using the building. The General Handbook (section 38.5.8) asks that the service be simple and centered on the gospel, with talks and music rather than a long program of eulogies.

Endowed members are typically dressed in temple clothing, which the family or the ward's Relief Society helps arrange, and the grave is often dedicated by a Melchizedek Priesthood holder at the cemetery. Funeral home fees, the casket and the cemetery are still paid separately, so the savings come mainly from the venue and volunteer help.
