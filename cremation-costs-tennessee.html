Cremation Costs in Tennessee (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 42%

Tennessee runs a 42% cremation rate — the Deep South's slowest shift, with traditional burial still prevailing in Middle and West Tennessee rural counties. Marshall Donnelly Combs in Nashville and R.S. Lewis & Sons in Memphis handle the largest metro cremation volumes; direct cremation runs about $1,600 and full-service cremation $5,400. The Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers licenses every establishment, and Title 46 of the Tennessee Code — the Cemetery and Burial Services Act — governs cemetery operations and pre-need trusts.

Cost ranges on this page draw on the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report, which documents a national cremation rate of roughly 60.5%. Consumer price disclosures follow the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and Tennessee-specific licensing is administered by the Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers.

Tennessee Cemetery Act and Pre-Need Trusts

Tennessee Code Title 46 — the Cemetery and Burial Services Act — requires cemetery companies to maintain a perpetual care trust and regulates pre-need cemetery and funeral contracts. Pre-need cremation funds must be trusted under state rules, which consumer-law advocates consider middle-of-the-road compared to Florida's stricter 50% requirement. The Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers, housed under the Department of Commerce and Insurance, licenses every funeral establishment and crematory in the state.

Nashville and Memphis: Two Markets, Two Cultures

Marshall Donnelly Combs has operated in Nashville since 1905 and serves as the city's primary mainstream provider. Cremations route through its Hillsboro Road location. In Memphis, R.S. Lewis & Sons — founded in 1904 — is the oldest Black-owned funeral home in the United States still operating under its original family and serves as the largest African-American cremation provider in West Tennessee. Pricing in both markets hovers near the state $1,600 direct cremation average; full-service cremation at Marshall Donnelly Combs occasionally runs above $6,000 for elaborate chapel services.

Waiting Period and Cremation Authorization

Tennessee doesn't enforce a rigid statutory hold like South Carolina's 24 hours or Florida's 48, but the state Board requires medical examiner or attending physician sign-off on the death certificate before cremation, and the legal next of kin must sign the cremation authorization. In practice the process from death to return of remains typically runs 5 to 10 days. The medical examiner pauses cases for unusual deaths, which can extend timelines in Shelby (Memphis) and Davidson (Nashville) counties.

Middle Tennessee note: Cremation rates in Davidson (Nashville) and Knox (Knoxville) counties run closer to 50%, while rural West and Middle Tennessee counties stay near 30%. The urban-rural split is the single biggest predictor of a family's likely choice here.

Where to Scatter Ashes in Tennessee

  • Great Smoky Mountains National Park — Free dispersal permit issued by the park superintendent. Scattering off-trail is permitted in many areas.
  • Cherokee and Pisgah National Forests — Free USFS permit.
  • Tennessee State Parks — Ranger permission required; generally accommodating.
  • Private land — Owner's written permission suffices; no state permit.
  • Cumberland and Tennessee Rivers — Scattering permitted outside marked recreation zones.

Financial Assistance

Tennessee Department of Human Services administers a small burial/cremation benefit for indigent decedents — typically $500 to $900 depending on county. The funeral home applies on the family's behalf after showing estate insufficiency. Veterans' families may inquire about no-cost inurnment at Mountain Home National Cemetery in Johnson City or Nashville National Cemetery in Madison. Social Security's $255 one-time death payment applies to all families regardless of disposition.

Answers to the Questions We Hear Most

How much does cremation cost in Tennessee?

Direct cremation in Tennessee averages $1,600 — close to the national median. Marshall Donnelly Combs in Nashville, R.S. Lewis in Memphis, and most independent operators across the state price within $200 of that figure. Full-service cremation runs $5,400. Chattanooga and Knoxville track slightly below Nashville; Memphis pricing matches Nashville.

What kinds of cremation services can I choose in Tennessee?

Three paths: direct cremation at $1,600 with no ceremony, cremation with a memorial service held afterward at a church or private venue, and full-service cremation at $5,400 with open-casket viewing and ceremony beforehand. Marshall Donnelly Combs and R.S. Lewis both offer witness cremation on request; alkaline hydrolysis is licensed under Tennessee Board rules but not widely offered yet.

What legal requirements apply to cremation in Tennessee?

Tennessee requires the legal next of kin's signed cremation authorization and physician or medical examiner sign-off on the death certificate before cremation. Implanted devices like pacemakers are removed beforehand. The Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers enforces these rules. Title 46 of the Tennessee Code — the Cemetery and Burial Services Act — governs cemetery and pre-need trust matters.

Can I scatter ashes in Tennessee?

Great Smoky Mountains National Park issues free dispersal permits for ash scattering in many off-trail areas. Cherokee and Pisgah National Forests require a free USFS permit. Tennessee State Parks generally allow scattering with ranger permission. Private land needs the owner's written permission. Cumberland and Tennessee River scattering outside recreation zones is permitted. Charter scattering on lakes runs $150 to $500.

What is driving Tennessee's cremation rate?

Tennessee's 42% rate is held down by strong church traditions favoring burial in the rural counties of Middle and West Tennessee. In Nashville and Knoxville the rate is closer to 50%. The roughly $3,800 savings over traditional burial is the primary driver of growth; younger and urban families choose cremation at rates well above the state figure.

What is the timeline for cremation in Tennessee?

End-to-end, most Tennessee cremations complete in 5 to 10 days from death. The medical examiner's sign-off and the funeral home's paperwork take the bulk of that time. Direct cremation at $1,600 moves fastest because no ceremony interrupts the timeline. Shelby County and Davidson County occasionally pause for unusual-death investigations, which can extend timing a few days.

Can I hold a funeral service before cremation in Tennessee?

Yes — Marshall Donnelly Combs, R.S. Lewis, and most full-service Tennessee funeral homes offer viewing and ceremony in the chapel followed by cremation, at around $5,400. Families who prefer a memorial after cremation usually choose direct cremation and hold the ceremony at their church or home, which shortens the calendar and reduces overall cost.
