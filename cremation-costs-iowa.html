Cremation Costs in Iowa (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 What changed: What Iowa's medical examiner cremation permit costs (new section) Cremation Rate: 50%

About half of Iowa deaths, 50%, now end in cremation, and direct cremation near $1,500 sits far below the $6,700 typical of a full traditional funeral. Iowa defines cremation in Iowa Code §156.1 as reducing remains to bone fragments by heat and flame. For unclaimed ashes, §144.27 lets a funeral director, after 180 days, ask the Department of Veterans Affairs whether the person was a veteran eligible for a veterans cemetery. Cremation costs in Iowa can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Iowa: Request written General Price Lists.
  • Price direct cremation as standalone: Iowa funeral homes often bundle direct cremation into broader packages.

Checks to Make Before a Cremation in Iowa

When considering cremation in Iowa, keep these important points in mind:

  • Get the Iowa provider's General Price List before you agree to anything; the direct cremation line must state what it includes, and the price should cover transport, the cremation itself and a basic container for the ashes.
  • An Iowa provider offering direct cremation must let you use an unfinished wood box or other alternative container instead of a casket (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(b)).
  • Ask how long the Iowa crematory usually takes to return the ashes once the permit is issued, and whether the quoted price includes delivery or only pickup.
In Iowa you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Iowa bring a container, and the IA Board of Mortuary Science is who you call if a home in Iowa refuses it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Iowa?

Cremation costs in Iowa range from $1,500 for direct cremation to $5,400 for a full funeral service followed by cremation.

Which cremation options exist in Iowa?

Iowa providers typically offer three tiers. Direct cremation at $1,500 is the simplest Iowa tier: no service or viewing, the body cremated and the ashes returned. Traditional cremation at $5,400 adds a full funeral home service before the cremation, $3,900 more than the direct tier in Iowa.

Does Iowa have specific rules for cremation?

In short, Iowa has no fixed minimum waiting period, but the crematory still needs the death certificate, a cremation permit and a signed authorization from the next of kin or designated agent before it can proceed. The statute behind the Iowa timing rule, whether a funeral director must be involved, and the licensing board's phone number are listed in the cremation rules section further down this page.

Can I scatter ashes in Iowa?

Yes, but Iowa is stricter than many states. The public health rule on disposition, 641 IAC 97.13(3), makes scattering subject to local ordinances and cemetery rules, does not allow it on public property, and requires the owner's consent on private property. Cremation is itself treated as final disposition, so no further burial-transit permit is needed to take the remains home or to a scattering site.

For a scattering garden, check with the cemetery about fees and whether a family member may attend. Complaints about a crematory go to the Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (515-229-3939).

How many Iowa families choose cremation?

About half, by this guide’s estimate of roughly 50%, which trails the 63.4% national rate NFDA projected for 2025. For the families who choose it the savings are large: about $1,500 for direct cremation in Iowa versus around $6,700 for the funeral service in a traditional burial. Iowa has no waiting period, but a county or state medical examiner has to issue a cremation permit first (Iowa Code § 331.805).

How quickly can cremation be completed in Iowa?

From start to ashes, most cremations in Iowa take 4 to 10 days. Direct cremation at $1,500 moves fastest: with no viewing or service to schedule, the only wait is the Iowa cremation permit (Iowa sets no fixed waiting period; Iowa Code § 331.805(3)(b)).

Can I hold a funeral service before cremation in Iowa?

Yes. An Iowa funeral followed by cremation costs about $5,400 on this site's estimates, usually $4,320 to $7,020; direct cremation is about $1,500. The difference pays for the visitation, the service and a casket, which can be rented.

Iowa's funeral rules give a family time for a service: an unembalmed body may be held 72 hours after death, or 24 hours after the funeral home takes custody, and refrigeration at 38 to 42°F extends that by another 72 hours (481 IAC 900.6(3)). A county or state medical examiner must issue the cremation permit before cremation.

What Iowa's medical examiner cremation permit costs

Iowa caps what the medical examiner can charge for its cremation permit. The fee can be no more than $75, and the permit and other reasonable cremation expenses may be paid from the person's estate (Iowa Code § 331.805(3)(b); checked October 29, 2026). Under rules effective April 1, 2026, the family, next of kin or another person acting for the deceased pays the fee, and the permit is completed in the state's electronic death registry (Iowa Admin. Code 641—127.6).

  • What the examiner checks: the county medical examiner looks into the cause and manner of death before issuing the permit. The examiner has to view the body only if the death may be one of the kinds the law treats as a public-interest case, such as a violent or unexplained death.
  • In practice: in Polk County, an investigator goes to the crematory and examines the body if the death is not already a medical examiner case, then calls the physician. If the physician cannot be reached right away, the permit can be delayed.

Cremation rules and help in Iowa

The licensing office for cremation providers in Iowa is the Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing; its number is 515-229-3939.

For a cremation in Iowa, state law (Iowa Code § 331.805; Iowa Admin. Code 481-900.10(2)) reserves arranging a cremation for a licensed funeral director, according to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, so even the $1,500 direct cremation in the table runs through a licensee.

What the law says about timing: Iowa sets no waiting period before cremation, but a county or state medical examiner must first issue a cremation permit (Iowa Code § 331.805(3)(b)).

An Iowa county's general assistance office decides whether to pay for a cremation and how much; Linn County, for example, pays up to $1,000 for a resident's burial or cremation (Iowa Code § 252.27). (Source: Iowa Code §§ 252.25, 252.27.) The $255 Social Security death payment to a qualifying Iowa spouse or child would cover only part of the $1,500 direct cremation estimate above.

Cremated remains of an eligible Iowa veteran can be placed in a VA national cemetery columbarium or in-ground site at no charge for the space. Options in Iowa include Keokuk National Cemetery (319-524-5193) and Iowa Veterans Cemetery (515-727-3444). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Iowa

  1. Get written cremation prices from at least two Iowa providers and compare what each direct cremation price includes.
  2. Ask which office signs Iowa cremation permits where the death occurred, and how long it takes.
  3. Ask the Iowa county general assistance office whether it pays a cremation rate before choosing a provider.
  4. Note the board's number, 515-229-3939, before you pay a cremation provider.
