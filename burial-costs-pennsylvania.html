Burial Costs in Pennsylvania (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: Northeast

Pennsylvania's burial traditions span centuries, from the hallowed grounds of Gettysburg National Cemetery to the plain Amish graveyards of Lancaster County, from Philadelphia's elaborate Catholic cemetery network to the coal-country cemeteries of the anthracite region. The Keystone State's diverse cultural heritage — Irish and Italian Catholic, Amish and Mennonite, African-American, Jewish, Eastern European — has produced one of the richest cemetery landscapes in the nation. Burial costs are moderate to high, with Philadelphia and its suburbs commanding the highest prices and rural central Pennsylvania offering more affordable options.

What Drives Burial Costs in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania's burial costs are driven by two distinct metro markets — Philadelphia and Pittsburgh — along with a vast middle that is generally more affordable. The Philadelphia region, with its dense population and limited cemetery land, sees costs approaching Northeast corridor levels. The Archdiocese of Philadelphia operates one of the largest Catholic cemetery systems in the country, with dozens of cemeteries serving the area's large Catholic population.

Gettysburg National Cemetery is perhaps the most famous cemetery in America, established in 1863 following the Civil War's bloodiest battle. Pennsylvania also has numerous other national cemeteries, including Indiantown Gap National Cemetery (the busiest in the Northeast), Philadelphia National Cemetery, and the National Cemetery of the Alleghenies near Pittsburgh.

The Amish and Mennonite communities of Lancaster, Berks, and surrounding counties maintain a tradition of simple burial that stands in stark contrast to mainstream practices — handmade wooden caskets, no embalming, no vaults, and plain uniform headstones in community cemeteries, with total costs often under $2,000. The coal mining regions of northeastern Pennsylvania have their own distinctive cemetery culture, with many fraternal and ethnic organization cemeteries established by immigrant miners.

Cemetery Types Available in Pennsylvania

  • Catholic cemeteries — The Archdiocese of Philadelphia and dioceses across the state operate extensive cemetery systems. Holy Cross Cemetery, Calvary Cemetery, and dozens of others serve Pennsylvania's large Catholic population.
  • Historic cemeteries — Laurel Hill Cemetery in Philadelphia (National Historic Landmark), Allegheny Cemetery in Pittsburgh, and numerous Civil War-era cemeteries are among the most historically significant burial grounds in America.
  • National cemeteries — Gettysburg, Indiantown Gap, Philadelphia National Cemetery, and the National Cemetery of the Alleghenies serve veterans.
  • Amish and Mennonite cemeteries — Simple, unadorned community cemeteries in Lancaster County and surrounding areas. Available to community members.
  • Municipal cemeteries — Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, and many Pennsylvania boroughs and townships maintain public cemeteries.
  • Coal region cemeteries — Northeastern Pennsylvania's anthracite region has numerous ethnic and fraternal cemeteries established by Polish, Ukrainian, Lithuanian, and other immigrant mining communities.
  • Memorial parks — Modern memorial parks in suburban Philadelphia and Pittsburgh offer full-service options.

Casket and Vault Options

Pennsylvania funeral homes average $2,550 for caskets. Online retailers can save families $1,000 or more, with comparable caskets for $500-$2,000. The FTC Funeral Rule guarantees that Pennsylvania funeral homes must accept third-party caskets without handling fees. Amish communities build their own simple wooden caskets at minimal cost.

Most Pennsylvania cemeteries require burial vaults, particularly in the wetter eastern part of the state where high water tables are common. Concrete liners start around $800. Amish cemeteries notably do not require vaults, and some green burial grounds also waive this requirement.

Consumer Rights for Burial in Pennsylvania

  • FTC Funeral Rule — All Pennsylvania funeral homes must provide itemized General Price Lists.
  • PA State Board of Funeral Directors — Regulates funeral directors and handles consumer complaints.
  • Embalming not required — Pennsylvania does not mandate embalming. Refrigeration is an acceptable alternative.
  • Casket purchase freedom — Third-party caskets must be accepted without handling fees.
  • Home burial permitted — Pennsylvania allows burial on private property with proper local approvals.
  • Preneed protections — Pennsylvania's Funeral Director Law governs prepaid funeral contracts with consumer trust fund requirements.

Green and Natural Burial in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania has a growing green burial movement. Penn Forest Natural Burial Park near Pittsburgh is one of the state's most established conservation burial grounds. Several other Pennsylvania cemeteries have developed natural burial sections. The state's strong Amish and Mennonite burial traditions provide a longstanding cultural model for simple, natural burial. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Pennsylvania?

A traditional burial costs approximately $8,500 for the funeral service plus $4,200 for a cemetery plot. Total costs range from $16,480 to $23,230. Philadelphia is the most expensive market, with rural central Pennsylvania significantly more affordable.

Is a burial vault required in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania has no state law requiring vaults. Most cemeteries require them as policy, particularly in eastern Pennsylvania where high water tables are common. Amish cemeteries and green burial grounds typically do not require vaults.

What are the cheapest burial options in Pennsylvania?

Direct burial in a rural community cemetery with an online casket can cost $6,000-$9,000. Amish burial traditions offer the most affordable model at under $2,000. Veterans receive free burial at Indiantown Gap or other national cemeteries in the state.

What are Amish burial traditions in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania Amish practice simple burial — handmade wooden caskets, no embalming, no vaults, and plain uniform headstones. The community prepares the body, builds the casket, and digs the grave collectively. Total costs are typically under $2,000.

Is Gettysburg National Cemetery still accepting burials?

Gettysburg National Cemetery has extremely limited availability for new interments. Most Pennsylvania veterans are served by Indiantown Gap National Cemetery, which has ample capacity. The National Cemetery of the Alleghenies near Pittsburgh also serves western Pennsylvania veterans.

Is a burial vault required in Pennsylvania?

Many cemeteries in Pennsylvania require a burial vault or grave liner, though this is typically a cemetery policy rather than a state law. Vaults prevent the ground from settling. Ask the cemetery about their specific requirements and alternative options.

What are the cheapest burial options in Pennsylvania?

The most affordable burial options in Pennsylvania include direct burial (no viewing or service), which can save thousands. Green burial, which skips embalming and uses a biodegradable casket or shroud, is another cost-effective option available at some Pennsylvania cemeteries.
