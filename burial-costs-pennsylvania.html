Burial Costs in Pennsylvania (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Northeast

Pennsylvania has a denser network of cemeteries than almost any other state — a legacy of its Quaker, Moravian, Catholic, Orthodox, Jewish, and mining-town communities, each of which left behind active burial grounds that still operate today. For the Pennsylvania family planning a traditional burial in 2026, the state average runs $8,500 for the funeral service and $4,200 for a cemetery plot, though the actual number swings sharply depending on whether the family uses a nonprofit or denominational cemetery versus a commercial memorial park. Full-line-item totals fall between $16,480 and $23,230. Eligibility and benefits fall under the VA National Cemetery Administration (38 U.S.C. § 2402) and the VA burial allowance rules at 38 CFR § 3.1600.

Put the pieces together and a Pennsylvania burial in 2026 typically costs between $16,480 and $23,230 — with Philadelphia-metro Catholic and Jewish cemeteries often sitting at the low end of that band and commercial memorial parks in the Philadelphia and Pittsburgh suburbs sitting at the high end.

The Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors

Pennsylvania is one of only a handful of states where a funeral director must be licensed and co-own the funeral home — the rule is administered by the Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors, under the Department of State. This has two practical effects for families. First, it keeps the number of Pennsylvania funeral homes relatively stable, so the same established family-owned names (Kirk & Nice in the Philadelphia area, D'Alessandro in South Philadelphia, John A. Freyvogel in Pittsburgh, Bacha in the Mon Valley) show up decade after decade. Second, it means consumers can look up a director's license and disciplinary history on the Board's website before choosing a provider — a step worth taking for any pre-need contract. Pennsylvania also requires pre-need contracts to be backed either by 100 percent trust or by a qualifying insurance policy with specific disclosure language.

Why Pennsylvania's Cemetery Landscape Shifts the Total

Pennsylvania's founding communities each built their own cemeteries, and most of them are still open for interment. Laurel Hill in Philadelphia (1836, a National Historic Landmark) and Allegheny Cemetery in Pittsburgh (1844) are the best-known nonprofits; Catholic cemeteries operated by the Archdiocese of Philadelphia, Diocese of Pittsburgh, Diocese of Harrisburg, and Diocese of Scranton cover most of the state; Jewish cemeteries operated by Congregations affiliated with the Jewish Federations of Greater Philadelphia and Greater Pittsburgh handle Jewish burials; and Pennsylvania's coal-region boroughs still run municipal cemeteries at public-agency rates. The spread between the cheapest of these and the most expensive private memorial park in the same county can reach $3,000–$5,000 on the plot line alone. Get written quotes from at least one nonprofit, one denominational, and one commercial cemetery before deciding.

Winter Interments and the Frozen-Ground Surcharge

Most Pennsylvania cemeteries add a frozen-ground charge from roughly December through March, or require a winter vault storage arrangement until spring interment. This is not a universal policy — some cemeteries absorb the winter cost, others pass through $300–$800. Ask for the written winter interment fee before signing, and if storage is required, ask for the per-month rate and the expected spring interment window. Western Pennsylvania grounds in the higher elevations of Cambria, Somerset, and Elk counties typically carry the steepest winter surcharges in the state.

Pennsylvania tip: The FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) allows you to buy a casket from any retailer without a handling fee. Pennsylvania does not add any state requirement on top of that — so a casket ordered from an independent retailer or online vendor is delivered to the funeral home at no extra charge.

Cutting the Pennsylvania Bill

  • Get written pricing from one nonprofit cemetery: Laurel Hill in Philadelphia and Allegheny Cemetery in Pittsburgh publish plot and interment pricing on request, and those numbers become a useful anchor against commercial memorial parks in the same county.
  • Ask denominational cemeteries about parishioner or member rates: Catholic and Jewish cemeteries in Pennsylvania routinely offer reduced pricing for active congregation members.
  • Check VA and state veterans cemeteries: National Cemetery of the Alleghenies (Bridgeville), Indiantown Gap National Cemetery (Annville), and Washington Crossing National Cemetery (Newtown) serve most of the state — eligible veterans and spouses get plot, opening-and-closing, vault, and marker at no cost.
  • Confirm the winter line item in writing: Ask explicitly whether frozen-ground or storage charges apply in the month of interment, and get the amount in writing before signing.
  • Explore natural burial grounds: Green Meadow at Fountain Hill (Bethlehem), Penn Forest Natural Burial Park (Pittsburgh), and West Laurel Hill's Nature's Sanctuary (Bala Cynwyd) all run certified green or hybrid sections that price well below the traditional-cemetery average.
  • Buy the casket independently: Under the FTC Funeral Rule the funeral home cannot charge a handling fee or refuse delivery. Casket buying guide.
  • For low-income families, the state benefit is modest: Pennsylvania's Department of Human Services offers a burial benefit of up to $750 through County Assistance Offices (supplemented by a county funeral benefit of up to $750) — together a maximum of $1,500. See our payment assistance guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Pennsylvania?

A traditional Pennsylvania burial in 2026 averages $8,500 for the funeral service and $4,200 for a cemetery plot, with full-line-item totals between $16,480 and $23,230. Philadelphia-metro Catholic and Jewish cemeteries typically sit toward the low end of the range; commercial memorial parks in the Philadelphia and Pittsburgh suburbs sit toward the high end. Western Pennsylvania grounds sometimes add a $300–$800 frozen-ground surcharge in December–March.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania does not require vaults by statute. Most private memorial parks and many denominational cemeteries in the state require either a vault or the cheaper grave liner as written policy; some nonprofit and natural burial grounds prohibit both. Penn Forest in Pittsburgh, West Laurel Hill's Nature's Sanctuary, and Green Meadow at Fountain Hill all allow burial without a vault or liner. Always ask for the written interment specification before paying a deposit.

How can I reduce burial costs in Pennsylvania?

The biggest lever in Pennsylvania is the cemetery choice. The state's density of nonprofit, Catholic, Jewish, Orthodox, and municipal cemeteries means a comparable plot can cost $3,000–$5,000 less than the nearest commercial memorial park. Beyond that, confirm the winter interment charge in writing, buy the casket from an independent retailer under the FTC Funeral Rule, and check eligibility at National Cemetery of the Alleghenies, Indiantown Gap, or Washington Crossing for qualifying veterans.

How do I compare cemetery costs in Pennsylvania?

Request an itemized written price sheet from every cemetery — plot, opening-and-closing, vault or liner requirement, endowed care, and any residency, parishioner, or member discount. Pennsylvania is unusual in how often nonprofit and denominational cemeteries undercut commercial memorial parks, so always include at least one of each type in the comparison. Also ask explicitly about the winter surcharge and any pre-need trust arrangement — Pennsylvania law requires 100 percent trust backing or a qualifying insurance policy on pre-need contracts.

What is an all-in burial estimate for Pennsylvania?

A full Pennsylvania burial, line by line, is approximately $8,500 funeral service, $4,200 plot, $2,550 casket, $1,680 vault, $2,100 opening-and-closing, and $1,500–$3,000 marker — total $16,480 to $23,230. Veterans eligible for interment at a Pennsylvania national cemetery deduct roughly $8,000 from that figure; parishioners of a denominational cemetery typically save $1,000–$3,000 versus the private market.

Can I choose a natural or green burial in Pennsylvania?

Yes. Pennsylvania has a growing group of Green Burial Council–aligned grounds — Penn Forest Natural Burial Park near Pittsburgh, West Laurel Hill's Nature's Sanctuary (Bala Cynwyd), and Green Meadow at Fountain Hill (Bethlehem) all offer full natural burial (no embalming, biodegradable container, no vault) — and an expanding list of traditional cemeteries now keep a hybrid section. Our green burial options guide covers the distinctions.
