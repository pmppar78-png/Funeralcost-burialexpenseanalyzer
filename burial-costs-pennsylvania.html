The total cost of burial in Pennsylvania typically ranges from $16,480 to $23,230 depending on choices made, though cemetery fees are often billed separately from funeral home services. Within that total, the basic services fee commonly runs about $2,500 to $3,500, and the cost of a funeral can rise quickly when caskets vary by material and design.

  • Traditional burial: This is the most expensive option and usually includes viewing, embalming, a hearse, graveside service, and burial. For comparison, the average cost of a traditional funeral is about $8,300 nationally, though Pennsylvania totals can run higher once cemetery charges are included.
  • Direct burial: No embalming, no funeral ceremony, no viewing. This is one of the lowest-cost burial options, though cremation costs for direct cremation in Pennsylvania are often lower still at about $695 to $1,695.
  • Green burial: Minimal preparation, biodegradable casket or shroud, and no vault. Greener burials can lower the overall cost because they avoid embalming and expensive vaults.

These are the three most common burial options in Pennsylvania, but not every funeral home offers all of them. Many providers also offer cremation services as an alternative to burial, and families can use a funeral cost calculator to compare cremation, burial, and service prices, while traditional funerals usually include more facility use, staffing, and transportation than simpler arrangements.

How to Reduce Direct Burial Costs in Pennsylvania

Burial costs in Pennsylvania can be managed with smart planning:

  • Confirm winter pricing up front: Pennsylvania cemeteries frequently add frozen-ground charges from December through March, or require winter vault storage until spring interment.
  • Denominational and municipal cemeteries: Pennsylvania has one of the highest densities of nonprofit cemeteries in the country.
  • Shop outside the immediate metro: A 20-30 minute drive from Philadelphia into a lower-cost county can reduce plot prices substantially without changing the family's ability to visit.

Frequently Asked Questions about Financial Assistance

How much does burial cost in Pennsylvania?

The overall cost of a burial funeral in Pennsylvania is approximately $8,500 for the funeral service plus $4,200 for a cemetery plot. When you add a burial vault ($1,680), graveside opening and closing labor often ranging from $800 to $1,500, casket ($2,550), and headstone ($1,000-$3,000), the total typically ranges from $16,480 to $23,230, similar to what families see in city-specific guides like Indianapolis funeral cost breakdowns.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Pennsylvania?

A liner is usually an acceptable substitute — always ask the specific Pennsylvania cemetery for their written policy.

How can I reduce burial costs in Pennsylvania?

Planning ahead through pre planning can also reduce the financial burden on the family and save money.

How do I compare cemetery costs in Pennsylvania?

What is an all-in burial estimate for Pennsylvania?

Adding every line item, burial in Pennsylvania usually totals $16,480 to $23,230: funeral service about $8,500, plot about $4,200, casket about $2,550, vault about $1,680, opening-and-closing about $2,100, and headstone $1,000-$3,000. Final expense insurance is a type of funeral insurance and a life insurance policy that can help pay funeral expenses. In Pennsylvania, preplanning contracts must be funded through a trust or escrow account, and an irrevocable funeral trust can shield up to $12,000 for Medicaid calculations, similar to protections discussed in our overview of funeral costs and assistance programs in Vermont.

Can I choose a natural or green burial in Pennsylvania?

A growing number of Pennsylvania cemeteries now offer green burial sections (biodegradable container, no embalming, no vault), and the total cost is usually below a traditional burial. If a dedicated green burial ground is not nearby, some families in Pennsylvania use a hybrid approach (traditional plot without embalming). By contrast, cremation has a mandatory 24-hour waiting period in Pennsylvania, and the authorization form must be signed by next of kin for the deceased. Pennsylvania law also allows home funerals without a funeral director, which gives families more flexibility in making arrangements at the end of life.

Burial rules and help in Pennsylvania

Funeral homes handling burials in Pennsylvania are licensed by the Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors (1-833-367-2762). Cemetery sales in Pennsylvania may fall under a different office, so ask the board who oversees the cemetery you are considering.

Pennsylvania is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict families from caring for their own dead. You can buy only the services you need, but someone must still file the Pennsylvania death certificate and get the permit.

A burial in Pennsylvania goes ahead once the permit is issued; for cremation, a 24-hour wait applies (49 Pa. Code § 13.212).

Pennsylvania has a public program that can help pay for a burial when the family cannot (Pennsylvania DHS); the amounts and deadlines are on our Pennsylvania funeral cost page. Ask about it before you buy a Pennsylvania plot, since help may be limited or capped.

A Pennsylvania veteran's grave in a national cemetery costs the family nothing for the plot, opening and closing, or marker. In Pennsylvania, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Indiantown Gap National Cemetery (717-865-5254); National Cemetery of the Alleghenies (724-746-4363); Washington Crossing National Cemetery (215-222-7510); state veterans cemeteries: Pennsylvania Soldiers' and Sailors' Home Cemetery (814-871-4531). Source: VA National Cemetery Administration.

Next steps

  1. Request itemized prices from Pennsylvania providers by phone or in person; under the FTC Funeral Rule you only pay for what you choose.
  2. Ask whether the Pennsylvania cemetery lets you buy the headstone elsewhere.
  3. Check eligibility with your county assistance office (Pennsylvania DHS) first; some programs refuse claims after a contract is signed.
  4. For a veteran, ask Indiantown Gap National Cemetery about eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. Keep the board's number, 1-833-367-2762, in case a provider will not honor its price list.
