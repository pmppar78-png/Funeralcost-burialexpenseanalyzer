Help Paying for a Funeral in Wisconsin

  • Wisconsin's WFCAP is run locally by county or tribal agencies, and its payments stop if the total funeral or cemetery bill passes the program's limits.
  • To collect the one-time $255 from Social Security, a Wisconsin widow, widower or eligible child must call 1-800-772-1213, and the claim has to be made within two years.
  • VA reimburses part of a Wisconsin veteran's funeral through Form 21P-530EZ, filed by whoever paid; deaths not connected to service on or after October 1, 2026 qualify for up to $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for the plot, as our veteran burial benefits guide explains.
  • More options for Wisconsin families, including Medicaid and charity help, are in our funeral payment assistance guide and how to pay for a funeral with no money.

What's Specific to Wisconsin (Midwest)

Independent, family-owned funeral homes still anchor Wisconsin's market more than in the coasts, and they generally price closer to the NFDA national averages than corporate-chain locations. Asking a Wisconsin provider whether they are independently owned or part of a national group is a quick way to predict where their GPL will sit. Wisconsin's Funeral Directors Examining Board, part of the Department of Safety and Professional Services, regulates every home the same way and does not survey prices, so the General Price List each must give you under the FTC Funeral Rule is where any gap shows up.

What to Do When Planning a Funeral in Wisconsin

If you are currently arranging a funeral in Wisconsin, here is a step-by-step approach that can save you time, stress, and money:

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in Wisconsin or burial in Wisconsin.

For the steps after a death in Wisconsin, see our what to do when someone dies guide or printable funeral planning checklist.

Wisconsin death certificates: $20, then $3

The Wisconsin Vital Records Office charges $20 for the first copy of a death record and $3 for each additional copy of the same record, so five copies cost $32; the $20 is a non-refundable search fee if nothing is found (Wisconsin DHS, requesting a vital record; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Which version: a Fact of Death certificate leaves out cause and manner of death and is enough for most banks; insurers usually want the Extended Fact of Death certificate, which needs a direct and tangible interest for deaths in the last 50 years.
  • Where: any county Register of Deeds, the Milwaukee or West Allis city health offices, or by mail to P.O. Box 309, Madison, WI 53701 (about 10 business days).

Wisconsin funeral licensing, WFCAP aid and veterans burial options

In Wisconsin, funeral directors and funeral establishments hold licenses from the Department of Safety and Professional Services. The department's number, 608-266-2112, is where to check a credential or complain about a home that will not give you its price list or adds charges you did not choose. State law also limits sales pressure: Wis. Stat. §445.12 bars a funeral director from soliciting a funeral, or paying someone else to steer one, whether before or after a death.

A Wisconsin family does not have to hand every task to a funeral home. The Funeral Consumers Alliance leaves Wisconsin off its list of nine states with funeral-director requirements, which means you can buy only the services you actually want and handle the rest yourselves.

Cremation has to wait at least 48 hours after death under Wis. Stat. 979.10, so a cremation booked for the next morning is not possible.

Wisconsin is one of the few states with a dedicated funeral aid program. The Wisconsin Funeral and Cemetery Aids Program, run with county and tribal agencies, pays up to $1,500 toward funeral costs and up to $1,000 toward cemetery or crematory costs, and it caps the size of the total bill it will help with (Wisconsin DHS). Because of that cap, raise WFCAP with the county or tribal agency before the arrangements grow past its limits.

Veterans have four cemeteries to consider. Northwoods National Cemetery (906-396-9276) is booked by the funeral home through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office at 800-535-1117. The three state cemeteries take requests directly: Central Wisconsin Veterans Memorial Cemetery (715-256-5000), Northern Wisconsin Veterans Memorial Cemetery (715-635-5360) and Southern Wisconsin Veterans Memorial Cemetery (262-878-5660). Wood National Cemetery in Milwaukee no longer accepts new burials, so Milwaukee-area families usually turn to the southern cemetery at Union Grove.

What to do next in Wisconsin

  1. Collect General Price Lists from two or more Wisconsin homes; on our figures direct cremation starts near $1,700 and a traditional service averages $7,200.
  2. If money is short, ask the county or tribal agency about WFCAP before signing a contract.

  4. For a veteran, compare Northwoods and the state cemeteries with the cost of a private plot, about $3,400 here.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a typical Wisconsin funeral cost in 2026?

Most Wisconsin families paying for a full traditional service see the bill settle around $7,200, 13% below the $8,300 national benchmark. Cremation with ceremony runs closer to $5,800, while a stripped-down direct cremation in Wisconsin can land near $1,700. Cemetery plot fees in Wisconsin add about $3,400. Actual prices shift a lot depending on which Wisconsin provider you call first, which is why comparing is non-negotiable.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Wisconsin?

It is. A Wisconsin direct cremation near $1,700 costs about $5,500 less than the $7,200 traditional funeral, and close to $8,900 less after about $3,400 in cemetery costs. WFCAP can also put up to $1,000 toward crematory or cemetery charges for eligible families.

What does the FTC Funeral Rule mean for me in Wisconsin?

In Wisconsin, as everywhere in the US, the FTC Funeral Rule requires an itemized General Price List, phone price quotes, and no fee for using a casket bought elsewhere. According to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, Wisconsin does not require families to hire a funeral director for every step. You can confirm a Wisconsin license or file a complaint with the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services at 608-266-2112.

What is the best way to reduce funeral expenses in Wisconsin?

Getting two or three Wisconsin General Price Lists and comparing them line by line often saves $1,000 or more. Wisconsin also limits sales pressure: under Wis. Stat. §445.12, a funeral director may not solicit a funeral, or pay anyone to steer one, before or after death. Sellers of prepaid burial agreements may not approach someone in a hospital or care facility, contact relatives of a person whose death appears imminent, or go door to door. Township and county cemeteries are often half the price of private ones; call the county clerk. Direct cremation, at $1,700, is the lowest-cost option in the state. Check veteran benefits, Medicaid funeral help and the Social Security death payment before you sign.

Does Wisconsin require embalming?

Not in most cases. Wisconsin law does not make embalming routine, and the FTC Funeral Rule forbids a home from saying it is legally required unless it can cite the specific law. If there will be no public viewing, ask about refrigeration instead.

Where does Wisconsin rank nationally for funeral costs?

Wisconsin's $7,200 average for a traditional funeral puts it 13% below the $8,300 national median. Pricing in Wisconsin is near or slightly below the national average, reflecting moderate costs of living across the region, and the Midwest region as a whole tends to price moderate to lower than other parts of the US.

What is the least expensive way to handle a funeral in Wisconsin?

In Wisconsin, direct cremation at about $1,700 is the most affordable path: just transport, cremation, and the return of the remains. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $3,600 in Wisconsin before cemetery fees and is the cheapest burial option.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Wisconsin?

Ask three Wisconsin homes for General Price Lists and compare their direct cremation and immediate burial lines. Wisconsin's ban on solicitation (Wis. Stat. §445.12) means no one should pressure you into a choice, and the FTC Funeral Rule lets you bring a casket or urn from elsewhere without a fee.
