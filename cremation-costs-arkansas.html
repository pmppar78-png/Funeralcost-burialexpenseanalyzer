Cremation Costs in Arkansas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 42%

Cremation in Arkansas costs less than almost anywhere in the Mid-South — a direct cremation averages $1,500, which is among the lowest state-level numbers in the country. Part of the reason is low operating overhead for Arkansas funeral homes, part is the Roller Funeral Homes network, which runs multiple Arkansas locations and prices direct cremation aggressively. The state's 42 percent cremation rate sits below the national line but has nearly doubled in a decade as Arkansas Baptist and Methodist congregations have grown more comfortable with cremation as a disposition choice. Below: what the Arkansas State Board of Embalmers, Funeral Directors, Cemeteries and Burial Services requires, where the cost variation hides, and how to talk to a funeral director in Arkansas when you are price-shopping.

The Roller Factor in Arkansas Cremation Pricing

One name dominates Arkansas funeral services more than any other: Roller. Roller Funeral Homes operate more than a dozen Arkansas locations — from Little Rock to Fort Smith, Fayetteville, Conway, Russellville and Hot Springs — and the chain's uniform direct cremation pricing acts as an informal price ceiling for much of the state. Independent funeral homes in smaller Arkansas towns routinely price at or below Roller's published number to stay competitive, which is why the state's direct cremation average sits at $1,500 rather than the $1,800-$2,000 common in neighboring states. When you call Arkansas funeral homes for written General Price Lists, ask specifically whether the direct cremation price includes the cremation container, the mileage for transport, and return of cremated remains — those three line items are where the bundled-versus-unbundled pricing games get played.

Regulatory Oversight in Arkansas

Arkansas cremation is regulated by the State Board of Embalmers, Funeral Directors, Cemeteries and Burial Services — one of the broadest mandates of any state funeral regulator because it also covers cemeteries and pre-need contracts. Before a cremation can take place:

  • A cremation authorization form must be signed by the legal next-of-kin under Arkansas Code Annotated § 20-17
  • The death must be certified by a physician, coroner, or medical examiner
  • A burial-transit permit must be issued by the local registrar
  • Arkansas recognizes a 24-hour holding period from time of death
  • Implanted cardiac devices must be removed before cremation

Questions worth asking any Arkansas funeral director

  • Is the cremation performed on-site or contracted to a third-party crematory?
  • Is refrigeration included, and for how many days before extra charges apply?
  • What is the typical turnaround from authorization to return of ashes?
  • Which identification procedures (numbered tags, photo ID at retort) are followed?
  • Is there an additional charge for witnessing the cremation?
Cost-Saving Tip: You do not need to purchase an urn from the cremation provider. Urns can be purchased independently for significantly less. The FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to use a container purchased elsewhere.

Scattering in the Natural State

Arkansas takes the "Natural State" nickname seriously, and families who want to scatter a loved one's ashes outdoors have a broad legal runway. Ozark-St. Francis National Forest and Ouachita National Forest both allow dispersed scattering with a free-use permit from the ranger district. Buffalo National River, one of only two federally designated national rivers, permits scattering with a superintendent-issued Special Use Permit. Arkansas state parks including Petit Jean, Mount Magazine and Devil's Den require advance approval from the park superintendent. Private property scattering is unrestricted with owner consent. At-sea scattering isn't geographically relevant in Arkansas, but families do scatter on inland waterways like the Arkansas River, the White River, and Lake Ouachita — check with the Corps of Engineers for federally managed sections.

Assistance Programs Arkansas Families Can Access

  • Arkansas Crime Victims Reparations Board: For deaths resulting from violent crime, reimbursement up to $7,500 for funeral or cremation costs.
  • Social Security lump-sum death benefit: $255 to a surviving spouse or dependent child.
  • County indigent programs: Pulaski, Benton and Washington Counties maintain limited indigent cremation programs administered through the county quorum court.
  • Veteran benefits: Free cremated-remains interment at Arkansas State Veterans Cemetery in Birdeye or North Little Rock National Cemetery. The VA provides a plot allowance and, for service-connected deaths, full funeral reimbursement.
  • Arkansas Department of Human Services: In limited TANF-hardship circumstances, emergency funeral assistance may be available.

Why Arkansas's Cremation Rate Keeps Climbing

In 2010, barely 18 percent of Arkansas deaths resulted in cremation. By 2024, that figure had climbed to about 42 percent. The growth tracks a national pattern but has been steeper in Arkansas because the state started from a lower base. Southern Baptist Convention statements over the past two decades have been carefully neutral on cremation, leaving the decision to individual congregations and families — and congregations have largely followed the families. The Arkansas cost gap between direct cremation ($1,500) and full-service traditional burial (typically $7,500-$10,000 once plot, vault, and marker are included) is the single biggest practical driver.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does cremation cost in Arkansas in 2026?

Cremation in Arkansas runs from roughly $1,500 for the simplest direct option up to about $5,000 when paired with a viewing or ceremony. Urns, memorial services, and interment of ashes are priced separately — typically $50-$3,000 for an urn, $500-$2,000 for a memorial, and $200-$600 for scattering or inurnment. Your final number depends on which tier you pick.

What types of cremation are available in Arkansas?

Families in Arkansas can choose from three main types: direct cremation ($1,500) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service (ceremony held after cremation, often more flexible on timing); and traditional cremation ($5,000) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each option can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget.

What legal requirements apply to cremation in Arkansas?

A 24-48 hour waiting period applies before cremation in Arkansas, along with written authorization from the legal next of kin. Some Arkansas counties require a medical examiner's permit before release. Pacemakers, implanted defibrillators, and certain other implants are removed beforehand. And under the federal Funeral Rule, you do not need to buy a full casket — an alternative cremation container is sufficient.

Is ash scattering legal in Arkansas?

In most cases yes, but the specifics depend on where. In Arkansas you can generally scatter on private land (with owner permission) or at sea beyond 3 nautical miles under EPA rules. National park scattering often requires a free permit. Check Arkansas local ordinances and any park-specific rules before proceeding. Some families prefer columbarium niches, urn burial, or memorial jewelry to avoid the permitting question entirely.

Why is cremation growing in Arkansas?

Arkansas has below the national average, though cremation rates have been rising steadily, with a cremation rate of 42%. Factors driving cremation adoption include lower cost compared to traditional burial (saving $4,600 or more), greater flexibility for memorial services, environmental considerations, and changing cultural preferences. The national cremation rate has exceeded 60% and continues to rise annually.

What is the timeline for cremation in Arkansas?

The cremation itself runs 2-3 hours in Arkansas. Factor in the mandatory 24-48 hour waiting period before cremation, then 3-7 business days for the crematory to process paperwork and prepare the ashes for pickup or delivery. A direct cremation ($1,500) is the fastest end-to-end because no ceremony sits between death and cremation.

Do I have to skip the service if I choose cremation in Arkansas?

Not at all. Arkansas providers offer cremation with a full service — visitation, ceremony, and then cremation instead of burial — for about $5,000. Memorial services after cremation are also common, letting families choose any venue or date. Only direct cremation at $1,500 omits the service entirely.
