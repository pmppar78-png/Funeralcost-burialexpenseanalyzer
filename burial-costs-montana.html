Burial Costs in Montana (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Mountain

Burying someone in Montana involves a logistical problem that does not exist in most states: frost depth and elevation can shut cemetery ground operations from November through April in parts of the Rockies and high plains. Factor that in, and a conventional burial in Montana runs between $13,180 and $18,510 — slightly above the national midpoint, largely because the thin population outside Billings and Missoula limits funeral-home competition. Local ranchers and county clerks often know cheaper cemetery ground than Google does.

Embalming ($710) is not required under Montana law unless the body is being held beyond the statutory time limit or transported out of state by common carrier.

Winter, Elevation, and the Ground That Will Not Open

Cemeteries in Bozeman, Helena, Butte, and most of Flathead County close ground operations once the frost line passes roughly 40 inches — in practice, late November through early April. Billings and the eastern plains have shorter freeze windows; Kalispell can stretch longer. Funeral homes keep remains refrigerated (typically $50-$100 per day) or in a vault on cemetery grounds until the ground releases. Ask directly whether the quoted opening-and-closing fee assumes a winter hold, because a February death can carry four months of storage before interment, and families who do not ask sometimes receive the storage invoice separately in May.

Where Montana Actually Buries

Billings concentrates most of the state's metropolitan burials — Mountview Cemetery (city-run, opened 1882) and Yellowstone National Cemetery for eligible veterans handle the bulk. In Missoula, the city cemetery on Tower Street has dedicated pricing for residents. Bozeman's Sunset Hills sits above the M trailhead and prices accordingly; Helena's Forestvale is the state capital's oldest continuously operating burial ground. Beyond the towns, county-level and small-community cemeteries (Anaconda, Livingston, Red Lodge, Hamilton, Havre) routinely price plots 40-60% below Billings private rates. Eligibility and benefits fall under the VA National Cemetery Administration (38 U.S.C. § 2402) and the VA burial allowance rules at 38 CFR § 3.1600.

What Montana Law Actually Allows

The Montana Board of Funeral Service, under the Department of Labor and Industry, licenses funeral directors, embalmers, and establishments. Montana is one of a dozen states that still permits home funeral care and private-land burial with comparatively modest paperwork — a death certificate, compliance with local zoning setbacks from water sources, and registration of the burial location with the county. For ranching families who already own the land, that legal reality is not a novelty; it is a continuation of a long practice. The FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) binds every Montana funeral home to the General Price List disclosure and the right to bring in an outside casket without a handling fee.

Assistance and Veteran Benefits

Montana does not operate a named state burial assistance program, but the Department of Public Health and Human Services administers emergency burial funds at the county level when a decedent qualifies for TANF or has no family able to pay. The state runs three veterans cemeteries — Fort Harrison in Helena, Eastern Montana State Veterans Cemetery near Miles City, and the Western Montana State Veterans Cemetery in Missoula — in addition to Yellowstone National Cemetery. All four are no-cost for eligible veterans, with reduced pricing for spouses and dependents.

Where the Savings Live

  • Ask about frost and winter hold up front: Confirm in writing whether the quoted opening-and-closing includes winter storage, and at what daily rate. A four-month hold at $50/day adds $6,000 that no price list shows.
  • Call the county clerk: Montana rural cemetery fee schedules usually live only with the county clerk, not online. For Park, Stillwater, Beaverhead, Madison, and similar counties, this single call can surface plots 40-60% below Billings private rates.
  • Private-land burial where eligible: If the family owns qualifying land and can meet zoning and setback rules, Montana is one of the most permissive states for home burial. The paperwork is real but the plot cost is zero.

Common Questions

How much does burial cost in Montana?

A conventional Montana burial totals $13,180 to $18,510 once you add the $7,100 service, $3,200 plot, $2,130 casket, $1,280 vault, $1,600 opening-and-closing, and a marker at $1,000-$3,000. Winter holds at higher elevations can add storage fees the quote sheet does not always show — ask for the storage line item specifically.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Montana?

Montana law does not require a vault, but virtually every commercial and municipal cemetery in the state requires a sealed vault or a cheaper concrete liner. Frost heave in Montana soil is real enough that the requirement is not arbitrary. The liner option is what most families actually use — the price delta against a full vault can approach $1,000. Ask for the written cemetery policy before the plot deposit.

How can I reduce burial costs in Montana?

Three moves matter most in Montana: call the county clerk (not Google) for rural cemetery plot rates that can run 40-60% below Billings private cemeteries; buy the casket from an outside retailer the funeral home must accept under the FTC Funeral Rule; and if the family owns qualifying rural land, consider private-land burial — Montana is one of the more permissive states, though zoning and setback rules still apply.

What is an all-in burial estimate for Montana?

The realistic Montana range is $13,180 to $18,510, built from $7,100 service + $3,200 plot + $2,130 casket + $1,280 vault + $1,600 opening-and-closing + $1,000-$3,000 marker. Winter deaths can push the total $1,000-$6,000 higher depending on the storage window before the ground can be opened in spring.

Can I choose a natural or green burial in Montana?

Yes. Green burial is well established in Montana — the Billings and Missoula areas both have dedicated natural burial grounds, and Montana's relatively permissive rules on home funeral care and private-land burial mean a genuine green burial (no embalming, biodegradable container, shallow interment, native vegetation) is more accessible here than in most of the country. Total cost typically lands well below a conventional burial.
