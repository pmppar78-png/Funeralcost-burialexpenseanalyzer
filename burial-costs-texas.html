Burial Costs in Texas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: South

Texas is a cemetery state in an unusual sense. Chapter 711 of the Texas Health and Safety Code explicitly permits family cemeteries on private land — and rural counties from the Panhandle to the Rio Grande Valley still use them. Meanwhile the urban burial economy runs through the Texas State Cemetery in Austin (where governors, writers, and Stephen F. Austin himself are buried), Glenwood in Houston's Fourth Ward, and Oakwood in San Antonio's King William neighborhood. Total burial costs across this range fall between $13,660 and $19,220, but the actual spread is much wider than the averages suggest.

Most Texas burials land between $13,660 and $19,220. That range assumes commercial-cemetery burial with full funeral-home services. Families using a ranch or church cemetery under the Chapter 711 family-cemetery statute can and do bury for substantially less.

The Texas family cemetery statute (Health & Safety Code Ch. 711)

Texas law preserves a right that most states have effectively erased: burial on private, non-cemetery land. Under Chapter 711, landowners outside municipal corporate limits can establish family cemeteries on their own property subject to setback rules, deed recording, and county notification. For rural ranching families, this means plot fees, opening-and-closing, and perpetual-care charges can drop to zero. The obligations are real — the cemetery becomes a burden on the title in perpetuity — but the statute remains one reason Texas has some of the widest burial-cost variation in the country. County clerks and the Texas Funeral Service Commission handle questions about proper establishment.

A Houston-specific shopping tactic

Harris County has the densest funeral-home competition in the state, and the Houston Medical Center-area providers rarely match the pricing of homes on the far east and southwest sides. A family willing to drive 20 minutes for arrangement meetings regularly cuts the professional-services fee by $2,000. Request three General Price Lists on the same afternoon — the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) guarantees them at no charge — and line them up. The biggest variation in Texas is not the funeral-service fee but the casket markup, which commonly runs 300–400%. The casket buying guide and best online casket retailers show the wholesale floor.

Three religious-tradition quirks that affect invoices

Catholic burials along the Rio Grande Valley — Brownsville, Laredo, El Paso — often include a vigil and rosary that extend facility use and push invoices up. Jewish burial at Beth Israel in Houston or Dallas's Jewish cemeteries proceeds on a same-day or next-day schedule that removes embalming, viewing, and facility rental from the bill and typically lands under $7,000 total. Church of Christ congregations across Central Texas often arrange interment directly through a ministering family at plot costs closer to $600 than to a commercial park's $3,400. Ask.

No-embalming Texas

Texas does not require embalming for timely burial. A closed-casket service, direct burial, or Jewish same-day interment can legitimately skip the $720 embalming fee. The Texas Funeral Service Commission publishes the rule and fields complaints against providers who insist otherwise. This aligns with the federal FTC Funeral Rule, under which Texas funeral homes must also provide itemized General Price Lists and cannot surcharge third-party caskets.

Two regulators, not one: The Texas Funeral Service Commission licenses funeral establishments and handles mortuary complaints. The Texas Department of Banking oversees cemetery perpetual-care trust funds for commercial cemeteries. If you hit a problem with the cemetery rather than the funeral home, file with Banking. Most Texas families do not realize those are separate channels.

Where Texas veterans actually save

Texas is the best-served state in the country for VA burial eligibility: Fort Sam Houston National, DFW National, Houston National, and Kerrville National all operate at full capacity, with the state-run Rio Grande Valley State Veterans Cemetery adding coverage in South Texas. Grave, liner, opening and closing, and government marker are all at no cost for eligible veterans and qualifying dependents. Families with a veteran decedent can cut a traditional Texas burial bill by $10,000 or more; full eligibility details in veteran burial benefits. Eligibility and benefits fall under the VA National Cemetery Administration (38 U.S.C. § 2402) and the VA burial allowance rules at 38 CFR § 3.1600.

Green burial, indigent programs, and the cost floor

  • Eloise Woods near Cedar Creek — certified green burial, roughly $2,500–$4,000 all-in with no vault or embalming.
  • Ethician Family Cemetery near Huntsville — similar conservation-burial model.
  • Harris County Indigent Burial Program — documented, with published eligibility thresholds; direct burial is the default.
  • Bexar and Dallas county programs — exist but with narrower eligibility; always apply early.
  • Hybrid green sections — increasingly offered at commercial Texas cemeteries; more in green burial options.

Broader pathways are covered in cheapest funeral options and funeral payment assistance. For monument work, local Texas stoneworkers routinely undercut cemetery-vendor markers 25–35% — the headstone cost guide covers the mechanics.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do Texas families spend on burial?

Most Texas burials total $13,660 to $19,220. The service runs about $7,200, plot $3,400, casket $2,160, vault $1,360, opening and closing $1,700, headstone $1,000–$3,000. Dallas and Houston metros tend toward the upper end; rural counties and family cemetery arrangements under Chapter 711 can come in well under $10,000.

What are the cheapest burial options in Texas?

Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery, DFW National Cemetery, and Houston National Cemetery are free for eligible veterans. A family cemetery on private rural land under Chapter 711 can eliminate plot, opening-and-closing, and perpetual-care fees entirely. Eloise Woods near Cedar Creek offers certified green burial for about $2,500–$4,000. Harris, Bexar, and Dallas counties all maintain limited indigent-burial programs.

Once I add every line item, what does burial actually cost in Texas?

The Texas arithmetic: service $7,200 + plot $3,400 + casket $2,160 + vault $1,360 + opening-closing $1,700 + headstone $1,000–$3,000 = $13,660–$19,220. El Paso and the Rio Grande Valley often run below that range; Austin and the wealthier Dallas suburbs run above.

Is green burial available in Texas?

Yes. Eloise Woods near Cedar Creek (east of Austin) and Ethician Family Cemetery near Huntsville are the two primary certified natural burial options in the state. Several traditional cemeteries also allow green burial in dedicated sections. Texas's rural family-cemetery tradition under Chapter 711 has historically included many essentially green burials without vaults or embalming.
