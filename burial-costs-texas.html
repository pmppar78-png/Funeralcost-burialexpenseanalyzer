Burial Costs in Texas (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: South

Everything is bigger in Texas — and that includes the burial market. As the second-largest state by both area and population, Texas encompasses wildly different funeral markets in Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, San Antonio, Austin, and El Paso, along with vast rural stretches where burial costs are among the lowest in the nation. The state's strong Hispanic Catholic traditions, significant military presence, and the outsized influence of Service Corporation International (SCI, headquartered in Houston) all shape a burial landscape that demands careful comparison shopping. This guide helps Texas families navigate the state's diverse burial options and costs.

What Drives Burial Costs in Texas

Texas burial costs vary enormously by location. Houston — headquarters of Service Corporation International, the world's largest funeral company — and Dallas-Fort Worth represent the most expensive markets, with well-established memorial parks charging $3,000 to $8,000+ for plots. San Antonio and Austin fall in the middle, while the Rio Grande Valley, West Texas, and the rural Panhandle offer some of the most affordable burial in the country.

The Hispanic Catholic tradition of burial is profoundly important in Texas, particularly in San Antonio, the Rio Grande Valley, and El Paso. Catholic cemeteries operated by the various Texas dioceses serve large communities, and the tradition of elaborate Dia de los Muertos observances reflects the deep cultural significance of burial in these communities.

Texas has more military installations than any other state — Fort Cavazos (formerly Fort Hood), Fort Bliss, Joint Base San Antonio, and many others — creating a substantial veteran population. Multiple national cemeteries including Houston National Cemetery, Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery, and Dallas-Fort Worth National Cemetery provide free burial for eligible veterans.

Cemetery Types Available in Texas

  • Corporate memorial parks — SCI-owned and other corporate cemeteries dominate the Houston and Dallas markets. These tend to be the most expensive options but offer extensive amenities.
  • Catholic cemeteries — Texas dioceses operate cemetery networks in all major cities. San Fernando Cemetery in San Antonio is one of the most significant Catholic burial grounds in the Southwest.
  • Municipal cemeteries — Austin, San Antonio, and many Texas cities maintain public cemeteries with regulated pricing.
  • National cemeteries — Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, Fort Sam Houston, Fort Bliss, and Kerrville National Cemeteries serve the state's large veteran population.
  • Rural community cemeteries — Small-town Texas cemeteries, often maintained by local associations, offer remarkably affordable plots — sometimes under $300.
  • Family cemeteries — Texas law protects burial on private property, and family cemeteries on ranches and farms are common throughout rural areas.

Casket and Vault Options

Texas funeral homes average $2,220 for caskets. The Texas market is competitive, with numerous online and retail casket sellers, especially in Houston and Dallas. The FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to use third-party caskets. Most Texas cemeteries require vaults, though many rural and family cemeteries do not. Texas clay soils can cause significant ground settling without a vault.

Consumer Rights for Burial in Texas

  • FTC Funeral Rule — All funeral homes must provide itemized pricing.
  • Texas Funeral Service Commission — Regulates funeral establishments and handles complaints.
  • Embalming not required — Texas does not mandate embalming, though it is required for public viewing unless alternative preservation is used.
  • Casket purchase freedom — Third-party caskets must be accepted without handling fees.
  • Home burial permitted — Texas allows burial on private property with compliance with local regulations.
  • Family cemetery protections — Texas law protects access to family cemeteries.
  • Preneed protections — Texas law governs prepaid funeral contracts with trust and insurance funding requirements.

Green and Natural Burial in Texas

Texas has a growing green burial movement. Eloise Woods Natural Burial Park near Austin was one of the first dedicated natural burial grounds in the state. Several other Texas cemeteries have developed green sections. The state's vast rural land and permissive home burial laws provide additional options for natural burial. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Texas?

Total costs range from $13,960 to $19,680 statewide. Houston and Dallas are most expensive. Rural Texas can offer complete burial for under $7,000.

Is a burial vault required in Texas?

No state law requires vaults. Most urban cemeteries require them, but many rural and family cemeteries do not.

What are the cheapest burial options in Texas?

Direct burial in a rural community cemetery with an online casket can cost under $5,000. Some rural cemetery plots cost under $300. Veterans receive free burial at Texas's multiple national cemeteries.

How does SCI affect funeral costs in Texas?

Service Corporation International, headquartered in Houston, operates dozens of funeral homes and cemeteries across Texas. Their prices tend to be at or above market average. Shopping between SCI-owned and independent providers is important for Texas families seeking competitive pricing.

What Hispanic burial traditions are common in Texas?

Hispanic Catholic burial traditions are deeply significant in Texas, particularly in San Antonio, the Rio Grande Valley, and El Paso. These include velorios (wakes), Catholic funeral masses, burial in Catholic cemeteries, and ongoing memorial observances including Dia de los Muertos. Catholic cemeteries throughout Texas serve these communities.

Is a burial vault required in Texas?

Many cemeteries in Texas require a burial vault or grave liner, though this is typically a cemetery policy rather than a state law. Vaults prevent the ground from settling. Ask the cemetery about their specific requirements and alternative options.

What are the cheapest burial options in Texas?

The most affordable burial options in Texas include direct burial (no viewing or service), which can save thousands. Green burial, which skips embalming and uses a biodegradable casket or shroud, is another cost-effective option available at some Texas cemeteries.
