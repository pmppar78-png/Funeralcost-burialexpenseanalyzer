Funeral Costs in Mississippi: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: South

Mississippi's funeral market is still dominated by Black-owned and family-run providers with century-plus roots — Wright & Ferguson in Jackson, Century (Hall's) Funeral Home in McComb, and clusters of independent Delta and Hill Country operators who price well below national chains. The Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service licenses providers under Title 73 Chapter 11 of the state code, and the state's 28% cremation rate — the lowest of any state we cover — reflects deeply held Southern Baptist, AME, and COGIC burial traditions. Translation: most Mississippi families are buying a full-service homegoing, and the price tiers reflect that.

The Mississippi Homegoing Tradition and Its Price Signature

In most Mississippi communities, a funeral is a homegoing — a multi-day observance anchored in a Baptist, AME, or COGIC church, with visitation, a church service that often runs two hours, processional to the cemetery, and a repast hosted by the church's women's ministry. This pattern anchors the state's traditional-funeral average near $5,900 and keeps cremation comparatively rare at 28%. Ministers, musicians, and program-printing costs usually flow through the church rather than the funeral home, but obituary publication in the Clarion Ledger, Hattiesburg American, or Meridian Star adds $150–$400 that some families miss on the first-pass quote.

Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service and Title 73 Chapter 11

The Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service, based in Jackson, licenses funeral directors, embalmers, and establishments under Title 73 Chapter 11 of the Mississippi Code. Preneed funeral contracts are regulated by the Mississippi Secretary of State's Securities Division, with an 85% trust requirement on preneed funds and a cancellation right that protects the principal. Complaints about billing misrepresentation, embalming claims, or refund disputes go to the Board at its Jackson office. Mississippi also requires funeral homes to accept third-party caskets and urns without surcharge, mirroring the FTC Funeral Rule.

Magnolia State Cemetery Landscape: Church, Private, and the MS Veterans Plots

Mississippi's cemetery market is unusual — a large share of burials still occur in church-owned cemeteries attached to the decedent's home parish, which often price plots between $300 and $1,200 for active members, a fraction of commercial memorial parks. Lakewood Memorial Park in Jackson, Floral Hills in Hattiesburg, and Biloxi National Cemetery anchor the commercial and federal tiers. Mississippi State Veterans Cemetery at Newton and the two national cemeteries (Biloxi and Natchez) provide no-cost burial, vault, and marker for eligible veterans.

Mississippi Division of Medicaid and County Pauper Burial

Mississippi Medicaid does not directly cover funeral costs, but the state relies on a county pauper burial system under Section 41-39-5 of the Mississippi Code: when a decedent has no family able to pay, the county board of supervisors must arrange for burial or cremation at county expense. Benefit amounts vary by county — Hinds, Harrison, and Forrest counties process the largest volumes, and the county-appointed provider often bills $750–$1,500 for a minimal service. Families experiencing hardship should contact the circuit clerk's office in the county where death occurred before arranging services.

Church Cemeteries, Commercial Markups, and the Hidden Line Items on a Mississippi Invoice

Mississippi's price gap between church-cemetery burial and commercial memorial parks runs thousands of dollars, and the secondary charges magnify it. A 2a.m. first call — common in rural counties with one on-duty director for 30 miles — may carry a $200–$400 after-hours fee. Commercial cemeteries in Jackson and Gulfport often require a specific vault (Wilbert, Doric, or Trigard) that triples the $500 baseline; church cemeteries rarely require a vault at all. Embalming is not legally required in Mississippi for most services, but funeral homes sometimes quote it as "required for our facility" — ask to see the state statute, because under the FTC Funeral Rule that claim is actionable. And third-party caskets from Costco or Walmart cannot legally be surcharged under either federal rule or the Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service's own standards.

Get three quotes: the church-affiliated provider the family has used for generations, a Jackson or Gulfport commercial chain, and a direct-cremation specialist. The church provider usually wins on community fit but is rarely the cheapest line-by-line.

The Church Burial, Direct Cremation, and Veteran Paths: Mississippi's Sub-$3,000 Options

Mississippi offers three routes well below the $5,900 state average. Burial in a home-church cemetery for an active member often lands between $2,500 and $3,500 all-in, including a simple service, a $300–$1,200 plot, and a vault the cemetery may not require. Direct cremation through a dedicated Gulf Coast or Jackson provider runs $1,400 with no viewing or ceremony — the cremated remains return to the family within two weeks. Veterans can use Biloxi National Cemetery, Natchez National Cemetery, or Mississippi State Veterans Cemetery at Newton for no-cost burial, grave-opening, vault, and government marker, leaving only the funeral-home services as a family expense.

Legal Protections When Buying a Funeral

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Mississippi must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in Mississippi has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Your state attorney general's office can also assist with consumer protection complaints.

Hurricane Season Logistics and Gulf Coast Repatriation

June through November, Gulf Coast Mississippi funeral homes operate under a different risk calculus: a named storm threatening the coast can force evacuation of the decedent inland, a process that may cost $400–$900 in transport beyond a normal call. Ochsner, Riemann, and Bradford-O'Keefe on the coast maintain evacuation agreements with inland providers in Hattiesburg and Jackson. Families arranging services during hurricane season should explicitly ask whether an evacuation contingency is included in the quote — it rarely is, and finding out in a Category 2 is the wrong time.

Questions Worth Asking First

What is the average price of a funeral in Mississippi right now?

A traditional funeral in Mississippi averages $5,900, about 25% below the $7,848 US median. Cremation with a service runs $4,800, and direct cremation starts near $1,400. Church-owned cemetery plots remain common and often price $300–$1,200 for members — well below Lakewood Memorial Park in Jackson or commercial memorial parks. Wright & Ferguson GPLs in Jackson read different from Century in McComb, so a three-provider comparison is the right baseline.

Does cremation save money compared with burial in Mississippi?

Yes, but Mississippi's 28% cremation rate is the lowest in the region — Baptist, AME, and COGIC homegoing traditions keep burial dominant. Direct cremation at $1,400 versus a traditional homegoing at $5,900 with a commercial plot can leave a $5,000+ gap. Many Mississippi families still choose traditional burial even when cremation is available, because the multi-day church service is itself the point.

What are my consumer rights at Mississippi funeral homes?

The FTC Funeral Rule applies, and Mississippi's Title 73 Chapter 11 adds state-level protections on licensing, preneed trust handling, and disclosure. The Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service in Jackson handles licensing complaints; preneed disputes go to the Mississippi Secretary of State's Securities Division, which regulates preneed contracts under the 85% trust rule.

Is embalming legally required in Mississippi?

No. Mississippi law does not require embalming for ordinary services; refrigeration is an accepted alternative. Many Mississippi homegoing services include an open-casket viewing — which often leads providers to recommend embalming — but that is a service preference rather than a legal mandate. Under the Funeral Rule, a provider cannot tell you embalming is required when it is not.

How do funeral costs in Mississippi compare to other states?

Mississippi's $5,900 traditional-funeral average is among the lowest in the country, about 25% below the $7,848 national median. Lower cost of living, lower real-estate overhead, and the prevalence of church-owned cemeteries all contribute. The Gulf Coast and Jackson metro price higher than the Delta and Hill Country by 10–15%.

Which funeral option costs the least in Mississippi?

Direct cremation at roughly $1,400 is the lowest-priced disposition. For burial families, a church-owned cemetery plot combined with a no-frills graveside service at an independent provider can come in under $3,500 total. County pauper burial under Section 41-39-5 is available for qualifying families at no out-of-pocket cost.

Where do I look for low-cost funeral providers in Mississippi?

Start with three GPLs from a mix of a legacy Black-owned provider like Century Funeral Home, a larger regional like Wright & Ferguson, and a Gulf Coast or Delta independent. The Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service maintains a public licensee directory. Veterans should price-check against Biloxi National Cemetery and Mississippi State Veterans Cemetery before committing to a commercial plot.
