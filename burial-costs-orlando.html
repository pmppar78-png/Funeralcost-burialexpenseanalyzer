Burial Costs in Orlando, Florida (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Florida

Orlando's cemetery map is shaped less by denomination than by the city's growth pattern — a cluster of historic in-town grounds (Greenwood Cemetery on Greenwood Street dates to 1880 and is still owned by the City of Orlando), a ring of suburban memorial parks built in the boom years after 1960, and Florida National Cemetery in Bushnell about an hour north serving Central Florida veterans. A traditional 2026 burial here runs roughly $8,580 for the funeral home's basic services and $3,960 for a plot, with full-line-item totals between $16,104 and $22,638.

Orlando Cemeteries: Greenwood, Woodlawn, and the Suburban Parks

Greenwood Cemetery on East Greenwood Street has been owned and operated by the City of Orlando since 1880, and its pricing reflects that public-agency character: plot fees currently sit in the low end of Orlando's $1,900–$7,900 range, and the cemetery publishes its fee schedule online — an openness that private memorial parks in the metro usually do not match. Woodlawn Cemetery (Gotha) and Glen Haven Memorial Park cover the west metro. Chapel Hill Memorial Gardens, Palm Cemetery in Winter Park, and All Saints Cemetery (Diocese of Orlando) cover other quadrants. Families with a loved one eligible for VA benefits should look first at Florida National Cemetery in Bushnell — plot, opening-and-closing, vault, and an upright government marker are all provided at no cost to eligible veterans, their spouses, and dependent children.

Florida Preneed Rules and the Orlando Funeral Market

Florida requires that pre-need funeral contracts be backed by either a 70 percent trust deposit or an insurance policy written to specific disclosure standards — this is administered by the Florida Department of Financial Services, Bureau of Funerals, Cemeteries, and Consumer Services. The practical effect for Orlando families is that every written pre-need quote must disclose trust or insurance backing, cancellation rights, and the name of the trustee. Do not sign a paper that lacks that disclosure box. Separately, Florida's Funeral Consumers Alliance of Central Florida publishes a periodic price survey of Orlando-area funeral homes that is worth reading before you request quotes.

Orlando tip: Above-ground entombment is more common in Central Florida than people realize, because Florida's high water table can complicate below-ground interments in some areas. In a cemetery that offers both, a crypt in the community mausoleum is occasionally priced below the equivalent ground plot plus vault — ask for quotes both ways.

Ways to Cut the Orlando Bill

  • Start with Greenwood's published fee sheet: Because the City of Orlando is a public agency, its cemetery fee schedule is a public record. That schedule becomes a useful anchor when you request quotes from private memorial parks in the metro.
  • Ask about church and diocesan cemeteries: All Saints Cemetery (Diocese of Orlando) and Catholic sections at other cemeteries price their plots below the commercial memorial-park average, especially for parishioners.
  • Compare ground plot plus vault vs. community mausoleum: In Central Florida's water-table conditions, crypt pricing occasionally undercuts an equivalent below-ground interment. Always ask for both quotes.
  • Check Florida National Cemetery eligibility: For qualifying veterans the Bushnell facility provides plot, vault, and marker at no charge — that alone takes $7,000 off an Orlando burial.
  • Use the 16-casket rule: Florida funeral homes must display or offer at least 16 caskets covering a meaningful price range, and under the FTC Funeral Rule they must also accept a third-party casket without a handling fee. Casket buying guide.
  • Request the written General Price List: Orlando has a large, competitive funeral-home market; three written GPLs almost always surface a several-thousand-dollar spread on identical service packages.
  • For low-income families, contact the Medical Examiner's office: Orange County operates an indigent burial program through its Medical Examiner's office for decedents whose families cannot pay and who do not qualify for other programs.
Save on Caskets: Under the FTC Funeral Rule, funeral homes must accept caskets purchased from third-party retailers without charging handling fees. Compare online casket retailers to save significantly.

What Families Want to Know

How much does burial cost in Orlando?

A 2026 traditional burial in Orlando runs about $8,580 for the funeral service plus $3,960 for a cemetery plot. Adding a vault ($1,584), opening-and-closing ($1,980), casket ($2,574), and marker ($1,500–$3,000), the all-in bill lands between $16,104 and $22,638. City-owned Greenwood Cemetery sits at the low end of that range; suburban memorial parks and All Saints (Diocese of Orlando) sit higher.

Is a burial vault required in Orlando?

Florida does not require vaults by statute. Most Orlando cemeteries require either a vault or the cheaper grave liner as a matter of written cemetery policy, principally because of Central Florida's sandy soil and high water table. Greenwood and several other grounds accept a liner in lieu of a full vault — ask for the written interment specification before paying a deposit. Liners typically save $500 to $2,000 versus a concrete or polymer vault.

What are the cheapest burial options in Orlando?

Orlando's lowest-cost burials usually come from one of four places: a direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) booked against three written General Price Lists; a plot at city-owned Greenwood Cemetery using the published fee schedule; Florida National Cemetery in Bushnell for eligible veterans (plot, vault, and marker at no cost); or a community mausoleum crypt, which in some Central Florida cemeteries ends up priced below an equivalent plot-plus-vault package.

How do Orlando burial costs compare to Florida?

Orlando sits slightly above the Florida state average — $8,580 funeral service and $3,960 plot versus $7,800 and $3,600 statewide — and is roughly level with Tampa. Miami is the state's most expensive metro ($9,750 service, $4,500 plot); Jacksonville is cheaper ($8,190 / $3,780). The premium in Orlando reflects metro land values and the density of suburban memorial parks built in the 1960s and 1970s that still dominate the market.
