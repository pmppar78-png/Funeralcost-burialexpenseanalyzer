Burial Costs in New Jersey (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Northeast

New Jersey sits at the top of the national burial price range, and the reason is simple: the densest population in the country is trying to bury on land that sells for suburban residential rates. A conventional Garden State burial runs $19,700 to $27,760, with the plot alone routinely clearing $5,000 at the historic cemeteries in Bergen, Essex, Hudson, and Union counties. State regulation here is also stricter than average — New Jersey is one of the few places where cemetery pricing is genuinely regulated, not just disclosed voluntarily.

Where Burial Actually Happens in New Jersey

The historic Catholic cemeteries — Holy Cross in North Arlington (operated by the Archdiocese of Newark), Holy Name in Jersey City, Gate of Heaven in East Hanover, and Most Holy Redeemer in Matawan — remain the centers of Catholic burial in north Jersey. The Jewish cemeteries concentrated in Paramus, Elmwood Park, and the Clifton area (including Beth Israel and Cedar Park) serve a significant share of the state's Jewish families. Rosedale Cemetery in Orange, Fairview Cemetery in Fairview, and George Washington Memorial Park in Paramus cover non-denominational metro burials. Beverly National Cemetery in Burlington County and Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Wrightstown handle eligible-veteran burials. Funeral-home pricing reflects a mix of independents (M. John Scanlan in Pompton Plains, Galante in Hoboken) and chain locations; the price dispersion between the two categories often tops $3,000 on the service line alone.

New Jersey's Unusual Regulatory Structure

New Jersey is one of a small number of states where cemeteries are substantively regulated. The New Jersey Cemetery Board, inside the Department of Community Affairs, licenses cemetery companies and enforces pricing disclosure requirements that go beyond the FTC Funeral Rule. Cemeteries in New Jersey must file a current price list with the Board, and any increase requires regulatory review — a level of oversight that does not exist in Pennsylvania, New York, or any Southern state. Pre-need cemetery sales require 100% trust placement under New Jersey Revised Statutes 45:27-36. Funeral homes are regulated separately by the New Jersey State Board of Mortuary Science, with the FTC Funeral Rule enforced on top of state rules.

Winter and the Bergen County Clay

North Jersey soil is dense clay, and frost depth reaches 36-42 inches through much of Bergen, Passaic, and Sussex counties. Cemeteries there typically charge winter surcharges of $500-$1,500 between mid-December and late March, or hold remains in vault storage until ground operations resume in spring. Shore-area cemeteries in Monmouth, Ocean, and Atlantic counties have shorter freeze windows but higher water tables, which can add drainage or alternative-burial costs. Ask every cemetery on your shortlist specifically about winter handling before a service estimate is finalized.

Financial Assistance Specific to New Jersey

The New Jersey Department of Human Services administers the NJ FamilyCare Burial Assistance program — benefit levels are modest but meaningful for SNAP, TANF, and General Assistance recipients. Municipal welfare offices under the Optional Municipal Welfare program have separate discretionary authority for indigent burials. Veterans have two federal options (Beverly National, William C. Doyle in Wrightstown) at no cost, plus BGen Doyle's state-operated pricing for spouses that sits well below commercial cemetery rates. For prospective buyers, New Jersey's strict 100% pre-need trust requirement makes it one of the safer states in the country to purchase a pre-need contract — the money is legally protected.

How to Compress the New Jersey Total

  • Use the Cemetery Board's price files: Because New Jersey cemeteries must file current pricing with the state, you can request that information directly from the Cemetery Board rather than taking the cemetery's verbal quote at face value.
  • Denominational cemetery savings: Archdiocese of Newark Catholic cemeteries and the concentrated Jewish cemeteries in Paramus and Elmwood Park frequently price plots $2,000-$3,000 below commercial memorial parks in the same county.
  • Cross the Delaware for plot savings: Families with roots in southwestern Jersey sometimes find that a Pennsylvania cemetery across the bridge prices plots $2,000-$4,000 lower. Transport adds cost, but the math often still favors it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do New Jersey families spend on burial?

New Jersey burials total $19,700 to $27,760 on the full arithmetic: $10,200 service, $5,000 plot, $3,060 casket, $2,000 vault, $2,500 opening-and-closing, and a $1,000-$3,000 marker. Those numbers put New Jersey among the five most expensive states for burial — Bergen, Hudson, Essex, and Union county cemeteries anchor the top; South Jersey and Pennsylvania-adjacent cemeteries anchor the bottom.

Is a burial vault required in New Jersey?

New Jersey law does not require a vault, but the dense clay soil and freeze-thaw cycles in north Jersey give every major cemetery there a practical reason to require a sealed vault or a concrete liner. The liner is typically several hundred dollars cheaper and is acceptable at Holy Cross, Rosedale, Gate of Heaven, and most other major cemeteries. Get the specific cemetery's written rule before the plot deposit.

How can I reduce burial costs in New Jersey?

Four moves have the biggest dollar impact: use a denominational cemetery (Archdiocese of Newark Catholic cemeteries, or the concentrated Jewish cemeteries in Paramus and Clifton) where plots price $2,000-$3,000 below commercial parks; buy the casket outside the funeral home under the FTC Funeral Rule with no handling fee; pull current cemetery pricing from the New Jersey Cemetery Board rather than relying on the cemetery's verbal quote; and consider direct burial through an independent funeral home — typically under $5,500 all-in.

How do cemetery prices differ across New Jersey?

Wide variance driven by land values and denomination. A plot at Gate of Heaven in East Hanover or George Washington Memorial Park in Paramus easily clears $5,000. A similar plot at a South Jersey municipal cemetery can run half that. New Jersey is unusual in that the New Jersey Cemetery Board actively regulates cemetery pricing — you can pull filed price schedules directly from the Board, which gives you a comparison baseline that does not exist in most states.

What is the total cost of burial in New Jersey including everything?

Full arithmetic: service ($10,200) + plot ($5,000) + casket ($3,060) + vault ($2,000) + opening-and-closing ($2,500) + marker ($1,000-$3,000) = $19,700 to $27,760. Winter surcharges in north Jersey can push the January/February total $500-$1,500 higher; confirming the winter handling line in writing before any contract is signed is the single most commonly missed cost control.

Can I choose a natural or green burial in New Jersey?

Yes. New Jersey has several cemeteries with dedicated green burial sections, including Steelmantown Cemetery in Woodbine (one of the earliest certified conservation burial grounds in the country) and hybrid sections at some municipal cemeteries. State law permits home funeral care and unembalmed burial within the statutory timeframe. A genuine green burial in New Jersey often clears under $10,000 all-in, materially below the conventional $19,700-$27,760 range.
