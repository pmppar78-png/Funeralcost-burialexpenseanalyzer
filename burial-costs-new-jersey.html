How to Reduce Burial Costs in New Jersey

Burial costs in New Jersey can be managed with smart planning:

  • Request itemized pricing from every provider: Funeral homes in New Jersey must provide a General Price List. Cemeteries are not required to, but most in the New Jersey area will share one if you ask specifically for the itemized price sheet rather than a "package."
  • Confirm winter pricing up front: New Jersey cemeteries frequently add frozen-ground charges from December through March, or require winter vault storage until spring interment.
  • Denominational and municipal cemeteries: New Jersey has one of the highest densities of nonprofit cemeteries in the country.
  • Shop outside the immediate metro: A 20-30 minute drive from Newark into a lower-cost county can reduce plot prices substantially without changing the family's ability to visit.

How New Jersey Burial Compares With the Region

Winter burials in New Jersey can be delayed or carry a frozen-ground surcharge; some cemeteries charge an additional $500-$1,500 from December through March, or offer winter vault storage until spring interment. If the death occurs between late November and early March, ask every New Jersey cemetery on your shortlist about winter handling fees specifically; they are rarely volunteered up front.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do New Jersey families spend on burial?

Typical New Jersey burials total between $23,260 and $27,760. Of that, the New Jersey funeral service runs about $10,200, the cemetery plot $5,000, the casket around $3,060, the vault $2,000, opening-and-closing $2,500, and a headstone $500-$5,000.

Is a burial vault required in New Jersey?

Burial vaults are required by most cemeteries in New Jersey as a matter of cemetery policy (to prevent ground settling), though they are rarely mandated by state law. Vault costs in New Jersey typically range from $800 to $10,000.

How can I reduce burial costs in New Jersey?

Three options cut New Jersey burial costs the most: choose direct burial (interment without viewing, ceremony, or embalming); go with green or natural burial at a New Jersey cemetery that offers it (biodegradable container, no embalming, often a smaller plot fee); and buy the casket from an independent or online retailer rather than the funeral home; the casket alone can cost noticeably less.

How do cemetery prices differ across New Jersey?

A useful tip: New Jersey has many nonprofit, municipal, and denominational cemeteries that undercut private memorial parks, and winter pricing can add a separate line item between December and March.

What is the total cost of burial in New Jersey including everything?

The first answer above lists each item; in New Jersey, cash advance items such as certified death certificates, an obituary notice and flowers are added on top of the $23,260 to $27,760 range.

Can I choose a natural or green burial in New Jersey?

A growing number of New Jersey cemeteries now offer green burial sections (biodegradable container, no embalming, no vault), and the total cost is usually below a traditional burial. If a dedicated green burial ground is not nearby, some families in New Jersey use a hybrid approach (traditional plot without embalming).

Burial rules and help in New Jersey

Funeral homes handling burials in New Jersey are licensed by the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs (973-504-6425). Cemetery sales in New Jersey may fall under a different office, so ask the board who oversees the cemetery you are considering.

A burial in New Jersey cannot be handled entirely by the family: N.J. Stat. § 26:6 requires a licensed funeral director for filing the death certificate, moving the body, and being present at the final disposition, as the Funeral Consumers Alliance summarizes it, while the New Jersey cemetery itself opens and closes the grave.

A New Jersey burial needs a disposition permit. If you switch to cremation instead, a 24-hour wait applies (N.J.S.A. 26:7-18.1).

New Jersey keeps its two allowances separate: up to $714 for the cemetery, claimed on its own form, and up to $3,056 for the funeral home, with up to $1,570 in family help not counted (N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5). (Source: N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5.)

Eligible New Jersey veterans get a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. In New Jersey, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Finn's Point National Cemetery (215-222-7510, cremated remains only); state veterans cemetery: Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery (609-738-2400) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration). Finn's Point, in Pennsville, now takes cremated remains only, so a casket burial for a New Jersey veteran usually means the state's Doyle cemetery in Wrightstown.

Next steps for a burial in New Jersey

  1. Compare at least two New Jersey cemeteries as well as two funeral homes, since the plot, grave opening and marker are priced separately.
  2. Ask the New Jersey cemetery whether it requires an outer burial container before you shop for one.
  3. Make sure the New Jersey cemetery files its own WFNJ-11C claim with the county welfare agency.
  4. For a veteran, ask Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery about eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. A complaint call to 973-504-6425 is free if a funeral home misleads you about burial costs.
