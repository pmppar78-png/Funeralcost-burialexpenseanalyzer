Burial Costs in New Jersey (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 24, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Reworded every section of the New Jersey burial page by hand, from the cost answers through the rules, regulators and benefits. Region: Northeast

A burial in New Jersey costs more than in Delaware or Pennsylvania: our estimate for a complete burial is $23,260 to $27,760, with about $10,200 of that going to the funeral home and $5,000 to the plot. The state also requires a licensed funeral director to be involved. This page lists each charge, explains the permit rules, and shows where New Jersey families can still spend less.

How to Reduce Burial Costs in New Jersey

Even at New Jersey prices, a few choices make a real difference:

  • Get itemized prices from both sides. A New Jersey funeral home must give you its General Price List. A cemetery does not have to, but most will provide an itemized price sheet if you ask for it instead of a package.
  • Ask what winter adds. Between December and March some New Jersey cemeteries charge for frozen ground or store the casket until a spring burial.
  • Compare nonprofit cemeteries. New Jersey has many church, municipal and other nonprofit cemeteries, and their plots often cost less than a private memorial park's.
  • Search past Newark. A cemetery 20 to 30 minutes from Newark in a lower-cost county can charge much less for a plot and still be close enough for visits.

How New Jersey Burial Compares With the Region

Cold weather can affect a New Jersey burial. Some cemeteries add a frozen-ground charge, often $500 to $1,500, between December and March, and some offer to hold the casket in a winter vault until spring. If a death comes in late fall or winter, put the question to each cemetery yourself, since the fee is seldom listed in the first quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the price of a burial in New Jersey?

Our New Jersey estimate is $23,260 to $27,760 in all. That is made up of roughly $10,200 for the funeral home's service, $5,000 for a plot, $3,060 for a casket, $2,000 for a vault and $2,500 for opening and closing the grave, plus a headstone at $500 to $5,000.

Does a New Jersey burial need a vault?

Usually yes, but because the cemetery requires it, not the state. A vault or liner stops the ground above the grave from sinking. In New Jersey these cost about $800 to $10,000; ask whether a simple liner satisfies the cemetery.

What are the best ways to save on a New Jersey burial?

Start with direct burial, which skips the viewing, the service and embalming. If a New Jersey cemetery near you offers green burial, its biodegradable container and smaller plot fee can save more. And compare the funeral home's casket with one from an independent or online seller, which often costs noticeably less.

Do New Jersey cemeteries charge different prices?

Yes, quite a lot. Church, municipal and other nonprofit cemeteries in New Jersey often charge less than private memorial parks, and some cemeteries add a separate winter charge between December and March, so compare full written prices.

What extra costs come on top of a New Jersey burial?

Cash advance items. The funeral home pays for things like certified death certificates, an obituary and flowers and adds them to the bill, beyond the $23,260 to $27,760 New Jersey range.

Does New Jersey allow green or natural burial?

It does. Some New Jersey cemeteries have green sections, where burial is in a biodegradable container with no embalming and no vault, and the cost is usually lower than a conventional burial. If none is nearby, a family can still choose an ordinary plot without embalming.

The New Jersey burial permit and the 10-day return

A New Jersey burial needs a permit from the local registrar, issued on the state form or through the New Jersey Electronic Death Registration System. It names the person and the cause of death and states that a satisfactory death certificate has been filed before it grants permission to bury (N.J.S.A. 26:6-16; checked October 29, 2026).

The paperwork does not end at the graveside. After the burial, the cemetery notes the date on the permit and sends it back to the local registrar, so the endorsed permit becomes the official record of when and where the burial happened. If a family later finds a gap in the record, ask the cemetery and the local registrar for that endorsed permit.

New Jersey burial rules, regulators and benefits

The New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs (973-504-6425) licenses the state's funeral homes. Cemetery sales can fall under a different office, so ask who regulates the particular cemetery you are considering before you buy a plot.

Unlike most states, New Jersey does not let a family handle a burial entirely on its own. N.J. Stat. § 26:6 requires a licensed funeral director to file the death certificate, move the body and attend the final disposition, as the Funeral Consumers Alliance explains; the cemetery itself opens and closes the grave. Every burial needs a disposition permit, and if the plan changes to cremation, a 24-hour wait applies (N.J.S.A. 26:7-18.1).

Public help with the cost

New Jersey pays its two burial allowances separately: up to $714 to the cemetery, which it claims on its own form, and up to $3,056 to the funeral home. Up to $1,570 contributed by family is not counted against them (Source: N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5).

For an eligible veteran, a national cemetery provides the grave, the opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost. Finn's Point National Cemetery in Pennsville (215-222-7510) now accepts cremated remains only, so a casket burial for a New Jersey veteran usually goes to the state's Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Wrightstown (609-738-2400) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

What to do next in New Jersey

  1. Get prices from at least two cemeteries and two funeral homes, since the plot, grave opening and marker are each priced separately.
  2. Before buying an outer burial container, confirm whether the cemetery requires one.
  3. If the county is paying, make sure the cemetery files its own WFNJ-11C claim with the county welfare agency.
  4. For a veteran, ask the Doyle cemetery about eligibility before paying for a private plot.
  5. If a funeral home misleads you about burial prices, a call to 973-504-6425 is free.
