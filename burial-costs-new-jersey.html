Burial Costs in New Jersey (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: Northeast

New Jersey ranks among the most expensive states in the nation for burial, driven by its dense population, limited land availability, and high cost of living. With nearly 9.3 million residents packed into the nation's most densely populated state, cemetery space is at a premium — particularly in North Jersey, where proximity to New York City pushes costs even higher. New Jersey's rich cultural diversity means burial traditions here span Italian Catholic, Irish, Jewish, Hindu, Muslim, and many other religious and ethnic customs, each with specific cemetery and service requirements. This guide details what New Jersey families can expect to pay and how to protect their interests.

What Drives Burial Costs in New Jersey

New Jersey's burial costs are among the highest in the country for several interconnected reasons. Land scarcity is the primary driver — in the densely developed northern and central corridor, cemetery land competes with residential and commercial real estate worth millions per acre. Many older cemeteries in Newark, Jersey City, and other urban areas are at or nearing capacity, pushing families toward newer, more expensive memorial parks in the suburbs.

The state's strong religious and ethnic cemetery networks also shape the market. The Catholic Archdiocese of Newark and the Dioceses of Trenton, Metuchen, Paterson, and Camden operate extensive cemetery systems with dedicated sections for Catholic burial. Jewish cemeteries — both Orthodox and non-Orthodox — are numerous, particularly in North and Central Jersey, with specific requirements for plain wooden caskets, no embalming, and same-day or next-day burial that can affect overall costs. Hindu and Sikh communities often prefer cremation, but Muslim burial traditions require prompt interment without embalming, and several New Jersey cemeteries have designated Islamic sections.

Corporate funeral home consolidation has also impacted pricing. Service Corporation International (SCI) and other national chains operate numerous funeral homes across New Jersey, and their pricing tends to sit at or above market averages. Independent funeral homes, which still represent a significant share of the market, offer a wider range of pricing.

Cemetery Types Available in New Jersey

  • Catholic cemeteries — The largest organized cemetery system in New Jersey. Holy Cross, Gate of Heaven, Holy Name, and dozens of other Catholic cemeteries serve communities statewide. Pricing is generally moderate compared to private memorial parks.
  • Jewish cemeteries — Numerous Jewish cemeteries serve Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, and secular Jewish communities. Many are affiliated with specific synagogues or Jewish burial societies (chevra kadisha).
  • Municipal cemeteries — Some New Jersey towns maintain public cemeteries, though this is less common than in New England. Evergreen Cemetery in Hillside and Rosedale Cemetery in Linden are notable examples.
  • National cemeteries — Beverly National Cemetery in Burlington County and Finn's Point National Cemetery in Salem County serve eligible veterans. Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Wrightstown is the state-operated veterans cemetery.
  • Private memorial parks — Large corporate-owned memorial parks with mausoleum complexes, cremation gardens, and premium lawn sections represent the highest-cost burial option in the state.
  • Historic cemeteries — Several colonial-era and Revolutionary War-era cemeteries remain in use, including the Old Paramus Reformed Church Cemetery dating to the 1720s.

Casket and Vault Options

With an average casket price of $3,060 at New Jersey funeral homes — well above the national average — there is strong financial incentive for families to shop around. Online casket retailers deliver throughout New Jersey, typically offering comparable caskets for $800 to $2,500. Several New Jersey casket showrooms also sell directly to consumers. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, no funeral home in New Jersey can refuse a third-party casket or charge a fee for handling it.

Note that Jewish burial tradition calls for a simple, all-wood casket without metal hardware — these are available from specialized Judaic funeral suppliers for $500 to $1,500, often less expensive than standard caskets.

Virtually all New Jersey cemeteries require a burial vault or grave liner. Given the state's clay-heavy soil and high water tables in many areas, vaults serve important practical purposes. Concrete liners start around $800, while high-end protective vaults can exceed $10,000.

Consumer Rights for Burial in New Jersey

New Jersey provides some of the strongest funeral consumer protections in the country:

  • FTC Funeral Rule — All New Jersey funeral homes must provide itemized General Price Lists. You cannot be forced to purchase package deals.
  • NJ Prepaid Funeral Act — New Jersey requires 100% of prepaid funeral funds to be placed in trust. Consumers can transfer their contract to another funeral home or cancel for a full refund (minus any services already rendered).
  • Embalming not required by state law — New Jersey does not mandate embalming. However, if you choose a public viewing, the funeral home may require embalming or provide alternative preservation options.
  • NJ Cemetery Act — The New Jersey Cemetery Board regulates all cemeteries in the state, ensuring proper maintenance of perpetual care funds and transparent pricing.
  • Right to a simple container — If you choose direct burial, you are entitled to use an unfinished wood box or alternative container rather than a traditional casket.
  • Complaints — The NJ State Board of Mortuary Science handles complaints against funeral homes, while the NJ Cemetery Board addresses cemetery-related issues.

Green and Natural Burial in New Jersey

Despite its urban density, New Jersey has made strides in green burial. Steelmantown Cemetery in the Pine Barrens of Atlantic County is one of the most recognized natural burial grounds in the Northeast, offering burial in biodegradable caskets or shrouds without vaults or embalming in a restored natural landscape. Several other New Jersey cemeteries have begun offering green burial sections, and the state's active funeral consumer advocacy community continues to push for expanded options. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in New Jersey?

A traditional burial in New Jersey costs approximately $10,200 for the funeral service plus $5,000 for a cemetery plot. Including vault, headstone, and grave opening fees, total costs typically range from $19,700 to $27,760. North Jersey (Bergen, Essex, Morris counties) is significantly more expensive than South Jersey.

Is a burial vault required in New Jersey?

While New Jersey has no state law requiring vaults, virtually all cemeteries in the state mandate a vault or concrete grave liner. The state's clay-heavy soil and high water tables make vaults particularly important. Some natural burial grounds, such as Steelmantown Cemetery, waive this requirement.

What are the cheapest burial options in New Jersey?

Direct burial is the most affordable option, costing $3,000-$5,000 for the funeral home portion. Choosing a South Jersey cemetery, purchasing a casket online, and declining embalming can bring total costs to $10,000-$14,000. Green burial at Steelmantown Cemetery or another natural burial site is another cost-effective choice. Eligible veterans receive free burial at the Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery.

Why is burial so expensive in New Jersey?

New Jersey's high burial costs stem from land scarcity (the most densely populated state), high cost of living, urban real estate pressure on cemetery land values, and corporate funeral home consolidation. North Jersey's proximity to New York City further inflates prices. The combination of limited cemetery space and strong demand pushes costs well above the national average.

Are there options for Jewish burial in New Jersey?

Yes. New Jersey has an extensive network of Jewish cemeteries serving Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, and secular communities. Jewish burial societies (chevra kadisha) assist with traditional preparation. Jewish burial tradition uses simple all-wood caskets without metal, which are actually less expensive than standard caskets, typically $500-$1,500.
