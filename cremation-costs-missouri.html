Cremation Costs in Missouri (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 52%

Missouri's 52% cremation rate puts it right on the national median — a Show-Me State halfway point between Midwestern traditionalism and urban cost-consciousness. St. Louis and Kansas City metros drive most of the cremation volume, with Baue Funeral Homes, Heartland Cremation, and D.W. Newcomer's dominant names. This guide breaks down what families actually pay across Missouri in 2026, and where the Kansas border quietly changes the math. Cost ranges on this page are drawn from the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report, which recorded a U.S. cremation rate of roughly 60.5 percent — the benchmark against which Missouri's state rate is compared below. Pricing rules follow the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and licensing in Missouri is administered by the Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors.

Kansas City state-line arbitrage, and why it matters

The single most practical money-saving maneuver available to Kansas City-area Missouri families has nothing to do with negotiating. It's geography. Cremation providers on the Kansas side of State Line Road — in Johnson and Wyandotte counties — regularly post direct cremation prices $200-$600 below equivalent Missouri operators. Missouri law permits a Kansas funeral home to handle a Missouri decedent provided the Missouri death certificate and cremation authorization are filed correctly, and a crematory on the Kansas side simply needs the transit permit. Heartland Cremation, based in KC, operates on both sides of the border and advertises a unified price. Worth shopping.

Inside the Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors

Missouri's regulator — the State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors — handles crematory licensure, pre-need trust audits, and complaint investigations out of Jefferson City. Three things in Missouri's rulebook (RSMo Chapter 333) that families should know going in:

  • 24-hour minimum hold. No cremation until at least 24 hours have elapsed since death, regardless of medical examiner sign-off. Some counties extend this for drug-related deaths.
  • Separate crematory authorization. The cremation authorization form is distinct from the funeral arrangement contract. Missouri families have successfully disputed charges by noting they never signed the specific cremation form.
  • 80% pre-need trust requirement. If you pre-pay, 80% must be trusted with an approved institution. Ask for the trust account number and institution name in writing — the board requires providers to disclose this.

Assistance and veteran options Missouri families miss

Missouri HealthNet burial allowance. The state Medicaid program (MO HealthNet) recognizes a burial reserve of up to $1,500 as a non-countable asset for Medicaid-eligible beneficiaries. Families planning pre-need for an aging parent on Medicaid can set aside up to $1,500 in a qualifying burial trust without jeopardizing benefits — enough to cover direct cremation in full at most Missouri providers.

Veterans buried at Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery (south St. Louis) qualify for free interment of cremated remains, a government marker, and perpetual care — a benefit worth roughly $2,000-$3,500 in Missouri market terms. Missouri also maintains state veterans cemeteries in Springfield, Higginsville, Bloomfield, and Fort Leonard Wood. Eligibility extends to the veteran's spouse.

Three provider types Missouri families encounter

Legacy full-service homes — Baue in St. Louis, D.W. Newcomer's in KC — quote the highest direct cremation prices (commonly $2,000-$2,400) but include embalming credit toward a traditional service if family plans change. Dedicated cremation providers like Heartland Cremation and Cremation Society of Missouri operate high-volume retorts and sit at the low end ($895-$1,295 in metro markets). Rural family-owned homes in Ozark and Bootheel counties often transport to a third-party crematory 50+ miles away, adding mileage that isn't always itemized. Ask who owns the retort.

Where Missouri prices actually land, by metro

  • St. Louis metro: Direct cremation $1,195-$2,200. Deepest provider roster in the state; highest pricing spread.
  • Kansas City (Missouri side): $1,395-$2,100. Shop the Kansas side for $200-$600 savings.
  • Springfield / Joplin: $1,495-$1,895. Fewer players, tighter spread.
  • Columbia / Jefferson City: $1,595-$1,995. University Hospital's MO Anatomical Board donation program is an alternative path.
  • Rural Missouri: $1,795-$2,400+. Transport distance to retort is the variable. Ask.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical price of cremation in Missouri?

Missouri cremation pricing lives in two tiers: direct cremation — transport, cremation, and return of ashes — starting around $1,600, and full-service cremation with a ceremony landing near $5,500. Urns ($50-$3,000), memorial venues ($500-$2,000), and scattering or inurnment fees ($200-$600) are separate line items most providers will quote on request. Source: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; CANA 2024 Annual Report; Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

What types of cremation are available in Missouri?

Families in Missouri can choose from three main types: direct cremation ($1,600) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service (ceremony held after cremation, often more flexible on timing); and traditional cremation ($5,500) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each option can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget. Source: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; CANA 2024 Annual Report; Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

Does Missouri have specific rules for cremation?

Yes. Missouri requires written authorization from the legal next of kin, a standard 24-48 hour waiting period, and in some counties a permit from the medical examiner before cremation can proceed. Pacemakers and certain implants must be removed first. The FTC Funeral Rule (federal) separately guarantees you do not need a casket for cremation — a basic cremation container suffices. Source: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; CANA 2024 Annual Report; Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

Why is cremation growing in Missouri?

Missouri's 52% reflects three pressures: affordability, flexibility on the memorial timeline, and a slow erosion of the "burial by default" expectation. The gap between direct cremation and full-service burial can easily clear five figures, which is usually the largest single factor. Source: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; CANA 2024 Annual Report; Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

How quickly can cremation be completed in Missouri?

From start to ashes, most cremations in Missouri take 4 to 10 days. The cremation itself is 2-3 hours, but the mandatory 24-48 hour pre-cremation waiting period and the 3-7 business day processing window dominate the timeline. Direct cremation at $1,600 moves fastest since there is no viewing or service to schedule beforehand. Source: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; CANA 2024 Annual Report; Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Missouri?

Yes. Missouri funeral homes regularly arrange visitation and a ceremony before cremation takes place — the full-service option typically runs around $5,500. If the ceremony timing is flexible, a memorial service after cremation (at any venue, anytime) is another option and usually costs less than the full traditional route. Source: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; CANA 2024 Annual Report; Missouri State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).
