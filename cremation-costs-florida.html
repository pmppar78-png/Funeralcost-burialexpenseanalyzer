Cremation Costs in Florida (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 62%

Florida runs one of the country's largest cremation markets by volume, and it shows in the price. Direct cremation averages $1,800 statewide — aggressive pricing comes from Neptune Society's dominant share, Anderson-McQueen in St. Petersburg, and the National Cremation Society, all of which built out the high-throughput infrastructure that keeps unit costs low. Full-service cremation averages $6,200. At 62%, Florida's cremation rate is well above the national midpoint, and the Florida DFS Division of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services enforces several requirements — a statutory 48-hour hold before cremation and a 50% trust requirement on pre-need contracts — that you will not see in most states.

Florida's $4,400 spread between direct cremation and full-service is one of the largest in the Southeast. That is not accidental: Neptune Society and National Cremation Society compete hard on the direct cremation floor while traditional funeral homes like Anderson-McQueen maintain premium pricing for families wanting a chapel service.

Florida's 48-hour hold and why it matters financially

Florida Statute 497 requires a minimum 48-hour hold between the time of death and cremation — one of the longer statutory waits in the country. For families hoping to move quickly for out-of-state relatives, this is a hard floor. Financially, the 48 hours means your funeral provider has to refrigerate or otherwise preserve the body for longer than they would in, say, Texas. Most Florida providers fold that refrigeration cost into the direct cremation base price, but some itemize it separately — ask. Counties with heavy snowbird populations (Pinellas, Sarasota, Lee, Collier) occasionally run into medical examiner backlogs that extend the real-world timeline to 4-6 days.

Florida's 50% pre-need trust requirement

If you are pre-paying for cremation in Florida, state law requires the funeral provider to deposit at least 50% of the pre-need funds into a state-regulated trust, and the trust earnings flow back to your contract. This is consumer protection against the provider going out of business before the service is needed — a real risk in a consolidating industry. Some providers advertise 100% trust deposits as a selling point; that is above the statutory minimum and genuinely better. The Florida DFS Division of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services publishes a searchable database of licensed pre-need sellers at myfloridacfo.com — worth checking before signing anything.

Why Neptune Society dominates Florida cremation

Neptune Society operates more Florida locations than any other provider and handles a disproportionate share of the state's direct cremation volume. The model is straightforward: standardized direct cremation packages, high-throughput retorts, membership-style pre-need sales aimed at the retiree market. Pricing starts below the statewide $1,800 average in most markets. The tradeoff is limited ceremony options — Neptune is not a traditional funeral home. Families wanting viewing, a chapel, or clergy coordination typically use Anderson-McQueen (Tampa Bay), SCI-owned Dignity Memorial brands, or one of the remaining independent full-service operators.

Scattering at sea off Florida's coasts

  • Atlantic scattering charters operate out of Fort Lauderdale, Miami, Jupiter, and Cocoa Beach; typical price $300-$800 for a charter that travels the EPA-required 3 nautical miles offshore.
  • Gulf scattering runs out of Clearwater, St. Pete Beach, Fort Myers, and Naples. Winter pricing is lower; hurricane season complicates scheduling.
  • Florida Keys reef scattering through Eternal Reefs or similar programs creates a structural marine habitat with cremated remains; prices range $3,500-$7,500 and include an on-site ceremony.
  • Intracoastal scattering in certain Florida waters requires a letter to the EPA within 30 days of scattering.

Florida DCF assistance for indigent cremation

The Florida Department of Children and Families administers the state's indigent cremation benefit for decedents whose estates and families cannot pay. County-level programs (Miami-Dade, Broward, Hillsborough, Orange, Duval) run alongside it with their own thresholds and contracting funeral homes. The benefit is modest — low four figures at most — and it reimburses the provider, not the family directly.

Urn purchase tip: Florida follows the FTC Funeral Rule. Neptune, Anderson-McQueen, National Cremation Society and every other Florida provider must accept an urn you buy elsewhere without charging a handling fee.

What Families Want to Know

What is the typical price of cremation in Florida?

Florida's direct cremation average is $1,800, but Neptune Society, National Cremation Society and a handful of independents routinely price below that in Tampa, Orlando and Miami. Full-service cremation at Anderson-McQueen, Dignity Memorial locations, or traditional independents averages $6,200. Scattering-at-sea charters add $300-$800; Eternal Reefs memorial placements range $3,500-$7,500.

What types of cremation are available in Florida?

Three common paths. Direct cremation ($1,800) is how the majority of Florida's 62% cremation decisions are handled. Cremation with a later memorial is popular for families scattered across the country, letting a Florida snowbird's ashes be held for a spring service up north. Full-service cremation ($6,200) includes viewing, chapel service, and ceremonial transport to the crematory; many traditional Florida funeral homes still structure this as their default offering.

Does Florida have specific rules for cremation?

Florida Statute 497 requires a 48-hour minimum hold between death and cremation — longer than most states. The Florida DFS Division of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services licenses establishments and requires signed authorization from the legal next of kin plus medical examiner signoff for any unattended death. Pre-need cremation contracts have a 50% trust deposit requirement protecting consumers if the provider fails. Implanted medical devices must be removed.

How quickly can cremation be completed in Florida?

The statutory 48-hour hold sets the floor. Most Florida direct cremations return ashes within 5-10 business days, depending on how quickly the death certificate clears and whether the medical examiner is involved. High-volume operators in Pinellas and Miami-Dade occasionally run into capacity constraints during winter snowbird season, extending the timeline to two weeks.

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Florida?

Yes — that is exactly what the $6,200 full-service cremation covers. Anderson-McQueen, SCI-owned Dignity Memorial chapels, and most Florida funeral homes offer viewing, service, and procession. Families wanting the ceremony but less cost often arrange direct cremation with Neptune or National Cremation Society and then hold a memorial later at a parish, a beach, or a rental venue — typically saving $3,000+.
