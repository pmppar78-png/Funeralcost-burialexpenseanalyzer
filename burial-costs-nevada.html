Burial Costs in Nevada (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: West

Nevada has one of the country's highest cremation rates — roughly 72% of dispositions statewide — and that single statistic shapes everything about burial pricing here. Cemeteries in Las Vegas and Reno operate in a market where their primary customers have already largely defected, which forces commercial memorial parks to lean on premium packages for the minority who still choose burial. Across the state the result is a $15,220 to $21,420 burial average, but the spread between the aggressive providers and the bundled-package providers is wider than almost anywhere else in the West.

Adding the full column — $8,000 service + $3,800 plot + $2,400 casket + $1,520 vault + $1,900 opening-and-closing + $1,500 marker — the Nevada total lands between $15,220 and $21,420. Embalming ($800) is not required under Nevada law when burial occurs within the statutory window, though funeral homes often recommend it if there will be a public viewing.

Las Vegas, Reno, and the In-Between

Clark County runs the bulk of Nevada burials — Palm Eastern Mortuary & Cemetery on Eastern Avenue, Davis Funeral Home & Memorial Park on Eastern and on Rancho, and Bunkers Memory Gardens off North Las Vegas Boulevard are the dominant commercial names. The Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City handles most eligible-veteran burials in the south and is no-cost for veterans. In Washoe County, Mountain View Cemetery on Stoker Avenue (Reno's oldest, dating to 1875) and the Northern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Fernley cover the Reno-Sparks area. Between the metros the rural county cemeteries in Elko, Ely, and Tonopah price well below the Las Vegas commercial rate, and small-town funeral homes in places like Pahrump and Winnemucca often take rural Clark and Washoe overflow.

The State's Regulatory Layer

The Nevada Funeral and Cemetery Services Board licenses funeral directors, embalmers, crematories, cemeteries, and pre-need sellers out of Carson City — one of the few states where cemeteries and funeral homes share a single regulator. Pre-need contracts require 75% trust placement (or an equivalent insurance product) under Nevada law. The FTC Funeral Rule applies to every Nevada funeral home: General Price List disclosure at the start of any in-person price discussion, and outside caskets must be accepted at no handling fee. Because Nevada regulates cemeteries as well, you can ask the Board directly for the disciplinary history of any licensed cemetery company — a consumer-protection lever most states do not offer.

Assistance and Indigent Burial

Clark County operates one of the more defined indigent-burial programs in the West — the Clark County Office of the Coroner handles unclaimed remains, and the Clark County Social Service agency has limited emergency funeral assistance for residents meeting low-income thresholds. Washoe County runs a parallel program through its Human Services Agency. Nevada does not administer a statewide burial assistance fund, but the two state veterans cemeteries (Boulder City and Fernley) plus the nearby Dayton VA section cover eligible veterans at no cost. TANF-eligible families may also access limited emergency assistance through the Division of Welfare and Supportive Services.

What Readers Typically Ask

How much does burial cost in Nevada?

A conventional Nevada burial totals $15,220 to $21,420 once the $8,000 service, $3,800 plot, $2,400 casket, $1,520 vault, $1,900 opening-and-closing, and a $1,000-$3,000 marker are added. Las Vegas commercial cemeteries anchor the top of that range; rural county cemeteries and green-burial sections inside Las Vegas and Reno cemeteries anchor the bottom.

Do I need a vault for burial in Nevada?

Nevada law does not require a vault, but Palm Eastern, Davis Memorial Park, Bunkers, Mountain View in Reno, and essentially every other commercial Nevada cemetery require either a sealed vault or a concrete liner. The liner is the cheaper substitute — often by $800 or more. Confirm the specific cemetery's written rule before the plot is purchased.

How do I compare cemetery costs in Nevada?

Nevada is unusual in that the Nevada Funeral and Cemetery Services Board regulates cemeteries and funeral homes under one license — you can request disciplinary records on any licensed cemetery company directly. Ask each cemetery in writing for the plot, opening-and-closing, vault-or-liner rule, perpetual care, and any residency restriction. Also ask specifically whether they have a green burial section — these often run 30-50% below the traditional sections of the same cemetery but are rarely marketed prominently.

Once I add every line item, what does burial actually cost in Nevada?

Full arithmetic: service ($8,000) + plot ($3,800) + casket ($2,400) + vault ($1,520) + opening-and-closing ($1,900) + marker ($1,000-$3,000) = $15,220 to $21,420. The Nevada cremation rate of 72% gives burial-focused providers real pricing flexibility — getting a direct-cremation quote first often surfaces a better burial number than starting the conversation with burial.
