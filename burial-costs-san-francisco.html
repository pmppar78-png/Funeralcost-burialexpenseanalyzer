Burial Costs in San Francisco, California (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: California

There is no active burial in San Francisco proper. The city passed an ordinance in 1900 banning new interments inside municipal limits and, in 1902, effectively closed existing cemeteries — the remains were exhumed and moved south to a two-square-mile town founded for the purpose: Colma. When a San Francisco family arranges a burial, the grave is almost certainly in Colma's "City of the Dead," which holds more than 1.5 million interred residents against roughly 1,500 living ones, and cemetery pricing reflects that monopoly.

Full-service traditional burial for a San Francisco family — with the cemetery in Colma — generally lands between $27,648 and $38,961. The figure is higher than any other California city on this site, and the single biggest driver is not the funeral home: it is the plot.

Why Colma holds the price-setting power for SF burials

Because San Francisco has no active cemetery within its boundaries, Colma functions as a closed market for the city's burial needs. Cypress Lawn (established 1892), Italian Cemetery, Olivet Memorial Park, Holy Cross Catholic, Home of Peace, Hills of Eternity, and Woodlawn all operate within that tiny footprint. Competition exists, but only between those operators, and all of them carry the overhead of being the sole option for a wealthy urban customer base. The result is a plot market that prices 30 to 50 percent above the broader California average and roughly twice what a family would pay for a comparable grave in Sacramento or Fresno.

The cemetery-plus-mortuary arithmetic in San Francisco

  • Traditional burial — Funeral home in SF or Daly City, burial in Colma. Most common pairing.
  • Direct burial — Allowed under California's 48-hour refrigeration rule without embalming. Strips roughly $3,000 to $5,000 off the typical invoice.
  • Green or natural burial — Fernwood Cemetery in Mill Valley is the nearest certified natural burial ground; Cypress Lawn also maintains a limited natural section. Learn about green burial
  • Mausoleum entombment — Cypress Lawn and Italian Cemetery maintain substantial mausoleums; entombment eliminates the vault line item but rarely reduces the total.
  • National cemetery — San Francisco National Cemetery at the Presidio was closed to new interments in 1991. Eligible veterans now use San Joaquin Valley National Cemetery near Gustine or Sacramento Valley.
The 48-hour refrigeration rule: California does not require embalming. If the mortuary has refrigeration — nearly all San Francisco providers do — a family can legally proceed with a closed-casket ceremony, private viewing, or direct burial and decline the $1,431 embalming charge. The California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau handles complaints when providers misrepresent this.

Practical ways to cut a San Francisco burial bill

  • Get the Colma cemetery quote before the SF funeral home quote. The plot is the line item with the widest spread — Olivet is significantly cheaper than Cypress Lawn for an equivalent single grave.
  • Use a Daly City or South San Francisco funeral home. Providers outside the 415 area code often come in several thousand dollars under an identically itemized quote from a Pacific Heights or Mission District mortuary.
  • Skip embalming under California's refrigeration rule. A $1,431 line item removed with no legal risk.
  • Ask for a liner rather than a vault. Several Colma cemeteries accept a concrete grave liner at roughly a third of vault cost.
  • Purchase the casket from a third party. The FTC Funeral Rule forbids any handling surcharge. Casket buying guide | Best online casket retailers
  • San Francisco Medical Examiner's Cemetery Services Division. The city arranges indigent-burial cremation and interment for qualifying decedents whose families cannot pay; eligibility runs through the Medical Examiner's office.
  • Order the monument independently. Headstone cost guide

Regional context: the Bay Area burial market

The 1902 ordinance still dictates the geography of Bay Area burial more than a century later, and anyone shopping for a plot near the city will find themselves driving south on El Camino Real to Colma. Families willing to cross the bridges — to Oakland's Mountain View Cemetery, St. Mary's in San Rafael, or Mill Valley's Fernwood — can sometimes reduce the cemetery portion of the bill by thousands. Cremation, now chosen by roughly two-thirds of California families, remains the practical response to San Francisco's unique burial economics; a direct cremation with a Golden Gate scattering can be completed for under $1,500.

What Families Want to Know

How much does burial cost in San Francisco?

A traditional burial in San Francisco costs approximately $14,310 for the funeral service plus $7,020 for a cemetery plot. Including vault ($2,808), opening/closing fees ($3,510), casket ($4,293), and headstone ($1,500-$3,000), the total ranges from $27,648 to $38,961.

Is a burial vault required in San Francisco?

California has no state-level vault requirement. The requirement comes from the Colma cemeteries themselves — Cypress Lawn, Holy Cross, Olivet, Italian, Home of Peace and the rest each enforce their own policy, mostly to stabilize ground around grounds-crew equipment. A concrete grave liner is accepted in place of a full vault by several of them and runs roughly 60% less. Confirm with the specific cemetery before accepting a mortuary's vault price.

What are the cheapest burial options in San Francisco?

Direct burial at Olivet Memorial Park in Colma, with a third-party casket and no embalming under California's 48-hour refrigeration rule, typically comes in the lowest for a conventional burial. Fernwood Cemetery in Mill Valley offers natural burial at roughly half the cost of a Cypress Lawn traditional plot. For families without means, the San Francisco Medical Examiner's Cemetery Services Division handles indigent cases. Eligible veterans can use San Joaquin Valley National Cemetery near Gustine at no charge.

How do San Francisco burial costs compare to California?

San Francisco is the most expensive California burial market covered here, running about 35% above the statewide average. The funeral service averages $14,310 against $10,600 statewide, and the cemetery plot $7,020 against $5,200. The premium is almost entirely a Colma effect — no active cemetery has operated inside San Francisco city limits since the 1902 ordinance, which concentrated all burial demand onto the dense cluster of cemeteries a few miles south.
