Funeral Costs in Fort Worth, Texas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Texas

Fort Worth sits in one of the most pricing-diverse metro funeral markets in America. DFW spans two distinct pricing cultures — Fort Worth's older establishment chapels (Greenwood, Robertson Mueller Harper, Mount Olivet) and the glass-and-steel Dallas suburbs where SCI and the Dignity Memorial brand dominate. Same metro, same 48% statewide cremation rate, very different price sheets. Traditional services at Mount Olivet can run 20% under a comparable package at a Park Cities Dignity home just thirty miles east.

DFW Pricing Split: Fort Worth vs. Dallas on Identical Services

The Trinity River functions as an informal price boundary. A traditional service at Greenwood or Mount Olivet west of the river typically quotes $7,500–$8,400; the same package at a Dignity Memorial chapel in Park Cities or North Dallas hits $9,000–$10,500. Robertson Mueller Harper's Alta Mere and Hulen locations split the difference. For families willing to cross the metro, pulling GPLs from both sides can uncover $1,500+ in savings on comparable services.

Your Rights in Fort Worth

FTC Funeral Rule Protection Every funeral home in Fort Worth must provide an itemized General Price List, let you choose only the services you want, and accept caskets purchased elsewhere. You are never required to buy a package. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

Mount Olivet and the North Texas Historic-Cemetery Premium

Mount Olivet Cemetery on East Lancaster has been operating since 1907 and remains one of North Texas's most requested burial grounds. Plot pricing there runs $4,800–$7,200 depending on section and proximity to the mausoleum — well above the $3,740 Fort Worth average. Greenwood Memorial Park and Laurel Land tend to quote similarly. Catholic Calvary Cemetery in East Fort Worth comes in measurably lower at $2,800–$4,200, and municipal Oakwood Cemetery lower still. Check plot pricing directly with the cemetery, not the funeral home.

Tarrant County Indigent Burial Assistance

Under Texas Health and Safety Code Section 694, Tarrant County Commissioners Court contracts with local providers for indigent dispositions — typically direct cremation at a fixed rate. The qualifying threshold is strict: the decedent must have an insolvent estate, no responsible next of kin willing to pay, and no pre-need contract. Families should contact the Tarrant County Medical Examiner's Office for the application. DFW Dallas-Fort Worth National Cemetery offers free burial to eligible veterans.

Hidden Fees to Watch For in Fort Worth

Many families in Fort Worth encounter unexpected charges. Watch for these common hidden fees:

  • Casket handling fees — Illegal under the FTC Funeral Rule, but some providers still add them
  • Mandatory embalming claims — Texas does not require embalming in most circumstances. Know your consumer rights
  • Package markups — Bundles often include unnecessary services. Compare line items. What funeral homes don't tell you
  • Weekend/holiday surcharges — Can add 15–25% to the total cost
  • Cross-metro transport fees — If using a Dallas-side provider for a Fort Worth service, mileage can add $150–$350

Frequently Asked Questions

What are funeral costs in the Fort Worth area?

Fort Worth's 2026 average for a traditional service is $7,920 — roughly 10% above Texas's $7,200 statewide figure, though about 4% under Dallas's $8,280. Direct cremation at Greenwood, Robertson Mueller Harper, or Mount Olivet clears around $1,870; cremation with a service runs $6,380; and a plot at Mount Olivet sits around $3,740. Premium cemetery sections at Mount Olivet can push past $7,200.

Is cremation or burial more common in Fort Worth?

Burial still leads in Fort Worth. The city tracks the Texas statewide 48%/52% cremation-to-burial split, with the older Anglo-Protestant and Latino Catholic traditions keeping traditional services more common than in Austin. That said, direct cremation at $1,870 is saving working-class Tarrant County families thousands versus the roughly $11,000 full burial.

Is Fort Worth more or less expensive than the US average for funerals?

Fort Worth sits about 1% above the $7,848 U.S. median. Lower than Dallas, Houston, and Austin, but notably above El Paso and San Antonio. The east-of-the-Trinity Dignity Memorial premium pulls metro averages up; staying west in Fort Worth proper keeps costs closer to the Texas average.

What is the best way to compare Fort Worth funeral homes?

Request written GPLs from Greenwood, Robertson Mueller Harper, and Mount Olivet — all three are Fort Worth institutions with significant market share. Add one direct-cremation specialist and, if you're willing to cross the Trinity, one Dallas-side Dignity Memorial chapel. The Texas Funeral Service Commission will confirm current licensing status; the FTC Funeral Rule requires every provider to hand over the GPL on request.

What is direct cremation in Fort Worth and how much does it cost?

Direct cremation in Fort Worth typically costs $1,870 — Greenwood and Robertson Mueller Harper both offer simple-cremation tiers beginning in the $1,700–$1,900 range, while independent Tarrant County cremation specialists on the west side sometimes quote as low as $1,450. The price covers transport, cremation, basic container, and return of remains. No embalming is required under Texas law.

What are the cheapest funeral choices in Fort Worth?

Direct cremation at $1,870 sets the bottom of the range. Tarrant County's indigent burial contract under H&S Code Section 694 covers qualifying low-income decedents. Direct burial in Oakwood or municipal cemeteries avoids most funeral-home service fees. DFW National Cemetery provides free burial for eligible veterans. Online caskets undercut funeral-home inventory by 50–70%.
