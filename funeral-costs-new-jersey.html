What's Specific to New Jersey (Northeast)

Winter timing affects Northeastern funerals more than most families expect — cemetery interment can be delayed in New Jersey between December and March, which sometimes shifts the cost structure toward cremation with later interment or memorial.

Payment Assistance in New Jersey

  • Relatives in New Jersey can put up to $1,570 of their own money toward the funeral without reducing the General Assistance allowance, so family help and public help can be combined.
  • The $255 Social Security death payment goes only to an eligible surviving spouse or child; in New Jersey, as elsewhere, they apply by calling 1-800-772-1213 within two years of the death.
  • For a New Jersey veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. What to Do When Planning a Funeral in New Jersey

For anyone planning a funeral in New Jersey now or soon, these steps — in roughly this order — prevent the most common expensive mistakes:

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in New Jersey or burial in New Jersey.

Funeral rules and help in New Jersey

Funeral homes in New Jersey are licensed by the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs (973-504-6425). That office takes New Jersey complaints about pricing, misleading sales, or a home that will not hand over its price list.

New Jersey is one of nine states where a licensed funeral director must be involved in some steps: filing the death certificate, moving the body, and being present at the final disposition (N.J. Stat. § 26:6, as summarized by Funeral Consumers Alliance). A New Jersey family can still do the rest of a funeral itself, such as washing and dressing the body or holding the service at home.

Cremation timing: New Jersey requires a 24-hour wait before cremation (N.J.S.A. 26:7-18.1).

New Jersey WorkFirst/General Assistance can pay up to $3,056 for the funeral and $714 for the cemetery ($3,770 total); family contributions up to $1,570 do not reduce it (N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5). (Source: N.J.A.C. 10:90-8.5.)

A New Jersey funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Finn's Point National Cemetery (215-222-7510, cremated remains only) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemetery, Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery (609-738-2400), takes requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in New Jersey

  1. Get written price lists from at least two New Jersey funeral homes before you agree to anything.
  2. Decide early whether you want embalming and a viewing in New Jersey; skipping them is often the largest saving.
  3. Contact the New Jersey county welfare agency before the funeral; the funeral home and cemetery each file their own claim.
  4. For a veteran, price Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery against a private New Jersey plot before choosing.
  5. Keep the board's number, 973-504-6425, in case a funeral home will not honor its price list.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in New Jersey?

The only reliable way to pin down your number is to pull General Price Lists from two or three Newark-area providers and compare line by line.

Does cremation save money compared with burial in New Jersey?

A direct cremation in New Jersey runs about $2,400, whereas a traditional funeral with burial averages $10,200 before adding the plot — so the gap can easily exceed $7,800. New Jersey's cremation rate of 48% sits below the 63.4% national rate NFDA projected for 2025.

What does the FTC Funeral Rule mean for me in New Jersey?

The federal Rule gives you an itemized General Price List, prices by phone and the right to buy only what you choose. New Jersey writes much of the same into its own rules: funeral homes must give you a general price list, a casket price list and an outer burial container price list (N.J.A.C. 13:36-9.7), and a signed Statement of Funeral Goods and Services Selected that itemizes each charge (N.J.A.C. 13:36-1.9).

For a prepaid funeral, New Jersey requires the money to go into a funeral trust or a funeral insurance policy, and the price lists and statement must be given at the time of the sale (N.J.A.C. 13:36-11.2).

How can I save on funeral costs in New Jersey?

Start by lining up General Price Lists from two or three Newark-area providers before you commit to anything. Request frozen-ground pricing separately if the death is between December and March — New Jersey cemeteries often charge extra in winter. Municipal, Catholic, and Jewish cemeteries in New Jersey generally price below private memorial parks by $1,000 or more. Direct cremation at $2,400 remains the lowest-cost disposition in every New Jersey market.

Is embalming legally required in New Jersey?

Not in New Jersey, as long as the body is kept cold. N.J.A.C. 8:9-1.1 does not allow a body to remain unburied or uncremated in New Jersey longer than 48 hours unless it is embalmed or kept at 45°F or below. It also bars bringing an unembalmed body into the state more than 48 hours after death, except under an emergency waiver from the Commissioner of Health.

For an in-state funeral with refrigeration, the roughly $1,020 that embalming adds on our New Jersey estimate is a choice, and a disputed charge can be raised with the State Board of Mortuary Science through the Division of Consumer Affairs (973-504-6425).

Where does New Jersey rank nationally for funeral costs?

New Jersey's $10,200 average for a traditional funeral puts it 23% above the $8,300 national median. Pricing in New Jersey is above the national average due to higher costs of living, real estate, and labor in the region, and the Northeast region as a whole tends to price higher than other parts of the US.

What is the cheapest funeral option in New Jersey?

Direct cremation is the lowest-cost option in New Jersey, starting at roughly $2,400. Direct burial comes next at around $5,100 and still avoids viewing, ceremony, and embalming, though the $5,000 New Jersey plot estimate still applies.

Where do I look for low-cost funeral providers in New Jersey?

Start with GPL requests from at least three New Jersey funeral homes — that alone exposes the outliers. Reach out to the Funeral Consumers Alliance for New Jersey for member-run lists, shortlist dedicated direct cremation providers, ask every funeral home about their simplest no-frills package, and see if a nonprofit or cooperative provider serves your area of New Jersey.
