Funeral Costs in New Jersey: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: Northeast

New Jersey sits in the densest funeral-home market in the country, with Ippolito-Stellato in Lyndhurst, M John Scanlan in Pompton Plains, Santangelo in Hoboken, and Preferred Cremation of New Jersey in Totowa serving Bergen, Hudson, Essex, and Passaic counties inside a 20-mile ring. The $10,200 traditional average puts New Jersey 30% above the $7,848 national median — a gap driven by Manhattan-adjacent real estate, the New Jersey Cemetery Board's regulated cemetery pricing, and a 48% cremation rate that still lags the national curve by a dozen points. Direct cremation through Preferred Cremation of NJ, Cremation Society of New Jersey, or Neptune in Clifton starts at $2,400, but the spread between providers inside the same zip code can reach $1,800.

The New Jersey Cemetery Board and Regulated Plot Pricing

New Jersey is one of a handful of states with a dedicated cemetery regulator separate from the funeral board: the New Jersey Cemetery Board, established under the New Jersey Cemetery Act of 1971 (N.J.S.A. 45:27) and housed within the Division of Consumer Affairs. Every non-religious cemetery in the state — Rosedale in Linden, George Washington Memorial Park in Paramus, Hollywood Memorial Park in Union, Laurel Grove in Totowa — must file price schedules with the Board and cannot raise rates without notice. That caps some of the worst gouging, but it also means the published schedule is the schedule: there is little room to negotiate a single plot down from $3,000–$9,000. Catholic cemeteries run by the Archdiocese of Newark (Holy Name in Jersey City, Holy Cross in North Arlington, Holy Sepulchre in East Orange) set their own pricing separately and typically run $1,200–$2,500 below the regulated market for Catholic decedents. Jewish cemeteries — Mount Lebanon in Iselin, Beth David in Kenilworth, King Solomon in Clifton — similarly operate under religious-exemption pricing that requires congregational affiliation.

The Preferred Cremation and Neptune Price War in North Jersey

The 48% cremation rate in New Jersey, low by national standards, masks a price war in the Meadowlands and Route 46 corridor. Preferred Cremation of New Jersey in Totowa, Cremation Society of New Jersey in East Windsor, Neptune Society in Clifton, and Greenlawn Cremation in Wall have pushed direct-cremation quotes from $2,400 down to $1,695–$1,995 in Bergen and Passaic counties, with the $1,695 floor now advertised by multiple providers. Full-service funeral homes like Ippolito-Stellato, M John Scanlan, and Santangelo still quote $2,400–$2,800 because their non-declinable basic services fee runs $2,400–$3,100 — the same overhead that makes their traditional casketed funerals among the highest in the country. If cremation is the choice, asking a traditional home to price-match a direct-cremation specialist is rarely effective; the structural cost difference is real.

New Jersey State Board of Mortuary Science and the 48-Hour Rule

The New Jersey State Board of Mortuary Science (njconsumeraffairs.gov/mort) licenses every funeral director and "mortuary establishment" operating in the state, and it enforces one of the stricter embalming and refrigeration rules in the country under N.J.A.C. 13:36. Unembalmed remains must be refrigerated at 40°F or below within a limited window if final disposition is not scheduled promptly, and several New Jersey counties impose local variations on top of state rules. New Jersey also requires a 24-hour minimum before cremation, with a 48-hour wait in practice at most crematories to allow for medical examiner review in Monmouth, Ocean, and Hudson counties where overdose-related deaths trigger additional screening. Preneed contracts in New Jersey are regulated under N.J.S.A. 45:27-17, with 100% trust requirement on merchandise and services; consumer complaints go through the Board or the Division of Consumer Affairs.

New Jersey Funeral Assistance — What Counties and State Actually Pay

New Jersey does not run a standalone state funeral program, but assistance is layered through county welfare offices, Medicaid, and veteran benefits. The New Jersey Funeral Assistance Program administered by county welfare agencies pays up to $1,570 toward a funeral and $524 toward a cemetery for residents on SSI, WorkFirst NJ General Assistance, or Medicaid with Kinship Legal Guardianship — levels lawmakers last adjusted in the early 2000s and still meaningfully below the cost of the cheapest direct cremation in most counties. Medicaid permits an irrevocable burial contract up to $1,500 plus an exempt burial space. Eligible veterans receive free interment at Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Wrightstown (Burlington County) and at the Gloucester County Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Williamstown, which New Jersey's Department of Military and Veterans Affairs operates independent of the federal Beverly National Cemetery. The Social Security $255 lump sum and VA burial allowance of $893–$2,000 remain the baseline.

Charges most families only see once they sign

Most of the pricing surprises in New Jersey come from a predictable list of add-ons. Look for these specifically when reviewing any funeral home quote:

  • Casket handling fee — Some funeral homes add a surcharge for caskets purchased elsewhere, despite this being illegal under the FTC Funeral Rule
  • Mandatory embalming — Funeral homes may claim embalming is required. In New Jersey, it is almost never legally required. Know your rights
  • Cemetery "perpetual care" fees — An ongoing maintenance charge, typically $200–$1,000, added on top of the plot price
  • Vault/liner surcharge — Cemeteries may require a specific vault brand or type, inflating costs by $500–$2,000+
  • Documentation and filing fees — Death certificates, permits, and filing fees can add $200–$600
  • Weekend/holiday premium — Services held outside business hours may carry a 15–25% surcharge
  • "Package" markups — Bundled packages often include services you don't need. Always compare line-by-line. What funeral homes don't tell you

Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in New Jersey must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in New Jersey has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Your state attorney general's office can also assist with consumer protection complaints.

Cross-River Logistics: The Manhattan and Pennsylvania Transfer Questions

New Jersey's density creates a specific cost problem that families elsewhere never confront: deaths that happen in one jurisdiction and get buried in another, with the Hudson, Lincoln, or Holland Tunnel in between. A New York City hospital death followed by burial at Holy Name in Jersey City triggers interstate transfer paperwork under both New York Public Health Law and New Jersey N.J.A.C. 13:36, typically $600–$1,200 in duplicate filing and livery. Cross-river transfers between Philadelphia hospitals and Camden, Gloucester, or Burlington County cemeteries carry similar fees. Winter interment in northwest New Jersey — Sussex, Warren, and western Morris counties — occasionally stalls between late December and early March, though Atlantic and Cape May cemeteries rarely freeze hard enough to delay burial. Ask any New Jersey funeral home about cross-state transfer fees as a separate line item before the packaged "basic services" number obscures it.

What does the FTC Funeral Rule mean for me in New Jersey?

The FTC Funeral Rule plus N.J.A.C. 13:36 and N.J.S.A. 45:7-32 give New Jersey families the right to an itemized General Price List, the right to decline any service, the right to use an outside casket or urn without surcharge, and protection from any false claim that embalming is legally required. The New Jersey State Board of Mortuary Science at njconsumeraffairs.gov/mort handles licensing complaints, and the New Jersey Cemetery Board separately regulates cemetery pricing under the 1971 Cemetery Act.

How can I save on funeral costs in New Jersey?

Pull General Price Lists from at least three New Jersey providers — ideally one traditional full-service home, one direct-cremation specialist like Preferred Cremation of NJ or Cremation Society of New Jersey, and one religious or cooperative option. Catholic and Jewish cemeteries undercut regulated commercial cemeteries by $1,200–$2,500 for affiliated decedents. Apply for the New Jersey Funeral Assistance Program through your county welfare office (up to $1,570 funeral + $524 cemetery for SSI/WorkFirst/Medicaid eligibles) and confirm VA eligibility for Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Wrightstown or Gloucester County Veterans Memorial in Williamstown.

Where does New Jersey rank nationally for funeral costs?
