What Drives Funeral Costs in Georgia

What you pay for a funeral in Georgia depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in Georgia, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether Georgia state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. South pricing is generally lower than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in Georgia routinely differs by 10-30%.

The state's 45% cremation rate shapes pricing too.

Your Consumer Rights in Georgia

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Georgia must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Georgia has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What's Specific to Georgia (South)

Homegoing services, repast meals, and strong church involvement remain part of Georgia funeral culture, and many congregations will cover or reduce the service-venue portion of the cost for members. When pricing a traditional funeral in Georgia, ask explicitly whether the quote assumes the service at the funeral home versus at a church — the latter often drops the facility fee by $500-$1,500.

Payment Assistance in Georgia

  • Families in Georgia who have no money for a funeral should reach the Georgia county social services office first; signing with a provider before the office approves can cost you the help.
  • To collect the one-time $255 from Social Security, a Georgia widow, widower or eligible child must call 1-800-772-1213, and the claim has to be made within two years.
  • For a Georgia veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. Eligibility rules for Georgia families are in our veteran burial benefits guide.

What to Do When Planning a Funeral in Georgia

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in Georgia or burial in Georgia.

Funeral rules and help in Georgia

The Georgia State Board of Funeral Service licenses funeral directors and establishments in Georgia; call 404-424-9966 to check a license or file a complaint.

Hiring a funeral home is a choice, not a legal requirement, for most steps in Georgia; Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count it among the nine states with funeral-director rules. Georgia paperwork and permits still apply.

If the family chooses cremation, Georgia sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (O.C.G.A. § 31-10-20; Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. r. 511-1-3-.23).

No verified statewide burial benefit is listed here for Georgia. The county social-services or human-services office in Georgia is the place to ask, and our assistance guide explains the federal options. Social Security pays a one-time $255 to a qualifying surviving spouse or child in Georgia, as in every state.

Eligible Georgia veterans get a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. In Georgia, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Georgia National Cemetery (770-479-9300); state veterans cemeteries: Georgia Veterans Memorial Cemetery - Glennville (912-654-5398); Georgia Veterans Memorial Cemetery - Milledgeville (478-445-3363). Source: VA National Cemetery Administration.

Next steps in Georgia

  1. Ask every Georgia provider for its General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule says they must give it to you.
  2. Decide early whether you want embalming and a viewing in Georgia; skipping them is often the largest saving.
  3. Ask your Georgia county human-services office about indigent burial help before you commit.
  4. Veterans' families can schedule through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office; Georgia National Cemetery is listed above.
  5. A complaint call to 404-424-9966 is free if a provider misleads you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in Georgia right now?

Current averages in Georgia come in at $6,900 for a traditional funeral, $5,600 for cremation with a service, and from $1,700 for direct cremation — with the traditional figure sitting 17% below the $8,300 US median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $3,200 when applicable.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Georgia?

In Georgia, direct cremation at $1,700 costs about $5,200 less than the $6,900 traditional funeral, and roughly $8,400 less once about $3,200 in cemetery costs is added.

What rights do Georgia families have when arranging a funeral?

Under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR 453), every Georgia funeral home must give you an itemized General Price List, quote prices by phone, and accept a casket or urn you bought elsewhere without a handling fee. Georgia is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. Complaints about a Georgia funeral home go to the Georgia State Board of Funeral Service (404-424-9966).

How do I keep funeral costs down in Georgia?

Three moves cut the most: compare at least three Georgia General Price Lists side by side before choosing a provider, strip packages down to only the services you actually want, and choose direct cremation ($1,700) if cost is the priority.

Does Georgia require embalming?

Georgia law does not require embalming in most situations.

Is Georgia cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Compare the Georgia figures in the cost table on this page with the national median of $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, or $6,280 with cremation (NFDA 2023). Prices inside Georgia vary by provider, so the General Price Lists you collect matter more than any average.

Which funeral option costs the least in Georgia?

Direct cremation carries the lowest price tag in Georgia, from roughly $1,700. Direct burial is the next step up at about $3,450 and is still considerably cheaper than a traditional service.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Georgia?

Call several Georgia funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Georgia home cannot charge a fee for it.
