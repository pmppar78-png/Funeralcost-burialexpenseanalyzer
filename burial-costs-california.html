How to Reduce Burial Costs in California

Burial costs in California can be managed with smart planning:

  • Look for hybrid or natural burial sections: Several California cemeteries have added green or natural burial sections without advertising them prominently.
  • Consider certified conservation burial grounds: California is one of the more active states for true conservation burial. These grounds protect land in perpetuity and price simply, typically $4,240-$7,420 all-in versus $24,160+ for traditional burial in California.
  • Use the competitive cremation market as leverage: With 65% of California families choosing cremation, burial-focused providers often have pricing flexibility they do not advertise.

Regional Context for Burial in California

Scattering at sea, on private land, and in certain federal wilderness areas is more practical in California than in the inland US, and families in Los Angeles increasingly choose cremation with a natural scattering over traditional burial. When burial is still the choice, California green burial grounds and Jewish and LDS cemeteries tend to offer the most competitive plot pricing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in California?

A traditional burial in California costs approximately $10,600 for the funeral service plus $5,200 for a cemetery plot. When you add a burial vault ($2,080), opening and closing fees ($2,600), casket ($3,180), and headstone ($500-$5,000), the California total typically ranges from $24,160 to $28,660.

Are burial vaults mandatory in California?

California law does not require one, but a cemetery may. The state Cemetery and Funeral Bureau's consumer guide says a cemetery can require an outer burial container to keep the grave from settling, and that you do not have to buy that container from the cemetery, as long as it meets the cemetery's standards for size, shape and material.

The same guide explains the two types. A vault surrounds the casket completely, while a grave liner covers only the top and sides, and neither one stops decomposition. If a California cemetery insists on its own vault, or says a sealed vault preserves the body, you can raise it with the Bureau.

How can I reduce burial costs in California?

Direct (immediate) burial skips embalming, viewing and a ceremony at the funeral home, so it is usually the lowest-cost burial on a California price list. You may supply your own casket in California; the FTC Funeral Rule forbids a handling fee for it. A VA national cemetery burial costs eligible California veterans nothing for the plot, opening and closing, or headstone.

How do I compare cemetery costs in California?

Cemetery costs in California vary significantly even within the same city. In particular, green and hybrid burial sections in California cemeteries often sit 30-50% below traditional plots, and conservation burial grounds are more established here than in most regions.

What is an all-in burial estimate for California?

Adding every line item, burial in California usually totals $24,160 to $28,660: funeral service about $10,600, plot about $5,200, casket about $3,180, vault about $2,080, opening-and-closing about $2,600, and headstone $500-$5,000.

Can I choose a natural or green burial in California?

A growing number of California cemeteries now offer green burial sections (biodegradable container, no embalming, no vault), and the total cost is usually below a traditional burial. If a dedicated green burial ground is not nearby, some families in California use a hybrid approach (traditional plot without embalming).

Burial rules and help in California

Funeral homes handling burials in California are licensed by the California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau (916-574-7870). Cemetery sales in California may fall under a different office, so ask the board who oversees the cemetery you are considering.

Because the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count California among the nine states that require a funeral director, a California family may arrange a burial without one; the cemetery still sets its own rules for the grave, and the burial permit must be in hand.

A burial in California goes ahead once the permit is issued; for cremation, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (Health & Safety Code § 103050).

For a burial the family cannot pay for, a California county steps in under Health & Safety Code § 7104; the county coroner or public administrator decides how and where the body is buried or cremated. (Source: Cal. Health & Safety Code § 7104.)

Eligible California veterans get a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. In California, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Bakersfield National Cemetery (661-867-2250); Los Angeles National Cemetery Annex (310-268-4675, cremated remains only); Miramar National Cemetery (858-658-7360); Riverside National Cemetery (951-653-8417); Sacramento Valley National Cemetery (707-693-2460); San Joaquin Valley National Cemetery (209-854-1040); state and tribal veterans cemeteries: Big Sandy Rancheria Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 559-374-0066); California Central Coast Veterans Cemetery (831-393-9371); Northern California Veterans Cemetery (866-777-4533); Yurok Veterans Cemetery (tribal, 707-482-1350) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps for a burial in California

  1. Price the California cemetery separately: plot, opening and closing, vault or liner, and marker are often sold apart from the funeral home.
  2. Ask the California cemetery for its rules on grave opening times, vaults and markers.
  3. Ask the California public administrator whether a county burial is possible, or only cremation, before buying a plot.
  4. If the person served, ask Riverside National Cemetery what paperwork it needs, usually starting with the DD214 discharge record.
  5. Report a funeral home that misprices a burial to the board at 916-574-7870.
