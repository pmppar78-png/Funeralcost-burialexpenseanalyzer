Cremation Costs in Columbus, Ohio (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Ohio Cremation Rate: 55%

Columbus is the fastest-growing metro in Ohio, and its cremation market reflects that. New direct-cremation specialists — notably Oak Grove Cremation Service in Westerville and Columbus Direct Cremation on the city's east side — have entered the market in the last five years and pushed the effective floor below the $1,870 published figure. Meanwhile, the heritage funeral homes around the Short North, German Village, and Upper Arlington (Schoedinger, Rutherford-Corbin, Moreland) continue to run full-service operations with retort capacity of their own. Franklin County's newly expanded vital-records system handles the paperwork end, and Ohio's crematory inspector program keeps the facilities themselves accountable.

National context: the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA 2024 Annual Report put the U.S. cremation rate near 60.5 percent, and Columbus providers operate under the oversight of the Ohio Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors. Families ordering death certificates typically deal with the Columbus Public Health Vital Statistics office.

How to Save on Cremation in Columbus

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Ohio: Request written General Price Lists. Differences between providers in the same market commonly exceed $500-$1,200 for identical direct cremation services.
  • Price direct cremation as standalone: Ohio funeral homes often bundle direct cremation into broader packages. Ask for the pure direct cremation price — transport, cremation, return of ashes only — to see the true floor.
  • Scattering locations worth researching locally: Private land (with permission), certain national forests with a permit, and inland waterway scattering are typically options in Ohio. Check local ordinances before choosing a specific location.
  • Buy urns independently: Save 50-80% versus funeral home urn pricing. The FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to use any urn or container. Urn buying guide
  • Hold a memorial separately: A memorial at a church, park, or home costs a fraction of a funeral home ceremony and can be scheduled whenever the family is ready.
  • Check assistance programs: Veteran benefits, Medicaid assistance, and other programs may cover part or all of cremation costs.

The Ohio Crematory Inspector Program

Ohio is one of only a handful of states that employs a dedicated crematory inspector. Under Ohio Revised Code 4717.14, the Ohio Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors maintains a facility licensure regime that requires biennial on-site inspections of every crematory in the state — including a physical check of the retort, the chain-of-custody log, the refrigeration unit, and the processing and pulverizing equipment. Columbus crematories, because of the metro's size, see the inspector more often than smaller markets. This oversight is not cosmetic: Ohio revoked or suspended several crematory licenses between 2018 and 2024 after routine inspections surfaced chain-of-custody gaps. For consumers, the practical implication is that a Columbus crematory operating today has met a higher bar than crematories in many neighboring states.

Columbus Market Tiers and What They Include

The Columbus cremation market now splits into three relatively distinct tiers:

  1. Standalone direct cremation providers ($1,350–$1,700): Oak Grove Cremation Service, Columbus Direct Cremation, and the Cremation Society of Mid-Ohio operate retort-owning, no-chapel businesses with minimal fixed overhead. Their quotes typically include transport within Franklin County, the cremation itself, and return of ashes in a basic container.
  2. Hybrid funeral homes ($1,800–$2,400): Schoedinger, Rutherford-Corbin, Egan-Ryan, and Moreland offer both full-service funerals and direct cremation. The direct-cremation product is usually priced at the upper end of this range, reflecting the overhead of running a chapel fleet and a visitation suite.
  3. Traditional multi-generation homes ($2,500–$3,200): A handful of premium homes in Upper Arlington, Bexley, and Dublin bundle enhanced packaging, a basic urn, and grief-support materials into what they still label as direct cremation. Families paying at this tier are often paying for the home's reputation rather than the commodity cremation service.

Franklin County Paperwork

Columbus Public Health operates the local registrar's office and handles death-certificate filing electronically through Ohio's EDRS system. The Franklin County Coroner's Office on North James Road retains jurisdiction over any death that is sudden, unattended, or the result of trauma — roughly 30 percent of county deaths pass through the ME before being released to a funeral home. Ohio's 24-hour minimum hold applies before any cremation can proceed, and The Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center, Nationwide Children's, Mount Carmel, and OhioHealth all participate in the EDRS filing workflow that lets providers move faster than the paper-era norms.

Scattering Around Central Ohio

Scioto, Olentangy, and Big Walnut are Columbus's three main rivers, and all three are under Ohio EPA jurisdiction rather than federal EPA. Ohio does not prohibit scattering in state waters, but the Ohio Department of Natural Resources asks families to notify the district office for scatterings near Hoover Reservoir, Alum Creek Reservoir, and Delaware Lake — all water supplies for the metro. Scattering in the Metro Parks system (Highbanks, Blacklick Woods, Three Creeks) requires a free written permit coordinated with the park manager. The largest Columbus cemetery, Green Lawn (founded 1848), operates a dedicated scattering garden and sells columbarium niches in its North and South walls. Beyond the metro, the Wayne National Forest (southeast Ohio) and Hocking Hills State Park are the most-visited scattering destinations for Columbus families.

Assistance for Columbus Residents

  • Franklin County Job and Family Services Indigent Burial Program: Up to $750 for direct cremation for qualifying low-income decedents without an estate. One of the more actively used county programs in Ohio.
  • Ohio Victims of Crime Compensation Program: Up to $7,500 in funeral and cremation expenses for homicide victims, administered by the Ohio Attorney General's office.
  • Dayton National Cemetery (52 miles west): The closest VA national cemetery for Columbus-area veterans. Free interment, columbarium niches, headstone or marker.
  • Catholic Diocese of Columbus — Resurrection and Mount Calvary Cemeteries: Columbarium niches generally priced well below commercial crematoria in the suburbs.
  • Social Security lump-sum: $255 to a qualifying surviving spouse or child — applied for through the SSA or the funeral home.

What Readers Typically Ask

How much does cremation cost in Columbus?

Cremation costs in Columbus range from $1,870 for direct cremation to $6,160 for a full funeral service followed by cremation. These costs are close to the Ohio state average of $1,700 for direct cremation. Additional expenses may include urns ($50-$3,000), memorial services ($500-$2,000), and scattering fees ($200-$600).

Where can I compare cremation prices in Columbus?

Call three or four Columbus cremation providers and ask specifically for their General Price List, which the FTC Funeral Rule entitles you to receive. Look at the direct-cremation figure and the separately listed basic-services fee. Resources like Parting.com and Funeral Consumers Alliance can round out your comparison with reviews and price ranges from other Columbus families.

Can I scatter ashes in Columbus?

Scattering laws in the Columbus area follow Ohio state regulations. Generally, you may scatter ashes on private property with owner permission, at sea (3+ nautical miles offshore per EPA regulations), and in some designated areas. Check local ordinances in Columbus for specific rules. Alternatives include columbarium niches, burial of ashes, and memorial keepsakes.
