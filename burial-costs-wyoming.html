Burial Costs in Wyoming (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Mountain

Whether Wyoming burial is a family tradition or simply the right choice for your loved one, knowing the real numbers before you walk into a funeral home matters. Average totals in Wyoming run from $12,980 to $18,250, but careful comparison — especially on caskets and cemetery choice — can cut thousands off that bill.

Full burials in Wyoming generally fall between $12,980 and $18,250, though the true range is wider because the state is so geographically varied — a county-maintained cemetery in Sheridan prices very differently from a private memorial park in Cheyenne.

Elevation, Frost, and the Winter Burial Window

Wyoming's average elevation is the second-highest in the country after Colorado, and many Wyoming cemeteries — particularly above 6,500 feet in the Bighorns, Wind Rivers, and Laramie Range — either close for mechanical grave-opening during hard frost or charge a substantial surcharge (typically $400-$900 on top of the standard opening-and-closing fee) to break frozen ground in winter. Families faced with a December or January death at elevation sometimes use refrigerated holding at the funeral home through spring thaw, which itself can add $50-$150 per day. When comparing quotes, ask every Wyoming cemetery explicitly about their winter policy in writing — "does the cemetery hold remains until spring, and what is the storage fee" is the exact question that matters.

Wyoming's Permissive Family and Home Burial Laws

Wyoming is one of the more permissive states in the country for family-directed funerals and home burial on private land. Wyoming law does not mandate that a licensed funeral director handle the body, and burial on family-owned rural acreage is broadly legal subject to county health board setback requirements and a properly filed death certificate. On working ranches and in long-held family parcels across Sweetwater, Fremont, and Converse counties, this is a real option that eliminates cemetery plot and opening-and-closing charges entirely. Wyoming's Funeral Service Practitioners Board handles licensing for funeral establishments that you do choose to engage, and it takes complaints about price disclosure or preneed issues. According to the Wyoming Board of Funeral Service Practitioners, all licensed establishments must comply with state price-disclosure and consumer-protection rules.

Cheyenne, Casper, and Beyond

  • Mount Olivet Cemetery, Cheyenne — Catholic cemetery, plots typically $2,400-$4,600 all in.
  • Lakeview Cemetery, Cheyenne — city-owned, one of the more affordable memorial parks in southeast Wyoming.
  • Highland Cemetery, Casper — municipal, wide price range by section.
  • Cheyenne National Cemetery and the Oregon Trail State Veterans Cemetery (Evansville) — both free for eligible veterans and spouses. Eligibility and benefits fall under the VA National Cemetery Administration (38 U.S.C. § 2402) and the VA burial allowance rules at 38 CFR § 3.1600.
  • County and rural cemeteries — fee schedules usually held by the county clerk; plot costs often $400-$1,500.
Your Federal Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453): Any Wyoming funeral establishment must provide a General Price List without obligation, cannot require embalming (Wyoming law does not mandate routine embalming), and cannot charge a surcharge or handling fee for a casket you purchase from an independent retailer. This matters in Wyoming because transport distances are long and online casket delivery is standard — an online 20-gauge steel casket delivered to a Casper funeral home is often $1,200 less than the same casket on the funeral home's price list.

Transport Distances Make Pre-Arrangement Particularly Important

Rural Wyoming funeral homes frequently travel 60-150 miles one-way to reach a decedent or a cemetery, and mileage is a real line item that drives the total up faster than families expect. Before finalizing an arrangement, ask specifically what the funeral home's mileage rate is, whether it is charged one-way or round-trip, and whether the receiving cemetery's opening-and-closing fee includes equipment mobilization if the cemetery is off the main road. When burial will happen in a different county from where the death occurred, getting both funeral-home and cemetery quotes from the burial-county side rather than the death-county side sometimes saves hundreds.

Paying for a Wyoming Burial — Veterans, County, and Nonprofit Help

Eligible veterans and spouses can use Cheyenne National Cemetery or Oregon Trail State Veterans Cemetery at no plot or opening-and-closing cost; the VA also provides a government headstone. Wyoming does not operate a statewide indigent burial program, but individual counties maintain modest burial funds for unclaimed or indigent decedents — the county coroner's office is the correct starting point. Some Wyoming congregations and fraternal organizations (Masons, Elks, Knights of Columbus) maintain their own burial assistance for members. For green burial, a small but growing list of Wyoming cemeteries accept natural burial in designated sections; our green burial guide lists current options. Questions Worth Asking First

What is the full price of burial in Wyoming?

A full burial in Wyoming comes in at about $6,900 for the funeral service, $3,200 for the cemetery plot, $2,070 for a casket, $1,280 for a vault, $1,600 for opening and closing, and $1,000-$3,000 for a headstone. Totals generally land between $12,980 and $18,250 depending on choices.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Wyoming?

Not by state law, but almost always by cemetery policy in Wyoming. Cemeteries require a vault or a less expensive grave liner to keep the ground stable over time. A liner is usually an acceptable substitute — always ask the specific Wyoming cemetery for their written policy. Vault pricing spans $800 to $10,000 in the state.

What are the cheapest burial options in Wyoming?

The most affordable burial options in Wyoming include: direct burial (no viewing or ceremony, body buried shortly after death) which eliminates embalming and facility costs; green or natural burial using a biodegradable container and no embalming, available at select Wyoming cemeteries; and purchasing a casket from an independent retailer rather than the funeral home, which can save 50-70% on casket costs alone. See our affordable funeral options guide for more strategies.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Wyoming?

Ask each Wyoming cemetery for a full itemized price sheet covering the plot, opening-and-closing fees, vault or liner requirements, perpetual-care charge, and any residency or denominational rules. Cemeteries are not covered by the FTC Funeral Rule, so they will only provide these details when asked directly. Notably, rural Wyoming cemeteries served by county clerks often price far below Cheyenne-area private cemeteries, and seasonal ground-closing windows at elevation can affect the interment timeline.

What is an all-in burial estimate for Wyoming?

Adding every line item, burial in Wyoming usually totals $12,980 to $18,250: funeral service about $6,900, plot about $3,200, casket about $2,070, vault about $1,280, opening-and-closing about $1,600, and headstone $1,000-$3,000. Shopping funeral homes and cemeteries as separate bills is what bends the total number down.

Is green burial available in Wyoming?

Green burial options are available in some areas of Wyoming. Green burial uses biodegradable containers, no embalming, and often costs less than traditional burial. Not all cemeteries in Wyoming offer green burial sections, so you may need to research options in your area. Some families also consider hybrid approaches, such as traditional caskets with no embalming. See our green burial guide for more details.
