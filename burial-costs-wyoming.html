Burial Costs in Wyoming (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Wyoming burial guide rewritten around town and district cemeteries, the plot certificate and the state's $1,500 payment, and an unsourced 40-60% savings claim removed. Region: Mountain

We estimate a Wyoming burial at $15,550 to $20,050, counting the cemetery's charges as well as the funeral home's. The funeral service is about $6,900, the plot about $3,200 and opening the grave about $1,600. Many Wyoming cemeteries are run by towns or cemetery districts with their own rules, so this page covers how to price them, the plot certificate, the state's $1,500 payment and the Cheyenne and Evansville veterans cemeteries.

Paying Less for a Wyoming Burial

Ways a Wyoming family can spend less:

  • Ask for itemized prices. A Wyoming funeral home must hand you its General Price List. Cemeteries do not have to, so ask the town or district for its written fee schedule.
  • Check winter rules. Ask whether the cemetery opens graves when the ground is frozen, or holds the body until spring, and what any storage costs.
  • Call the town or district. Many Wyoming cemeteries are run by towns or cemetery districts (Wyo. Stat. 35-8-303), and their lot prices can differ from a private cemetery's, so ask the town clerk or district board.
  • Ask about a family cemetery. Wyoming allows a private family burial ground if it is surveyed, platted and recorded with the county clerk (Wyo. Stat. 35-8-101); you still need the permit and any county approval. Our home funeral guide covers the paperwork.

Few Cemeteries, Long Distances in Wyoming

Outside Cheyenne, a family may have only one or two cemeteries within reach, so confirm the price before the service rather than after. Ask the cemetery for its opening, liner and marker charges in writing; our table puts the opening at $1,600, and a quote well above that is worth questioning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a Wyoming burial cost?

Wyoming, piece by piece on our model: $6,900 for the service itself, $3,200 for the plot, $2,070 for a casket, $1,280 for a vault and $1,600 for digging and filling the grave, plus $500 to $5,000 for a headstone, for $15,550 to $20,050 in all.

Is a vault needed for a Wyoming grave?

Wyoming has no statewide vault requirement and no statewide cemetery board. Many Wyoming cemeteries are run by towns or by special cemetery districts, which adopt their own rules (Wyo. Stat. 35-8-303), so the answer is in that district's or town's rules.

Ask the district or the town clerk for the written rule on outer containers, including whether a liner is enough. If a funeral home in Wyoming says state law requires a vault, that is not correct, and the FTC Funeral Rule forbids the claim (16 CFR 453.3(c)).

What is the lowest-cost burial in Wyoming?

Direct (immediate) burial skips embalming, viewing and a ceremony at the funeral home, so it is usually the lowest-cost burial on a Wyoming price list. You may supply your own casket in Wyoming; the FTC Funeral Rule forbids a handling fee for it. A VA national cemetery burial costs eligible Wyoming veterans nothing for the plot, opening and closing, or headstone.

How should I compare Wyoming cemeteries?

Start with the town clerk or the cemetery district, since many Wyoming cemeteries are public. Ask each for the lot price, the opening charge, its liner rule and whether it opens graves in winter, and get the answers in writing.

What does the whole Wyoming bill come to?

Added up, our estimate is $15,550 to $20,050. Expect cash advance items on top, such as death certificates and an obituary, which the funeral home pays out and passes on at cost. Wyoming's $1,500 state payment does not cover cemetery costs.

Can I find a green burial in Wyoming?

We could not find a dedicated green or conservation burial ground in Wyoming, so the options are a conventional cemetery that will waive its vault rule, or a natural burial ground in a neighboring state. Ask the town or cemetery district first, since each sets its own rules.

Some Wyoming families consider burial on their own land. A private family cemetery must be surveyed, platted and recorded with the county clerk (Wyo. Stat. 35-8-101), and you still need the burial-transit permit and any county approvals before the burial.

The certificate that proves you own a Wyoming plot

In Wyoming a cemetery plot is usually transferred with a certificate, not a full deed. The cemetery's owner signs it, and for a town or cemetery corporation the seal is attached and the clerk or secretary attests it. The certificate names the buyer and identifies the lot or block by number. It gives the buyer and their heirs a permanent right to use that ground for burial, subject to the cemetery's own rules (Wyo. Stat. § 35-8-102; checked October 29, 2026).

The certificate can be recorded with the county clerk without being notarized again. A family that bought spaces decades ago and has lost the paperwork can ask the county clerk's office for a recorded copy, or ask the cemetery for its own records, before paying for a new plot.

Wyoming burial oversight and help with the cost

The Wyoming State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners (307-777-5403) licenses funeral homes and takes complaints. Town and district cemeteries set their own rules, so ask the cemetery who oversees it.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance counts nine states where a funeral director must be involved; Wyoming is not among them, which leaves a family free to arrange a burial itself, with the permit and within the cemetery's rules.

The burial needs a disposition permit. If plans change to cremation, Board rules bar it within 24 hours of death without the county coroner's written permission (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5).

State and county help

The Department of Family Services pays up to $1,500 toward the burial of a person on POWER, SSI or Medicaid without enough estate, after other resources, plus up to $500 to carry a qualifying veteran to the veterans cemetery; cemetery costs are not included, and county commissioners cover others without means (W.S. 42-2-103(c); W.S. 18-3-504(c)). The $255 Social Security death payment is paid to an eligible widow, widower or child, never to the cemetery.

Veterans

Eligible Wyoming veterans get a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. In Wyoming, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Cheyenne National Cemetery (307-778-7316); state veterans cemetery: Oregon Trail State Veterans Cemetery (307-235-6673) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration). Cheyenne National Cemetery is in the state's southeast corner, and the Oregon Trail State Veterans Cemetery is in Evansville, next to Casper. The Wyoming Veterans Commission was founded in 1981 to receive the federal funding that established the Evansville cemetery, and it still oversees it.

Wyoming burial steps

  1. Get the plot, opening, liner and marker prices from the cemetery separately from the funeral home's quote.
  2. Ask about winter openings and storage before you set a date.
  3. If the state payment applies, set aside money for the cemetery, which it does not cover.
  4. Have a veteran's DD214 discharge papers ready and call Cheyenne or Evansville about eligibility.
  5. If a funeral home's prices turn out to be wrong, the Board takes complaints at 307-777-5403.
