Cremation Costs in Nebraska (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 50%

Nebraska's cremation rate hit 50% in the early 2020s and has been climbing about a point per year — a Plains-state median shaped by Omaha and Lincoln urbanization set against deeply traditional agricultural counties farther west. Direct cremation runs $1,500; a full service with visitation, $5,300. The real differentiator among Nebraska providers is whether they operate their own retort or route bodies to a regional partner.

Cost ranges on this page draw on the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report, which documents a national cremation rate of roughly 60.5%. Consumer price disclosures follow the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and Nebraska-specific licensing is administered by the Nebraska Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers.

Omaha and Lincoln: where Nebraska's cremation market actually competes

Ninety percent of Nebraska's licensed crematories sit along the I-80 corridor between Omaha and Lincoln. Heafey Hoffmann Dworak Cullen operates Omaha's longest-running cremation program; Braman Mortuary and Roeder Mortuary fill out the Omaha metro. Lincoln families typically compare Wyuka Funeral Home (attached to historic Wyuka Cemetery), Kremer Funeral Services, and Butherus-Maser-Love. Metro competition keeps direct cremation pricing in the $1,295-$1,895 window — narrower than in most Midwestern states.

Outside the corridor, the picture changes. Panhandle counties (Scotts Bluff, Cheyenne, Kimball) route most bodies to a crematory in Scottsbluff; central Sandhills families often see their loved one transported to Grand Island or Kearney, with mileage quietly added.

Nebraska's 24-hour hold, and why the weekend matters

Under Nebraska Revised Statute § 71-1339, no cremation may occur within 24 hours of death without medical examiner authorization. In practice, this means a Friday evening death in a small Nebraska town — where the county attorney serves as coroner and doesn't work weekends — can mean a Tuesday or Wednesday cremation. Three practical consequences:

  • Refrigeration or temporary preservation fees can run $75-$150/day.
  • Rural providers without in-house refrigeration may require embalming for weekend holds, adding $700-$900.
  • Ask explicitly whether the provider has on-site refrigeration. Many don't.
Nebraska Funeral Directing and Embalming Board. The state regulator audits crematories biennially and publishes disciplinary actions on the Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services site. Before signing a pre-need contract, a free search of the director's name in the board's disciplinary database takes two minutes and has saved Nebraska families from operators under active investigation.

Omaha National Cemetery and Nebraska veteran benefits

Nebraska gained its first national cemetery in 2016 when Omaha National Cemetery opened on 236 acres in Sarpy County. Eligible veterans receive no-cost interment of cremated remains in a columbarium niche, a government-furnished urn niche cover, perpetual care, and full military honors on request. The Nebraska State Veterans Cemeteries at Alliance and North Platte accept cremated remains as well. Either option replaces $1,200-$2,500 in private columbarium or niche costs.

Nebraska HHS assistance — who qualifies, what it pays

Families with limited resources may qualify for the Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services Aid to the Aged, Blind, or Disabled (ADC) burial allowance, which contributes up to $605 toward funeral or cremation expenses when the decedent's estate cannot cover costs. County general assistance programs in Douglas, Lancaster, and Sarpy counties layer additional indigent funeral funds on top — Douglas County typically adds $1,000-$1,500 toward a verified cremation, pushing Nebraska's effective public safety net to roughly $1,600-$2,100 when all sources stack.

A Nebraska price-shopping checklist

  • Request the General Price List in writing from three metro providers — required under the FTC Funeral Rule.
  • Confirm in writing whether transport is capped (most Omaha-Lincoln providers include up to 30-50 miles) or per-mile.
  • Ask who operates the retort. Several Nebraska "cremation services" route to the same third-party facility.
  • Ask for the crematory license number — verify on the NE DHHS licensing portal.
  • For veterans, apply for National Cemetery Administration eligibility before finalizing disposition plans. See our veteran benefits overview.

Questions From Families

What is the typical price of cremation in Nebraska?

Nebraska cremation pricing lives in two tiers: direct cremation — transport, cremation, and return of ashes — starting around $1,500, and full-service cremation with a ceremony landing near $5,300. Urns ($50-$3,000), memorial venues ($500-$2,000), and scattering or inurnment fees ($200-$600) are separate line items most providers will quote on request.

What are Nebraska's cremation regulations?

Nebraska follows standard cremation regulations requiring a mandatory waiting period (typically 24-48 hours) before cremation. Written authorization from the legal next of kin is required, and some counties require a permit from the medical examiner. Pacemakers and certain implants must be removed beforehand. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, you are not required to purchase a casket for cremation — an alternative container is sufficient.

Where are ashes allowed to be scattered in Nebraska?

Nebraska scattering rules depend on the specific location. Private land is generally fine with the owner's permission; at sea requires being at least 3 nautical miles offshore per EPA rules; many state and national parks need a permit (and in some cases prohibit it entirely). Check the local ordinance before choosing a spot. Families in Nebraska also often opt for columbarium niches, burial of the urn, or keepsake jewelry in place of scattering.

What is driving Nebraska's cremation rate?

Nebraska's 50% cremation rate reflects near the national average. The cost delta alone explains a lot — families save roughly $5,200 by choosing direct cremation over a traditional funeral in Nebraska. Add in the greater flexibility for memorial service timing, the environmental considerations, and the broad cultural shift (US cremation rate is now over 60% and still climbing), and the trend is easy to see.

How long does cremation take in Nebraska?

In Nebraska, the cremation process itself takes 2 to 3 hours. However, there is typically a mandatory 24 to 48 hour waiting period before cremation can proceed. After cremation, ashes are usually ready for pickup or delivery within 3 to 7 business days. Direct cremation ($1,500) has the shortest total timeline since no viewing or ceremony is involved.

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Nebraska?

Yes. Nebraska funeral homes regularly arrange visitation and a ceremony before cremation takes place — the full-service option typically runs around $5,300. If the ceremony timing is flexible, a memorial service after cremation (at any venue, anytime) is another option and usually costs less than the full traditional route.

State-level oversight rests with the Nebraska Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers.
