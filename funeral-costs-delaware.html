Funeral Costs in Delaware: What Families Pay in 2026

Reviewed: February 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Delaware Funeral Board Region: Northeast

Delaware may be the second-smallest state by area, but its funeral market carries characteristics that reflect its unique position between the major Mid-Atlantic metropolitan areas. With approximately 60 funeral homes serving fewer than one million residents, Delaware offers a compact market where families often have longstanding relationships with their local funeral provider. The state's geography creates a natural north-south pricing divide: northern Delaware, anchored by Wilmington and its proximity to Philadelphia, sees pricing that reflects the higher cost of living in the I-95 corridor, while Sussex County's beach communities and rural areas in the south are more affordable. Delaware's moderate cremation rate of 55% and its close proximity to providers in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Maryland give Delaware families more options than the state's small size might suggest.

Funeral Cost Breakdown in Delaware

Delaware's funeral costs fall in the moderate-to-high range nationally, reflecting the state's Mid-Atlantic cost of living. Northern Delaware, particularly New Castle County, is heavily influenced by the Philadelphia metro economy — wages, real estate, and the cost of doing business all mirror what you would find across the border in southeastern Pennsylvania. The smaller number of funeral homes in the state (approximately 60) means less price competition than in larger states, but Delaware's proximity to providers in neighboring states gives families additional options. The DuPont corporate legacy — once the state's dominant employer — has historically shaped community institutions in northern Delaware, including some funeral homes with longstanding ties to the area's industrial heritage.

What Drives Funeral Costs in Delaware

The Delaware Board of Funeral Services regulates all funeral directors, embalmers, and funeral establishments in the state. The Board enforces licensing standards, investigates complaints, and oversees preneed funeral contract regulations. Delaware's preneed laws require protections for consumer funds paid in advance for funeral arrangements.

Delaware's small geographic footprint creates a unique market dynamic. The state can be driven end to end in about two hours, which means families are not limited to providers in their immediate area. More significantly, families in northern Delaware can easily access funeral homes in southeastern Pennsylvania (Chester County, Delaware County) and southern New Jersey (Salem County, Gloucester County), creating de facto competition that Delaware's small number of in-state providers might not otherwise face.

Wilmington's diverse population — including significant African-American, Catholic, and Jewish communities — drives demand for various funeral traditions. The city's African-American churches, particularly in the Baptist and AME traditions, are important gathering places for funeral services. Catholic wakes and funeral masses remain common in Wilmington's Italian-American and Irish-American neighborhoods. Dover, as the state capital, presents a mid-range market with a mix of military families (Dover Air Force Base) and state government employees. Sussex County's beach communities (Rehoboth, Lewes, Bethany) serve both year-round residents and a large seasonal population, with funeral homes in the area accustomed to coordinating with families who may live part-time elsewhere.

The market consolidation picture in Delaware is moderate. A few larger corporate-affiliated homes operate in the Wilmington area, but many of the state's approximately 60 funeral homes remain family-owned and community-oriented. This independence helps maintain a personal touch that many Delaware families value.

Cremation vs. Burial in Delaware

Delaware's 55% cremation rate sits near the national average, reflecting the state's moderate, Mid-Atlantic sensibility. The rate is slightly higher in northern Delaware, where a more diverse and mobile population is less tied to burial traditions, and somewhat lower in slower-paced, more traditional southern Delaware communities. Dover Air Force Base families — accustomed to relocating frequently — often prefer cremation for its flexibility. The state's proximity to Philadelphia's extensive cemetery network also means some Delaware families opt for burial in Pennsylvania cemeteries where family plots already exist.

Delaware Funeral Regulations

The Delaware Board of Funeral Services oversees the funeral profession in the state. Key regulations include:

  • Preneed contracts — Delaware regulates preneed funeral contracts to protect consumers who plan ahead. Funds must be properly secured, and contracts must include specific disclosure language.
  • Licensing — Funeral directors and embalmers must be licensed by the Board after completing education requirements, national board examinations, and supervised practice.
  • No embalming mandate — Delaware does not require embalming by state law. Refrigeration is an accepted alternative for preserving remains when there is a delay between death and final disposition.
  • Facility standards — All funeral establishments in Delaware must meet standards set by the Board, including preparation room requirements and public viewing facilities.
  • Cross-state services — Delaware's small size means families sometimes use funeral homes in neighboring Pennsylvania, New Jersey, or Maryland. Be aware that the laws of the state where the funeral home is located will govern the transaction.

Your Consumer Rights in Delaware

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Delaware must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

Delaware consumers benefit from both federal FTC protections and oversight by the Delaware Board of Funeral Services. The Board investigates complaints and can take disciplinary action against licensees. The Delaware Department of Justice, Consumer Protection Unit, can also assist with complaints about funeral home practices. Because Delaware's small market means fewer providers to choose from, it is especially valuable to exercise your right to compare prices — and remember that funeral homes just across the state border in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, or Maryland may offer competitive alternatives. File complaints with the Board of Funeral Services, the Delaware DOJ, or the FTC at ftc.gov. The Funeral Consumers Alliance also provides guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Delaware?

The average traditional funeral in Delaware costs approximately $8,600, about 10% above the national average. Wilmington and northern New Castle County are the most expensive areas (averaging $9,000-$10,000), while Dover falls near the state average and Sussex County offers slightly lower pricing. Direct cremation at $2,000 is the most affordable option. Cemetery plots average $4,200 but can be considerably less in smaller, rural cemeteries in southern Delaware.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Delaware?

Yes. Direct cremation at $2,000 costs less than a quarter of a traditional burial at $8,600, and burial adds additional cemetery plot costs averaging $4,200. The total cost difference between direct cremation and traditional burial with all cemetery expenses can exceed $10,000. Delaware's 55% cremation rate shows the state is near the national midpoint in cremation adoption.

Can I use a funeral home in Pennsylvania or New Jersey?

Yes. Delaware's small size means many families, particularly in northern Delaware, live just minutes from funeral homes in Pennsylvania or New Jersey. This is perfectly legal — the funeral will be governed by the laws of the state where the funeral home is located. Cross-state comparison shopping can sometimes uncover meaningful price differences. Just be aware of any differences in state regulations regarding preneed contracts, embalming, or other requirements.

What are my consumer rights at Delaware funeral homes?

Delaware consumers are protected by both the federal FTC Funeral Rule and state oversight from the Delaware Board of Funeral Services. You must receive a General Price List, can select only services you want, and can use caskets purchased elsewhere. The Delaware DOJ Consumer Protection Unit provides additional enforcement. With only about 60 funeral homes statewide, comparing prices is especially important to ensure you are getting fair value.

Does Delaware require embalming?

No, Delaware does not require embalming by law. Funeral homes may require it for open-casket viewing as a matter of policy, but refrigeration is always an alternative. If you plan a quick burial or cremation without viewing, embalming is unnecessary. Always ask your funeral director about alternatives if embalming is presented as mandatory.

Are there veteran cemeteries in Delaware?

Yes. Delaware operates two state veterans cemeteries: the Delaware Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Bear (New Castle County) and the Sussex County Veterans Memorial Cemetery near Millsboro. Both provide free burial for eligible veterans and their dependents, including a grave, marker, and opening/closing fees. Dover Air Force Base families have additional support through the base's casualty assistance office.
