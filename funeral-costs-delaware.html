Funeral Costs in Delaware: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: Northeast

Delaware's three-county footprint — New Castle, Kent, Sussex — makes the state one of the easier places in the country to price-shop a funeral in an afternoon. Chandler Funeral Homes along the U.S. 13 corridor, McCrery & Harra in Wilmington, and Spicer-Mullikin across New Castle County each publish distinct General Price Lists, and the spread between the highest and lowest quote for an identical traditional service routinely crosses $4,700. Delaware's Compact Tri-County Market

With roughly 75 licensed funeral establishments serving just under a million residents, Delaware's market concentration is unusual. The Board of Funeral Service Practitioners in Dover licenses directors under Title 24, Chapter 31, and the entire state can be driven end to end in under two hours — meaning families in Lewes can realistically compare Wilmington, Dover, and Rehoboth quotes the same morning. That compression pushes the spread: a Chandler GPL in Selbyville rarely matches a McCrery & Harra quote on Delaware Avenue in Wilmington, even for identical services.

Frozen Ground and the December-to-March Problem

Delaware's clay-heavy soils north of the Chesapeake & Delaware Canal freeze harder than the state's mild reputation suggests. Some New Castle County cemeteries add a $400–$750 winter grave-opening charge, and others will delay interment entirely until March. If the death occurs between Thanksgiving and the spring thaw, ask each cemetery — not just the funeral home — whether the fee applies and whether an above-ground crypt or delayed burial with cremation is on the table.

Line Items Families Miss on the First Read

Under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), amended in 2022 to tighten General Price List disclosure, every Delaware home must hand you an itemized sheet in person. What that sheet often buries:

  • Third-party casket "handling" surcharge — Prohibited under the FTC Funeral Rule, but still attempted. Push back and walk out if it isn't removed.
  • Embalming "required for viewing" language — It's a provider policy, not Delaware law. Refrigeration is a legal substitute in virtually every scenario.
  • Perpetual care add-ons — Delaware cemeteries running under private trust law add $250–$950 on top of the plot.
  • Vault brand lock-ins — Gracelawn and Silverbrook specify outer burial containers; the upgrade from basic to "sealed" can run $1,400.
  • Certified death certificates — Delaware charges $25 for the first, $15 each additional through the Office of Vital Statistics.
  • After-hours transport — Removals after 8 p.m. or on weekends often carry a $200–$350 premium.
  • Bundled-package inflation — Spicer-Mullikin, Chandler, and McCrery & Harra all offer packages; line-itemizing typically shaves 10–18%.

Legal Protections When Buying a Funeral

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Delaware must comply with the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in Delaware has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov, contact the Delaware Board of Funeral Service Practitioners, or reach out to the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Your state attorney general's office can also assist with consumer protection complaints. Mortality comparisons between states rely on CDC NCHS mortality data.

DSS Burial Assistance: What Low-Income Delawareans Can Claim

The Delaware Division of Social Services administers a burial assistance program capped at $1,500 for qualifying Medicaid, SSI, and TANF recipients. The application must be filed within 60 days of death through a DSS caseworker, and the funeral home bills DSS directly — families should not pay up front and seek reimbursement. Combined with the $255 Social Security lump-sum benefit and, for qualifying veterans, a $948 non-service-connected burial allowance from the VA, the out-of-pocket on a direct cremation can drop close to zero.

What Readers Typically Ask

What is the average price of a funeral in Delaware right now?

Per the NFDA 2024 General Price List Study, Delaware's 2026 averages land at $8,600 for a traditional burial service, $6,800 for cremation with a service, and from $2,000 for direct cremation — that traditional figure sits about 10% above the $7,848 U.S. median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $4,200 when ground interment is chosen. Because the Delaware Board of Funeral Service Practitioners does not set pricing, quotes from Chandler, McCrery & Harra, and Spicer-Mullikin can diverge by $4,000 or more on the same package — always request the itemized GPL in person.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Delaware?

Materially cheaper. Direct cremation at roughly $2,000 saves a Delaware family around $6,600 versus the $8,600 traditional funeral plus a $4,200 plot. The state's cremation rate runs 55% — above New Jersey and Maryland, below Pennsylvania — and the tri-county footprint means a Wilmington family can realistically price a Sussex County direct-cremation provider and still collect the ashes within the same week.

What rights do Delaware families have when arranging a funeral?

Every Delaware funeral home operates under the FTC Funeral Rule (1984, amended 2022): they must deliver an itemized General Price List in person, accept third-party caskets and urns without a handling surcharge, let you decline any service you don't want, and refrain from inventing legal requirements. State-level oversight comes from the Delaware Board of Funeral Service Practitioners in Dover, which handles complaints, license discipline, and preneed trust enforcement under Title 24, Chapter 31.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Delaware?

Call at least three GPLs in writing — Chandler down U.S. 13, McCrery & Harra in Wilmington, and one Sussex-County direct-cremation specialist is a reasonable spread. Ask the cemetery separately about winter grave-opening fees if the death falls between December and March. Catholic cemeteries like Cathedral in Wilmington typically price $800–$1,200 under private memorial parks. Then stack the $255 Social Security benefit, any VA allowance, and DSS Burial Assistance (up to $1,500 for Medicaid/SSI/TANF recipients) before choosing a service tier.

Does Delaware require embalming?

No. Delaware law does not mandate embalming in any standard disposition scenario — refrigeration satisfies the state's handling requirements. A funeral home may still require it as a house policy for open-casket viewings, but that's a business rule, not a legal one. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, no provider may claim embalming is legally required without citing the specific statute, and in Delaware there isn't one to cite.

Is Delaware cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Delaware sits roughly 10% above the $7,848 national median at $8,600 for a traditional service. Wilmington's real-estate costs, Northeast labor rates, and the relatively short distance to Philadelphia's supplier network all push pricing up. Sussex County, served by smaller independent homes, tends to come in 6–9% lower than New Castle County quotes.

Which funeral option costs the least in Delaware?

Direct cremation wins on price at roughly $2,000 — transport, cremation, and return of the ashes, nothing else. The Sussex County direct-cremation specialists often quote under $1,800. Direct burial ranks second at about $4,300 once a simple plot in a municipal or religious cemetery is factored in, still well under a traditional service.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Delaware?

Pull three written General Price Lists — Delaware providers must supply them on request. Phone the Funeral Consumers Alliance of Greater Philadelphia, which covers New Castle and northern Kent counties, for vetted independents. Direct-cremation specialists along the U.S. 13 corridor routinely undercut Wilmington full-service homes by 40–55%. Ask specifically for the basic-services-only figure, and compare Catholic and Jewish cemetery plot pricing against Gracelawn or Silverbrook before committing.

Families arranging a funeral in Delaware for the first time often move faster than they need to. The following sequence slows things down just enough to compare options without adding undue delay:

  1. Take a breath. Unless there are legal or medical time constraints, you typically have 24–72 hours before decisions must be finalized.
  2. Request General Price Lists from at least 2–3 funeral homes in your area of Delaware. They are legally required to provide them. Questions to ask funeral homes
  3. Decide on disposition: cremation in Delaware or burial in Delaware. This is the single biggest cost decision.
  4. Choose only the services you need. Embalming, premium caskets, and elaborate arrangements are optional. Read what funeral homes don't tell you
