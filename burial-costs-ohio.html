Burial Costs in Ohio (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Midwest

Ohio's cemetery map is unusually dense. Between the three big-C cities — Cleveland, Columbus, Cincinnati — and the dozens of municipal, township, and congregational grounds that serve smaller communities, most Ohio families have several meaningfully different price points within thirty minutes of home. A full traditional burial in Ohio averages between $13,560 and $19,090 in 2026, anchored by a $7,100 funeral-home service fee and a $3,400 plot.

Once every bill is on the table, an Ohio burial totals between $13,560 and $19,090. Casket, cemetery, and the rural-versus-urban choice explain almost all of the spread.

Three cities, three cemetery cultures

Ohio's largest cities each anchor a distinct cemetery market. Lake View Cemetery in Cleveland's Little Italy is a 285-acre arboretum-style ground where the Garfield, Rockefeller, and Ness families are buried; its section pricing reflects historic character, and inventory in choice sections is limited. Spring Grove Cemetery and Arboretum in Cincinnati is the second-largest cemetery in the United States and is priced across a deep tier structure, from simple lawn sections to premium historic areas. Green Lawn Cemetery in Columbus, founded in 1848, serves much of central Ohio and similarly offers multiple tiers. Woodland Cemetery and Arboretum in Dayton rounds out the set of historic landscape cemeteries families should at least get a quote from.

Funeral homes: Busch, Schoedinger, and the independents

Busch Funeral Homes operates multiple locations across the Cleveland area and is a long-standing reference for pricing in Northeast Ohio. Schoedinger, founded in 1855, serves most of Greater Columbus. Independent, family-owned firms in smaller Ohio cities — especially across the Mahoning Valley and Southeast Ohio — routinely price services $1,500 to $3,000 below the regional chains. Every Ohio funeral home is required to produce a General Price List on request under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453).

Ohio's cemetery tax districts and township cemeteries

Ohio's township and municipal cemeteries operate under a long-standing framework in the Ohio Revised Code (Chapters 517 and 759). Many townships fund cemetery operations through small property-tax levies and set plot fees at well below market. A family burying a loved one in a rural Ohio township cemetery may pay $400 to $1,200 for a plot and a fraction of the metro opening-and-closing rate. The county recorder or township trustees' office is the right first call; these grounds often do not advertise online. Ohio also requires pre-need funeral contracts to be funded through regulated trust or insurance arrangements under the Ohio Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors' oversight.

County-by-county burial assistance

The Ohio Department of Job and Family Services oversees indigent burial programs that are administered at the county level. Amounts and eligibility thresholds vary: Cuyahoga, Franklin, and Hamilton counties generally pay $1,000 to $1,500 directly to the funeral provider for qualifying cases, while smaller counties may have more limited programs. Funerals covered by these programs must stay within a published cap; ask the county Job and Family Services office about the current ceiling before selecting a provider.

Veterans across Ohio

Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery in Seville and Dayton National Cemetery at the Dayton VA medical center are the two primary federal options for eligible veterans. Both provide the gravesite, opening and closing, a government-issued marker, and perpetual care at no cost. Ohio Veterans Home Cemetery in Sandusky serves residents of the state veterans home. Eligibility and benefits fall under the VA National Cemetery Administration (38 U.S.C. § 2402) and the VA burial allowance rules at 38 CFR § 3.1600.

Ways Ohio burial totals come down

  • Call the township trustees. Township cemeteries across Ohio are a genuinely underused option and frequently reduce the plot line by $2,000 or more versus a metropolitan memorial park.
  • Compare funeral homes on the telephone. Ohio is large enough that prices vary widely; a thirty-minute round of calls to three independents in your county commonly reveals a $2,000 gap on the same package.
  • Use a third-party casket. Every Ohio funeral home must accept one under the FTC Funeral Rule; online pricing regularly runs 50 to 70 percent below funeral-home retail. Casket buying guide
  • Ask about concrete liners. Most Ohio cemeteries accept a liner in place of a full sealed vault. The liner runs around $800 to $1,200, compared with $3,000 and up for a premium vault.
  • Green burial at Foxfield and beyond. Ohio has growing natural burial options — Foxfield Preserve in Wilmot is a Green Burial Council hybrid, and several traditional cemeteries now offer natural sections that skip the embalming and vault lines entirely. Green burial options
Township cemeteries first: Ohio's county recorder offices can point you to active township and municipal cemeteries that rarely appear online. Those grounds often produce the lowest-cost legitimate option in the state.

Questions From Families

What is the full price of burial in Ohio?

A full traditional burial in Ohio averages $7,100 for the funeral service, $3,400 for a cemetery plot, $2,130 for a casket, $1,360 for a vault, $1,700 for opening and closing, and $1,000 to $3,000 for a marker. Totals generally land between $13,560 and $19,090. Urban cemeteries like Lake View and Spring Grove are priced above that range; township cemeteries across the state fall well below it.

Is a burial vault required in Ohio?

Ohio law does not require a vault. Most Ohio cemeteries require an outer container, but a concrete liner satisfies the rule at nearly every ground, including Lake View, Spring Grove, Green Lawn, and the large majority of township cemeteries. A basic liner is typically $800 to $1,200, compared with $3,000 and up for a sealed vault. Ask the cemetery for its written container specification before buying.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in Ohio?

The least expensive path in Ohio combines a township or municipal cemetery plot, a direct burial service, and a third-party casket delivered to the funeral home. A green burial at Foxfield Preserve or a hybrid section elsewhere is another strong option, typically running $2,000 to $3,500 below a traditional burial because embalming and vault lines are removed.

What is the best way to shop cemeteries in Ohio?

Start with the county recorder or the township trustees' office to identify the municipal and township cemeteries serving the area where burial will happen. Then request itemized fee schedules from at least one historic nonprofit (Lake View, Spring Grove, Green Lawn, Woodland Dayton), one memorial park, and one township ground. Cemeteries are not bound by the FTC Funeral Rule, so the transparency comes from you asking.

What is the total cost of burial in Ohio including everything?

Including service, plot, casket, vault, opening and closing, and marker, a full traditional burial in Ohio totals $13,560 to $19,090 on average. Cleveland, Columbus, and Cincinnati cluster at the top of that range; rural counties and smaller cities come in well under it. The largest savings almost always sit in the plot and casket lines.

Does Ohio have green burial cemeteries?

Yes. Foxfield Preserve in Wilmot is the state's best-known conservation burial ground, certified by the Green Burial Council. Natural burial sections now exist at Spring Grove in Cincinnati and at a growing number of smaller Ohio grounds. Green burial in Ohio typically means no embalming, a biodegradable container, and no vault, bringing the total below a traditional service.
