Burial Costs in Ohio (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: Midwest

Ohio's burial traditions run deep, anchored by some of the most historically significant cemeteries in the Midwest. From the grand Victorian-era Spring Grove Cemetery in Cincinnati — one of the largest and most beautiful in the nation — to Lake View Cemetery in Cleveland and hundreds of small-town graveyards across the state, Ohio offers a wide range of burial options at moderate prices. With three major metro markets (Cleveland, Columbus, and Cincinnati), a strong network of perpetual care cemeteries, and unique cultural traditions including Amish burial customs, Ohio's burial landscape is varied and accessible.

What Drives Burial Costs in Ohio

Ohio's burial costs are moderate by national standards, reflecting the state's middle-of-the-road cost of living. The state's three major metro areas — Greater Cleveland, Greater Columbus, and Greater Cincinnati — drive the higher end of pricing, with established memorial parks and Catholic cemeteries charging $2,500 to $6,000 or more for plots. Columbus, as the fastest-growing of the three, has seen the most price pressure on cemetery space.

Ohio has a particularly strong system of perpetual care cemeteries, where ongoing maintenance is funded through trust accounts established when plots are purchased. This provides assurance that the cemetery grounds will be maintained indefinitely, but it adds to the upfront plot cost. The Ohio Cemetery Law requires that a portion of every plot sale go into a perpetual care trust fund.

The Amish communities in Holmes, Wayne, and surrounding counties maintain their own burial traditions — simple wooden caskets handmade by community members, no embalming, no vaults, and plain headstones. These practices result in burial costs that are a fraction of mainstream prices, typically under $2,000 total. Dayton National Cemetery serves the state's significant veteran population with free burial.

Cemetery Types Available in Ohio

  • Historic garden cemeteries — Spring Grove Cemetery in Cincinnati (founded 1845) and Lake View Cemetery in Cleveland (1869) are National Historic Landmarks with extraordinary landscapes, monuments, and cultural significance. Plots in premium sections can exceed $10,000.
  • Catholic cemeteries — The Archdioceses of Cincinnati and Cleveland and the Dioceses of Columbus, Toledo, Youngstown, and Steubenville operate extensive cemetery systems. Calvary Cemetery in Cleveland and Gate of Heaven in Cincinnati are among the largest.
  • Municipal cemeteries — Many Ohio cities and townships operate public cemeteries with regulated pricing. Green Lawn Cemetery in Columbus is one of the largest municipal cemeteries in the state.
  • National cemeteries — Dayton National Cemetery is the primary national cemetery, with Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery near Rittman also serving veterans.
  • Amish cemeteries — Simple, unadorned cemeteries in the Amish country of northeast and central Ohio follow plain traditions. These are generally available only to Amish community members.
  • Township and community cemeteries — Hundreds of small cemeteries maintained by Ohio townships offer affordable burial across rural areas.

Casket and Vault Options

Ohio funeral homes average $2,130 for caskets. Families can save considerably by purchasing online — Ohio has excellent delivery coverage from major online casket retailers. The FTC Funeral Rule guarantees that Ohio funeral homes must accept third-party caskets without handling fees. In Amish communities, handmade wooden caskets are built by community members at minimal cost.

Ohio's perpetual care cemetery system means that most cemeteries require burial vaults to maintain ground level and appearance. Concrete liners start around $800, with sealed protective vaults ranging to $10,000. Some older township cemeteries and Amish cemeteries do not require vaults.

Consumer Rights for Burial in Ohio

  • FTC Funeral Rule — All Ohio funeral homes must provide itemized General Price Lists and cannot require package purchases.
  • Ohio Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors — Regulates funeral professionals and handles consumer complaints.
  • Ohio Cemetery Law (ORC Chapter 1721) — Governs cemetery operations, requires perpetual care trust funds, and provides consumer protections.
  • Embalming not required — Ohio does not mandate embalming. Refrigeration is an acceptable alternative.
  • Casket purchase freedom — Third-party caskets must be accepted without handling fees.
  • Preneed protections — Ohio requires preneed funeral funds to be placed in trust, with consumer cancellation and transfer rights.
  • Home burial permitted — Ohio allows burial on private property with local zoning approval and proper filing.

Green and Natural Burial in Ohio

Ohio has a growing green burial movement, though certified natural burial grounds are still developing. Foxfield Preserve in Wilmot (near the Amish country) is one of Ohio's most established conservation burial grounds, offering natural burial on permanently protected land. Several other Ohio cemeteries have introduced green burial sections. The state's strong Amish burial tradition — which has always been essentially green burial — provides a longstanding cultural model. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Ohio?

A traditional burial in Ohio costs approximately $7,100 for the funeral service plus $3,400 for a cemetery plot. Total costs range from $13,560 to $19,090. Cleveland, Columbus, and Cincinnati are the most expensive markets, while rural southeastern Ohio and western counties are more affordable.

Is a burial vault required in Ohio?

Ohio has no state law requiring vaults, but most perpetual care cemeteries require them to maintain ground level. Ohio's cemetery law emphasizes perpetual care, making vault requirements standard practice at most established cemeteries. Some township cemeteries and green burial grounds may waive this requirement.

What are the cheapest burial options in Ohio?

Direct burial in a township or community cemetery with an online-purchased casket can cost $5,000-$8,000 total. Green burial at Foxfield Preserve offers another affordable option. Veterans receive free burial at Dayton National Cemetery or Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery.

How do Amish burial traditions work in Ohio?

Ohio Amish communities practice simple burial — handmade wooden caskets built by community members, no embalming, no vaults, and plain uniform headstones in community cemeteries. Total burial costs in Amish communities are typically under $2,000. The entire community participates in funeral preparations, reducing reliance on commercial funeral services.

What are Ohio's most historic cemeteries?

Spring Grove Cemetery in Cincinnati (1845) and Lake View Cemetery in Cleveland (1869) are both National Historic Landmarks and among the most significant garden cemeteries in America. Green Lawn Cemetery in Columbus (1848) is another notable historic site. These cemeteries combine active burial grounds with park-like landscapes and are open for public visits.
