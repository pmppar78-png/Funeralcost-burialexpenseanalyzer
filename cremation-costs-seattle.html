Cremation Costs in Seattle, Washington (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Washington Cremation Rate: 76%

Seattle runs the highest metropolitan cremation rate in the United States at roughly 76%. Evergreen-Washelli on Aurora Avenue — the largest funeral home in Washington — and Bonney-Watson, in business since 1868, handle the bulk of conventional cremations. A direct cremation costs about $2,625; a cremation with ceremony and viewing runs around $8,500. Seattle is also home to Recompose, the first U.S. company licensed to perform natural organic reduction — the 2019 state law legalizing it set the template other states have since copied.

Natural Organic Reduction at Recompose: What the $7,000 Includes

Washington's 2019 Senate Bill 5001 made it the first state to legalize human composting — marketed commercially as natural organic reduction, or NOR. Recompose, based in Kent, runs the largest facility in the country. The current package starts at $7,000 and covers transport from Washington or Oregon, placement in a vessel filled with wood chips, straw, and alfalfa, a 30-day decomposition cycle followed by 30 days of curing, and return of roughly a cubic yard of compost to the family. Return's optional; unclaimed soil is donated to Bells Mountain Conservation Forest in southwest Washington. Herland Forest, a nonprofit in Wahkiacus, offers a lower-cost NOR option at around $3,000.

Seattle's Main Providers

  • Evergreen-Washelli (Aurora Avenue N): Full-service funeral home and cemetery. On-site crematory. Direct cremation close to the $2,625 median.
  • Bonney-Watson: Seattle's oldest funeral firm, founded 1868. Multiple locations. Handles Catholic, Jewish, and secular services.
  • Cremation Society of Washington: Direct-cremation specialist with flat-rate pricing in the $1,500 to $2,200 range for members. Membership is a small one-time fee.
  • Recompose: NOR only. Does not perform flame cremation.

Alkaline Hydrolysis (Water Cremation)

Washington legalized alkaline hydrolysis under the same 2019 statute that permitted NOR. A handful of providers — Elemental Cremation & Burial chief among them — offer it in the Seattle area at roughly $2,500 to $3,500. The process dissolves soft tissue using a heated potassium hydroxide solution and returns pulverized bone to the family in the same way flame cremation does. Families who want a lower-carbon option but don't want composting tend to land here.

Washington's Regulatory Floor

The Washington State Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers licenses every funeral establishment, funeral director, and embalmer in the state. Refrigeration is required if final disposition isn't completed within 24 hours, which shapes how quickly direct-cremation providers move. A signed cremation authorization from the legal next of kin and the medical examiner or coroner's release are mandatory; most Seattle providers complete the timeline in 7 to 10 days. Washington DSHS administers a state-funded burial/cremation benefit for low-income households of up to $500 that funeral homes apply for on the family's behalf.

Puget Sound detail: Scattering in Puget Sound waters is permitted beyond three nautical miles under federal EPA rule; Seattle-area charter operators file the NOAA report for families. Unattended scatterings start around $150.
Cost-Saving Tip: You do not need to purchase an urn from the cremation provider. The FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to use any urn or container, including ones purchased independently. See our urn buying guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Seattle?

Direct cremation in Seattle starts at $2,625 through Evergreen-Washelli, Bonney-Watson, or a Cremation Society of Washington membership. A full-service cremation with viewing reaches $8,500. The Washington state average for direct cremation is $2,100, so Seattle runs about $525 above the state norm. Natural organic reduction through Recompose is a separate category at roughly $7,000.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Seattle?

Yes. A Seattle cemetery plot at Evergreen-Washelli, Acacia Memorial Park, or Calvary Catholic runs $5,000 to $12,000 before vault, marker, and opening/closing. Direct cremation at $2,625 saves about $8,375 against a traditional burial. Even Recompose's $7,000 NOR is usually cheaper than a standard plot-based burial once every cemetery line item is added.

Can I scatter ashes in Seattle?

Scattering in Puget Sound beyond three nautical miles is permitted under EPA rule; charter operators in Shilshole and Edmonds handle attended scatterings for $250 to $800. Olympic, Mount Rainier, and North Cascades National Parks allow dispersal with a free permit from the park superintendent. Washington State Parks permit scattering in many locations with the ranger station's approval. Private property needs the owner's written permission under RCW 68.50.
