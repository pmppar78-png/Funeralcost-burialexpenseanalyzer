Help Paying for a Funeral in Wyoming

  • Wyoming's $1,500 payment does not cover cemetery costs, and it can add up to $500 to carry a qualifying veteran to the veterans cemetery (W.S. 42-2-103(c)).
  • The $255 Social Security death payment goes only to an eligible surviving spouse or child; in Wyoming, as elsewhere, they apply by calling 1-800-772-1213 within two years of the death.
  • VA reimburses part of a Wyoming veteran's funeral through Form 21P-530EZ, filed by whoever paid; deaths not connected to service on or after October 1, 2026 qualify for up to $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for the plot, as our veteran burial benefits guide explains.

Your Consumer Rights in Wyoming

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Wyoming must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. Licensing is handled by the Wyoming State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners, which began in 1915 as the Board of Embalming. When the Legislature renamed it in 2014, it also dropped the separate funeral director license, so everyone licensed to practice funeral service in Wyoming is now a funeral service practitioner.

If you believe a funeral home in Wyoming has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. To report a Wyoming funeral home, use the board's public complaint process. Its office in the Emerson Building no longer takes walk-ins, so call 307-777-5403 to set up a visit.

How Wyoming Differs From Its Mountain Neighbors

Wyoming has some of the more progressive disposition laws in the country, including accommodations for home funerals and family-led arrangements in several counties. If your family wants a less commercialized approach to a Wyoming funeral, it is legally easier here than in much of the Northeast, though you still need to comply with death certificate, transportation, and disposition paperwork. What Drives Funeral Costs in Wyoming

Several forces shape funeral pricing in Wyoming. Regionally, the Mountain trends toward lower-than-average costs relative to the $8,300 US median, and within Wyoming that gap widens between a dense metro like Cheyenne and the smaller towns: city prices routinely sit 10-30% higher.

Ordering death records from Cheyenne

Wyoming's fee is $25 for the first copy and $20 for every one after it, so a family wanting four for the bank, insurer, pension and title office pays $85 (Wyoming Department of Health, certified copy application; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Who can order: the state seals a death record for 50 years, and until then only relatives or people with a legal or financial stake, such as an insurer claim, can get a copy.
  • Where to send it: mail or bring the application to Vital Statistics Services at 2300 Capitol Avenue, Cheyenne, WY 82002; questions go to 307-777-7591.

Funeral rules and help in Wyoming

Funeral homes in Wyoming are licensed by the Wyoming State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners (307-777-5403). That office takes Wyoming complaints about pricing, misleading sales, or a home that will not hand over its price list.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Wyoming among the nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so a Wyoming family may arrange much of a funeral itself. The Wyoming death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

If the family chooses cremation, Wyoming requires a 24-hour wait before cremation under board rules (035-4 Wyo. Code R. 4-5).

Wyoming pays up to $1,500 toward the burial or cremation of a person on POWER, SSI or Medicaid without enough estate, after other resources, plus up to $500 to carry a qualifying veteran to the veterans cemetery (W.S. 42-2-103(c)); cemetery costs are not included, and county commissioners cover others without means (W.S. 18-3-504(c)). (Source: W.S. 42-2-103(c); W.S. 18-3-504(c).) The $255 Social Security lump sum goes only to an eligible spouse or child and must be claimed within two years; it does not go to the Wyoming funeral home.

A Wyoming funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Cheyenne National Cemetery (307-778-7316) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemetery, Oregon Trail State Veterans Cemetery (307-235-6673), takes requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in Wyoming

  1. Ask every Wyoming funeral home for its General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule says it must give you one when you ask in person.
  2. Ask the Wyoming funeral home whether embalming is actually needed for your plans.
  3. Ask the Wyoming Department of Family Services about the $1,500 payment if the person was on POWER, SSI or Medicaid.
  4. If the person served, have the funeral home ask about Cheyenne National Cemetery before you buy a private plot.
  5. Report a funeral home that hides or ignores its price list to the board at 307-777-5403.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a typical Wyoming funeral cost in 2026?

Most Wyoming families paying for a full traditional service see the bill settle around $6,900, 17% below the $8,300 national benchmark. Cremation with ceremony runs closer to $5,600, while a stripped-down direct cremation in Wyoming can land near $1,600. Cemetery plot fees in Wyoming add about $3,200. Actual prices shift a lot depending on which Wyoming provider you call first, which is why comparing is non-negotiable.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in Wyoming?

Direct cremation in Wyoming starts around $1,600; a traditional funeral with burial averages $6,900, not counting the cemetery plot or vault. For most Wyoming families, that is a difference of at least $5,300.

What are my consumer rights at Wyoming funeral homes?

The federal FTC Funeral Rule gives Wyoming families the right to an itemized price list, to prices over the phone, and to buy only the goods and services they want. Wyoming is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. The Wyoming State Board of Funeral Service Practitioners, 307-777-5403, licenses Wyoming funeral homes and takes complaints.

What is the best way to reduce funeral expenses in Wyoming?

The biggest lever is comparison shopping: two or three GPLs from different Wyoming providers, compared line by line, consistently saves families $1,000 or more. Home and family burial is legal in parts of Wyoming with permits and can eliminate cemetery fees entirely. At $1,600, direct cremation is the cheapest disposition option available in Wyoming.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Wyoming?

The FTC Funeral Rule bars a Wyoming funeral home from saying embalming is required by law when it is not, and its price list must state that embalming is not required except in certain special cases. If a Wyoming provider says embalming is needed, ask which law or cemetery rule it is citing.

How do funeral costs in Wyoming compare to other states?

Wyoming's traditional-funeral average of $6,900 sits 17% below the $8,300 national median, and the Mountain region overall trends toward moderate to lower pricing than the rest of the country.

What is the least expensive way to handle a funeral in Wyoming?

In Wyoming, direct cremation at about $1,600 is the most affordable path: just transport, cremation, and the return of the remains. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $3,450 in Wyoming before cemetery fees and is the cheapest burial option.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in Wyoming?

Call several Wyoming funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Wyoming home cannot charge a fee for it.
