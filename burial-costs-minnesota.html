Burial Costs in Minnesota (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: Midwest

Minnesota's burial traditions reflect the North Star State's distinctive blend of Scandinavian, German, and increasingly diverse immigrant heritage. The many Lutheran church cemeteries that dot the countryside — from the iron-red hills of the Range to the fertile farmland of the south — speak to the Norwegian, Swedish, and German families who settled the land. Today, the Twin Cities metro area serves a remarkably diverse population including large Somali, Hmong, and Latino communities, each bringing unique burial customs and needs. Fort Snelling National Cemetery, overlooking the confluence of the Mississippi and Minnesota rivers, is one of the nation's most visited and revered military burial grounds. Minnesota's burial costs are moderate, with significant variation between the Twin Cities and the state's rural communities.

What Drives Burial Costs in Minnesota

Scandinavian Cemetery Traditions

Minnesota's Norwegian and Swedish heritage is visible in the many Lutheran church cemeteries across the state. These tidy, well-maintained burial grounds often feature granite headstones quarried from Minnesota's own deposits and reflect the understated Scandinavian aesthetic of simple dignity.

Diverse Burial Customs

The Twin Cities' Somali community — the largest in the U.S. — follows Islamic burial traditions requiring prompt burial, simple shroud wrapping, and specific grave orientation. Minnesota's Hmong community maintains traditional burial practices involving multi-day ceremonies. Both communities need cemeteries that accommodate their specific requirements.

Plot Prices by Area

Twin Cities metro: $2,000-$5,500. Rochester: $1,500-$4,000. Duluth: $1,200-$3,500. St. Cloud: $1,000-$3,000. Rural Minnesota: $400-$1,500.

Winter Burial Challenges

Minnesota's harsh winters mean that grave excavation from November through March requires ground-thawing equipment, adding $300 to $800 to opening costs. Some smaller rural cemeteries may defer winter burials to spring.

Notable Minnesota Cemeteries

Fort Snelling National Cemetery in Minneapolis is one of the most revered national cemeteries, with over 200,000 interments. Lakewood Cemetery in Minneapolis is the city's premier private cemetery. Minnesota State Veterans Cemetery in Little Falls serves veterans.

Cemetery Types Available in Minnesota

  • Church cemeteries — Lutheran, Catholic, and other denominational cemeteries statewide.
  • Municipal cemeteries — Available in most Minnesota communities.
  • Veterans cemeteries — Fort Snelling National Cemetery and Minnesota State Veterans Cemetery. Learn about veteran burial benefits
  • Private memorial parks — In the Twin Cities metro area.

Casket and Vault Options in Minnesota

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, Minnesota families can buy caskets from any retailer, saving 50-80%. Minnesota's granite industry means locally sourced headstones may be more affordable.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Minnesota?

A traditional burial costs approximately $7,700 for the funeral service plus $3,600 for a plot. Total costs range from $14,540 to $20,450. Twin Cities costs are higher than Greater Minnesota.

Is a burial vault required in Minnesota?

No state law requires vaults. Most cemeteries require them due to Minnesota's severe freeze-thaw cycle. Some green burial sections may waive this requirement.

Can you be buried in winter in Minnesota?

Yes, in most communities. Ground-thawing equipment adds $300-$800 to the cost. Some rural cemeteries may defer winter burials to spring. Fort Snelling National Cemetery conducts year-round burials.

Are there Islamic burial options in Minnesota?

Yes. Several Twin Cities cemeteries have sections accommodating Islamic burial requirements, serving Minnesota's large Somali and other Muslim communities. Funeral homes specializing in Islamic burial are available in the Minneapolis-St. Paul area.
