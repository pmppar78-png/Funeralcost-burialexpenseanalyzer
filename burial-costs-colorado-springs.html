Burial Costs in Colorado Springs, Colorado (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Colorado

Colorado Springs has the highest per-capita veteran population of any major U.S. city — Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever, Cheyenne Mountain, and the Air Force Academy all sit in or near El Paso County — and that reality changes the burial math for a meaningful share of local families. Between Pikes Peak National Cemetery and the military support infrastructure, a significant percentage of Colorado Springs burials land well below the commercial range of $15,981 to $22,491 quoted by area funeral homes.

The commercial burial range in El Paso County — $15,981 to $22,491 — covers the funeral service, plot at a mid-tier cemetery like Evergreen or Memorial Gardens, vault, opening and closing, casket, and headstone. That range excludes the flowers and reception that most Colorado Springs services include, and it assumes no veterans' benefit is applied. For the large fraction of local families with military connection, the practical bill is usually considerably lower.

Pikes Peak National Cemetery Changed the Math

Pikes Peak National Cemetery opened southeast of Colorado Springs in 2018 on roughly 374 acres and is now the region's primary veteran burial ground. Before it existed, local veterans who wanted interment at a VA national cemetery had to travel to Fort Logan in Denver — an hour and change in each direction. Pikes Peak's opening and closing, plot, vault, perpetual care, and government-issued headstone are all covered at no charge for eligible veterans and spouses; with military-honors ceremony support available through the local military units, the total out-of-pocket for a Colorado Springs veteran's family often comes down to the funeral home service fee alone. Given the city's demographics, that benefit applies to far more households than it does in most American metros. The U.S. Air Force Academy Cemetery on the Academy grounds is a much smaller site limited to Academy-connected decedents; it is not a substitute for Pikes Peak for most military families.

The Historic and Commercial Cemeteries in the Pikes Peak Region

Evergreen Cemetery, platted in 1871 and located off Hancock Expressway, is Colorado Springs's historic cemetery and still sells plots. It holds Spencer Penrose and several of the city's founders. Memorial Gardens Cemetery off Constitution Avenue is the large commercial memorial park on the north side of the city. Fairview Cemetery in Manitou Springs, the Crystal Valley Cemetery in Manitou, and several smaller mountain-community cemeteries (Green Mountain Falls, Palmer Lake, Woodland Park) round out the regional options. At elevation — Colorado Springs sits at roughly 6,035 feet, with surrounding cemeteries running as high as 8,300 feet in the nearby communities — winter grave opening can require mechanical frost-breaking and carry modest surcharges, although the seasonal closures that affect higher-elevation mountain cemeteries rarely affect the city itself.

Military Survivor Benefits You Can Layer With the Cemetery Benefit

Beyond Pikes Peak, Colorado Springs families arranging a veteran's burial should confirm eligibility for the VA burial allowance (a reimbursement toward funeral home expenses, typically $300–$2,000 depending on whether the death was service-connected and whether the veteran was hospitalized under VA care), the government-issued headstone or marker (provided free for eligible veterans at private cemeteries too, not just national ones), and the Presidential Memorial Certificate. The Fort Carson Survivor Outreach Service and Peterson Space Force Base Survivor Benefit Plan counselors help Colorado Springs families navigate the paperwork and will typically return calls quickly. For active-duty deaths, the military's Casualty Assistance Calls Officer handles most arrangements directly.

Colorado Springs vs. Denver and the State Average

Colorado Springs runs modestly above the state average on every line — about 5% above the Colorado mean — but materially below Denver, which sits at a roughly 15% premium over the state. Pueblo and Cañon City south of Colorado Springs come in lower still.

Colorado Springs–Specific Cost Reduction Moves

  • File for Pikes Peak National Cemetery eligibility first. For veterans, spouses, and certain dependents, this single step typically eliminates $6,000 or more from the bottom line before any funeral home negotiation begins.
  • Use your base Survivor Benefit counselor. Fort Carson, Peterson, Schriever, and the Academy all have counselors who will help you file VA burial allowance, government-headstone, and military-honors requests at no cost.
  • Shop Colorado Springs funeral homes aggressively. Colorado does not license individual funeral directors — the state registers establishments — so the pricing spread between providers is unusually wide. Get three itemized price lists.
  • Consider Pueblo or Cañon City cemeteries if the family has flexibility. Both sit within an hour of Colorado Springs and typically price plots and opening-and-closing fees 20–30% below the Pikes Peak region.
  • Ask about green burial in the region. A handful of Colorado Springs–area cemeteries offer natural-burial sections; the state's broader green-burial scene (Seven Stones in Littleton, Natural Funeral in Lafayette) is within driving range.
  • Buy the casket online, have it shipped to the funeral home. The FTC Funeral Rule requires acceptance with no handling fee. Casket guide | Retailers
  • Get the headstone priced through an independent monument dealer. Colorado Springs has several long-established monument companies that deliver-and-install at area cemeteries at lower cost than cemetery-supplied markers. Headstone guide
Save on Caskets: Under the FTC Funeral Rule, funeral homes must accept caskets purchased from third-party retailers without charging handling fees. Compare online casket retailers to save significantly.

A Few Questions We Keep Getting

How much does burial cost in Colorado Springs?

A commercial burial in Colorado Springs runs $8,400 for the funeral service, $3,990 for a plot at Evergreen or Memorial Gardens, $1,596 for a vault, $1,995 for opening and closing, $2,520 for a mid-range casket, and $1,500–$3,500 for a headstone — $15,981 to $22,491 total. For eligible veterans, interment at Pikes Peak National Cemetery covers the plot, vault, opening/closing, and government-issued headstone, which usually leaves only the funeral home service charge.

Is a burial vault required in Colorado Springs?

Colorado state law does not require a vault. Evergreen, Memorial Gardens, and most other commercial El Paso County cemeteries require one as a maintenance policy. Pikes Peak National Cemetery includes an outer burial container in its interment package for eligible veterans. Some smaller mountain and church cemeteries accept a concrete grave liner or sometimes no vault at all. Always verify the specific cemetery's policy before the funeral home adds a vault to the itemization.

What are the cheapest burial options in Colorado Springs?

Pikes Peak National Cemetery is free for eligible veterans and spouses — the single cheapest path for the huge local military-connected population. For non-veteran families, the lowest-cost options are direct burial at a small community cemetery in Manitou Springs, Palmer Lake, or Woodland Park using a casket ordered online, or transportation to Pueblo for a lower-cost commercial plot. Colorado's family-burial laws also allow private-land burial where county rules permit.
