Cremation Costs in Pennsylvania (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Cremation Rate: 52%

Pennsylvania's 52% cremation rate conceals two fundamentally different markets. In Philadelphia and its suburbs, cremation competes with deeply rooted Catholic, Jewish, and African American burial traditions in a high-cost urban environment. In Pittsburgh and western Pennsylvania, ethnic European communities (Polish, Italian, Slovak) have gradually embraced cremation, though traditional funerals with multi-day viewings remain more common than in most states. Between these anchors, central Pennsylvania — from the Amish country of Lancaster to the college town of State College — presents yet another dynamic, where Plain community opposition to cremation coexists with growing acceptance among the broader population.

What Drives Cremation Costs in Pennsylvania

Several factors shape the cremation market in Pennsylvania:

  • 24-hour waiting period: Pennsylvania requires a 24-hour waiting period after death, which is shorter than some neighboring states and helps keep storage costs manageable.
  • Philadelphia vs. Pittsburgh: Philadelphia's higher cost of living and commercial rents drive cremation costs well above western Pennsylvania levels. Pittsburgh has seen more cremation-only startups, creating better price competition. The $500-$1,000 gap between the two cities for direct cremation is significant for families weighing options.
  • Amish and Plain community influence: Lancaster County and surrounding areas have large Amish and Old Order Mennonite populations that largely oppose cremation on religious grounds. This concentrates cremation demand elsewhere in the state and means central Pennsylvania has fewer cremation-focused providers relative to its population.
  • Strong Catholic influence: Pennsylvania has a large Catholic population, particularly in Philadelphia and Pittsburgh. While the Catholic Church now permits cremation (since 1963), it prefers that cremated remains be interred rather than scattered. This creates demand for columbarium niches and cemetery cremation plots that providers market alongside cremation packages.
  • Dense funeral home network: Pennsylvania has one of the densest concentrations of funeral homes in the nation, many family-owned for generations. While this provides good competition, many are full-service operations where cremation is offered alongside traditional burial rather than as the primary focus.

Consumer Rights for Cremation in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania families are protected by federal and state regulations when arranging cremation:

  • FTC Funeral Rule: All Pennsylvania providers must present an itemized General Price List. You can choose only the services you need.
  • No casket required: An alternative container is sufficient for cremation. Providers cannot refuse service based on your container choice.
  • 24-hour waiting period: Written authorization from the legal next of kin is required. A cremation permit must be obtained from the local registrar.
  • Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors: The board regulates providers and handles consumer complaints.
  • Preneed protections: Pennsylvania requires that 100% of prepaid funeral and cremation funds be deposited in trust, offering strong consumer protection.
  • Right to provide your own urn: Providers cannot charge handling fees for urns purchased from outside sources.

Scattering and Memorial Options in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania offers several options for scattering cremated remains:

  • State forests and game lands: Pennsylvania has 2.2 million acres of state forest and 1.5 million acres of state game lands. Scattering is generally permitted with discretion. The Allegheny National Forest also allows scattering.
  • Pocono Mountains: Popular with families from both Philadelphia and New York. Scattering in state forest lands and along mountain streams is common.
  • Lake Erie: Northwestern Pennsylvania families can scatter in Lake Erie. No state permit is specifically required.
  • Delaware River: Scattering in the Delaware River and Delaware Water Gap is popular with eastern Pennsylvania families. The national recreation area allows scattering with a free permit.
  • Private property: Scattering on private land requires the landowner's written consent. Common across rural Pennsylvania.
  • Gettysburg considerations: Scattering at Gettysburg National Military Park requires a permit and must follow specific guidelines. Many families with Civil War heritage choose this meaningful location.
Cost-Saving Tip: Request itemized General Price Lists from at least three providers before making a decision. The FTC Funeral Rule guarantees your right to compare prices and choose only the services you need.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Pennsylvania?

Direct cremation in Pennsylvania averages $2,000, ranging from $1,400 to $2,800. Cremation with full service averages $6,400. Philadelphia is the most expensive market ($2,200-$3,000 for direct), while Pittsburgh offers more moderate pricing ($1,600-$2,200). Central and rural Pennsylvania generally fall between or below these ranges.

What types of cremation are available in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania offers direct cremation (most affordable), cremation with memorial service, and traditional cremation with full funeral (including multi-day viewing, which remains common in PA). The traditional approach — viewing followed by cremation rather than burial — is gaining popularity as a way to honor Pennsylvania's ethnic funeral customs while choosing cremation as the final disposition.

What are Pennsylvania's cremation regulations?

Pennsylvania requires a 24-hour waiting period. Written authorization from the legal next of kin is mandatory. A cremation permit from the local registrar is required. Pacemakers must be removed. No casket or embalming is required for direct cremation. The PA State Board of Funeral Directors oversees provider compliance. Prepaid cremation funds must be placed in trust.

Can I scatter ashes in Pennsylvania?

Yes. Pennsylvania's extensive state forests (2.2 million acres), game lands (1.5 million acres), and the Allegheny National Forest all permit scattering. The Pocono Mountains, Lake Erie, and the Delaware River are popular locations. Private land scattering requires landowner consent. Gettysburg National Military Park allows scattering with a permit.

Do Amish communities in Pennsylvania use cremation?

No. Amish and most Old Order Mennonite communities in Pennsylvania maintain a strong religious preference for simple ground burial, viewing cremation as contrary to their beliefs about the body. This does not affect non-Amish families in the same areas, but it means cremation providers are less common in heavily Plain communities like Lancaster, Mifflin, and parts of Chester counties.

Is Philadelphia or Pittsburgh cheaper for cremation?

Pittsburgh is generally $500-$1,000 cheaper than Philadelphia for comparable cremation services. Pittsburgh's lower cost of living and the presence of more cremation-focused providers create better value. Families in the Philadelphia suburbs may also save by comparing providers in Delaware or South Jersey, where prices may be lower.
