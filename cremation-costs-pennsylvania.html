Cremation Costs in Pennsylvania (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 22, 2026 Cremation Rate: 52%

Cremation pricing in Pennsylvania is simpler than traditional burial but can still vary by thousands of dollars between providers. Direct cremation starts around $2,000 while cremation with a full service averages $6,600. This guide walks through what's included at each price point and how Pennsylvania's 52% cremation rate shapes local options.

What to Know Before Choosing Cremation in Pennsylvania

When considering cremation in Pennsylvania, keep these important points in mind:

  • Get the Pennsylvania provider's General Price List before you agree to anything; the direct cremation line must state what it includes, and the price should cover transport, the cremation itself and a basic container for the ashes.
  • A Pennsylvania provider offering direct cremation must let you use an unfinished wood box or other alternative container instead of a casket (FTC Funeral Rule, 16 CFR 453.3(b)).
  • Ask how long the Pennsylvania crematory usually takes to return the ashes once the permit is issued, and whether the quoted price includes delivery or only pickup.
In Pennsylvania you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Pennsylvania bring a container, and the PA State Board of Funeral Directors is who you call if a home in Pennsylvania refuses it.

How to Save on Cremation in Pennsylvania

Cremation costs in Pennsylvania can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Pennsylvania: Request written General Price Lists.
  • Ask about current turnaround: Crematory capacity around Philadelphia can stretch turnaround to 10-14 days during peak periods.

Regional Context for Cremation in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, where winter complicates traditional burial, cremation has a practical scheduling advantage most families underestimate: ashes can be interred or scattered any time of year, which means the service does not have to be planned around frozen-ground delays. This alone pushes some Northeastern families toward cremation, even before considering the $6,500 cost difference versus traditional burial in Pennsylvania.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does cremation cost in Pennsylvania in 2026?

Cremation in Pennsylvania runs from roughly $2,000 for the simplest direct option up to about $6,600 when paired with a viewing or ceremony.

Which cremation options exist in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania providers typically offer three tiers. Direct cremation at $2,000 is the simplest — no service, no viewing, body cremated and ashes returned. Traditional cremation at $6,600 is a full funeral home service followed by cremation.

What are Pennsylvania's cremation regulations?

In short, Pennsylvania sets a 24-hour minimum before cremation, and the crematory also needs the death certificate, a cremation permit and a signed authorization from the next of kin or designated agent. The statute behind the Pennsylvania timing rule, whether a funeral director must be involved, and the licensing board's phone number are listed in the cremation rules section further down this page.

Is ash scattering legal in Pennsylvania?

Check Pennsylvania local ordinances and any park-specific rules before proceeding.

Why do so many Pennsylvania families choose cremation?

Pennsylvania sits with near the national average at 52%.

How long does cremation take in Pennsylvania?

In Pennsylvania, the cremation process itself takes 2 to 3 hours. Direct cremation ($2,000) has the shortest total timeline since no viewing or ceremony is involved.

Do I have to skip the service if I choose cremation in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania providers offer cremation with a full service — visitation, ceremony, and then cremation instead of burial — for about $6,600. Only direct cremation at $2,000 omits the service entirely.

Cremation rules and help in Pennsylvania

The licensing office for cremation providers in Pennsylvania is the Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors; its number is 1-833-367-2762.

Hiring a funeral home is a choice, not a legal requirement, for most steps in Pennsylvania; Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count it among the nine states with funeral-director rules. Pennsylvania paperwork and permits still apply.

Before a cremation can happen: Pennsylvania bars cremation until 24 hours after death; the county coroner must authorize it and the next of kin holds sole authority to consent (49 Pa. Code § 13.212).

Pennsylvania DHS pays up to $750 toward burial or cremation for eligible people, paid to an enrolled funeral director; resources of $1,500 or more disqualify the estate (55 Pa. Code ch. 283). (Source: Pennsylvania DHS.)

Cremated remains of an eligible Pennsylvania veteran can be placed in a VA national cemetery columbarium or in-ground site at no charge for the space. One option in Pennsylvania is Indiantown Gap National Cemetery (717-865-5254). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps in Pennsylvania

  1. Compare at least two Pennsylvania providers line by line, using their printed price lists.
  2. Allow at least 24 hours after death, plus time for the permit, before the cremation date.
  3. Contact your county assistance office (Pennsylvania DHS) early, because deadlines and limits can apply.
  4. A complaint call to 1-833-367-2762 is free if a provider misleads you.
