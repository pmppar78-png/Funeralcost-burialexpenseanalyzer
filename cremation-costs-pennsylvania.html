Cremation Costs in Pennsylvania (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 52%

Pennsylvania's 52% cremation rate sits on the national median, but the Commonwealth's 67-county structure creates a fragmented regulatory picture few other states match. Each county independently files the cremation permit, and Philadelphia, Allegheny, and Montgomery counties operate notably differently from rural Potter or McKean. Direct cremation runs about $2,000 statewide; full-service cremation, $6,600.

Cost ranges on this page draw on the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report, which documents a national cremation rate of roughly 60.5%. Consumer price disclosures follow the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and Pennsylvania-specific licensing is administered by the Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors.

Philadelphia cemetery permit filing, county by county

Pennsylvania's cremation permit requirement works at the county, not state, level. Each of the 67 counties handles the cremation permit slightly differently. The practical consequences for families:

  • Philadelphia County: The Philadelphia Medical Examiner's Office issues cremation permits for all Philadelphia deaths. Standard turnaround 24-48 hours; longer for any death requiring ME investigation (overdose, trauma, unattended).
  • Allegheny County (Pittsburgh): Allegheny County Medical Examiner issues permits. Similar 24-48 hour timeline.
  • Montgomery, Bucks, Delaware, Chester (Philadelphia collar): County coroner or deputy coroner issues. Faster in some suburbs, slower in others.
  • Rural counties (Potter, McKean, Clearfield, etc.): County coroner — typically an elected position, sometimes part-time — issues. Weekend deaths can mean Monday-Tuesday permits.

The PA State Board of Funeral Directors and its co-owner rule

Pennsylvania's funeral regulation sits at the PA State Board of Funeral Directors within the Department of State. Pennsylvania has one of the country's most unusual regulatory provisions — the co-owner rule: until recently, every Pennsylvania funeral home had to be owned by a licensed funeral director, blocking corporate consolidation more common in other states. The rule has loosened since 2010 but still shapes the market. What this means for consumers:

  • Pennsylvania has unusually high independent family-owned funeral home density — Kirk & Nice in Philadelphia, Plitt in Pittsburgh, Beinhauer in Pittsburgh suburbs, Alleva Funeral Home in northeast PA. These operators typically quote direct cremation in the $1,795-$2,495 range.
  • The large SCI/Dignity Memorial footprint common elsewhere is thinner in Pennsylvania.
  • Price comparison across three providers nearly always reveals a $400-$900 spread for identical direct cremation services.

Pennsylvania DHS Funeral Benefit and other assistance

Pennsylvania offers a distinct funeral assistance program through the Pennsylvania Department of Human Services:

  • PA Funeral Benefit (DHS): Up to $750 toward direct funeral expenses plus an additional $750 toward cemetery/cremation expenses for eligible low-income decedents. Total potential benefit: $1,500, paid directly to the provider.
  • Philadelphia Medical Examiner indigent cremation: For unclaimed decedents or families unable to afford any disposition, the Philadelphia ME contracts cremation services; a basic indigent urn is returned to the city if no family claims it.
  • Allegheny County, Montgomery County, and other county general assistance programs layer county-level funds on top of state DHS benefits.
Washington Crossing National Cemetery and the Alleghenies. Pennsylvania has two active National Cemetery Administration facilities: Washington Crossing National Cemetery (near Newtown, Bucks County) and the National Cemetery of the Alleghenies (Bridgeville, near Pittsburgh). Both accept cremated remains in columbarium niches at no cost to eligible veterans, providing government-furnished niche covers, perpetual care, and military honors. Either option saves Pennsylvania veterans $1,500-$3,500 versus private cemetery inurnment.

Pennsylvania provider pricing, by market

  • Philadelphia metro: $1,795-$2,995. Kirk & Nice, Goldsteins' Rosenberg's, Gearty-Delmore, and dedicated low-cost operators Neptune Society and Cremation Society of Pennsylvania set the range.
  • Pittsburgh metro: $1,595-$2,795. Plitt, Beinhauer, Schugar, and Cremation Society of Pittsburgh.
  • Lehigh Valley (Allentown-Bethlehem-Easton): $1,895-$2,695. J.S. Burkholder and Ashton Funeral Home set pricing.
  • Lancaster, Harrisburg, York: $1,695-$2,595. Traditional family-owned homes predominate.
  • Rural Pennsylvania (Potter, McKean, Elk): $1,995-$2,895. Transport distance adds variance.

Winter burial considerations in PA

One practical reason Pennsylvania's cremation rate has climbed: the state's northern counties experience genuine frost-in-ground from December through March. Traditional burial in these counties often involves a winter storage fee of $300-$900 until spring thaw. Cremation sidesteps the problem entirely — ashes can be inurned or scattered any time. Common Questions We Hear

What does cremation cost in Pennsylvania in 2026?

Cremation in Pennsylvania runs from roughly $2,000 for the simplest direct option up to about $6,600 when paired with a viewing or ceremony. Urns, memorial services, and interment of ashes are priced separately — typically $50-$3,000 for an urn, $500-$2,000 for a memorial, and $200-$600 for scattering or inurnment. Your final number depends on which tier you pick.

Which cremation options exist in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania providers typically offer three tiers. Direct cremation at $2,000 is the simplest — no service, no viewing, body cremated and ashes returned. Cremation with a later memorial keeps the ceremony but shifts it to a venue of your choice, offering timing flexibility. Traditional cremation at $6,600 is a full funeral home service followed by cremation. Each can be adjusted to match family preferences and budget.

What are Pennsylvania's cremation regulations?

Pennsylvania follows standard cremation regulations requiring a mandatory waiting period (typically 24-48 hours) before cremation. Written authorization from the legal next of kin is required, and some counties require a permit from the medical examiner. Pacemakers and certain implants must be removed beforehand. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, you are not required to purchase a casket for cremation — an alternative container is sufficient.

Is ash scattering legal in Pennsylvania?

In most cases yes, but the specifics depend on where. In Pennsylvania you can generally scatter on private land (with owner permission) or at sea beyond 3 nautical miles under EPA rules. National park scattering often requires a free permit. Check Pennsylvania local ordinances and any park-specific rules before proceeding. Some families prefer columbarium niches, urn burial, or memorial jewelry to avoid the permitting question entirely.

Why do so many Pennsylvania families choose cremation?

Pennsylvania sits with near the national average at 52%. The drivers are familiar: cremation costs far less than traditional burial (the direct-cremation-to-traditional-funeral gap is about $6,500 in Pennsylvania), families can schedule memorials on their own timeline, environmental concerns weigh on more people, and cultural norms around burial have shifted nationally past 60% cremation.

How long does cremation take in Pennsylvania?

In Pennsylvania, the cremation process itself takes 2 to 3 hours. However, there is typically a mandatory 24 to 48 hour waiting period before cremation can proceed. After cremation, ashes are usually ready for pickup or delivery within 3 to 7 business days. Direct cremation ($2,000) has the shortest total timeline since no viewing or ceremony is involved.

Do I have to skip the service if I choose cremation in Pennsylvania?

Not at all. Pennsylvania providers offer cremation with a full service — visitation, ceremony, and then cremation instead of burial — for about $6,600. Memorial services after cremation are also common, letting families choose any venue or date. Only direct cremation at $2,000 omits the service entirely.
