Funeral Costs in Virginia: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 30, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Norfolk and Richmond no longer show the repeated Virginia figure. Virginia Beach and Arlington keep their metro estimates, and the new note under the city cards cites the Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers at the Department of Health Professions. Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: South

Half of Virginia deaths, 50%, now end in cremation, and a traditional Virginia funeral averages about $8,200 on our estimate, almost exactly the $8,300 national median. Prices still differ a lot between Northern Virginia, Hampton Roads and the rural southwest.

Help With a Virginia Funeral Bill

  • For an unclaimed body, the Virginia city or county where the person lived pays, or the locality of death for a non-resident, once the local court approves (Va. Code § 32.1-309.2).
  • An eligible spouse or child can claim the one-time Social Security $255 by calling 1-800-772-1213, no later than two years after the death.
  • VA Form 21P-530EZ reimburses the person who paid for a Virginia veteran's funeral; when the death was not service-connected and occurred from October 1, 2026 on, VA pays up to $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for a plot (our veteran burial benefits guide explains the rules).
  • Medicaid, charities and county aid appear in our payment help overview and on our page about funerals with no savings.

  • Embalming described as required by law. Virginia almost never requires it, so ask the home to name the rule.

Virginia's Funeral Market

Chain ownership has raised prices in some Virginia markets, while family-owned homes still dominate others; asking who owns a home is a quick clue to its pricing.

Federal Rules That Protect You

Virginia homes are bound by the FTC Funeral Rule as well as state law: itemized prices, quotes by phone, and no forced packages.

Report a Virginia home that breaks the rule at ftc.gov; the Funeral Consumers Alliance can help you decide what to do first.

Why Virginia Prices Differ

Location is the main one. Homes in Virginia Beach, Arlington and other metros often price 10-30% above homes in small towns, even though the statewide estimate is within $100 of the national median of $8,300.

Virginia death certificates went up to $15 in July 2026

Since July 1, 2026, the Virginia Department of Health charges $15 for each certified copy of a death certificate, and the same $15 for a search when no record is found. The old fee was $12; Chapter 616 of the 2026 Acts of Assembly raised the cap in Va. Code § 32.1-273. There is no discount for extra copies (VDH Office of Vital Records; checked October 29, 2026).

Walk-in customers can get copies the same day at the Office of Vital Records, 8701 Park Central Drive in Richmond, from 7 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays. Standard online orders take about two weeks plus mail time, and VitalChek offers expedited service at extra cost. Many DMV offices also issue death certificates, adding a $2 processing fee to the $15.

  • A veteran's survivor who needs a copy for service-connected VA benefits gets it free, and the funeral director can request that copy for a surviving spouse.
  • At $15 a copy with no multi-copy discount, five certificates cost $75; count the accounts before ordering.
  • Life insurers, banks, pension plans and the DMV for a car title usually each want their own certified copy, while many utilities and credit card companies will accept a photocopy. Asking each one first can save several $15 fees.
  • Most families order their first copies through the funeral home, which adds them to the bill as a cash advance at the state's price.

Virginia's funeral board, local burial help and veterans cemeteries

If a funeral home in Virginia refuses to show prices or pressures you, the licensing body is the Virginia Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers, reachable at 804-367-4479. The board has nine members, sits within the Department of Health Professions, and besides funeral directors and embalmers it also licenses crematories, surface transportation and removal services, and courtesy card holders.

Virginia is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that make families use a funeral director, which leaves the shopping list up to the family. There is no set waiting period before a cremation, only a permit (Va. Code 54.1-2818.1).

When the family cannot pay

Virginia's cities and counties carry the cost for an unclaimed body: the locality where the person lived pays reasonable costs, or the locality of death for a non-resident, with the local court's approval (Va. Code § 32.1-309.2). Where a claimant cannot pay, the city or county may pay under § 32.1-309.1 (Va. Code §§ 32.1-309.1, 32.1-309.2). Social Security's $255, paid only to a qualifying spouse or child, is a sliver of the $8,200 average Virginia funeral in our table.

Veterans

Virginia's national cemeteries are Culpeper (540-825-0027), Danville (704-636-2661, cremated remains only) and Quantico (703-221-2183); burial there is arranged by the funeral director, who contacts the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office on 800-535-1117 to arrange it. The three state veterans cemeteries, Albert G. Horton Jr. In Suffolk, Southwest Virginia in Dublin and the Virginia Veterans Cemetery in Amelia, share one number: 855-482-8387.

Before signing in Virginia

  1. Lay two Virginia price lists side by side and compare each line.
  2. Settle the embalming-and-viewing question first, since dropping both is the biggest single saving.
  3. If money is short, ask the city or county social services office about § 32.1-309.1 before signing.
  4. For a veteran, set Quantico or Culpeper against the cost of a private plot.
  5. Write down the board's number, 804-367-4479, for any dispute over charges.

Frequently Asked Questions

What will a Virginia funeral cost in 2026?

We estimate a Virginia traditional funeral at about $8,200, roughly level with the $8,300 national median. A cremation with a ceremony comes to around $6,400, direct cremation about $1,900, and a plot adds around $4,000. Homes vary, so compare before choosing.

What does cremation save in Virginia?

At least $6,300. Direct cremation is about $1,900; the full service with burial is about $8,200, and the plot and vault are extra.

What protects a Virginia family buying a funeral?

Federal law, 16 CFR 453, guarantees a written itemized price list, quotes by phone, and acceptance of an outside casket or urn at no added charge. Virginia also lets families handle parts of a funeral themselves, according to the Funeral Consumers Alliance. The Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers, 804-367-4479, checks licenses and takes complaints.

Where can a Virginia family save most?

Shop around. Two or three Virginia price lists commonly disagree by $1,000 or more on the same services, and choosing direct cremation (about $1,900) cuts the total furthest.

Is embalming required in Virginia?

Almost never. Federal rules forbid a Virginia funeral director from presenting embalming as legally required when it isn't; refrigeration or a prompt burial are the usual alternatives. A home pressing for it should cite the regulation on paper.

Is Virginia expensive for funerals?

Not especially. Virginia's $8,200 traditional estimate is a hundred dollars under the $8,300 US median, and Southern states generally price at or below it.

What costs least in Virginia?

At roughly $1,900, direct cremation is Virginia's cheapest route; the fee pays for collecting the body, the cremation itself and handing back the ashes. Families set on a grave should know that direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $4,100 in Virginia, plus whatever the cemetery charges.

Which Virginia funeral home will be cheapest?

Phone three Virginia homes, get their General Price Lists, and line up the two cheapest items, direct cremation and immediate burial. If you buy a casket or urn online, the home must take it and cannot add a handling charge.
