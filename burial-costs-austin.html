Burial Costs in Austin, Texas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Texas

Austin's burial market has gotten more expensive faster than most comparable cities, and the reason is straightforward: the same population pressure that has reshaped Central Texas real estate has reshaped cemetery land availability inside the city limits. Plots at long-established Austin cemeteries price well above the Texas average, and new cemetery development has largely pushed out into Williamson, Hays, and Bastrop counties. Understanding where the line is drawn between the premium Austin options and the accessible Central Texas alternatives is now a meaningful part of planning a burial here.

Austin's cemetery inventory and the land-scarcity reality

The cemeteries most commonly used by Austin families:

  • Oakwood Cemetery — Austin's oldest cemetery, chartered 1839, operated by the City of Austin. Historic sections are full; limited new interment availability.
  • Austin Memorial Park Cemetery — City-operated on Hancock Drive in North Austin; limited remaining inventory.
  • Cook-Walden Capital Parks — large commercial memorial park in Pflugerville; mid-to-upper market pricing.
  • Assumption Cemetery — Roman Catholic Diocese of Austin cemetery on Interstate 35; mid-market.
  • Eloise Woods Community Natural Burial Park — in Cedar Creek (Bastrop County); one of Central Texas's earliest dedicated green burial grounds.
  • Central Texas State Veterans Cemetery — in Killeen, about an hour north; eligible veterans and spouses qualify for no-cost burial.

City of Austin cemetery pricing is published publicly and tends to sit below commercial alternatives. Whether space is available in a given section is a separate question; call the cemetery office before assuming.

Eloise Woods and the Central Texas green burial option

Eloise Woods Community Natural Burial Park in Cedar Creek has operated since 2010 as a conservation-style burial ground — no vaults, no embalming, biodegradable containers only, natural grave markers. Pricing runs materially below traditional Austin burial; the entire disposition (plot plus opening-and-closing) frequently comes in under $4,000. This is particularly relevant for Austin families whose environmental values align with a natural approach, but the economics also work for families prioritizing cost alone. Several Austin funeral homes have experience facilitating Eloise Woods burials and can handle the paperwork for an unembalmed remains transport.

Texas Funeral Service Commission: what it handles and what it doesn't

The Texas Funeral Service Commission regulates funeral establishments, embalmers, cemetery sellers, and pre-need sales. Austin consumer complaints — price disclosure failures, pre-need fund issues, unlicensed practice — route there. The commission's disciplinary-action page is public and updated regularly. The commission does not arbitrate private pricing disputes but a formal complaint often motivates a funeral home to revisit a contested invoice. For cemetery issues specifically, perpetual-care cemeteries fall within its jurisdiction; family cemeteries and small religious cemeteries sometimes don't.

Travis County programs: Travis County's indigent burial assistance is administered through Health and Human Services with modest funding. The Texas Crime Victims Compensation Program covers up to $6,500 in funeral expenses for qualifying violent-crime deaths, processed through the Office of the Attorney General. Starting these applications immediately — not after the service — is what determines whether families actually receive the funds.

Summer scheduling and the heat consideration

Austin's June-through-September heat pushes afternoon committal services off most cemetery schedules. Morning services are standard. Embalming is optional under Texas law when refrigeration is available; decline in writing if it doesn't fit your plans. Refrigeration at $50-$100/day still comes in well below a $720-$828 embalming charge for almost any timeline under a week.

A Few Questions We Keep Getting

How much does burial cost in Austin?

A traditional Austin burial totals $15,709-$22,103 — $8,280 funeral service, $3,910 plot, $2,484 casket, $1,564 vault, $1,955 opening-and-closing, $1,500-$3,500 for a monument. Burying through Eloise Woods in Cedar Creek or using the Central Texas State Veterans Cemetery in Killeen (for eligible veterans) reduces the number substantially. Direct burial at a Pflugerville or Bastrop cemetery with an online casket often lands in the $5,500-$7,500 range all-in.

Is a burial vault required in Austin?

Texas statute doesn't require vaults. Most Austin-area commercial cemeteries — Cook-Walden Capital Parks, Austin Memorial Park, Assumption — do require at least a liner. Eloise Woods waives the requirement entirely. Oakwood Cemetery's historic sections have their own older rules. Liners run $800-$1,500; full vaults climb to $10,000. Get the specific cemetery's spec in writing before accepting the funeral home's recommended vault product, which is often several tiers above what the cemetery actually requires.

What are the cheapest burial options in Austin?

Central Texas State Veterans Cemetery in Killeen is essentially no-cost for eligible veterans. Eloise Woods natural burial runs under $4,000 for the disposition itself. Direct burial at an outlying Travis, Williamson, or Bastrop county cemetery combined with a Costco or online casket routinely comes in at $5,500-$7,500 all-in. Austin's city-operated cemeteries (Oakwood, Austin Memorial Park) are generally cheaper than commercial options when new interment space is available — call to verify inventory.
