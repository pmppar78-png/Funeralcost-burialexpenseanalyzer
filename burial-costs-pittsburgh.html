Burial Costs in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Pennsylvania

Pittsburgh's topography — the rivers and the steep hillsides — shapes cemetery operations in ways most cost guides ignore. Allegheny Cemetery's 300 acres in Lawrenceville and Homewood Cemetery's 205 acres in Point Breeze both sit on graded slopes where opening-and-closing a grave costs more than on flat ground, and a half dozen of the Mon Valley's hill-built cemeteries routinely quote interment fees a step above Pennsylvania averages for the same reason. A 2026 traditional burial in Pittsburgh runs about $9,350 for the funeral service and $4,620 for a plot, with all-in totals between $18,128 and $25,553.

Allegheny, Homewood, and the Pittsburgh Cemetery Map

Allegheny Cemetery on Butler Street (founded 1844, one of the oldest rural cemeteries in the United States) is the anchor of Pittsburgh's nonprofit sector and prices plots and interment on a public fee schedule. Homewood Cemetery in Point Breeze (1878) is its architectural counterpart and runs a similarly transparent rate card. Calvary Catholic Cemetery on Hazelwood Avenue, along with the broader Catholic Cemeteries Association of the Diocese of Pittsburgh (St. Mary in Lawrenceville, Mount Carmel in Hopewell, Queen of Heaven in McMurray, and others), handles the majority of Catholic burials in the metro at parishioner pricing. Jewish burials cluster around Beth Shalom Cemetery and the network of congregational sections in Shaler and Penn Hills. National Cemetery of the Alleghenies in Bridgeville — opened 2005 and now serving most of southwestern Pennsylvania — provides plot, opening-and-closing, vault, and upright government marker at no cost to eligible veterans, saving roughly $8,000 on an otherwise conventional burial. Penn Forest Natural Burial Park in Verona is the metro's certified green burial ground and one of the first in the country.

The Hillside Surcharge and the Winter Question

Opening-and-closing fees in Pittsburgh trend higher than the Pennsylvania state average because so many of the metro's active cemeteries are cut into hillsides, which complicates equipment access and grave-wall stabilization. Pittsburgh's winter interment policies also vary more than in most metros: some cemeteries absorb the frozen-ground cost from December through March, others pass through $400–$800, and a small number require winter vault storage with a spring interment window. Ask every cemetery for the winter line item in writing before signing. Pennsylvania's 100 percent pre-need trust rule (or qualifying insurance backing) applies in Pittsburgh as everywhere in the state.

Practical Ways to Cut the Pittsburgh Bill

  • Request Allegheny Cemetery's fee schedule first: The public rate card becomes the benchmark for every other quote.
  • Price at least one Catholic Diocese cemetery: Calvary and the Catholic Cemeteries Association grounds typically offer parishioner plot pricing $1,000–$2,500 below the private memorial parks.
  • Check National Cemetery of the Alleghenies eligibility: The Bridgeville facility provides everything plot-side at no cost for qualifying veterans.
  • Consider Penn Forest for natural burial: Biodegradable container, no embalming, no vault — the full package usually totals $3,500–$5,500, meaningfully below any traditional Pittsburgh burial.
  • Confirm the winter line item in writing: Pittsburgh's frozen-ground policies are not standardized; ask for the number that would apply in the interment month.
  • Request opening-and-closing quotes from multiple cemeteries: Hillside grounds typically quote higher than flat-land grounds. If the family is flexible on location, the spread can be $800–$1,500.
  • Buy the casket from an independent retailer: FTC Funeral Rule bars any handling fee. Casket buying guide.
  • Allegheny County indigent burial: The Pennsylvania DHS Burial Benefit ($750) plus a county benefit of up to $750 combine for $1,500 for eligible low-income families. See our payment assistance guide.

Short Answers to Common Questions

How much does burial cost in Pittsburgh?

A 2026 traditional burial in Pittsburgh averages $9,350 for the funeral service plus $4,620 for a plot, with all-in totals between $18,128 and $25,553. Allegheny Cemetery and Catholic Diocese of Pittsburgh grounds typically sit at the lower end; North Hills and South Hills commercial memorial parks sit at the upper end. Expect a small hillside surcharge on opening-and-closing at many of the metro's older cemeteries.

Is a burial vault required in Pittsburgh?

Pennsylvania does not require a vault by statute. Most Pittsburgh cemeteries require either a vault or the cheaper grave liner as written policy. Penn Forest Natural Burial Park in Verona prohibits both — it is Pittsburgh's certified green burial ground. Allegheny Cemetery and Homewood Cemetery will sometimes accept a liner in lieu of a vault; always ask for the written interment specification before paying a deposit.

What are the cheapest burial options in Pittsburgh?

The lowest-cost Pittsburgh burials are typically at National Cemetery of the Alleghenies in Bridgeville (eligible veterans receive plot, vault, and marker at no cost), Penn Forest Natural Burial Park in Verona (roughly $3,500–$5,500 all-in for a certified green burial), or a parishioner plot at Calvary Catholic or another Diocese of Pittsburgh cemetery. Beyond that, Allegheny Cemetery's published nonprofit fee schedule is the natural benchmark for every other metro quote.
