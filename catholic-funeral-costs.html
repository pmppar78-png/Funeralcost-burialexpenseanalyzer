Catholic Funeral Requirements and Traditions

The Order of Christian Funerals sets out three rites: the vigil, the funeral liturgy (usually a Mass) and the committal at the grave. Which of them a family holds, and where, is what shapes the bill:

  • Burial is preferred, but cremation is allowed since 1963
  • Ashes must be interred in a cemetery, mausoleum, or columbarium, not scattered or kept at home
  • A funeral Mass is standard, typically celebrated by a parish priest
  • Open-casket viewing during the vigil is traditional but not required
  • The funeral typically takes place within 2-5 days of death

Our $7,000 to $15,000 range for a Catholic funeral is an estimate built for planning. No agency publishes it, and it does not come from the medians in the NFDA 2023 General Price List Study, which is still the most recent edition and was still quoted on NFDA's statistics page as of September 29, 2025. No diocesan price survey is cited for the Catholic total. The Catholic bracket sits around the NFDA median of $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, which leaves out the cemetery plot, the vault, and the monument, plus the Catholic rows in the table: a vigil, a funeral Mass offering, a casket, a Catholic cemetery plot, a vault, and a marker. The religion overview quotes this same $7,000 to $15,000 span. A parish family rarely pays for every line in the table, and several lines share costs, so the rows will not add up to the bracket; treat each cell as an estimate. Reviewed October 24, 2026.

Ways to Save on a Catholic Funeral

Parish families on a tight budget can also read our pages on affordable funeral options and payment assistance programs. When comparing funeral homes, ask each for its General Price List and compare the transfer, visitation and hearse lines, since those cover the vigil and the trip to church and cemetery.

Know Your Rights: The FTC Funeral Rule gives a Catholic family itemized prices, the right to decline items it does not want, and the right to use a casket or urn bought elsewhere. Those rules apply equally to a funeral home chosen by the parish and to one the family finds on its own.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Catholic funeral cost?

Plan on roughly $7,000 to $15,000, depending mainly on the cemetery, the casket and whether all three rites are held. Catholic parishes often have relationships with local funeral homes that may offer parishioner discounts.

Does the Catholic tradition require burial or cremation?

Catholic funerals follow a three-part structure: the vigil (wake), the funeral Mass, and the committal (burial). The Catholic Church has historically required burial over cremation, though since 1963 cremation has been permitted as long as ashes are interred in a sacred place; scattering of ashes is not allowed. The requirements section above explains the Church's rules on cremated remains, including interment in a cemetery, mausoleum or columbarium.

How can I save on a Catholic funeral?

Many Catholic cemeteries offer lower plot costs than private cemeteries. The church offering for the funeral Mass is typically a suggested donation, not a fixed fee. Catholic Charities and the St. Vincent de Paul Society may provide financial assistance for funeral costs in some dioceses.

Are there financial assistance programs for Catholic funerals?

Your parish office is the first place to ask about help. Staff at the parish office can tell you whether the parish or a local St. Vincent de Paul Society conference helps with funeral costs. Federal and state benefits sit alongside any parish support: Social Security's $255 lump-sum death payment, VA burial benefits for veterans and, in some states, Medicaid-related burial assistance, all described in our payment assistance guide.
