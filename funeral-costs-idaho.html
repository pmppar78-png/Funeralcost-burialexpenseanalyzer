By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: Mountain

The honest picture of Idaho funeral costs in 2026: a traditional service averages $6,700, cremation with ceremony sits near $5,400, and direct cremation can come in around $1,500. But averages hide the variance; in Idaho the same service can swing several thousand dollars between providers in the same city. This guide walks through what Idaho families actually pay and where the pricing gaps show up.

Payment Assistance in Idaho

  • An Idaho county pays an indigent burial or cremation at its own established or negotiated rate, and if no one takes charge of a body within 14 days, the coroner arranges it (Idaho Code § 31-3412).
  • To collect the one-time $255 from Social Security, an Idaho widow, widower or eligible child must call 1-800-772-1213, and the claim has to be made within two years.
  • VA reimburses part of an Idaho veteran's funeral through Form 21P-530EZ, filed by whoever paid; deaths not connected to service on or after October 1, 2026 qualify for up to $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for the plot, as our veteran burial benefits guide explains.

Rights Every Funeral Buyer Has in Idaho

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Idaho must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Idaho has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What Sets Idaho Apart (Mountain)

Mountain-state funeral pricing in Idaho (avg $6,700) is usually moderate, but the Boise area can run noticeably higher than rural parts of the state. Fuel and transportation appear on GPLs in Idaho more often than in denser regions; if the funeral home is more than 30 miles from the residence or cemetery, mileage charges can add $100-$400 to the final bill.

Funeral rules and help in Idaho

The Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses licenses funeral directors and establishments in Idaho; call 208-334-3233 to check a license or file a complaint. Licensing decisions there rest with the State Board of Morticians, whose members include a public member as well as licensed morticians, and its page links to the process for filing a complaint against a licensee.

Hiring a funeral home is a choice, not a legal requirement, for most steps of a funeral in Idaho; the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count Idaho among the nine states with funeral-director rules, and you can buy only the services you want.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Idaho sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (IDAPA 24.08.01.100; Idaho Code § 39-268).

Idaho Code § 31-3412 requires the board of county commissioners to provide a burial or cremation for an indigent person who dies in the county, paid at no more than the county's established or negotiated rate; if no one takes charge of a body within 14 days, the coroner arranges it and the county bears the unpaid cost. (Source: Idaho Code § 31-3412.) Social Security's one-time $255 death payment, paid only to a qualifying spouse or child, is small next to the $6,700 average Idaho funeral in the table.

An Idaho funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Snake River Canyon National Cemetery (208-732-7499) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemeteries, Idaho State Veterans Cemetery at Blackfoot (208-701-7161) or Idaho State Veterans Cemetery at Boise (208-780-1340), take requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in Idaho

  1. Compare at least two Idaho funeral homes line by line, using their printed price lists.
  2. Ask the Idaho funeral home whether embalming is actually needed for your plans.
  3. Ask the Idaho board of county commissioners about indigent help under § 31-3412 before choosing a funeral home.
  4. For a veteran, price Snake River Canyon National Cemetery against a private Idaho plot before choosing.
  5. Keep the board's number, 208-334-3233, in case a funeral home will not honor its price list.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in Idaho right now?

Current averages in Idaho come in at $6,700 for a traditional funeral, $5,400 for cremation with a service, and from $1,500 for direct cremation, with the traditional figure sitting 19% below the $8,300 US median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $3,000 when an Idaho family chooses burial.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Idaho?

In Idaho, direct cremation at $1,500 costs about $5,200 less than the $6,700 traditional funeral, and roughly $8,200 less once about $3,000 in cemetery costs is added.

What rights do Idaho families have when arranging a funeral?

The federal FTC Funeral Rule gives Idaho families the right to an itemized price list, to prices over the phone, and to buy only the goods and services they want. According to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, Idaho does not require families to hire a funeral director for every step. You can confirm an Idaho license or file a complaint with the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses at 208-334-3233.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Idaho?

Three moves cut the most: compare at least three Idaho General Price Lists side by side before choosing a provider, strip packages down to only the services you actually want, and choose direct cremation ($1,500) if cost is the priority. Home and family burial is legal in parts of Idaho with permits and can eliminate cemetery fees entirely.

Does Idaho require embalming?

Only if the body is held more than 24 hours without refrigeration. Idaho's funeral rules (IDAPA 24.08.01.100) require a body held longer than 24 hours before burial or cremation to be embalmed or kept at 36°F or colder. Health officials may also order or forbid embalming to control infectious disease (IDAPA 16.02.10.068).

Cremation in Idaho needs the county coroner's written authorization first. Embalming costs about $670 on this page's estimate; with refrigeration you can decline it for direct cremation or immediate burial.

Is Idaho cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Idaho's $6,700 traditional-funeral average is 19% below the $8,300 national median (NFDA 2023).

Which funeral option costs the least in Idaho?

Direct cremation carries the lowest price tag in Idaho, from roughly $1,500. Direct burial is the next step up at about $3,350, which is $3,350 below a traditional Idaho funeral.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Idaho?

Ask at least three Idaho providers for their General Price Lists and compare direct cremation and immediate burial lines. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Idaho home cannot charge a fee for it.
