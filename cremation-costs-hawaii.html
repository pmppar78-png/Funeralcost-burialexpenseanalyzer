What to Know Before Choosing Cremation in Hawaii

When considering cremation in Hawaii, keep these important points in mind:

  • Ashes: The cremated remains (typically 3-7 pounds) are returned to the family in a container or urn of your choice.
In Hawaii you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Hawaii bring a container, and the HI Dept. of Health is who you call if a home in Hawaii refuses it.

How to Save on Cremation in Hawaii

Cremation costs in Hawaii can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Hawaii: Request written General Price Lists.
  • Choose a flat-rate direct cremation provider: Hawaii has a mature, competitive direct cremation market. Flat-rate providers in and around Honolulu publish transparent pricing and rarely leave much room to negotiate lower; pick on turnaround and reputation rather than haggling.
  • Look into outdoor scattering options: Private land (with permission), Pacific scattering services, national forests with a free permit, and several Hawaii state parks allow ash scattering.

For more ways to reduce costs, see our affordable funeral options guide or learn how to pay for a funeral with no money.

Regional Context for Cremation in Hawaii

For a scattering at sea off Hawaii, the EPA's burial-at-sea rule requires going at least three nautical miles from shore and reporting the scattering to the EPA within 30 days. This flexibility is one reason Hawaii families so often choose cremation over burial. Dedicated scattering services in Hawaii typically run $200-$800 depending on location and witnessing arrangements.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical price of cremation in Hawaii?

Hawaii cremation pricing lives in two tiers: direct cremation — transport, cremation, and return of ashes — starting around $3,000, and full-service cremation with a ceremony landing near $8,200.

What kinds of cremation services can I choose in Hawaii?

Three paths are standard in Hawaii: (1) direct cremation — $3,000, just the cremation itself, no service; (2) cremation with memorial — cremation first, ceremony later at a location of your choice, priced in between; (3) cremation with full funeral — $8,200, ceremony and viewing before cremation.

What are Hawaii's cremation regulations?

In short, Hawaii has no fixed minimum waiting period, but the crematory still needs the death certificate, a cremation permit and a signed authorization from the next of kin or designated agent before it can proceed. For the exact Hawaii citation, the funeral-director question and who takes complaints, see the cremation rules and help section lower on this page.

Where are ashes allowed to be scattered in Hawaii?

Hawaii scattering rules depend on the specific location. Families in Hawaii also often opt for columbarium niches, burial of the urn, or keepsake jewelry in place of scattering.

What is driving Hawaii's cremation rate?

The cost delta alone explains a lot — direct cremation costs an estimated $8,000 less than a traditional funeral in Hawaii.

How long does cremation take in Hawaii?

In Hawaii, the cremation process itself takes 2 to 3 hours. Direct cremation ($3,000) has the shortest total timeline since no viewing or ceremony is involved.

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Hawaii?

Yes. A Hawaii family can hold a viewing or service before cremation, and many providers rent a casket for it. Plan the date around the law: Hawaii sets no waiting period before cremation; a health department permit is required and cannot issue until a death certificate is filed (HRS §§ 338-23, 338-25).

Cremation rules and help in Hawaii

The licensing office for cremation providers in Hawaii is the Hawaii Department of Health; its number is 808-586-8000.

Hiring a funeral home is a choice, not a legal requirement, for most steps in Hawaii; Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count it among the nine states with funeral-director rules. Hawaii paperwork and permits still apply.

Before a cremation can happen: Hawaii sets no waiting period before cremation; a health department permit is required and cannot issue until a death certificate is filed (HRS §§ 338-23, 338-25).

No verified statewide burial benefit is listed here for Hawaii. The county social-services or human-services office in Hawaii is the place to ask, and our assistance guide explains the federal options. The federal Social Security lump sum available in Hawaii is $255, paid only to a qualifying spouse or child.

A Hawaii veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. One option in Hawaii is National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (808-532-3720, cremated remains only). See cem.va.gov.

What to do next

  1. Ask every Hawaii provider for its General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule says they must give it to you.
  2. Ask the provider how long the Hawaii cremation permit usually takes where you live.
  3. Ask your Hawaii county human-services office about indigent burial help before you commit.
  4. Keep the board's number, 808-586-8000, in case a provider will not honor its price list.
