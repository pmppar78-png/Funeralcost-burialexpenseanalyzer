Burial Costs in Las Vegas, Nevada (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Nevada

Las Vegas is the most cremation-dominant major city in the country — Clark County cremation rates hover near 72% — and that shapes everything about how burial works here. Fewer working cemeteries, fewer full-service funeral homes doing traditional burial volume, and a Nevada Funeral and Cemetery Services Board that regulates a comparatively small pool of licensees. When a family still wants traditional burial, the realistic 2026 total in Clark County is $16,742 to $23,562. Palm Eastern Mortuary & Cemetery, Woodlawn Cemetery on Las Vegas Boulevard North, and Davis Memorial Park are the anchor operators; the Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City is the primary veterans option.

Clark County traditional burial totals in 2026 sit between $16,742 and $23,562. The pricing is higher than Nevada's statewide average because the Las Vegas metro concentrates nearly all of the state's commercial cemetery capacity — rural Nevada cemeteries exist but are impractical for metro families.

The Las Vegas cemetery short list

Palm Eastern Mortuary & Cemetery on East Eastern Avenue is a combined funeral home and cemetery and the largest single operator in the valley. Plot pricing runs $3,500–$6,500 in standard sections with mausoleum crypts and premium areas going higher. Woodlawn Cemetery on Las Vegas Boulevard North — the city's oldest active cemetery, opened 1914 — carries a historic-section premium similar to Palm Eastern's higher tiers. Davis Memorial Park on North Jones Boulevard, Palm Downtown Mortuary & Cemetery (Main Street), and Bunkers Memory Gardens round out the primary inventory. Several of these operators have changed hands — Palm Mortuary is owned by SCI/Dignity Memorial — and their pricing reflects corporate chain markups.

The Catholic Diocese of Las Vegas operates Our Lady of Mercy Cemetery in northeast Las Vegas, which typically prices plots at $2,800–$4,200 with diocesan services included. Outside the metro, small rural cemeteries in Searchlight, Sandy Valley, Logandale, and Moapa exist but have very limited capacity and are rarely used by metro families.

Desert-climate cemetery economics

Las Vegas cemeteries spend significantly on irrigation and landscaping to keep memorial parks green in the Mojave — water rates from the Las Vegas Valley Water District have climbed every year for a decade. That cost is built into plot pricing and perpetual-care fees here in a way that doesn't apply in Midwestern or Eastern cemeteries. Ground burial in Clark County requires a sealed concrete vault at almost every operator; caliche-layer soil makes grave-opening slower than in non-desert markets, which is why opening-and-closing fees in Las Vegas run $1,700–$2,400, toward the higher end nationally.

Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery. In Boulder City, 30 miles southeast of downtown Las Vegas, this state-operated veterans cemetery provides no-cost burial for eligible Nevada-resident veterans and $300 plot cost for spouses and dependents. Combined with federal VA burial benefits, a qualifying veteran family's out-of-pocket can fall under $2,500 all-in. The cemetery is operated by the Nevada Department of Veterans Services; interments are scheduled directly through the cemetery office rather than the national VA scheduling line.

Where the Las Vegas bill moves

  1. Corporate chain dominance. Palm Mortuary, Bunkers, and most major Las Vegas operators are chain-owned. Independent alternatives exist — Kraft-Sussman Funeral Services, Affordable Cremation & Burial Services — and their GPL pricing typically runs $1,500–$3,500 below the chains for equivalent service packages. Under the FTC Funeral Rule every Las Vegas funeral home must provide a GPL on request.
  2. Cremation leverage. With 72% of Clark County families choosing cremation, burial-focused providers have pricing flexibility they don't advertise. Opening the conversation by mentioning that direct cremation is your alternative sometimes surfaces better burial pricing, particularly at independents.
  3. Casket sourcing. Online casket retailers ship to Las Vegas. Walmart, Costco, Titan Casket, and Overstock deliver to Clark County funeral homes, which cannot refuse or charge a handling fee under the FTC rule. Savings $800–$2,500.
  4. Vault type. Virtually every Clark County cemetery requires a sealed concrete vault — not a liner. Shop vault pricing separately; third-party vault companies exist and can undercut cemetery pricing by $400–$900.
  5. Pre-need contracts. Nevada's Division of Insurance oversees pre-need funeral contracts. Nevada Revised Statutes require pre-need funds to be held in trust, but contract review before signing is the single best consumer-protection step in a market dominated by chain operators.

Green burial in the Mojave

True green burial is effectively unavailable within Clark County — no certified natural burial grounds currently operate in the Las Vegas Valley. Private land burial is legal under Nevada Revised Statutes Chapter 451 subject to county health department approval and a 150-yard setback from water sources, but the practical caliche soil and desert conditions make farm-style burial logistically difficult. Families wanting a green-burial approach typically either cremate locally (with eventual scattering) or transport remains to a Southern California or Utah natural burial ground — the Sky's End Cemetery near Corralitos (northern California) and several Utah Wasatch Front options are within reasonable transport range.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Las Vegas?

A 2026 Las Vegas traditional burial lands between $16,742 and $23,562. Direct burial at an independent like Kraft-Sussman combined with a Davis Memorial Park or Our Lady of Mercy plot can close under $10,500. Full service at Palm Eastern with a premium casket, mausoleum crypt, and bronze monument can exceed $28,000. The Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City removes most of the bill for eligible Nevada-resident veterans.

Is a burial vault required in Las Vegas?

Nevada state law does not require a vault, but virtually every Clark County cemetery requires a sealed concrete vault rather than a liner. Palm Eastern, Woodlawn, Davis Memorial Park, Bunkers, and Palm Downtown all enforce sealed-vault policies. The caliche soil layer in the Las Vegas Valley makes vaulted interment the local norm. Confirm specific requirements in writing with the cemetery before ordering, and shop vault pricing separately — third-party vault companies can undercut cemetery pricing.

What are the cheapest burial options in Las Vegas?

Las Vegas is expensive for traditional burial because of its chain-dominated cemetery inventory and sealed-vault requirements. The lowest-cost routes: direct burial through an independent (Kraft-Sussman, Affordable Cremation & Burial) paired with a Our Lady of Mercy or Davis Memorial Park plot, casket purchased online under the FTC Funeral Rule, and shopped vault from a third-party vault company. For eligible veterans, Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City is the single largest cost reducer available.

How do Las Vegas burial costs compare to Nevada?

Las Vegas runs about 10% above the Nevada statewide average — funeral service $8,800 vs. $8,000, plot $4,180 vs. $3,800. Reno and the Carson Valley price slightly below Las Vegas. Rural Nevada counties (Elko, White Pine, Lincoln, Nye) have much cheaper plot options at township and community cemeteries but are impractical for most Las Vegas-metro families. The 72% Clark County cremation rate means metro-Vegas burial pricing is effectively a concentrated commercial market.
