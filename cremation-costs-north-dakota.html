Cremation Costs in North Dakota (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 55%

North Dakota's funeral economy is small, concentrated, and unusually regulated by a single board — the ND State Board of Funeral Service oversees every licensed funeral home, embalmer, and crematory in the state. Cremation volumes have grown steadily; at 55%, the state now tracks above the national average, with Fargo-area providers like Boulger Funeral Home handling most of the Red River Valley's demand. Direct cremation statewide averages $1,500 and cremation with a full service averages $5,300. The 24-hour wait between death and cremation is among the shortest in the country.

The $1,500 direct cremation figure is unusually consistent across North Dakota's 50+ licensed funeral homes — a flatness that reflects both the state's small market and the ND State Board of Funeral Service's single-regulator oversight. Families in smaller towns like Minot, Bismarck, and Grand Forks should still request a written GPL before signing anything; low variance does not mean no variance.

The ND Board of Funeral Service as Sole Regulator

North Dakota consolidates funeral home, embalmer, and crematory licensing under the ND State Board of Funeral Service — no separate cemetery commission, no split between health department and professional board. When a complaint arises, it goes to one agency. Boulger Funeral Home in Fargo and its peers file with the same licensing authority, which keeps compliance costs predictable and tends to compress the direct cremation floor.

24-Hour Wait and the Red River Valley Cycle

North Dakota imposes a statutory 24-hour wait between death and cremation — the same as Louisiana, half of New York's. Paired with the short Fargo-Moorhead crematory run, a direct cremation authorized Monday morning routinely returns ashes by Thursday. The practical exception is winter: when deaths occur in remote rural counties, ground-transport delays during blizzard season can add a day or two even at a well-organized provider.

County Welfare and Indigent Cremation

North Dakota does not operate a statewide indigent-cremation fund. Instead, the 53 county social services agencies each administer a burial/cremation assistance program under state guidelines. Cass County (Fargo) typically pays up to $1,200; Burleigh County (Bismarck) slightly less. The ND Department of Human Services sets the floor, but counties decide the cap. Boulger and most area providers handle the paperwork when asked.

How to Save on Cremation in North Dakota

  • Get written GPLs from three providers: Boulger Funeral Home (Fargo) and two others — variance is smaller than most states but not zero.
  • Apply to county social services early: Cass, Burleigh, and Grand Forks counties administer separately; caps differ.
  • Skip embalming for direct cremation: Not required under ND Board of Funeral Service rules or the FTC Funeral Rule.
  • Choose a cemetery columbarium over a funeral home urn garden: Typically $400–$1,500.
  • Buy urns independently: See our urn buying guide.
  • Check assistance programs: Veteran benefits, Medicaid assistance, and other programs may cover part or all of cremation costs.

Questions From Families

What does cremation cost in North Dakota in 2026?

Direct cremation averages $1,500 statewide; cremation with a full funeral service averages $5,300. Add $50–$300 for a basic urn, $500–$2,000 for a memorial service, and $400–$1,500 for columbarium interment. Pricing is unusually flat across Fargo, Bismarck, Minot, and Grand Forks because the ND State Board of Funeral Service is a single regulator.

What types of cremation are available in North Dakota?

Direct cremation at $1,500 (no ceremony, ashes returned after the 24-hour wait). Cremation with a memorial held later at a church, lodge, or community hall — very common in smaller ND towns. Traditional cremation with full service at $5,300 through Fargo providers like Boulger Funeral Home. The single-regulator state means all three tiers are offered with near-identical paperwork.

What legal requirements apply to cremation in North Dakota?

North Dakota requires a 24-hour wait between death and cremation, written authorization from the legal next of kin, and pacemaker/defibrillator removal. The ND State Board of Funeral Service — the single regulator for funeral homes, embalmers, and crematories — enforces these rules. No casket is required under the FTC Funeral Rule; an alternative cremation container suffices.

Is ash scattering legal in North Dakota?

Yes, on private land with consent, on the Missouri and Red Rivers without a permit, and on national grassland like the Little Missouri with a National Forest permit. Theodore Roosevelt National Park allows scattering with advance written permission. Columbarium niche interment at church cemeteries across Fargo, Bismarck, and Grand Forks typically runs $400–$1,500 as a straightforward alternative.

Why is cremation growing in North Dakota?

At 55%, ND tracks above the national average — a meaningful shift for a state with strong Lutheran and Catholic traditions. Winter ground-burial logistics (frozen soil, delayed interment until spring) push many families toward cremation with later inurnment. The $3,800 gap between direct cremation and full service is also a significant driver in a rural economy.

What is the timeline for cremation in North Dakota?

The 24-hour statutory wait, the cremation itself (2–3 hours), and 3–5 business days for ash return. Boulger Funeral Home in Fargo typically returns ashes within a week. Winter weather and remote-county transport can add a day or two. Direct cremation at $1,500 has the shortest end-to-end timeline.

Do I have to skip the service if I choose cremation in North Dakota?

No. Cremation with a full funeral service runs $5,300 — ceremony before cremation — and most ND funeral homes including Boulger offer it. A later memorial at a church, Legion hall, or family home is the more common choice, typically $500–$2,000. Only direct cremation at $1,500 omits the ceremony entirely.
