Burial Costs in Kansas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 Region: Midwest

A Kansas burial starts with three figures on our 2026 estimates: about $6,600 for the funeral service, about $2,900 for a cemetery plot, and $500-$5,000 for a headstone. Once the vault, grave opening, casket and headstone are counted, the Kansas total comes to $14,590 to $19,090.

A burial is billed by two businesses, the funeral home and the cemetery. NFDA's 2023 national median for a funeral with viewing and burial is $8,300 ($6,280 when cremation replaces burial), or $9,995 once a vault is included, and the plot, grave opening and marker are charged on top. Embalming, with a national median of $845, is usually only worth paying for when there will be a viewing or a delay.

With every cemetery fee included, a Kansas burial usually lands between $14,590 and $19,090, depending on the choices the family makes.

Kansas Burial Choices Compared

  • Traditional burial: A traditional funeral with viewing, body preparation, and the funeral ceremony before cemetery burial. This higher-cost, full service burial has a national median of $8,300 (NFDA 2023), or $9,995 with a vault, before local cemetery choices push totals higher.
  • Direct burial: Also called immediate burial, this low-service option means the body is buried shortly after death without embalming, viewing, or ceremony. It typically costs about $1,500 to $4,000.
  • Learn about green burial

For comparison, direct cremation has an NFDA 2023 median of $2,645 with a family-supplied container, while a funeral with viewing and cremation has an NFDA 2023 median of $6,280; families who want to estimate and compare these choices across locations can use our funeral cost calculator for cremation, burial, and services.

On average cremation saves about $6,390 compared with burial, and it avoids ongoing cemetery costs. Cremation is expected to account for more than half of U.S. deaths by 2045.

Cutting the Cost of a Kansas Burial

Where Kansas families find savings:

  • Ask cemeteries for their own prices. The FTC Funeral Rule does not reach cemeteries, but most in Kansas hand over a price sheet if asked.
  • Ring the township or county clerk. Many Kansas township, county and lodge cemeteries are not listed online at all.
  • Try a family-owned home. Independents are still common in Kansas and tend to price nearer the NFDA medians than chain-owned locations.
  • Consider a direct burial. Burying promptly with no viewing or ceremony is a common, accepted choice in Kansas.
  • Look hard at the casket. Since 1984 the FTC Funeral Rule has required funeral homes to itemize their charges, so the casket price is shown on its own. A metal casket has a national median of $2,500 (NFDA 2023), premium models cost several times that, and a simpler one saves real money.

How Burial Pricing Works Across Kansas

Midwestern funeral pricing in Kansas is generally transparent: independent family-owned funeral homes still dominate here more than in the coasts, and they tend to be more willing to itemize and negotiate, especially if you follow a structured approach like our step-by-step guide to comparing funeral prices. Geographic location influences both funeral expenses and cemetery charges, even within Kansas, so a GPL walkthrough on the phone with two or three Kansas providers is usually enough to see where the price points sit, identify the package markups, and ask how cemetery fees and burial plots differ by area.

Town and nonprofit cemeteries generally charge less than corporate ones, so call both kinds.

Location matters inside the state too. Our Kansas estimate for the funeral service is about $6,600, under NFDA’s $8,300 national median, yet a Johnson County or Wichita home may quote more than one in a small western Kansas town. Our Kansas funeral cost guide breaks out the same service by type.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a Kansas burial cost in total?

Start with about $6,600 for the funeral service and $2,900 for the plot. Add the vault ($1,160), grave opening and closing ($1,450), casket ($1,980) and headstone ($500-$5,000), and a Kansas burial usually totals $14,590 to $19,090.

Does Kansas law require a burial vault?

No Kansas statute requires a vault or liner. Each Kansas cemetery sets its own rule, so ask the sexton or cemetery board for it in writing before assuming you need one, and ask whether a liner counts.

Kansas regulates cemetery money rather than cemetery merchandise. Cemetery corporations register with the Kansas Secretary of State, keep a permanent maintenance fund (K.S.A. 17-1312) and file quarterly sales reports (K.S.A. 17-1312a). If a Kansas funeral home tells you state law requires a vault, that claim is barred by the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR 453.3(c)).

How do Kansas families lower burial costs?

In addition, many families use funeral planning to plan ahead through prepaid funeral plans or pre-need arrangements that lock in current prices and reduce final expenses. These plans are usually tied to a specific funeral home or company and can lower stress for relatives later.

How should I compare Kansas cemeteries?

Cemetery costs in Kansas vary significantly even within the same city, and for families planning a service without life insurance or burial insurance this variation can feel overwhelming, making it especially important to understand funeral costs when uninsured. Ask for the funeral home service fee or basic services fee separately so no service fee is mixed into burial plots or other cemetery charges. The death certificate and other funeral arrangements paperwork can also create small administrative charges that should appear on the itemized bill. In particular, township, county, and fraternal cemeteries in Kansas are often only listed through the county clerk and frequently price plots well below commercial memorial parks.

What does a complete Kansas burial add up to?

Adding every line item, burial in Kansas usually totals $14,590 to $19,090: funeral service about $6,600, plot about $2,900, casket about $1,980, vault about $1,160, opening-and-closing about $1,450, and headstone $500-$5,000. Veterans may qualify for burial benefits, including burial in a VA national cemetery or another national cemetery with a free gravesite and headstone. In some cases, veterans can also receive up to $2,000 for burial expenses. Social Security may pay a one-time $255 benefit to an eligible surviving spouse after the person died. FEMA funeral assistance may apply for disaster-related deaths. State programs, local departments, and local charities may offer financial assistance to cover funeral costs, including cremation or burial aid for low-income families.

Is green burial possible in Kansas?

Green burial sections, which take a biodegradable container and skip embalming and the vault, are spreading across Kansas cemeteries and usually total less than a conventional burial; where no such ground is close, some Kansas families pick a hybrid, a regular plot with no embalming. Part of any lot price is earmarked by statute. K.S.A. 17-1301c defines the share a cemetery corporation must put into its permanent maintenance fund as 15% of a burial lot's purchase price with a $25 floor, 10% (at least $100) of a community mausoleum crypt and 5% (at least $50) of a garden mausoleum crypt or niche.

Kansas death certificates for the burial paperwork

The cemetery, the bank and any insurer will each want a certified death certificate. The Office of Vital Statistics at the Kansas Department of Health and Environment charges $20 for every certified copy, including each additional copy, under K.A.R. 28-17-6. The first $20 is a search fee covering five years of records; if no record is found, the office keeps it (KDHE Office of Vital Statistics; checked October 29, 2026).

Ordering through VitalChek or by phone at 877-305-8315 adds a $15 expedited fee, and orders through the state's IKAN mobile app cost $20 plus $5. Walk-in service at the Topeka office runs 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays.

  • Kansas does not discount extra copies, so five certificates cost $100 whichever route you use; count how many you really need.
  • Ask the funeral home what it charges per copy, and compare that with the $20 state fee.

Who oversees Kansas burials, and where to turn

Funeral homes that arrange Kansas burials are licensed by the Kansas State Board of Mortuary Arts (785-296-3980), which can also point you to the right office for a cemetery complaint.

No Kansas law makes a family hire a funeral director for a burial; the state is not among the nine on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list. That means you can deal with a cemetery yourself on the plot, the opening of the grave and the container it will take. A disposition permit is still needed for burial, and if the plan changes to cremation there is no set waiting period, only a permit (K.S.A. 65-1762; 65-2426a).

Unclaimed bodies and county burial

If no one claims the body, the county coroner arranges the burial, and the county general fund covers whatever the person's own property and the state assistance program do not (K.S.A. 22a-215; K.S.A. 22a-215; K.S.A. 39-713d). The $255 Social Security payment to a spouse or child would not cover even the $2,900 plot in our Kansas estimate.

Where Kansas veterans can be buried free

An eligible veteran gets the grave, its opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no charge in a national cemetery. Kansas has three: Leavenworth (913-727-1376), Fort Scott (913-212-4612) and Fort Leavenworth (913-684-4348, cremated remains only). The state runs four more veterans cemeteries, at Fort Dodge (620-682-7564), Fort Riley near Manhattan (785-717-3050), WaKeeney (785-743-5685) and Winfield (620-705-6300) (VA National Cemetery Administration).

Before buying a Kansas plot

  1. Get the cemetery's prices separately; plot, opening, vault and marker are often not on the funeral home's list.
  2. Ask for the cemetery's written rules on opening hours, vaults and markers.
  3. If there is no money, call the county coroner before anyone buys a plot.
  4. For a veteran, check eligibility at Leavenworth or a state veterans cemetery first.
  5. If a funeral home misstates burial costs, the board's line is 785-296-3980.
