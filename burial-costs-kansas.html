Burial Costs in Kansas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Midwest

Kansas keeps burial costs closer to the national floor than most states. The Kansas State Board of Mortuary Arts licenses funeral directors and embalmers; cemetery pricing sits outside that board's jurisdiction and is managed at the municipal, church, or township level. That split is actually an advantage — a Kansas family will often pay urban funeral-home rates in Wichita, Topeka, or Overland Park, then bury at a rural or township cemetery where plot fees still read like they were set in 1995. The realistic all-in total in 2026 runs $12,110 to $16,990.

Kansas totals sit between $12,110 and $16,990 — one of the lowest mid-range bands in the country. The rural-urban spread is the defining feature here.

Wichita, Topeka, Overland Park: where the metro pricing lives

Maple Grove Cemetery in Wichita (1886), Old Mission Cemetery in Wichita, and Highland Cemetery in Junction City are among the oldest active cemeteries in Kansas and their pricing reflects their historic land value. Topeka Cemetery, established 1859 and the oldest in the state, runs a premium section tier that tops $5,500 for certain plots. Overland Park has several Johnson County cemetery companies — Shawnee Mission Memory Gardens, Floral Hills, and the Catholic archdiocesan Resurrection Cemetery among them — typically pricing standard plots $2,800–$3,800.

Step outside the metros and the pricing inverts. Kansas has more than 4,000 active cemeteries — most of them township, pioneer, church, or fraternal (Odd Fellows, Masons, GAR). A plot at a rural township cemetery in Butler, Reno, Saline, or Cowley County frequently lists at $500–$1,200, including perpetual care. Kansas counties — through the county clerk or register of deeds — maintain reasonably current lists of cemeteries within the county and can point families to the custodian contact.

Fort Leavenworth and Leavenworth National Cemetery

Kansas hosts two national cemeteries within a mile of each other: Leavenworth National Cemetery (larger, accepting new interments) and Fort Leavenworth National Cemetery (older, capacity-constrained). For eligible veterans the grave, vault, marker, and opening-and-closing are free; spouses are eligible to be buried alongside under the standard VA benefit. Kansas also operates a state veterans cemetery system with locations in WaKeeney, Fort Dodge, Fort Riley, and Winfield for families outside the Leavenworth catchment.

The Kansas township cemetery advantage. Kansas Statute 15-1001 et seq. authorizes townships to establish and maintain cemeteries as a government function. Many of these cemeteries are still governed by an elected township board and set plot fees based on covering mowing, fencing, and record-keeping — not on commercial margins. Plot fees under $800 are common, and residency requirements are sometimes waived for former residents or their descendants. Call the township clerk for the township where burial will occur.

Natural burial in Kansas

Greenacres Memorial Park in Wichita and several smaller church cemeteries across eastern Kansas accept green-burial requests (no embalming, biodegradable container, no vault). Private-land burial is legal in Kansas under KSA 17-1302, subject to county setbacks and deed recording. Across the Flint Hills and western Kansas tallgrass country, private farmstead burials — subject to county health department approval — remain a legitimate option for rural families.

Kansas funeral pricing under the FTC rule

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, every Kansas funeral home must provide a General Price List to any consumer on request. Getting two or three GPLs from Kansas independents — Downing & Lahey in Wichita, Broadway Mortuary, Penwell-Gabel in Topeka — side-by-side with a corporate chain-owned quote (Dignity Memorial operates several Kansas locations) typically reveals $1,500–$3,000 of negotiable difference on identical service packages. Under the same rule, Kansas funeral homes cannot refuse a third-party casket and cannot charge a handling fee — a straightforward $800–$2,500 saving for families willing to order online.

Reader Questions

How much does burial cost in Kansas?

The 2026 Kansas traditional burial range is $12,110 to $16,990. A direct burial arranged at a Wichita or Topeka independent using a township cemetery plot can close under $7,000. At the top end, a full service at Topeka Cemetery or Maple Grove in Wichita with a premium casket and bronze monument can push $19,000. Leavenworth National Cemetery removes the grave, vault, marker, and opening-closing entirely for eligible veterans.

Are burial vaults mandatory in Kansas?

No state statute requires them. Urban commercial cemeteries in Wichita, Topeka, and the Kansas City metro almost uniformly require either a vault or a concrete liner; the liner is roughly 40% of the vault cost. Rural Kansas township cemeteries frequently still permit direct-in-ground interment with no outer enclosure — a real cost difference that shows up most on Flint Hills, Western Kansas, and Sunflower State church cemeteries. Confirm in writing with the specific cemetery before ordering.

How can I reduce burial costs in Kansas?

The single largest Kansas lever is cemetery choice: township cemeteries under KSA 15-1001 et seq. frequently list plots at $500–$1,200 versus $2,800+ at urban commercial memorial parks. Call the township clerk for the township where burial will occur. The second lever is casket sourcing under the FTC Funeral Rule, which saves $800–$2,500. The third is shopping 2–3 funeral home GPLs — Kansas independents (Downing & Lahey, Broadway Mortuary, Penwell-Gabel) routinely come in below corporate chain pricing.

How do I compare cemetery costs in Kansas?

Call the county clerk or register of deeds first — they maintain the only complete list of cemeteries within each Kansas county, including pioneer, township, Odd Fellows, Masonic, and GAR grounds that never appear in an internet search. Request written price sheets covering plot, opening-closing, vault policy, perpetual care, and any residency or denominational restrictions. Between a Johnson County commercial memorial park and a small Butler or Cowley County township cemetery, plot pricing can differ by 500% on identical plot dimensions.

What is an all-in burial estimate for Kansas?

Adding funeral service ($6,600), plot ($2,900), casket ($1,980), vault ($1,160), opening-and-closing ($1,450), and headstone ($1,000–$3,000) gives a 2026 Kansas total of $12,110 to $16,990. Township-cemetery-plus-direct-burial families regularly land between $6,500 and $9,000 all-in. The Kansas state veterans cemetery system (WaKeeney, Fort Dodge, Fort Riley, Winfield) is free for qualifying veterans.
