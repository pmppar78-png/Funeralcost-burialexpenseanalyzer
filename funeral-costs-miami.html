Funeral Costs in Miami, Florida (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Florida

Miami's funeral market is the product of a specific demographic history: Caballero Rivero has served the Cuban-American community since 1857, Van Orsdel is the English-speaking Miami establishment, Stanfill anchors Coral Gables, and Beth David Memorial Gardens serves South Florida's Jewish community. Florida's DFS Division of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services layers its 50% pre-need trust requirement and 48-hour cremation wait on top. 2026 pricing lands at $9,750 traditional, $2,250 direct cremation, and a 62% statewide cremation rate — with the Neptune Memorial Reef sitting as the most distinctive local cremation alternative in the country.

Neptune Memorial Reef: Miami's $6,500 Ocean Option

Three and a quarter miles off Key Biscayne, Neptune Memorial Reef is an underwater cemetery — a designed artificial reef where cremated remains are mixed with cement and set as part of the reef structure. A full sea interment package, including cremation, transport, and placement, runs around $6,500. The reef is now home to thousands of sea interments; Miami is the only U.S. city where this option is offered at scale. Caballero Rivero, Van Orsdel, and several direct-cremation specialists can coordinate with Neptune Society for the reef placement. Florida's 48-hour cremation wait applies normally.

Florida's 50% Pre-Need Trust Rule

Florida law requires funeral homes to trust at least 50% of funds collected for pre-need arrangements through the DFS Division. For Miami families considering pre-planning, this is a consumer-protection layer above what most states require — your funds are insulated from the provider's general operations. Caballero Rivero, Van Orsdel, and Stanfill all publish pre-need contracts under Florida Chapter 497. Ask the specific trust-company name on any Miami pre-need contract; it should be verifiable through the DFS database.

Miami's Cultural Funeral Traditions and Their Costs

Cuban-American Catholic tradition emphasizes a full wake, often over multiple days, with open casket and rosary services — Caballero Rivero has built its Miami pricing around this pattern, and traditional-funeral totals there routinely exceed $10,000. Beth David Memorial Gardens handles Miami's Jewish community under the tradition of same-day or next-day burial, which simplifies scheduling but prices burial at Beth David slightly above inland Dade County cemeteries. Haitian, Dominican, and Venezuelan families in Miami often repatriate remains, which adds $1,500–$3,500 in international transport — something most GPLs itemize only on request.

Your Rights in Miami

FTC Funeral Rule Protection Every funeral home in Miami must provide an itemized General Price List, let you choose only the services you want, and accept caskets purchased elsewhere. You are never required to buy a package. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

Regional Context: Funeral Pricing in the South

Florida's 48-hour cremation wait — imposed by DFS rule and typical of a few Southern states — forces a specific timeline on every Miami direct cremation. Refrigeration during that window runs $70–$110 per day at most Miami-Dade providers, versus a $975 embalming line. Florida's humidity makes refrigeration the standard choice when families are pacing their decisions, and the statute specifically allows refrigeration in lieu of embalming. Ask every Miami provider whether the 48-hour wait is factored into the published direct-cremation price — Caballero Rivero, Van Orsdel, and Neptune Society generally include it; smaller providers may not.

Charges to push back on

Many families in Miami encounter unexpected charges. Watch for these common hidden fees:

  • Casket handling fees — Illegal under the FTC Funeral Rule, but some providers still add them
  • Mandatory embalming claims — Almost never legally required. Know your consumer rights
  • Package markups — Bundles often include unnecessary services. Compare line items. What funeral homes don't tell you
  • Weekend/holiday surcharges — Can add 15–25% to the total cost

Common Questions We Hear

How much does a funeral cost in Miami?

Miami 2026 traditional-funeral averages run $9,750 — 25% above the $7,800 Florida figure and meaningfully higher than Jacksonville or Tampa. Caballero Rivero, Van Orsdel, Stanfill, and Beth David are the longtime Miami-Dade providers; the Neptune Memorial Reef off Key Biscayne provides a distinctive $6,500 ocean-reef alternative. Direct cremation starts at $2,250, cemetery plots average $4,500 inland and substantially more in Coral Gables or Key Biscayne.

Do more Miami families choose cremation or burial?

Miami's cremation rate sits slightly below Florida's 62% because of the area's large Catholic and Latin communities — Caballero Rivero has served Miami's Cuban-American Catholic community since 1857. The Neptune Memorial Reef is a notable Miami-specific cremation alternative: cremated remains incorporated into an underwater reef off Key Biscayne, about $6,500 all in. For traditional burial, Woodlawn Park and Flagler Memorial Park remain the dominant options.

Where does Miami rank nationally for funeral pricing?

Miami's $9,750 traditional average lands about 24% above the $7,848 national median — one of the ten most expensive metros in the country. Florida's 50% pre-need trust requirement and the state's mandatory 48-hour cremation wait under DFS Division of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services add compliance structure. Shopping GPLs across at least three Miami-Dade providers typically exposes $1,500–$2,500 in line-item differences.

Where can I compare funeral home prices in Miami?

Florida's DFS Division of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services licenses every Miami funeral home and publishes complaint records. Request GPLs by phone or email from Caballero Rivero, Van Orsdel on Coral Way, Stanfill in Coral Gables, and Beth David Memorial Gardens in Hollywood. The Funeral Consumers Alliance of Southern Florida publishes a comparison specific to Miami-Dade.

How much is direct cremation in Miami?

Direct cremation in Miami is $2,250 — transport, the mandatory Florida 48-hour wait, the crematory fee, the DFS permit, and return of cremated remains. Caballero Rivero and Van Orsdel publish direct-cremation tiers; several Miami-specific direct-cremation specialists like Neptune Society operate at or below that price. The Neptune Memorial Reef sea interment tier runs about $6,500 total and is the most distinctive Miami-only option.

How can Miami families find low-cost funeral options?

Direct cremation at $2,250 is the cheapest Miami disposition. Florida DCF offers funeral assistance for qualifying families, Social Security pays $255, and VA benefits at South Florida National Cemetery in Lake Worth include free interment. Third-party caskets via Costco or online cut $1,200 or more off the $2,925 Miami casket line. Burial in Miami-Dade municipal cemeteries like Miami City Cemetery runs $800–$1,500 below Woodlawn Park for equivalent lots.
