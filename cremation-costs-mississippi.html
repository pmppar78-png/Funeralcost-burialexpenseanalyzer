Cremation Costs in Mississippi (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Cremation Rate: 28%

Mississippi has the lowest cremation rate in the nation at approximately 28%, a figure that reflects the state's deeply rooted Baptist and evangelical Christian culture, where traditional burial has been the overwhelmingly preferred disposition for generations. However, even in Mississippi, cremation is growing -- the rate has nearly doubled over the past decade as economic pressures, changing attitudes, and the sheer cost of traditional burial have caused more families to consider cremation. Direct cremation in Mississippi averages $1,400, making it one of the most affordable states in the country for cremation services. Mississippi requires a 24-hour waiting period before cremation, and the state's regulatory framework is relatively straightforward.

What Drives Cremation Costs in Mississippi

Mississippi's cremation market is shaped by the state's unique cultural and economic landscape:

  • 24-hour waiting period: Mississippi requires a relatively short 24-hour wait before cremation, keeping storage-related costs lower than in states with longer waiting periods.
  • Lowest cost of living in the nation: Mississippi consistently ranks as the most affordable state in the U.S. This low cost of living translates directly into lower funeral home overhead, staffing costs, and cremation prices.
  • Strong Baptist and evangelical culture: Mississippi's religious landscape has historically opposed cremation, favoring traditional church-centered funerals with burial. This cultural preference means many funeral homes are primarily burial-oriented, and cremation-specific providers are still relatively rare in much of the state.
  • Economic necessity driving adoption: Mississippi has one of the lowest median household incomes in the country. As traditional burial costs have risen, more families are choosing cremation out of economic necessity, particularly when a $1,400 direct cremation can replace a $7,000-$12,000 traditional burial with vault.
  • Limited cremation providers in rural areas: Many of Mississippi's 82 counties have no crematories. Families in rural areas may need to transport remains to Jackson, the Gulf Coast, or other cities with cremation facilities, though some funeral homes arrange this transport as part of their service.
  • Gulf Coast influence: Mississippi's Gulf Coast communities (Biloxi, Gulfport, Ocean Springs) have higher cremation rates than the rest of the state, influenced by tourism, military retirees, and a more cosmopolitan population. Gulf scattering is a popular option in these communities.
  • Changing generational attitudes: Younger Mississippians are more open to cremation than previous generations. As these attitudes shift, the cremation rate is expected to continue its steady climb from its current 28%.

Mississippi Consumer Rights for Cremation

Mississippi families have the following protections:

  • FTC Funeral Rule: All Mississippi cremation providers must provide an itemized General Price List.
  • Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service: This board licenses funeral homes and crematories, sets standards, and handles consumer complaints.
  • Written authorization: Signed consent from the legally authorized person is required before cremation.
  • Right to use your own urn: Providers cannot refuse a third-party urn or charge handling fees.
  • No casket required: Under the FTC Funeral Rule, you are not required to purchase a casket for cremation. An alternative container is sufficient.

Scattering and Memorial Options in Mississippi

Mississippi offers these memorial options for cremated remains:

  • Gulf of Mexico scattering: Charter boats from Biloxi, Gulfport, and Pascagoula offer scattering ceremonies in the Gulf. EPA regulations require scattering at least three nautical miles from shore.
  • Mississippi River: Scattering in the Mississippi River is practiced by some families, particularly in the Natchez and Vicksburg areas. The river holds deep historical and cultural significance for the state.
  • Private property: Scattering on private land requires the property owner's written consent. Mississippi's rural landscape means many families have family land where scattering is meaningful.
  • Church cemeteries: Many Mississippi church cemeteries are beginning to offer columbarium niches or scattering gardens, making cremation more accessible within the church community structure that is central to Mississippi life.
  • Home retention: Mississippi law allows families to keep remains at home.
Cost-Saving Tip: Mississippi's low cost of living means cremation is already quite affordable compared to most states. For the lowest prices, look for cremation-only providers in the Jackson or Gulf Coast areas. If you are in a rural area with limited options, ask your local funeral home about their cremation pricing -- many burial-focused providers offer competitive cremation rates to serve this growing market. Also remember that you are never required to purchase a casket or urn from the cremation provider.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Mississippi?

Direct cremation in Mississippi averages $1,400, with prices ranging from $980 to $1,960, making it one of the most affordable states for cremation. Cremation with a full funeral service averages $5,200. The Gulf Coast and Jackson area have the most competitive pricing with the most providers.

What types of cremation are available in Mississippi?

Mississippi offers direct cremation, cremation with memorial service, and traditional cremation with a full funeral service. Gulf of Mexico scattering services are available from the Biloxi-Gulfport area. Options may be limited in rural counties.

What are Mississippi's cremation regulations?

Mississippi requires a 24-hour waiting period before cremation. Written consent from the legally authorized person is mandatory. The Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service regulates all providers.

Can I scatter ashes in Mississippi?

Yes. Gulf of Mexico scattering is available from charter boats out of Biloxi and Gulfport (3+ nautical miles from shore). Scattering on private land requires landowner consent. Some church cemeteries now offer scattering gardens. Home retention is permitted.

Why is Mississippi's cremation rate so low?

Mississippi's 28% cremation rate, the lowest in the nation, reflects the state's strong Baptist and evangelical Christian culture, which has traditionally favored burial. Church-centered funerals with cemetery burial have been the cultural norm for generations. However, the rate has nearly doubled in the past decade as economic pressures and changing generational attitudes have made cremation more acceptable, particularly in urban areas and along the Gulf Coast.

Are there crematories in rural Mississippi?

Many rural Mississippi counties do not have a crematory. Families in these areas typically work with a local funeral home that coordinates transport to a crematory in Jackson, the Gulf Coast, or another city. The transport cost is usually included in the funeral home's cremation package, though it is worth confirming. As cremation grows in Mississippi, more crematories are expected to open in underserved areas.
