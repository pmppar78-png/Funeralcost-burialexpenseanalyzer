Cremation Costs in Mississippi (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 28%

At 28%, Mississippi has the lowest cremation rate of any US state — less than half the national figure. Southern Baptist tradition and a thin network of crematories do most of the work there. For families who do choose cremation, this guide lays out current Magnolia State pricing, the providers that set the market, and the regulatory hoops set by the Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service. Cost ranges on this page are drawn from the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report, which recorded a U.S. cremation rate of roughly 60.5 percent — the benchmark against which Mississippi's state rate is compared below. Pricing rules follow the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and licensing in Mississippi is administered by the Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service.

What Mississippi's 28% cremation rate really means for families

Mississippi sits dead last nationally — a 28% cremation rate against a US average above 60%. The cause isn't price. Direct cremation at Wright Ferguson in Jackson or Baldwin-Lee on the Memphis border clears under $1,500, less than a full traditional funeral almost anywhere. The gap is cultural. Strong Southern Baptist opposition to cremation, particularly in the Delta and rural Piney Woods, keeps the traditional casket funeral dominant. The practical consequence for families choosing cremation: fewer crematories, longer turnaround times, and retort scheduling measured in days, not hours.

That low-volume environment also shapes pricing. Jackson, Gulfport-Biloxi, and Hattiesburg have enough provider density to create real competition — families who shop aggressively there can land direct cremation in the $980-$1,200 range. Get outside those metros and the map thins fast. A single funeral home in a Delta county with its own retort is effectively a monopoly, and prices reflect it.

The Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service, in plain English

Mississippi licenses cremation under the State Board of Funeral Service, which enforces a mandatory waiting period (commonly 24 hours from receipt of the death certificate), requires written authorization from the legal next of kin, and mandates that cremated remains be released in a sealed, identified container. Operators must hold a crematory license separate from the funeral director's license. Two practical notes the board quietly enforces that families rarely hear about:

  • County medical examiner sign-off. Several Mississippi counties require the coroner or ME to release the body before cremation can proceed, even in clear-cause natural deaths. Expect an extra 24-48 hours in Hinds, Harrison, and DeSoto counties.
  • Pre-need trust rules. Mississippi requires 85% of pre-need funds to be placed in trust — one of the looser standards in the Deep South. If you're pre-paying, ask for the trust institution's name and the trust receipt in writing.

Retort wait times: the hidden Mississippi cost driver

With only a few dozen licensed crematories statewide, the schedule backlog is real. A death on Friday in Oxford can mean a Tuesday cremation — five days of refrigeration or embalming fees the family didn't plan for. Ask two questions before signing:

Questions worth asking: (1) "What is your current turnaround from transfer-in to ashes-ready?" (2) "Do you operate your own retort or transport to a third-party crematory, and if so, where?" Providers who transport to Memphis or Mobile may tack on $200-$400 in mileage that doesn't appear on the General Price List under "direct cremation."

Where Mississippi families actually save money

  • Cross the state line near Memphis. North Mississippi families in DeSoto, Marshall, and Tate counties can legally engage a Tennessee provider — Memphis metro direct cremation often runs $200-$500 below comparable Mississippi prices, with shorter retort waits.
  • Request written GPLs from three Jackson-area providers. Jackson is the only Mississippi market with enough independent crematories for real price competition. Wright Ferguson, Lakewood Funeral Home, and Chancellor Funeral Home quote meaningfully different numbers on the same direct cremation.
  • Mississippi DHS county burial assistance. Most Mississippi counties administer a small indigent funeral allowance (commonly $500-$900) through the county Department of Human Services office. Ask the funeral director to apply on the family's behalf before signing the contract.
  • Veterans: Biloxi National Cemetery. No-cost interment of cremated remains for eligible veterans, with a standard government-issued urn niche marker. See our veteran benefits overview.
  • Buy the urn anywhere. The FTC Funeral Rule applies in Mississippi the same as everywhere else — no provider can refuse a family-supplied urn or charge a handling fee for one.

For broader cost-cutting, see affordable funeral options and how to pay for a funeral with no money.

A note on Mississippi's cremation trajectory

The Cremation Association of North America projects Mississippi will cross the 40% cremation mark sometime in the 2030s — still below the national average, but a meaningful shift. Rising prices for traditional burial, migration of younger Mississippians from rural counties to Jackson and the Coast, and a slow generational softening of religious opposition all point the same direction. Families planning now should assume the provider landscape will broaden over the next decade, with more dedicated cremation-only operators entering the Jackson and Gulf Coast markets.

Common Questions

What is the typical price of cremation in Mississippi?

Mississippi cremation pricing lives in two tiers: direct cremation — transport, cremation, and return of ashes — starting around $1,400, and full-service cremation with a ceremony landing near $4,800. Urns ($50-$3,000), memorial venues ($500-$2,000), and scattering or inurnment fees ($200-$600) are separate line items most providers will quote on request. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

What kinds of cremation services can I choose in Mississippi?

Three paths are standard in Mississippi: (1) direct cremation — $1,400, just the cremation itself, no service; (2) cremation with memorial — cremation first, ceremony later at a location of your choice, priced in between; (3) cremation with full funeral — $4,800, ceremony and viewing before cremation. Any of the three can be tailored to the family's specific wishes. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

What are Mississippi's cremation regulations?

Mississippi follows standard cremation regulations requiring a mandatory waiting period (typically 24-48 hours) before cremation. Written authorization from the legal next of kin is required, and some counties require a permit from the medical examiner. Pacemakers and certain implants must be removed beforehand. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, you are not required to purchase a casket for cremation — an alternative container is sufficient. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

Where are ashes allowed to be scattered in Mississippi?

Mississippi scattering rules depend on the specific location. Private land is generally fine with the owner's permission; at sea requires being at least 3 nautical miles offshore per EPA rules; many state and national parks need a permit (and in some cases prohibit it entirely). Check the local ordinance before choosing a spot. Families in Mississippi also often opt for columbarium niches, burial of the urn, or keepsake jewelry in place of scattering. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

What is driving Mississippi's cremation rate?

Mississippi's 28% cremation rate reflects below the national average, though cremation rates have been rising steadily. The cost delta alone explains a lot — families save roughly $4,500 by choosing direct cremation over a traditional funeral in Mississippi. Add in the greater flexibility for memorial service timing, the environmental considerations, and the broad cultural shift (US cremation rate is now over 60% and still climbing), and the trend is easy to see. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

How long does cremation take in Mississippi?

In Mississippi, the cremation process itself takes 2 to 3 hours. However, there is typically a mandatory 24 to 48 hour waiting period before cremation can proceed. After cremation, ashes are usually ready for pickup or delivery within 3 to 7 business days. Direct cremation ($1,400) has the shortest total timeline since no viewing or ceremony is involved. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Mississippi?

Yes. Mississippi funeral homes regularly arrange visitation and a ceremony before cremation takes place — the full-service option typically runs around $4,800. If the ceremony timing is flexible, a memorial service after cremation (at any venue, anytime) is another option and usually costs less than the full traditional route. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Mississippi State Board of Funeral Service; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.
