Consumer Rights in Funeral Pricing: What Families Need to Know in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 22, 2026 Last reviewed: October 24, 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Government Sources Editorial: Our Standards

Understand your legal rights when purchasing funeral services under the FTC Funeral Rule and state laws. Every funeral home in the United States must follow the same federal price rules, and most families never hear about them until after the bill is signed. This page lists those rights in the order you would use them, from the first phone call to the final statement.

Understanding Consumer Rights in Funeral Pricing

The federal rule is the FTC's Trade Regulation Rule on Funeral Industry Practices, known as the Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453). It covers funeral providers, meaning businesses that sell or offer both funeral goods and funeral services. States add their own rules on licensing, preneed contracts, cemeteries and crematories, and some go further than the federal minimum.

The Rule centers on four documents: the General Price List, the casket price list, the outer burial container price list, and the statement of funeral goods and services selected. Knowing when each one has to be handed over is most of what a family needs to use these rights.

What You Need to Know

The rights, in the order you will use them:

  • On the phone, a funeral home must give accurate price information from its price lists to anyone who asks, without requiring a name or a visit.
  • When an in-person conversation about arrangements begins, the home must hand you a General Price List you can keep. Read our FTC guide
  • Before showing caskets or outer burial containers, the home must show the casket price list and outer burial container price list.
  • The General Price List must include prices for direct cremation and immediate burial, and must say that you can choose only the items you want.
  • Embalming needs permission. The price list must state that embalming is not required by law except in special cases. Explore payment assistance
  • At the end of the arrangements, you must get a written, itemized statement of what you selected and the total, including any legal or cemetery requirement that made you buy something.
  • If a state or cemetery rule requires an item, the statement must say which rule.
  • A funeral home cannot refuse a casket you bought elsewhere, charge a fee to handle it, or require you to be there when it is delivered.
  • If caskets and outer burial containers are on separate lists, the General Price List must still show the price range for each, so you can see the spread before choosing.
  • Posting prices online does not replace the printed list: a funeral home must still hand you a General Price List to keep when an in-person discussion begins.
  • For direct cremation, the price list must give one price when you supply the container and another with an alternative container from the home, such as unfinished wood or cardboard.

Costs and Pricing

These rights matter because each item on a funeral bill can be priced and compared.

National medians from the NFDA 2023 General Price List Study include $2,495 for the basic services fee, $395 to transfer remains to the funeral home, $845 for embalming, and $2,645 for direct cremation with a family-supplied container. A funeral with viewing and burial has a median of $8,300 before cemetery costs.

Use the right to telephone prices to get the same three or four lines from several homes, then compare them on the funeral cost comparison worksheet. The basic services fee is the only item the Rule lets a home require; everything else can be accepted or declined. Each General Price List must carry an effective date, so note it when you compare; NFDA's 2023 survey also lists medians of $295 for other preparation of the body and $475 for use of facilities and staff for a viewing.

Your Consumer Rights

Practices the Funeral Rule prohibits:

  • Saying embalming, a casket for cremation, or an outer burial container is legally required when it is not. Full FTC guide
  • Refusing, or charging a fee for, a casket or urn bought from another seller. How to compare prices
  • Requiring a purchase as a condition of getting another item, other than the basic services fee.
  • Claiming a casket or vault will preserve the body forever or keep out water when that is not true.
  • Embalming for a fee without permission from a family member or other authorized person.

Report a violation to the Federal Trade Commission and the state funeral board. The Funeral Consumers Alliance publishes guidance on using these rights.

Practical Steps You Can Take

  1. Write down the arrangements you want before calling anyone.
  2. Phone two or three funeral homes and ask for their prices on those items.
  3. At the first meeting, ask for the General Price List if it is not offered.
  4. Ask to see the casket price list before looking at caskets, or bring one bought elsewhere.
  5. Give or withhold permission for embalming explicitly.
  6. Ask which items are required and by which law or cemetery rule.
  7. Check the itemized statement against the price lists before signing it.
  8. Keep copies of every price list and the statement in case of a dispute.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who enforces funeral pricing rights?

The Federal Trade Commission enforces the Funeral Rule nationally. State funeral boards and attorneys general enforce state law.

Do I have to visit a funeral home to get prices?

No. The Funeral Rule requires funeral homes to give price information over the phone, and you do not have to give your name.

What must the General Price List include?

Itemized prices for each service, including direct cremation and immediate burial, plus required disclosures about embalming and your right to choose items.

Is the basic services fee negotiable?

It is the one charge a funeral home may make non-declinable under the Rule. You can still compare it between homes.

Can a funeral home refuse a casket I bought elsewhere?

No. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, a funeral home may not refuse to handle a casket or urn you bought from someone else, and it may not charge you a fee for doing so (16 CFR 453.4(b)(1)). It also cannot make you be present when the casket is delivered.

That applies to caskets from online retailers, warehouse stores and local casket makers alike. If a funeral home tells you otherwise or adds a handling charge, you can report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to your state's funeral board.

What if a cemetery requires a vault?

Cemeteries can require an outer burial container. The funeral home's statement must name the cemetery requirement if it is the reason you bought one.

Where do I complain?

To the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov and to the state funeral board. See state funeral regulations for board contacts.

Does the Funeral Rule apply when planning ahead?

Yes. The price disclosure rights apply when you make arrangements in advance with a funeral provider, not only at the time of death. State law separately governs how prepaid money is held.

Do I have to pay for embalming before a direct cremation?

No. Embalming is not needed for direct cremation, and a funeral home cannot charge for embalming done without permission.
