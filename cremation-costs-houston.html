Cremation Costs in Houston, Texas (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Texas Cremation Rate: 48%

Houston is the Texas cremation market that matters most for volume — the metro's seven million residents and its enormous medical infrastructure produce tens of thousands of cremations a year. Direct cremation averages $1,955 in Houston in 2026, slightly above the Texas statewide $1,700 figure; full-service cremation averages $6,670. Houston National Cremation & Funeral Service handles significant volume as a dedicated operator, while Bradshaw-Carter Memorial & Funeral Services runs the traditional full-service side near the Museum District. The Harris County indigent cremation program — one of the country's largest by decedent count — also shapes the market.

National context: the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA 2024 Annual Report put the U.S. cremation rate near 60.5 percent, and Houston providers operate under the oversight of the Texas Funeral Service Commission. Families ordering death certificates typically deal with the Houston Health Department Bureau of Vital Statistics.

Houston's $1,955 direct cremation figure carries a bit of metro premium over the Texas statewide average, mostly reflecting downtown real estate costs and the higher hourly cost of running retorts under stricter Harris County air-quality compliance. Pricing inside the 610 Loop tends to run higher than in the suburbs.

Houston's volume providers

  • Houston National Cremation & Funeral Service — dedicated cremation-focused operator running multiple locations across the Houston metro; competitive direct cremation pricing and fast turnaround.
  • Bradshaw-Carter Memorial & Funeral Services — established traditional funeral home in the Museum District, handles cremation with full chapel service for families wanting the ceremony.
  • SCI-owned Dignity Memorial — multiple locations including Earthman, Funeraria del Angel Metropolitan, and others serving specific community markets.
  • Heights Funeral Home and smaller independents in neighborhoods like Montrose, the Heights, and Bellaire.

Harris County indigent cremation

Harris County operates one of the largest indigent cremation programs in the United States, handling thousands of unclaimed-decedent and means-qualified cases each year. The program contracts with a rotating roster of Houston funeral homes at a fixed rate. Families whose loved one has died without resources should contact Harris County Social Services at the point of death; the process is faster when initiated quickly. Neighboring counties — Fort Bend, Montgomery, Brazoria, Galveston — operate smaller parallel programs at roughly the same per-case reimbursement rate.

Texas embalming rules and what Houston providers actually require

The Texas Funeral Service Commission does not mandate embalming for any cremation. In practice, most Houston providers will hold an unembalmed body in refrigeration for the standard pre-cremation window at no extra line item — but if you are considering a viewing before cremation, the provider may recommend embalming (and the associated $700-$1,200 charge) for appearance reasons. That is a choice, not a requirement. Ask explicitly about the refrigeration-only path.

International repatriation through Houston

Houston's Texas Medical Center and its international diplomatic footprint make it a significant hub for international repatriation of cremated remains. Consulates of India, Pakistan, Mexico, Vietnam, Nigeria, and dozens of other countries operate in Houston, and Bradshaw-Carter, SCI-owned Dignity brands, and Houston National all have experience with the necessary paperwork: certified death certificate, translation, consular authentication, and sealed cremated-remains container. Total repatriation cost, including base cremation, typically runs $2,500-$4,500 depending on destination.

Scattering near Houston

Galveston Bay and the Gulf of Mexico are the dominant water scattering options. Charter boats operating out of Kemah, Seabrook, and Galveston itself run $300-$700 for an EPA-compliant 3-nautical-mile scattering. Sam Houston National Forest north of the city permits discreet scattering on undeveloped land. Brazos Bend State Park and Huntsville State Park require written permission. Many Houston Catholic families use the columbaria at Forest Park, Earthman Cemetery, and Catholic Cemeteries of the Archdiocese.

Urn purchase tip: Every Houston provider — Houston National, Bradshaw-Carter, Dignity Memorial brands, smaller independents — must accept an outside urn without a handling fee under the FTC Funeral Rule. Temporary plastic containers from the crematory are sufficient for scattering arrangements.

Short Answers to Common Questions

How much does cremation cost in Houston?

Houston's direct cremation average is $1,955 in 2026, with Houston National Cremation & Funeral Service frequently at the lower end of that range and traditional providers like Bradshaw-Carter at the higher end. Full-service cremation with chapel ceremony runs about $6,670. International repatriation of ashes through Houston's consulates adds $500-$2,500 depending on country of destination.

What types of cremation are available in Houston?

The three standard paths: direct cremation ($1,955) through Houston National or similar high-volume operators; cremation with a later memorial at a Texas Medical Center chapel, church, or family home; full-service cremation ($6,670) with viewing and chapel ceremony at Bradshaw-Carter or a Dignity Memorial location. The city's diverse religious communities mean cultural variations are common and well-accommodated.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Houston?

Yes. Direct cremation at $1,955 saves about $6,300 compared to traditional burial at Forest Park, Earthman, or Brookside Memorial Park once plot, vault and monument costs are added. Catholic families wanting columbarium inurnment at an Archdiocese cemetery still come in significantly under burial costs.

Can I scatter ashes in Houston?

Galveston Bay and Gulf charter-boat scattering is the most common Houston choice, available from Kemah, Seabrook, and Galveston docks at $300-$700. Sam Houston National Forest allows discreet scattering. Brazos Bend and Huntsville state parks require written permission from the park manager. Forest Park and Catholic Cemeteries of the Archdiocese operate columbaria for families wanting permanent inurnment.
