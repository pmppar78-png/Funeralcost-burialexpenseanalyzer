Burial Costs in Maryland (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: Northeast

A Maryland burial is regulated unusually tightly compared to most states. The Maryland State Board of Morticians and Funeral Directors requires two witnesses for any cremation authorization, and the state's cemeteries fall under some of the most detailed consumer-protection statutes on the East Coast. For a 2026 traditional interment, families in Baltimore, Montgomery, or Prince George's County should expect to spend between $17,940 and $25,300 once the funeral home, cemetery, and monument bills are summed.

The $17,940 figure corresponds to a straightforward Baltimore-area burial with a mid-priced casket and a flush marker. Moving into Green Mount, Druid Ridge, or Mount Olivet sections with upright monuments puts the full cost closer to $25,000.

What Green Mount, Druid Ridge, and Mount Olivet Actually Charge

Baltimore's three most-asked-about historic cemeteries price differently from one another and from the newer commercial parks in the suburbs. Green Mount Cemetery on Greenmount Avenue — the oldest of the three, with graves dating to 1839 — carries a premium for its historical sections and older family plots but sometimes offers unexpected value in newer areas. Druid Ridge in Pikesville generally sits mid-range for Baltimore County. Mount Olivet, Baltimore's Catholic cemetery on Frederick Road, typically prices below the others on the plot line but enforces its own denominational and vault requirements. Requesting all three itemized sheets side-by-side is the clearest way to see how real Maryland cemetery pricing behaves.

The Two-Witness Rule and Other Maryland-Specific Paperwork

Maryland requires that cremation authorization be signed by two witnesses — a detail that sometimes surprises families who arrive at the funeral home expecting the process to mirror a neighboring state. The same board, the Maryland State Board of Morticians and Funeral Directors, regulates itemized pricing and contract disclosures. For burial specifically, state statute does not require an outer container, but nearly every Maryland cemetery requires one by internal rule. Concrete grave liners, roughly half the cost of a full vault, usually satisfy that rule.

Disposition Paths Maryland Families Choose: Loudon Park, Dulaney Valley, and Beyond

  • Traditional burial — Viewing, service, casket, vault, and ground interment. The pattern most Maryland funeral homes default to.
  • Direct burial — Burial shortly after death without embalming, viewing, or ceremony. Published by most Baltimore-area funeral homes as a separate service.
  • Green burial — Limited but growing. Serenity Ridge in Windsor Mill and sections of a few rural Maryland cemeteries accept unembalmed, shrouded, or biodegradable-container interments.
  • Mausoleum entombment — Offered at Druid Ridge, Mount Olivet, and most commercial parks in suburban Baltimore; avoids vault costs but carries higher base pricing.
Funeral Benefit Program: Maryland's Department of Human Services administers a Funeral Benefit Program for eligible low-income residents. The benefit is modest but real — apply before paying the funeral home, not after.

Where the Savings Live

  • Compare three cemeteries, not one. Green Mount, Druid Ridge, and Mount Olivet all serve Baltimore families; plot pricing can swing $2,000 between them for comparable sections.
  • Drive to Harford or Carroll County. Plot prices in Bel Air, Westminster, or Hampstead typically run $1,500 to $3,000 below comparable Baltimore plots.
  • Use the Maryland DHS Funeral Benefit Program. Apply through your local Department of Social Services office; the paperwork needs to be filed before the funeral home is paid.
  • Buy the casket outside the funeral home. Savings of 50 to 70 percent are standard; Maryland funeral homes cannot refuse it or add a handling fee. Casket buying guide.
  • Ask about grave liners, not only vaults. Cemetery language often implies a sealed vault is required when a concrete liner would satisfy the same policy.

Common Questions We Hear

What do Maryland families spend on burial?

Most Maryland traditional burials total between $17,940 and $25,300. The funeral service averages $9,200, the cemetery plot $4,600, casket $2,760, vault $1,840, opening and closing $2,300, and the marker $1,000 to $3,000. Baltimore County cemeteries like Druid Ridge sit mid-range; Green Mount's older sections can push higher.

What are the cheapest burial options in Maryland?

Direct burial through a Baltimore funeral home eliminates embalming, viewing, and most facility fees. Harford, Carroll, and Frederick County cemeteries price plots well below Baltimore City. Sourcing the casket from an online retailer saves an additional $1,000 or more. Maryland's DHS Funeral Benefit Program can also cover a share of costs for eligible low-income families — apply before the funeral home invoice is paid.

How do cemetery prices differ across Maryland?

Meaningfully. Green Mount Cemetery, Druid Ridge, and Mount Olivet — all serving Baltimore — can price the same grade of plot within a range of $2,000 or more. Cemeteries fall outside the FTC Funeral Rule's scope, so the itemized sheet you need (plot, opening and closing, vault policy, perpetual care, and denominational or residency restrictions) must be specifically requested. Suburban cemeteries in Baltimore County tend to run noticeably below the city's historic grounds.

Once I add every line item, what does burial actually cost in Maryland?

Adding the lines — $9,200 funeral service, $4,600 plot, $2,760 casket, $1,840 vault, $2,300 opening and closing, $1,000 to $3,000 marker — delivers a working total of $17,940 to $25,300. Pricing two or three funeral homes and two or three cemeteries separately is the single most consistent way to hold that total near the lower end.

Is green burial available in Maryland?

Yes, but the footprint is still modest. Serenity Ridge Cemetery in Windsor Mill is Maryland's best-known natural burial ground; a handful of rural cemeteries in Harford and Carroll Counties also accept shrouded or biodegradable-container interments. Total cost for a Maryland green burial usually lands below a traditional one because the vault and embalming lines drop off entirely.
