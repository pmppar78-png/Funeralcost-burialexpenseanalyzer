Life Insurance & Funeral Costs: What Families Need to Know in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 22, 2026 Last reviewed: October 22, 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Government Sources Editorial: Our Standards

How to use life insurance to cover funeral costs including claims process, timing, and options. The common problem is timing: the funeral home wants a deposit this week, and the insurer pays after it receives a claim and a certified death certificate. This page covers how families bridge that gap.

Understanding Life Insurance & Funeral Costs

A life insurance policy pays the named beneficiary, not the funeral home and not the estate. The beneficiary can use the money for the funeral, but nothing in the policy requires it. Because the proceeds go straight to a named person, they generally do not pass through probate, and under 26 U.S.C. § 101(a) life insurance paid because of a death is generally not taxable income to the beneficiary.

Many funeral homes will accept an assignment of benefits: the beneficiary signs a form that directs the insurer to pay part of the policy to the funeral home, and the home waits for that payment instead of asking for cash up front. Whether a home accepts assignments, and what it charges for them, is a business decision, so ask before signing.

What You Need to Know

Before a family relies on a policy for the funeral:

  • Confirm the policy is in force and who the beneficiary is. If the beneficiary died first and no contingent beneficiary is named, the money may go to the estate and wait for probate.
  • Insurers need a claim form and a certified copy of the death certificate. Order several certified copies when the death certificate is filed.
  • Policies issued less than two years before the death can be reviewed under the contestability period, which can delay payment.
  • A funeral assignment pays the funeral home only the amount of its bill; the rest of the benefit still goes to the beneficiary.
  • If no one knows whether a policy exists, the NAIC's free Life Insurance Policy Locator asks participating insurers to search their records. Explore payment assistance
  • Employer group life insurance often ends or must be converted when someone leaves the job; check with the former employer's benefits office.
  • The FTC Funeral Rule applies however the funeral is paid, so ask for the itemized price list before assigning anything. Read our FTC guide
  • If the death was accidental, look for a separate accidental death policy or rider through an employer, bank or credit card; it pays in addition to any life coverage.
  • Beneficiaries can usually choose a lump sum instead of installments or a retained-asset account; ask the insurer which options the policy offers.

Costs and Pricing

Knowing the likely cost tells you how much of a policy to assign and how much to leave for other bills.

The NFDA 2023 General Price List Study puts the national median for a funeral with viewing and burial at $8,300 without cemetery costs, and $9,995 when a burial vault is included. A funeral with viewing and cremation has a median of $6,280, and direct cremation $2,645 with a family-supplied container.

Assign only the amount on the funeral home's signed statement of goods and services, not a round number. If the policy is small, compare direct cremation or immediate burial prices from two or three homes with the funeral cost comparison worksheet, so the benefit also covers the cemetery, the death certificates and the obituary. If the beneficiary is not the person arranging the funeral, the beneficiary has to sign the assignment, so bring them in before choosing a funeral home.

Your Consumer Rights

Rules that protect a beneficiary paying with insurance money:

  • The funeral home must give you its General Price List before you discuss arrangements in person. Full FTC guide
  • An assignment does not change your right to choose only the items you want. How to compare prices
  • A funeral home cannot refuse a casket or urn you bought elsewhere or charge a fee for using one.
  • Insurance itself is regulated by your state insurance department, not the FTC; the Funeral Rule expressly does not apply to the business of insurance (16 CFR § 453.8(c)).
  • Read the assignment form for any processing fee or interest, and keep a copy of what you signed.

Complaints about a delayed claim go to the state insurance department. Complaints about funeral pricing go to the state funeral board or the Federal Trade Commission.

Practical Steps You Can Take

  1. Find the policy documents, or search old bank statements for premium payments to an insurer.
  2. Call the insurer, report the death, and ask for the claim form and their payment timeline.
  3. Ask the funeral home how many certified copies it is ordering, and whether the vital records office can issue extra copies faster.
  4. Ask each funeral home whether it accepts insurance assignments and whether it charges a fee for them.
  5. Get the General Price List and decide on the arrangements before signing any assignment.
  6. Assign only the amount on the signed statement of goods and services.
  7. If no policy can be found, run a search through the NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator.
  8. Keep the claim number, the insurer's contact and a copy of the assignment together until the claim is paid.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a funeral home be paid directly from life insurance?

Often, yes, through an assignment of benefits that the beneficiary signs. The home is paid its bill from the claim and the remainder goes to the beneficiary. Not every home accepts assignments.

How long does a life insurance claim take?

It depends on the insurer and the paperwork. Delays usually come from missing death certificates, unclear beneficiaries, or a death within the two-year contestability period.

Is life insurance money taxable?

Proceeds paid because of the insured person's death are generally not taxable income to the beneficiary under 26 U.S.C. § 101(a). Interest paid on top of the benefit can be taxable.

What if the beneficiary has died?

The contingent beneficiary is paid. If there is none, the policy usually pays the estate, which can mean waiting for probate.

How do we find out whether a policy exists?

Check papers and bank records, ask past employers, and use the NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator, a free service that asks insurers to search for policies.

Does the Funeral Rule cover insurance?

No. The Funeral Rule covers funeral providers' pricing; insurance is regulated by state insurance departments.

Should we buy a bigger policy just for the funeral?

Compare the cost of the premiums with the price of the funeral you want. See our final expense insurance guide and prepaid plans for the alternatives.

Does a suicide clause affect the claim?

Most policies exclude suicide during the first two years, or one year in some states. After that period the death benefit is generally paid like any other claim.
