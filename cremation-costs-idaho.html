Cremation Costs in Idaho (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 65%

Idaho's cremation rate of 65% puts it ahead of most Mountain West neighbors — ahead of Colorado, slightly behind Washington and Oregon — and the pricing reflects a competitive market in the Boise metro with specialist operators like Bowman Funeral Directors and Cloverdale Cremation. Statewide direct cremation averages $1,500, one of the lower numbers in the country; full-service cremation runs $5,400. The Idaho Funeral Service Licensing Board oversees both funeral establishments and crematories, and Idaho's vast public lands — 63% of the state is federally owned — make outdoor scattering more accessible here than almost anywhere else. Cost ranges on this page are drawn from the NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report and the CANA (Cremation Association of North America) 2024 Annual Report, which recorded a U.S. cremation rate of roughly 60.5 percent — the benchmark against which Idaho's state rate is compared below. Pricing rules follow the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), and licensing in Idaho is administered by the Idaho State Board of Morticians.

Idaho's $3,900 spread between direct cremation and full-service is smaller than what you see in Colorado or Washington, mostly because traditional full-service cremation pricing stays moderate in markets outside the Treasure Valley. Boise pricing tends to run $150-$300 above the statewide average; Idaho Falls and Pocatello run slightly below.

Boise's specialist cremation providers

  • Bowman Funeral Directors — longstanding Boise operation with on-site crematory; handles both direct cremation and full-service arrangements, including significant LDS community volume.
  • Cloverdale Cremation and Funeral Services — dedicated cremation-focused operator serving Boise, Meridian, Nampa, and Eagle; competitive pricing on direct cremation.
  • Alden-Waggoner Funeral Chapel — Boise full-service with chapel and on-site retort.
  • Relyea Funeral Chapel — central Boise, full-service with cremation options.

Idaho Funeral Service Licensing Board requirements

The Idaho Funeral Service Licensing Board enforces a 24-hour hold between death and cremation, requires written authorization from the legal next of kin (with the statutory hierarchy of surviving spouse, adult children, parents, siblings, etc.), and requires a permit from the coroner for deaths not attended by a physician. Idaho does not require embalming for any disposition. Crematories must hold a current state license and maintain tracking procedures for cremated remains — a response to the same industry failures that triggered reforms elsewhere in the early 2000s.

Scattering on Idaho's public lands

More than 60% of Idaho is federal land, which transforms the scattering conversation. The Sawtooth, Payette, Boise, Salmon-Challis, Clearwater and Nez Perce National Forests all permit ash scattering on undeveloped land without a permit, with common-sense distance from trails, campgrounds and water sources. BLM-managed rangeland — enormous in southern Idaho — follows similar rules. Craters of the Moon National Monument, Yellowstone (the Idaho corner), and Hells Canyon National Recreation Area each have their own permit processes, all free or nominal. For families wanting a specific location — a favorite fishing hole on the Salmon River, a ridgeline in the Sawtooths — DIY scattering is essentially free once cremation is complete.

LDS community considerations

Idaho's substantial LDS population historically preferred burial, and Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints teaching traditionally emphasizes burial while permitting cremation where local law or circumstances require it. Practically, an increasing share of Idaho LDS families now choose cremation, often with a traditional funeral service followed by cremation and later interment or scattering. Bowman and other Boise-area providers handle these arrangements routinely and coordinate with LDS ward leadership when requested.

Rural Idaho transport logistics

Outside the Treasure Valley, Idaho Falls, and Coeur d'Alene, the nearest crematory may be 80-150 miles from the funeral home — common in the Panhandle, central Idaho mountains, and the southeast. Some funeral homes absorb that distance in their base direct cremation price; others bill mileage at $2-$4 per loaded mile. This can add $200-$600 to a rural Idaho cremation. When comparing quotes, ask specifically about the round trip to the crematory and back.

Urn purchase tip: Idaho providers follow the FTC Funeral Rule. For families scattering on public lands, the basic plastic container the crematory returns ashes in is entirely sufficient — no decorative urn required.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical price of cremation in Idaho?

Direct cremation averages $1,500 statewide in Idaho. Boise providers including Bowman Funeral Directors and Cloverdale Cremation quote in the $1,400-$1,800 range; rural providers sometimes run cheaper on base price but add transport mileage. Full-service cremation averages $5,400. Scattering on public lands is effectively free once the cremation is complete. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Idaho State Board of Morticians; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

What types of cremation are available in Idaho?

Three paths. Direct cremation ($1,500) through Cloverdale or a similar volume provider is the simplest. Cremation followed by scattering on Idaho public lands is distinctively common here given the state's geography. Full-service cremation ($5,400) with chapel ceremony is the traditional path and the default at many family-run Idaho funeral homes. LDS families can arrange cremation while still holding a ward service beforehand. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Idaho State Board of Morticians; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

Where are ashes allowed to be scattered in Idaho?

Idaho's enormous public-lands footprint makes scattering unusually accessible. National forests (Sawtooth, Payette, Boise, Salmon-Challis, others) permit discreet scattering without a permit. BLM rangeland is similarly open. Craters of the Moon, Hells Canyon NRA, and the Idaho corner of Yellowstone require free permits. Private property is legal with owner consent. The Salmon, Snake, and Clearwater rivers are common water-scattering choices under EPA notification rules. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Idaho State Board of Morticians; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

Why is cremation so popular in Idaho?

Idaho's 65% cremation rate reflects a combination of Mountain West cultural openness to cremation, the practical advantage of scattering on Idaho's public lands, and the cost gap versus traditional burial. Even within the LDS community — which traditionally favored burial — cremation has grown substantially over the past two decades. Rural Idaho's long distances between funeral homes and cemeteries also quietly push some families toward cremation for logistical reasons. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Idaho State Board of Morticians; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Idaho?

Yes — full-service cremation at $5,400 includes a visitation and chapel service at a Boise provider like Bowman, Alden-Waggoner, or Relyea. Alternatively, arrange direct cremation and hold a memorial later at a ward chapel, a mountain cabin, or a family home — a pattern increasingly common among Idaho families regardless of denomination. Cited: NFDA 2024 Cremation & Burial Report; Idaho State Board of Morticians; FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453); CANA 2024 Annual Report.
