Cremation Costs in Idaho (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Cremation Rate: 65%

Idaho's cremation rate of 65% has been growing rapidly, a trend accelerated by the LDS Church's shift to a more neutral stance on cremation in recent years. In a state with strong ties to The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, this doctrinal evolution has had a measurable impact on cremation adoption. Direct cremation in Idaho averages $1,500, making it one of the more affordable options in the Mountain West. Idaho does not impose a mandatory waiting period before cremation, and the state's relatively light regulatory framework keeps costs manageable. Outside of the Boise metro area, however, cremation providers are limited, and families in rural parts of the state may need to arrange transportation to a larger city.

What Drives Cremation Costs in Idaho

Idaho's cremation landscape is influenced by its unique demographic and geographic characteristics:

  • No mandatory waiting period: Idaho does not require a waiting period before cremation, which helps keep costs lower by eliminating mandated storage time.
  • LDS Church neutrality boosting rates: The LDS Church's move to a more neutral position on cremation has had a meaningful impact in Idaho, where a significant portion of the population is LDS. Cremation rates have accelerated as this religious barrier has diminished.
  • Limited providers outside Boise: The Boise metro area has the most options, but much of Idaho is rural with few cremation providers. Families in remote areas may face transportation charges.
  • Lower cost of living: Idaho's overall affordability keeps cremation costs below the national average.
  • Outdoor culture: Idaho's strong outdoor orientation means many families prefer scattering ashes in the state's mountains, rivers, and wilderness areas rather than maintaining a cemetery plot, which supports cremation adoption.
  • Scattering on public land: Idaho's extensive national forests, BLM land, and wilderness areas provide ample space for legal ash scattering, reducing demand for expensive memorial options.

Idaho Consumer Rights for Cremation

Idaho families have the following protections:

  • FTC Funeral Rule: All Idaho providers must supply an itemized General Price List.
  • Written authorization: Signed consent from the next of kin is required before cremation.
  • Right to use your own urn: Providers cannot refuse a third-party urn.
  • Idaho Board of Morticians: This board oversees cremation provider licensing.

Scattering and Memorial Options in Idaho

Idaho's rugged landscape provides exceptional scattering opportunities:

  • National forests: Idaho's extensive national forests (Boise, Salmon-Challis, Sawtooth, and others) generally permit ash scattering.
  • BLM land: Vast stretches of BLM land across Idaho are available for scattering.
  • Rivers: The Salmon River, Snake River, and other Idaho waterways are popular scattering locations.
  • Private property: Scattering requires landowner consent.
  • Home retention: Idaho law allows families to keep remains at home.
Cost-Saving Tip: Idaho's lack of a mandatory waiting period means cremation can proceed quickly, keeping costs down. Compare at least three Boise-area providers for the best prices, and ask about all-inclusive packages that cover transportation from rural areas.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Idaho?

Direct cremation in Idaho averages $1,500, ranging from $1,050 to $2,100. Cremation with service averages $5,400. Boise has the most competitive pricing. Idaho is one of the more affordable states for cremation in the Mountain West.

What are Idaho's cremation regulations?

Idaho has no mandatory waiting period before cremation, one of the simpler regulatory frameworks. Written authorization from the next of kin is required. Pacemakers must be removed. The Idaho Board of Morticians oversees provider licensing.

Can I scatter ashes in Idaho?

Yes. Idaho's extensive public lands make scattering straightforward. National forests, BLM land, and wilderness areas generally permit scattering. Rivers like the Salmon and Snake are popular locations. Private property requires landowner consent.

Has the LDS Church's stance on cremation changed?

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints has moved from discouraging cremation to a more neutral stance, stating that cremation is a matter of personal choice. This shift has contributed to Idaho's rising cremation rate, as a significant portion of the state's population is LDS. Families should consult with their bishop if they have specific religious concerns.

Are there cremation providers outside of Boise?

Yes, though options are more limited. Idaho Falls, Twin Falls, Pocatello, Coeur d'Alene, and Lewiston all have funeral homes offering cremation. In more remote areas of central Idaho, you may need to arrange transportation to a larger city, though some providers offer pickup services throughout the state.
