Funeral Costs in Texas: What Families Pay in 2026

Reviewed: February 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Texas Funeral Board Region: South

Everything is bigger in Texas, and that extends to its funeral market. With approximately 1,500 funeral homes serving nearly 30 million residents, the Lone Star State is one of the largest and most diverse funeral markets in the country. Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, and Austin each operate as distinct metropolitan markets with very different demographics and pricing dynamics. Texas is also the global headquarters of Service Corporation International (SCI), the world's largest funeral company, which gives the corporate chain a particularly strong presence in the state. Despite this, independent and family-owned funeral homes still serve the majority of Texas communities, and the state's massive Hispanic/Latino population has created a funeral industry that is genuinely bicultural.

Funeral Cost Breakdown in Texas

Texas's average traditional funeral cost of $7,200 is slightly below the national median, which may surprise those who think of the state as expensive. While Houston, Dallas, and Austin command premium prices, the vast swaths of rural Texas and the more affordable San Antonio and El Paso metros bring the statewide average down. The enormous variation -- a funeral in downtown Houston can cost twice what the same services cost in a small West Texas town -- makes statewide averages less meaningful here than in most states.

What Drives Funeral Costs in Texas

The Texas Funeral Service Commission regulates all funeral service providers in the state. Texas's funeral market is the second-largest in the nation by volume (behind only California), and its complexity reflects the state's remarkable geographic and cultural diversity. Service Corporation International (SCI), headquartered in Houston, is the world's largest funeral company and operates hundreds of locations throughout Texas under various names, making corporate chain influence stronger here than in most states.

Texas's massive Hispanic/Latino population -- over 40% of the state's residents -- profoundly shapes the funeral industry. Hispanic funeral traditions in Texas typically involve Catholic funeral masses, velorio (wake) gatherings, novena prayers following burial, and often a meal prepared by the community. Many funeral homes in San Antonio, El Paso, the Rio Grande Valley, and Hispanic neighborhoods of Houston and Dallas are bilingual operations that understand these cultural traditions intimately. The cost of Hispanic-oriented funeral services tends to be somewhat lower than average, reflecting both community preferences for simplicity and the competitive market among providers serving this population.

The state's other major cultural influences include a strong Baptist tradition (particularly in rural East Texas and the Bible Belt regions), a significant African-American funeral culture in Houston, Dallas, and East Texas, and a growing Asian-American population in Houston and Dallas that brings Buddhist, Hindu, and other Eastern funeral traditions. Texas's huge military presence -- Fort Cavazos (formerly Fort Hood), Fort Bliss, Joint Base San Antonio, and numerous other installations -- creates substantial demand for military funeral honors and veteran burial services. The state's oil industry communities in the Permian Basin and Gulf Coast regions have their own dynamics, with higher incomes sometimes supporting more elaborate funeral services.

Major Cities in Texas

Texas's major metros each represent distinct funeral markets. Houston, as the state's largest city and SCI's headquarters, has the most diverse and competitive market. Dallas and Austin command premium prices. San Antonio offers more moderate pricing influenced by its Hispanic traditions. El Paso and the Rio Grande Valley are among the most affordable areas.

Cremation vs. Burial in Texas

Texas's 48% cremation rate is slightly below the national average, reflecting the strong influence of both Baptist tradition and Hispanic Catholic culture, both of which have historically favored burial. However, the rate is climbing rapidly, particularly in Austin (where it exceeds 60%) and among younger, urban Texans. The state's Hindu and Buddhist communities in Houston and Dallas have always practiced cremation. Texas preneed regulations govern the sale of pre-arranged funeral plans, providing consumer protections for families planning ahead.

Texas Funeral Regulations

The Texas Funeral Service Commission oversees all funeral service providers. Key regulations include:

  • Preneed regulation -- Texas regulates preneed funeral contracts, requiring trust deposits and providing consumer protections for transfers and cancellations.
  • No embalming requirement -- Texas does not mandate embalming by law.
  • 48-hour cremation waiting period -- Texas requires a 48-hour wait before cremation, with a signed authorization from next of kin.
  • Price disclosure -- Texas requires compliance with the FTC Funeral Rule and enforces additional state-level pricing transparency requirements.
  • Cemetery regulation -- Texas has separate regulation for cemeteries through the Texas Department of Banking for perpetual care cemeteries.

Your Consumer Rights in Texas

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Texas must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

Given SCI's dominance in the Texas market, comparison shopping is especially important. Families should be aware that many funeral homes operating under familiar local names may actually be SCI-owned properties. File complaints with the Texas Funeral Service Commission, the Texas Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division, or the FTC. The Funeral Consumers Alliance has Texas affiliates. Always request the General Price List and compare at least three providers.

Payment Assistance in Texas

Families in Texas who need financial help with funeral expenses can explore these options:

  • Social Security death benefit -- A one-time $255 payment for eligible surviving spouses or children. Learn more
  • Veteran burial benefits -- Eligible veterans may receive burial allowances and free interment at multiple Texas state veterans cemeteries and national cemeteries including Houston National Cemetery and Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery. Veteran benefits guide
  • Texas HHSC burial assistance -- The Texas Health and Human Services Commission provides burial assistance for Medicaid recipients and through county indigent burial programs. Medicaid funeral assistance
  • County indigent burial -- Texas counties are required to provide burial or cremation for indigent residents. Benefits vary significantly by county.
  • Church and community support -- Texas's strong faith communities frequently provide direct funeral cost assistance through benevolence funds.
  • Crowdfunding -- GoFundMe campaigns for funeral costs are extremely common in Texas. Crowdfunding guide
  • Payment plans -- Many Texas funeral homes offer financing options. Payment plan options

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Texas?

The average traditional funeral in Texas costs approximately $7,200. A funeral with cremation averages $5,800, while direct cremation typically costs $1,700. Cemetery plots average $3,400 additional. Costs vary by provider and location within the state.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Texas?

Yes. Direct cremation in Texas averages $1,700 compared to $7,200 for a traditional burial service. The cremation rate in Texas is 48%.

What are my consumer rights at Texas funeral homes?

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, all funeral homes in Texas must provide an itemized General Price List, allow you to choose only services you want, and accept third-party caskets without penalty. File complaints with the state funeral board or the Attorney General.

How can I save on funeral costs in Texas?

Compare prices from at least three providers. Consider direct cremation or direct burial if a full service is not essential. Purchase caskets from third-party retailers. Check veteran benefits, state assistance, and community resources. Always request the General Price List from every provider.

Does Texas require embalming?

No. Texas does not legally require embalming. Refrigeration is an available alternative at most funeral homes. Some providers may have internal policies for viewings, but these are not state mandates.

How does SCI's headquarters in Houston affect Texas funeral prices?

Service Corporation International (SCI), the world's largest funeral company, is headquartered in Houston and operates hundreds of Texas locations -- often under acquired local names rather than the SCI brand. This means many families may not realize they are using a corporate chain. SCI-owned locations typically charge premium prices compared to true independents. Always ask whether a funeral home is independently owned or part of a corporate chain, and compare prices between corporate and independent providers.

What are Hispanic funeral traditions in Texas?

Texas's Hispanic/Latino community, which makes up over 40% of the state's population, maintains strong Catholic funeral traditions. These typically include a velorio (wake) the evening before the funeral, a Catholic funeral mass (misa de cuerpo presente), burial with a priest's committal prayers, and a novena (nine days of prayer) following the burial. Families often gather at the home for a meal after the service. Many Texas funeral homes in San Antonio, El Paso, the Rio Grande Valley, and urban Hispanic communities are bilingual and culturally knowledgeable. Hispanic-oriented funeral services are often moderately priced, as the community values simplicity and family togetherness over elaborate displays.

Are there special considerations for military funerals in Texas?

Texas has one of the largest military and veteran populations in the nation. Multiple national cemeteries serve the state, and military funeral honors are widely available. Fort Cavazos (Central Texas), Fort Bliss (El Paso), and Joint Base San Antonio all have installations that coordinate military funeral services. Texas also operates several state veterans cemeteries. Veteran families should contact the VA or their installation's casualty assistance office for guidance on benefits and honors.
