Funeral Costs in Memphis, Tennessee (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Tennessee

Memphis funeral culture runs along a historic corridor — R.S. Lewis & Sons on Vance Avenue has served Black Memphis families since 1908, Memorial Park Funeral Home on Poplar anchors the midtown service tradition, and Forest Hill Memphis operates two of the city's largest memorial cemeteries. 2026 pricing: $7,035 traditional average, $5,670 cremation with service, $1,680 direct cremation, and a 42% statewide cremation rate that Memphis itself tends to sit just below.

Beale Street Corridor: Memphis's Historic Black-Owned Funeral Tradition

R.S. Lewis & Sons, founded in 1908 on Vance Avenue, is one of the oldest continuously operating Black-owned funeral homes in the American South. The firm arranged the services for Rev. Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.'s remains after his 1968 assassination at the Lorraine Motel. That institutional continuity, combined with a cluster of other South Memphis and Orange Mound providers, gives Memphis a distinct homegoing tradition — full church service, motorcade, repast — that runs roughly 10–15% more than a non-ceremonial traditional funeral because of the service length and staffing. Families weighing cost against tradition often negotiate the facility fee by holding the service at the home church rather than the funeral home chapel.

Tennessee Cemetery Act (Title 46) and Memphis Plot Pricing

Tennessee's Cemetery Act under Title 46 requires perpetual-care funding on most commercial cemetery plots sold in the state. Forest Hill Memphis, Memorial Park, and Elmwood all operate under this framework, which bundles a perpetual-care charge into the plot price. Municipal and church cemeteries in Memphis — including smaller Baptist and AME-affiliated grounds — sometimes fall outside the perpetual-care requirement and can price $800–$1,400 below Memorial Park or Forest Hill for comparable lots. Elmwood, Memphis's historic 1852 garden cemetery, sits in a distinct price tier and is worth separate comparison.

Tennessee DHS Funeral Assistance for Memphis Families

The Tennessee Department of Human Services administers funeral and burial assistance for low-income families through its Families First program. In Memphis, the Shelby County DHS office processes applications; benefits are modest but stack with the Social Security $255 lump sum and with VA burial allowances at Memphis National Cemetery on Townes Avenue. Memorial Park Funeral Home and Forest Hill routinely handle the paperwork as part of standard intake; smaller providers may require the family to file directly. For veterans, Memphis National Cemetery provides free interment, a government headstone, and perpetual care — often the single largest burial-cost offset available.

Your Rights in Memphis

FTC Funeral Rule Protection Every funeral home in Memphis must provide an itemized General Price List, let you choose only the services you want, and accept caskets purchased elsewhere. You are never required to buy a package. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

Regional Context: Funeral Pricing in the South

Memphis summers are humid enough that some providers default to embalming in the arrangement conversation, but Tennessee does not require it. Refrigeration runs $55–$95 per day at Memphis providers versus the $704 embalming line — a meaningful difference for families choosing cremation or closed-casket burial within 48 hours. The tradition of a multi-day visitation with an open casket typically drives the embalming decision in Memphis, not the law. Ask directly: "Is embalming required by Tennessee law in my specific situation?" The Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers answer is almost always no.

Questions Worth Asking First

What is the typical funeral price in Memphis?

Memphis traditional-funeral averages in 2026 sit at $7,035, a small premium over the $6,700 Tennessee median. Direct cremation through Memorial Park Funeral Home, R.S. Lewis & Sons, or Forest Hill Memphis starts at $1,680. A Memorial Park or Elmwood plot runs about $3,150. Inclusive totals land between $1,680 for direct cremation and $12,296 for a full-service traditional burial with casket, vault, opening/closing, and marker.

What is Memphis's cremation-versus-burial split?

Memphis tracks Tennessee's 42% cremation rate — below the 60% national figure, partly because of the city's deep church community and the tradition of the full homegoing service. R.S. Lewis & Sons has served Memphis families since 1908 and continues to anchor the South Memphis homegoing culture. A memorial following cremation is the common hybrid for families wanting lower cost without giving up the service.

How do Memphis funeral costs compare to the national average?

Memphis at $7,035 is about $813 below the $7,848 US median for a traditional funeral. Nashville runs higher at $7,705. Within Memphis, the spread between Forest Hill, Memorial Park, and R.S. Lewis & Sons on identical services is typically $900–$1,400, which is the main reason to pull multiple GPLs.

How do I compare funeral quotes in Memphis?

The FTC Funeral Rule and the Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers both require General Price Lists on request. Phone or email the Memphis providers on Union Avenue, Poplar Avenue, and along Elvis Presley Boulevard. The Funeral Consumers Alliance of the Mid-South publishes a comparison list specific to Memphis and the surrounding metro.

Are there affordable funeral options in Memphis?

Direct cremation at $1,680 is the cheapest Memphis disposition. Direct burial without viewing runs about $4,830 with a Memorial Park or Elmwood plot. Tennessee DHS offers funeral assistance for qualifying low-income families, Social Security pays the $255 lump sum, and veteran benefits at Memphis National Cemetery on Townes Avenue include free interment and marker. Third-party caskets via Costco or online retailers cut $1,000–$1,400 off the funeral home casket line.
