Final Expense Insurance: What Families Need to Know in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 22, 2026 Last reviewed: October 24, 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Government Sources Editorial: Our Standards

Compare final expense insurance plans with coverage details, costs, eligibility, and honest analysis. Final expense insurance is a small whole life policy sold mainly to older adults to pay for a funeral and last bills. This page explains how the policies are built, where the costs hide, and how to compare one with simply saving the money.

Understanding Final Expense Insurance

Most final expense policies are permanent whole life insurance with a modest face amount, a fixed premium and no expiration date as long as premiums are paid. They come in three common forms. Fully underwritten or simplified issue policies ask health questions and pay the full benefit from the first day. Guaranteed issue policies ask no health questions, but usually pay only the premiums back, sometimes with interest, if death from natural causes occurs in the first two or three years.

The benefit is paid to the named beneficiary, who can use it for the funeral or anything else. That is the main difference from a prepaid funeral plan, which pays a specific funeral home for specific goods and services. Insurance is regulated by state insurance departments, not by the FTC Funeral Rule.

What You Need to Know

What to check before buying a policy:

  • Add up the premiums. A policy held for many years can cost more in total than its face amount, so compare the total with the benefit at your expected age.
  • Read the graded benefit or waiting period terms. If death in the first years pays only a return of premiums, a separate savings account may do the same job.
  • Ask whether the premium can rise. Most final expense whole life premiums are level, but some policies are term insurance that ends at a set age.
  • State law gives buyers a free-look period after the policy is delivered, during which it can be returned for a refund; ask how many days your state allows.
  • Check the insurer's financial strength rating from AM Best or another rating agency. Best burial insurance companies
  • Name a beneficiary and a contingent beneficiary, and tell them the policy exists, so the claim is filed promptly.
  • Cash value in a whole life policy can count as an asset for Medicaid; ask the state agency before buying if long-term care may be needed. Medicaid and funeral costs
  • Many insurers let the beneficiary assign part of the claim to the funeral home, so the home can be paid without the family advancing cash.
  • Agents are usually paid by commission on the policies they sell, so compare quotes from more than one company rather than one agent's product line.

Costs and Pricing

A useful way to size a policy is to price the funeral first.

The NFDA 2023 General Price List Study puts the national median for a funeral with viewing and burial at $8,300, or $9,995 with a vault, before the plot and marker. A funeral with viewing and cremation has a median of $6,280, and direct cremation $2,645 with a family-supplied container.

A person who wants direct cremation needs far less coverage than someone planning a traditional burial in a private cemetery. Get one or two General Price Lists from local funeral homes and add cemetery charges before choosing a face amount, and use the funeral cost comparison worksheet to record them. Then compare quotes from more than one insurer at the same face amount. The face amount does not grow, so a policy sized to today's prices covers less each year; by our calculation the BLS funeral expenses index rose about 10.3% from the 2023 average to September 2026.

Your Consumer Rights

Protections for final expense buyers and their families:

  • State insurance law, not the FTC Funeral Rule, governs the policy; the Rule does not apply to the business of insurance (16 CFR § 453.8(c)).
  • Your state insurance department licenses the agent and the insurer and takes complaints about sales practices and claims.
  • When the policy pays, the funeral home must still give the family an itemized General Price List. Full FTC guide
  • A beneficiary can buy only the funeral goods and services they want; the policy does not lock in a package. How to compare prices
  • Agents must give you the policy's terms in writing; keep the illustration and the policy together.

To check an agent's license or file a complaint, contact the state insurance department. Funeral pricing complaints go to the state funeral board or the Federal Trade Commission.

Practical Steps You Can Take

  1. Decide what kind of funeral and disposition you want, and get a local price for it.
  2. Check whether existing life insurance, savings or a VA benefit already covers that amount.
  3. Get quotes from at least two insurers for the same face amount and policy type.
  4. Ask each one whether there is a graded benefit period and what it pays in those years.
  5. Look up the insurer's financial strength rating and the agent's license.
  6. Compare the total premiums to your expected age with the face amount.
  7. Use the free-look period to read the delivered policy and return it if it is not what was described.
  8. Give the beneficiary a copy of the policy and the insurer's claims phone number.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is final expense insurance?

A small whole life policy, usually sold to people over 50, meant to cover a funeral and final bills. It pays the named beneficiary, who decides how to use the money.

Do I need a medical exam?

Usually not. Simplified issue policies ask health questions; guaranteed issue policies ask none but typically have a waiting period of two or three years for full benefits.

What happens if I die during the waiting period?

Most guaranteed issue policies return the premiums paid, sometimes with interest, for death from natural causes. Accidental death is often covered in full from the start.

Is final expense insurance better than a prepaid plan?

They do different things. Insurance pays cash to a person; a prepaid plan pays a funeral home for specific services. Compare the total cost of each against simply saving.

Can the premiums go up?

Most final expense whole life policies have level premiums. Some term policies sold under the same name end at a set age or rise at renewal, so read the policy type.

Who regulates final expense insurance?

Your state's insurance department. Under the McCarran-Ferguson Act, insurance is regulated mainly by the states, so the department in the state where you bought the policy licenses the insurer and the agent, reviews policy forms and takes complaints. Each state also sets its own free-look period for cancelling a new policy for a full refund.

The FTC Funeral Rule covers funeral homes, not insurers, so it does not apply to a final expense policy. Preneed contracts sold through a funeral home are a separate product, regulated in most states by the funeral board, the insurance department or both. You can find your state's insurance department through the National Association of Insurance Commissioners at content.naic.org.

How much coverage should I buy?

Price the funeral you want first. Direct cremation needs much less than a burial with a private plot. See funeral insurance comparison for how policy types differ.

Is a final expense payout taxable?

Generally no. Life insurance proceeds paid because of the insured's death are excluded from the beneficiary's gross income under 26 U.S.C. § 101(a).
