Funeral Costs in Pennsylvania: What Families Pay in 2026

Reviewed: February 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Pennsylvania Funeral Board Region: Northeast

Pennsylvania's funeral industry is a tale of two states: the Philadelphia metro in the east and Pittsburgh in the west anchor distinctly different funeral markets, while the vast middle of the Commonwealth -- from the Amish country of Lancaster to the former coal towns of the anthracite region -- carries its own traditions and pricing dynamics. As one of the nation's larger funeral markets, Pennsylvania offers families significant choice, though costs run above the national average. The state's deep Catholic, Jewish, and Amish heritage creates a remarkably varied funeral landscape that is unlike any other in the country.

Funeral Cost Breakdown in Pennsylvania

At $8,500 for a traditional funeral, Pennsylvania sits about $650 above the national median. The state's costs are driven upward by the Philadelphia metro, where urban overhead and the higher Northeast cost of living push prices well above the statewide average. Pittsburgh's market is somewhat more moderate, and central Pennsylvania's smaller communities offer the most affordable options. Pennsylvania's large funeral home industry -- the state is home to well over 1,000 licensed establishments -- provides significant choice for comparison shoppers.

What Drives Funeral Costs in Pennsylvania

The Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors regulates all funeral service providers in the Commonwealth. Pennsylvania's funeral market is large and diverse, with a mix of independent family-owned homes, regional chains, and significant corporate presence from SCI and other national companies, particularly in the Philadelphia and Pittsburgh metros.

Philadelphia's funeral market is heavily influenced by its Italian-American, Irish-American, African-American, and Jewish communities, each with distinct funeral traditions. The city's Catholic funeral culture remains strong, with elaborate church funeral masses and cemetery committal services. Philadelphia's African-American funeral homes are deeply woven into the fabric of communities like North and West Philadelphia, providing culturally significant "homegoing" celebrations. The Jewish community has well-established burial societies (chevra kadisha) and funeral homes that follow halacha.

Pittsburgh's market has its own character, shaped by Eastern European immigrant heritage (Polish, Ukrainian, Croatian) and a strong Catholic tradition. The coal country of northeastern and central Pennsylvania maintains a working-class funeral culture where communities rally around bereaved families with practical support. Lancaster County's Amish community handles funerals entirely within its own system -- simple wooden caskets, no embalming, and community cemeteries -- at a fraction of conventional costs. Pennsylvania's moderate cremation rate of 52% reflects the ongoing balance between these traditional communities and a broader trend toward simpler dispositions.

Major Cities in Pennsylvania

Philadelphia and Pittsburgh represent Pennsylvania's primary funeral markets, with distinctly different pricing structures and cultural influences. Allentown, Scranton, and Harrisburg offer moderate pricing. Rural central Pennsylvania and the Poconos are generally the most affordable areas.

Cremation vs. Burial in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania's 52% cremation rate is slightly above the national average but lower than many Northeast states. The strong Catholic traditions in Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, and coal country communities continue to support traditional burial, while younger and more secular populations in the Philadelphia suburbs and college towns (State College, for example) trend more toward cremation. The Amish practice exclusively traditional burial. Pennsylvania's comprehensive preneed regulations have made pre-planned burial arrangements popular, which also supports the burial rate.

Pennsylvania Funeral Regulations

The Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors oversees all licensed funeral service providers. Key regulations include:

  • Strong consumer protection -- Pennsylvania law provides detailed requirements for funeral home practices that go beyond the FTC Funeral Rule.
  • No embalming requirement -- Pennsylvania does not mandate embalming by law.
  • 24-hour cremation waiting period -- Pennsylvania requires a 24-hour wait before cremation.
  • Preneed protections -- Pennsylvania requires 100% of preneed funds to be deposited in trust, with strong consumer cancellation and transfer rights.
  • Funeral director licensure -- Pennsylvania has rigorous licensing requirements including a state exam and supervised residency.

Your Consumer Rights in Pennsylvania

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Pennsylvania must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

Pennsylvania consumers benefit from strong state-level protections. File complaints with the PA State Board of Funeral Directors, the Pennsylvania Attorney General's Bureau of Consumer Protection, or the FTC. The Funeral Consumers Alliance has active Pennsylvania affiliates. The significant price variation across the state makes comparison shopping especially valuable.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Pennsylvania?

The average traditional funeral in Pennsylvania costs approximately $8,500. A funeral with cremation averages $6,600, while direct cremation typically costs $2,000. Cemetery plots average $4,200 additional. Costs vary by provider and location within the state.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Pennsylvania?

Yes. Direct cremation in Pennsylvania averages $2,000 compared to $8,500 for a traditional burial service. The cremation rate in Pennsylvania is 52%.

What are my consumer rights at Pennsylvania funeral homes?

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, all funeral homes in Pennsylvania must provide an itemized General Price List, allow you to choose only services you want, and accept third-party caskets without penalty. File complaints with the state funeral board or the Attorney General.

How can I save on funeral costs in Pennsylvania?

Compare prices from at least three providers. Consider direct cremation or direct burial if a full service is not essential. Purchase caskets from third-party retailers. Check veteran benefits, state assistance, and community resources. Always request the General Price List from every provider.

Does Pennsylvania require embalming?

No. Pennsylvania does not legally require embalming. Refrigeration is an available alternative at most funeral homes. Some providers may have internal policies for viewings, but these are not state mandates.

Why is there such a price difference between Philadelphia and Pittsburgh?

Philadelphia's higher costs reflect its proximity to the more expensive Northeast corridor, higher real estate prices, and greater corporate chain presence. Pittsburgh, while still above the national average, benefits from a lower cost of living and a market that remains more influenced by independent, community-based providers. The difference can be $1,000-$2,000 or more for comparable services.

What are coal country funeral traditions in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania's anthracite coal region -- roughly the Scranton, Wilkes-Barre, and Hazleton area -- has a strong working-class funeral culture shaped by Irish, Welsh, Polish, and other immigrant communities. Funerals here tend toward traditional church services with community-wide participation. Many coal country funeral homes have served the same families for generations. The region's lower cost of living means that funeral prices are generally below the state average, making it one of the more affordable areas in the Northeast for funeral services.
