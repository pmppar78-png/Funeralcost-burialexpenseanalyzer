Your Consumer Rights in Pennsylvania

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Pennsylvania must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Pennsylvania has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What's Specific to Pennsylvania (Northeast)

Pennsylvania's Catholic, Jewish, Greek Orthodox, and Protestant communities each have distinct funeral customs, and Pennsylvania funeral homes generally know these well. If faith tradition matters, asking for a provider that regularly serves your community is usually more useful than asking for "the cheapest" — specialized providers in Pennsylvania are often well-priced within their niche because they do volume.

Payment Assistance in Pennsylvania

  • Pennsylvania DHS pays up to $750 to an enrolled funeral director, and an estate with $1,500 or more in resources is not eligible (55 Pa. Code ch. 283).
  • To collect the one-time $255 from Social Security, a Pennsylvania widow, widower or eligible child must call 1-800-772-1213, and the claim has to be made within two years.
  • For a Pennsylvania veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. Eligibility rules for Pennsylvania families are in our veteran burial benefits guide.

What to Do When Planning a Funeral in Pennsylvania

For anyone planning a funeral in Pennsylvania now or soon, these steps — in roughly this order — prevent the most common expensive mistakes:

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in Pennsylvania or burial in Pennsylvania.

Funeral rules and help in Pennsylvania

If a funeral home in Pennsylvania refuses to show prices or pressures you, the licensing body is the Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors, reachable at 1-833-367-2762.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Pennsylvania among the nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so a Pennsylvania family may arrange much of a funeral itself. The Pennsylvania death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Pennsylvania requires a 24-hour wait before cremation (49 Pa. Code § 13.212).

Pennsylvania DHS pays up to $750 toward burial or cremation for eligible people, paid to an enrolled funeral director; resources of $1,500 or more disqualify the estate (55 Pa. Code ch. 283). (Source: Pennsylvania DHS.)

A Pennsylvania funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Indiantown Gap National Cemetery (717-865-5254), National Cemetery of the Alleghenies (724-746-4363), or Washington Crossing National Cemetery (215-222-7510) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemetery, Pennsylvania Soldiers' and Sailors' Home Cemetery (814-871-4531), takes requests directly. Washington Crossing National Cemetery is in Newtown, Bucks County.

Next steps for a funeral in Pennsylvania

  1. Request itemized prices from Pennsylvania funeral homes by phone or in person; under the FTC Funeral Rule you only pay for what you choose.
  2. Consider a Pennsylvania memorial service after cremation or burial, which avoids embalming and viewing fees.
  3. Use a Pennsylvania funeral director enrolled with DHS, and ask the county assistance office about the $750 payment first.
  4. If the person served, have the funeral home ask about Washington Crossing National Cemetery before you buy a private plot.
  5. Report a funeral home that hides or ignores its price list to the board at 1-833-367-2762.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a typical Pennsylvania funeral cost in 2026?

Most Pennsylvania families paying for a full traditional service see the bill settle around $8,500 — near the $8,300 national benchmark. Cremation with ceremony runs closer to $6,600, while a stripped-down direct cremation in Pennsylvania can land near $2,000. Cemetery plot fees in Pennsylvania add about $4,200. Actual prices shift a lot depending on which Pennsylvania provider you call first, which is why comparing is non-negotiable.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Pennsylvania?

In Pennsylvania, direct cremation at $2,000 costs about $6,500 less than the $8,500 traditional funeral, and roughly $10,700 less once about $4,200 in cemetery costs is added.

What does the FTC Funeral Rule mean for me in Pennsylvania?

In Pennsylvania, as everywhere in the US, the FTC Funeral Rule requires an itemized General Price List, phone price quotes, and no fee for using a casket bought elsewhere. Pennsylvania is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. Complaints about a Pennsylvania funeral home go to the Pennsylvania State Board of Funeral Directors (1-833-367-2762).

What is the best way to reduce funeral expenses in Pennsylvania?

The biggest lever is comparison shopping — two or three GPLs from different Pennsylvania providers, compared line by line, consistently saves families $1,000 or more. Request frozen-ground pricing separately if the death is between December and March — Pennsylvania cemeteries often charge extra in winter. Municipal, Catholic, and Jewish cemeteries in Pennsylvania generally price below private memorial parks by $1,000 or more. At $2,000, direct cremation is the cheapest disposition option available in Pennsylvania.

Does Pennsylvania require embalming?

No. Under 49 Pa. Code § 13.201, a body held more than 24 hours after death must be embalmed, sealed in a container, or refrigerated at 35 to 40°F, unless that conflicts with religious belief or a medical examination. Once out of refrigeration, it must be buried, cremated or entombed within five hours, and an unembalmed body refrigerated longer than 36 hours generally should not be publicly viewed.

Those timing rules make the viewing the deciding question: without one, the roughly $850 embalming charge on our Pennsylvania estimate can be left off. An unapproved charge can be reported to the State Board of Funeral Directors at 1-833-367-2762.

Where does Pennsylvania rank nationally for funeral costs?

Pennsylvania's $8,500 estimate for a traditional funeral is close to the $8,300 national median and ties with Illinois and Vermont for 13th highest of the 50 states on this site's estimates. Its neighbors New Jersey ($10,200), New York ($10,800) and Maryland ($9,200) all come in higher.

Direct cremation in Pennsylvania is estimated at about $2,000. The national $8,300 figure is NFDA's 2023 survey median; the state numbers are this site's estimates, so use them to compare states rather than to predict a single bill.

What is the least expensive way to handle a funeral in Pennsylvania?

In Pennsylvania, direct cremation at about $2,000 is the most affordable path — just transport, cremation, and the return of the remains. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $4,250 in Pennsylvania before cemetery fees and is the cheapest burial option.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Pennsylvania?

Call several Pennsylvania funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Pennsylvania home cannot charge a fee for it.
