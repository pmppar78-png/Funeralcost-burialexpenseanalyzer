Funeral Costs in Hawaii: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: West

Hawaii has the highest cremation rate in the country — 70% of families choose it, versus 60% nationally — and that reality drives almost every pricing conversation on the islands. Traditional burial at Borthwick Mortuary in Honolulu or Dodo Mortuary on the Big Island runs $11,000 before the cemetery, while Ballard Family Mortuary and Pacific Wide Direct Cremation can dispatch a direct cremation for under $3,000. Land scarcity, inter-island transport, and the logistics of repatriation to the mainland all shape pricing in ways no other state experiences.

Inter-Island Transfer and Mainland Repatriation

When a death occurs on the Big Island, Maui, or Kaua'i and the family is on O'ahu — or vice versa — inter-island air transfer adds $800-$1,600 to the basic services bill. Hawaiian Airlines cargo handles most human remains transfers, and the charge compounds if the family decides on a service at a funeral home on a different island than where the death occurred. For mainland repatriation after a visitor's death in Hawaii, expect $2,400-$4,800 for air transfer to the West Coast plus additional costs if the final destination is further east. The Hawaii funeral home coordinates with a mainland receiving funeral home, and both bill separately.

Land Scarcity and What It Does to Plot Prices

O'ahu has not opened a new major cemetery in decades. Existing grounds — Diamond Head Memorial Park, Nu'uanu Memorial Park, Punchbowl (National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific, veterans only) — are running out of available space, which is why single-plot prices on O'ahu commonly exceed $10,000 and why urn-niche columbaria have become the default disposition for most Honolulu families. The Hawai'i State Veterans Cemetery at Kane'ohe offers free burial for eligible veterans and space-eligible spouses, which in a land-constrained state is a significantly larger benefit than it appears on paper. Families with lineal ancestral land can sometimes inter on private property, but that requires county health department and DLNR approvals and is rarely simple.

Line Items Hawaii Families Miss on the First Read

Hawaii invoices carry some charges that simply do not exist in mainland states. Watch these specifically:

  • Inter-island transfer — $800-$1,600 when the place of death and funeral home are on different islands.
  • Casket handling fee — Some Honolulu homes add $100-$300 for caskets shipped from mainland retailers. The FTC Funeral Rule prohibits this, and Hawaii DCCA enforces it.
  • Embalming for mainland transport — Required for shipment of unembalmed remains via commercial air. Budget $1,100-$1,500.
  • Perpetual care — O'ahu cemeteries typically add 10-15% of plot price for endowment care.
  • Scattering permits — Ocean scattering beyond three nautical miles is federally regulated; commercial scattering services handle this and charge $350-$850.
  • Cultural/Hawaiian protocol requests — Traditional chants, kahuna services, or specific lei orders add $200-$800 depending on the request.
  • Weekend/holiday premiums — Saturday services on O'ahu from Mililani Mortuary or Borthwick add 15-25%.

Buyer Protections to Remember

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Hawaii must comply with the FTC Funeral Rule codified at 16 CFR Part 453, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in Hawaii has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Licensee complaints in Hawaii also go to the Hawaii Board of Morticians, Embalmers, and Funeral Directors under DCCA, which handles licensing discipline.

MedQuest and County Burial Assistance in Hawaii

Hawaii does not operate a standalone funeral assistance program at the state level. Families meeting low-income thresholds can apply through the county — each of the four counties (Honolulu, Hawai'i, Maui, Kaua'i) handles its own indigent burial program under Hawaii Revised Statutes Chapter 346 with modest caps, typically $800-$1,500 toward a direct cremation. MedQuest (Hawaii's Medicaid program) will not pay funeral expenses directly, but prepaid irrevocable funeral accounts established while the deceased was on MedQuest are preserved as an exempt asset up to program limits. Native Hawaiian families may also qualify for Office of Hawaiian Affairs emergency assistance in certain circumstances. Eligible veterans can combine the VA burial allowance under 38 CFR § 3.1600 with free interment at the National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (Punchbowl) or the Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery. Apply through the Department of Human Services office in your county within 30 days of death.

Answers to Common Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Hawaii?

Traditional burial in Hawaii averages $11,000 — 40% over the $7,848 national median — and that figure excludes the cemetery plot itself, which on O'ahu commonly runs $8,000-$15,000 at places like Valley of the Temples, Diamond Head Memorial, or Mililani Memorial Park. Cremation with a service lands near $8,200, and direct cremation starts around $3,000 through specialists like Ballard Family Mortuary or Pacific Wide. If the place of death and the funeral home sit on different islands, add $800-$1,600 for inter-island transfer.

What rights do Hawaii families have when arranging a funeral?

The FTC Funeral Rule applies fully in Hawaii: itemized General Price Lists on request, the right to supply your own casket or urn, the right to decline any single service, and prohibition on false statements about legal requirements. The Hawaii Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs (DCCA) Professional and Vocational Licensing Division handles state-level funeral establishment licensing and consumer complaints. Prepaid funeral contracts in Hawaii are regulated separately and must be held in a state-approved trust.

How can I save on funeral costs in Hawaii?

Start with direct cremation from a specialist like Pacific Wide or Ballard at around $3,000 — the single biggest lever in Hawaii. If burial matters, compare O'ahu plot prices against neighbor-island cemeteries (Kaua'i, Maui, and Hawai'i Island all have lower plot prices). For veterans, the National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (Punchbowl) and Hawai'i State Veterans Cemetery at Kane'ohe offer free burial for eligible service members and space-eligible spouses — in a land-constrained state, this is an enormous benefit. Ocean scattering is another cost-effective option that honors Hawaiian cultural tradition.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Hawaii?

Not under Hawaii law for local disposition. Refrigeration, which most island funeral homes have, satisfies public health requirements for the typical arrangement window. Embalming does become effectively necessary when shipping unembalmed remains to the mainland by commercial air — budget $1,100-$1,500 for that. For local cremation or burial in Hawaii, you can decline embalming. The FTC Funeral Rule requires any home claiming embalming is mandatory to point to the specific statute, which does not exist for standard Hawaii dispositions.

Is Hawaii cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Hawaii is the most expensive funeral market in the continental-adjacent US — $11,000 for traditional burial against a $7,848 national figure, a 40% premium. When you add O'ahu cemetery plots at $8,000-$15,000, the total burial cost approaches New York City metro levels. The cost pressure is why 70% of Hawaii families choose cremation. Direct cremation at $3,000 remains the only affordable path in many markets on the islands.

What is the cheapest funeral option in Hawaii?

Direct cremation from a specialist provider — Ballard Family Mortuary, Pacific Wide Direct Cremation, Diamond Head Mortuary — comes in at $3,000 or slightly under. The service includes transfer from place of death, cremation, minimum required documentation, and return of cremated remains to family. No casket purchase is required; a basic alternative container is included. Direct burial is next at roughly $6,600, though the separate plot cost keeps total burial spending above $10,000 on O'ahu in almost all cases.
