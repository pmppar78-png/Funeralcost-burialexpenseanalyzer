Your Consumer Rights in Hawaii

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Hawaii must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Hawaii has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What's Specific to Hawaii (West)

Hawaii's high cremation rate has reshaped its funeral home market: full-service traditional funerals are less than half of all arrangements here, so providers who still do many traditional services in Hawaii tend to either cater to a specific community or compete aggressively on price. Ask each Hawaii provider for its split of cremation versus burial work when comparing; it predicts pricing posture in a state where 70% of families choose cremation.

Payment Assistance in Hawaii

  • Social Security death benefit: A one-time $255 payment for an eligible Hawaii spouse or child, claimed within two years of the death.
  • Payment plans: Some Hawaii funeral homes offer financing or installment plans; get the interest and terms in writing.

Funeral rules and help in Hawaii

Funeral homes in Hawaii are licensed by the Hawaii Department of Health (808-586-8000). That office takes Hawaii complaints about pricing, misleading sales, or a home that will not hand over its price list.

Hiring a funeral home is a choice, not a legal requirement, for most steps of a funeral in Hawaii; the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not count Hawaii among the nine states with funeral-director rules, and you can buy only the services you want.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Hawaii sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (HRS §§ 338-23, 338-25).

Hawaii's Department of Human Services can pay a lump sum equal to Social Security's death payment for a medical or financial assistance recipient who did not qualify for Social Security's own, and for an unclaimed body it pays the mortuary, crematory or hydrolysis provider up to $1,600 (Haw. Rev. Stat. § 346-15; HAR ch. 17-1745); applications are due within 60 days of the death. (Source: Haw. Rev. Stat. § 346-15; HAR ch. 17-1745.) Social Security's one-time $255 death payment, paid only to a qualifying spouse or child, is small next to the $11,000 average Hawaii funeral in the table.

A Hawaii funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (808-532-3720, cremated remains only) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemeteries, Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery (808-369-3575), Kauai Veterans Cemetery (808-241-4460), Lanai Veterans Cemetery (808-565-7086), East Hawaii Veterans Cemetery (808-961-8311), Maui Veterans Cemetery (808-876-4534), Molokai Veterans Cemetery (808-553-3222), or West Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery (808-961-8311), take requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in Hawaii

  1. Get written price lists from at least two Hawaii funeral homes before you agree to anything.
  2. Ask the Hawaii funeral home whether embalming is actually needed for your plans.
  3. If the person was on Hawaii medical or financial assistance, file the DHS death-payment application within 60 days of the death.
  4. For a veteran, price Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery against a private Hawaii plot before choosing.
  5. Keep the board's number, 808-586-8000, in case a funeral home will not honor its price list.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Hawaii?

The only reliable way to pin down your number is to pull General Price Lists from two or three Honolulu-area providers and compare line by line.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in Hawaii?

Direct cremation in Hawaii starts around $3,000; a traditional funeral with burial averages $11,000, not counting the cemetery plot or vault. For most Hawaii families, that is a difference of at least $8,000.

What rights do Hawaii families have when arranging a funeral?

The federal FTC Funeral Rule gives Hawaii families the right to an itemized price list, to prices over the phone, and to buy only the goods and services they want. According to the Funeral Consumers Alliance, Hawaii does not require families to hire a funeral director for every step. Complaints about a Hawaii funeral home go to the Hawaii Department of Health (808-586-8000).

How can I save on funeral costs in Hawaii?

Start by lining up General Price Lists from two or three Honolulu-area providers before you commit to anything. Conservation burial grounds and hybrid cemetery sections in Hawaii typically cost 30-50% less than traditional plots in the same cemetery. Direct cremation at $3,000 remains the lowest-cost disposition in every Hawaii market. Layer in veteran burial benefits (a free grave at Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery), the Hawaii Department of Human Services, and the $255 Social Security payment where you qualify.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Hawaii?

Federal law already stops a Hawaii funeral home from claiming embalming is legally required when it is not, and its price list must say embalming is unnecessary outside certain special cases; ask any home that pushes embalming to cite the rule in writing. Hawaii adds its own price protection. HRS §441-22.6 requires every mortuary, cemetery or pre-need funeral authority to give a prospective buyer a current price list for written approval, with each item priced separately, and §441-22.5 bars charging more than the signed list unless you ask for an added item and approve its price.

Is Hawaii cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Costs here are above the national average in most metro areas, though rural communities may see lower prices and the West region trends higher than the country overall.

What is the cheapest funeral option in Hawaii?

Direct cremation is the lowest-cost option in Hawaii, starting at roughly $3,000. Direct burial comes next at around $5,500 and still avoids viewing, ceremony, and embalming, though the $5,500 Hawaii plot estimate still applies.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in Hawaii?

Ask at least three Hawaii providers for their General Price Lists and compare direct cremation and immediate burial lines. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Hawaii home cannot charge a fee for it.
