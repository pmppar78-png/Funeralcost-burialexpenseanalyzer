Funeral Costs in Hawaii: What Families Pay in 2026

Reviewed: February 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, Hawaii Funeral Board Region: West

Hawaii ranks among the most expensive states in the nation for funeral services. The islands' geographic isolation drives up nearly every cost associated with end-of-life care, from transporting caskets and embalming supplies by sea to the extraordinary scarcity of burial land. At the same time, Hawaii's deeply multicultural population -- including Native Hawaiian, Japanese, Filipino, Chinese, and Korean communities -- sustains rich funeral traditions that shape what families choose and what providers offer. This guide walks you through what to realistically expect when arranging a funeral in the Aloha State.

Funeral Cost Breakdown in Hawaii

Hawaii's funeral costs are significantly above the national median of $7,848 for a traditional service. The primary driver is logistics: nearly every material item -- caskets, urns, embalming chemicals, vault liners -- must be shipped across the Pacific Ocean. Real estate prices on the islands push cemetery plot costs far above mainland averages. Even cremation, while considerably less expensive than burial, costs roughly double what families pay in affordable mainland states because of limited crematory capacity and high operational overhead.

What Drives Funeral Costs in Hawaii

Island logistics and shipping. Hawaii's mid-Pacific location means that caskets, urns, embalming fluids, and vault liners are shipped by container from the mainland, adding freight surcharges that simply do not exist in continental states. Transporting remains between islands (for example, from Maui to Oahu for cremation) can add $500 to $1,500 to total costs.

Regulatory oversight. Funeral homes and directors in Hawaii are licensed through the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs (DCCA), Professional and Vocational Licensing Division. The state requires funeral directors to hold active licenses and adhere to strict reporting requirements, which adds a modest compliance cost that is passed through to consumers.

Land scarcity and burial plot prices. Usable land on Hawaii's volcanic islands is extremely limited. Cemetery plots on Oahu can exceed $9,000 in premium locations, while even modest plots on neighbor islands start around $3,300. This scarcity is the single largest reason Hawaii's cremation rate has climbed to 70% -- one of the highest in the nation.

Cultural and religious diversity. Hawaii's multicultural population supports a wide range of funeral traditions. Native Hawaiian families often hold large community-based celebrations of life that can last for days. Japanese Buddhist traditions (common on Oahu and the Big Island) involve specific rituals, incense ceremonies, and multi-day observances. Filipino Catholic wakes frequently include overnight vigils. Chinese and Korean communities observe particular mourning periods and rituals. These culturally rich services often require specialized arrangements that affect pricing.

Limited competition. Hawaii has a small number of funeral homes relative to its population, particularly on neighbor islands. Maui, Kauai, and the Big Island each have only a handful of providers, which limits competitive pricing pressure.

Major Cities in Hawaii

Funeral costs in Hawaii vary by island and community. Honolulu on Oahu, home to nearly 70% of the state's population, has the most providers but also the highest real estate costs. Neighbor islands have fewer options and may require inter-island transport for cremation services.

Cremation vs. Burial in Hawaii

With a 70% cremation rate, Hawaii is firmly a cremation-preference state. The driving factor is not just cost but geography: limited burial land makes cremation a practical necessity for many families. Scattering ashes at sea is a beloved tradition in Hawaii, though federal regulations require scattering at least three nautical miles offshore. Some families choose to scatter ashes at meaningful locations on land with private property permission. For those who do choose burial, Hawaii's volcanic soil can create challenges for traditional in-ground interment, and above-ground options like columbariums are increasingly popular.

Hawaii Funeral Regulations

Hawaii's funeral industry is overseen by the DCCA Professional and Vocational Licensing Division. Key regulations that affect consumers include:

  • Licensing requirements — All funeral directors and embalmers must be licensed by the state. Funeral establishments must maintain a valid establishment license.
  • No embalming mandate — Hawaii does not require embalming by law. However, if remains will be transported by air between islands or to the mainland, airlines may impose their own requirements.
  • Preneed contract protections — Hawaii regulates preneed funeral contracts. Providers must deposit a percentage of preneed funds into a trust account, and consumers have the right to cancel with a refund.
  • Home funerals — Hawaii permits families to care for their own deceased, though a licensed funeral director must file the death certificate.
  • Green burial — While no dedicated natural burial grounds currently exist in Hawaii, the state does not prohibit green burial. The volcanic terrain and limited land present practical challenges for conservation burial.

Your Consumer Rights in Hawaii

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Hawaii must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

Beyond federal protections, Hawaii's Office of Consumer Protection (part of the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs) investigates complaints against funeral providers. The state's Unfair and Deceptive Acts or Practices law (HRS Chapter 480) provides additional consumer safeguards. If you encounter misleading pricing, pressure to purchase unnecessary services, or other concerns, you can file a complaint with the DCCA at cca.hawaii.gov, the FTC at ftc.gov, or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for independent guidance.

Payment Assistance in Hawaii

Given Hawaii's high funeral costs, many families need financial assistance. Here are the options available in the state:

  • Social Security death benefit — A one-time $255 payment for eligible surviving spouses or children. Learn more
  • Veteran burial benefits — Hawaii is home to the National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (Punchbowl), which provides free burial for eligible veterans. The Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery in Kaneohe also offers burial at no cost. Veteran benefits guide
  • Hawaii Department of Human Services — Low-income families may qualify for funeral assistance through the state's General Assistance program or through the county-administered burial assistance programs on each island.
  • County indigent burial programs — Each county (Honolulu, Hawaii, Maui, Kauai) maintains programs for families who cannot afford basic funeral expenses. Contact your county's social services office for eligibility details.
  • Crowdfunding — Particularly common in Hawaii's tight-knit communities, platforms like GoFundMe are widely used. Crowdfunding guide
  • Payment plans — Some Hawaii funeral homes offer financing, though options may be more limited on neighbor islands. Payment plan options

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a funeral cost in Hawaii?

The average traditional funeral in Hawaii costs approximately $11,000, making it one of the most expensive states for funeral services. A funeral with cremation averages $8,200, while direct cremation typically costs $3,000. Cemetery plots average $5,500 but can exceed $9,000 in premium Honolulu locations. These elevated costs reflect Hawaii's island logistics, limited land, and high cost of living.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Hawaii?

Yes, significantly. Direct cremation at $3,000 saves families roughly $8,000 compared to a traditional burial with all associated costs. This is the primary reason Hawaii's cremation rate has reached 70%. Many families combine cremation with ocean scattering ceremonies, which align with both Hawaiian cultural traditions and the practical reality of limited burial land.

What are my consumer rights at Hawaii funeral homes?

In addition to the federal FTC Funeral Rule protections (itemized pricing, freedom to choose only what you want, third-party casket acceptance), Hawaii consumers are protected under the state's Unfair and Deceptive Acts or Practices law. The DCCA licenses and oversees all funeral establishments. You can request a General Price List from any provider, and no funeral home can refuse to serve you because you purchased a casket elsewhere.

How can I save on funeral costs in Hawaii?

Given Hawaii's high costs, consider these strategies: choose direct cremation to avoid the most expensive service items; compare prices from providers on your island and neighboring islands; ask about simple container options instead of expensive caskets; explore ocean scattering instead of purchasing a cemetery niche; contact the National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific for veteran eligibility; and request the General Price List from every provider you contact.

Does Hawaii require embalming?

No. Hawaii does not legally require embalming. If a family chooses not to embalm, refrigeration must be used to preserve remains until disposition. Some airlines require embalming for inter-island or mainland transport of remains, so check with your carrier if transportation is involved. Many funeral homes offer refrigeration as a standard alternative.

Can I scatter ashes in the ocean in Hawaii?

Yes. Scattering cremated remains at sea is a deeply honored tradition in Hawaii. Under federal EPA regulations, ashes must be scattered at least three nautical miles from shore. Many families hire charter boats for private ceremonies, and several Honolulu-based companies specialize in ash-scattering services that include traditional Hawaiian blessings. No state permit is required for ocean scattering.

What happens if someone dies on a neighbor island without a funeral home?

Some smaller communities on Hawaii, Maui, Molokai, and Lanai have very limited or no local funeral homes. In these cases, remains typically need to be transported to a larger town or to Oahu. Inter-island transport costs range from $500 to $1,500 and can add significant time to the process. Planning ahead with a provider who serves your specific island is strongly recommended.
