Burial Costs in New Orleans, Louisiana (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Louisiana

Burial in New Orleans is unlike burial anywhere else in the United States. The water table sits so close to the surface that interment in the ground is rarely practical, and the city's cemeteries answered that geography more than a century ago with above-ground tombs, wall vaults, and the distinctly local Cities of the Dead. A traditional funeral-service fee here averages about $7,705, a plot or vault space starts near $3,680, and once casket, opening, and marker are included the bill for a New Orleans burial usually lands between $14,697 and $20,689.

Built out, a New Orleans burial lands between $14,697 and $20,689. The gap is wide because tomb choice, cemetery, and casket each carry a three- or four-figure swing, and the tradition of family tombs changes the math entirely for households who already own one.

Why the Cities of the Dead exist

The elevation of New Orleans runs from about six feet below sea level to just a few feet above it, and the water table responds accordingly. Colonial-era attempts at in-ground burial produced the well-known problem of coffins surfacing after heavy rain. By the late 1700s the city had pivoted to above-ground tombs, and today the three most visited cemeteries — St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 in Tremé, Lafayette Cemetery No. 1 in the Garden District, and Metairie Cemetery along Pontchartrain Boulevard — are dominated by family tombs, society vaults, and wall vaults rather than lawn sections. If your family already holds a tomb, your cost structure for a New Orleans burial is fundamentally different from what the table above describes.

Three tomb paths, three price ranges

  • Family tomb reopening. If a family tomb is available, the primary cemetery charge is the opening-and-closing fee (roughly $1,840 here) plus any required masonry work. Plot purchase is skipped. Funeral-home fees remain.
  • Wall vault (society or municipal). Purchased or rented from the cemetery. Often the least expensive entombment route in historic New Orleans cemeteries, and the most common option when no family tomb exists.
  • New lawn-crypt or in-ground plot. Available in newer cemeteries such as Metairie's lawn sections. This is the option that tracks most closely with the $3,680 plot average shown on the table.

Tomb restoration: the line item no one mentions first

A reopening on an older family tomb frequently surfaces masonry issues — cracked stucco, a failed seal, a marble tablet that needs replacement. Restoration costs in New Orleans are quoted separately from the opening fee and commonly run from several hundred dollars for a simple re-sealing into the low four figures for a full stucco and plaster restoration. Save Our Cemeteries, a local nonprofit, and the Archdiocese of New Orleans Cemeteries office can both point families to approved masons. Ask the cemetery for the list of approved contractors before selecting one on your own — unapproved work can create problems for future interments.

What Louisiana and Orleans Parish require

The Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors licenses funeral homes, enforces the federal FTC Funeral Rule, and investigates pricing complaints. Embalming is not required by state law; refrigeration within 24 hours is the standard alternative. Louisiana also regulates pre-need funeral contracts through its insurance and banking codes, with required trust or insurance funding. For families without resources, the Orleans Parish Coroner's office administers an indigent disposition program — documentation requirements are strict, and the program is handled as a direct cremation in most cases rather than a traditional burial.

Local homes and the long-standing providers

Lake Lawn Metairie Funeral Home, affiliated with Metairie Cemetery, is one of the longest-operating providers in the region and often sets the reference price for the metro area. Smaller neighborhood firms in Gentilly, Algiers, and the West Bank frequently quote $1,500 to $2,500 below Lake Lawn Metairie on the basic service fee. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, every funeral home in Louisiana is required to provide a General Price List on request, in writing, and to quote by phone. Cemetery pricing is a separate document you will need to ask for explicitly.

Where a New Orleans burial bill actually bends

  • Choose the cemetery before the funeral home. Family tombs, archdiocesan sections, and society vaults have meaningfully different pricing than new memorial parks. The cemetery decision drives more of the total than the funeral-home decision here.
  • Ask about wall vaults at older cemeteries. Wall-vault spaces, when available, are often the most affordable entombment option in the historic cemeteries and avoid the need for a vault liner entirely.
  • Separate the tomb opening from any restoration work. Cemeteries sometimes bundle them; ask for each on its own line so you can compare.
  • Bring your own casket. Louisiana funeral homes must accept a third-party casket under the FTC Funeral Rule. Savings on the $2,312 casket line frequently reach 50 to 70 percent. Casket buying guide | Online casket retailers
  • Veterans outside the metro. The Louisiana Veterans Cemetery in Leesville and the national cemeteries at Port Hudson and Alexandria waive most of the cemetery-side charges for eligible veterans and spouses.
  • Markers are negotiable. Metairie Cemetery and several archdiocesan cemeteries have monument rules, but within those rules third-party monument dealers routinely beat funeral-home and cemetery pricing on headstones and bronze plaques. Headstone cost guide
FTC Funeral Rule reminder: A New Orleans funeral home cannot refuse a casket you bought elsewhere or charge a handling fee for it. Compare online casket retailers before you sign a contract.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in New Orleans?

In 2026, a traditional above-ground entombment or ground burial in New Orleans runs about $7,705 for funeral-home services and $3,680 for a plot or lawn crypt. With casket ($2,312), vault or liner ($1,472), opening and closing ($1,840), and marker ($1,500 to $3,500), the full bill typically arrives between $14,697 and $20,689. Families with an existing tomb at St. Louis, Lafayette, or Metairie can avoid the plot-purchase line and pay a reopening charge instead.

Is a burial vault required in New Orleans?

Louisiana state law does not require a vault. Cemeteries in the New Orleans metro often do, and the justification is practical — the shallow water table and soft alluvial soil make ground settling an unusually persistent problem here. In above-ground tombs and wall vaults, an outer vault is not relevant. In newer lawn sections such as those at Metairie Cemetery, a concrete liner is usually accepted in place of a full sealed vault and runs noticeably less. Ask the specific cemetery what it will accept before you buy.

What are the cheapest burial options in New Orleans?

The least expensive routes in the metro are, in rough order: reopening an existing family tomb; purchasing a wall vault in an archdiocesan or society cemetery; and direct burial in a newer lawn section with a concrete liner rather than a sealed vault. Buying a casket from an online retailer saves most families $1,000 to $1,500 against the funeral-home price. Green burial options are limited within Orleans Parish but a small number of cemeteries in neighboring parishes accept natural burials.
