Funeral costs in New Hampshire run from about $2,100 for direct cremation up to $11,440 for a full traditional service with burial. The range is wide because funeral pricing in New Hampshire is not standardized: what you pay depends heavily on which provider you call first.

What Drives Funeral Costs in New Hampshire

What you pay for a funeral in New Hampshire depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in New Hampshire, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether New Hampshire state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. Northeast pricing is generally higher than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in New Hampshire routinely differs by 10-30%.

At 72%, New Hampshire's cremation rate is 8.6 points above the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

Your Consumer Rights in New Hampshire

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in New Hampshire must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

Help Paying for a Funeral in New Hampshire

  • New Hampshire towns and cities pay for a decent burial or cremation of a person who was receiving assistance, and they may recover the cost from the estate or liable relatives (RSA 165:3).
  • The $255 Social Security death payment goes only to an eligible surviving spouse or child; in New Hampshire, as elsewhere, they apply by calling 1-800-772-1213 within two years of the death.
  • For a New Hampshire veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. Eligibility rules for New Hampshire families are in our veteran burial benefits guide.

Planning a Funeral in New Hampshire: Where to Start

If you are currently arranging a funeral in New Hampshire, here is a step-by-step approach that can save you time, stress, and money:

  1. Decide on disposition: cremation in New Hampshire or burial in New Hampshire.

New Hampshire death certificates: $15, then $10

In New Hampshire a $15 search fee covers the first certified copy of a death certificate, each extra copy issued at the same time is $10, and the $15 is owed even if no record is found (NH Secretary of State, Division of Vital Records Administration; RSA 5-C:10; checked October 29, 2026). Five copies come to $55.

  • Your town clerk: all 234 city and town clerks issue copies as well as the state office at 9 Ratification Way, Concord.
  • Eligibility: you must show a "direct and tangible" interest and positive identification.
  • Card orders: VitalChek and EB2Gov add their own processing and shipping fees on top of the $15.

New Hampshire: the licensing office, town welfare burials and the state veterans cemetery

New Hampshire does not run a stand-alone funeral board office. Funeral directors, embalmers and funeral establishments are registered through the New Hampshire Office of Professional Licensure and Certification, which staffs the Board of Registration of Funeral Directors and Embalmers. Its line, 603-271-2152, is the place to confirm that a home's license is current or to lodge a complaint about a price list that was refused or a charge you never agreed to.

Families here have more room to act on their own than in some states. New Hampshire is absent from the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so relatives may handle much of the work themselves. The paperwork does not go away: someone still has to file the death certificate and obtain the disposition permit before burial or cremation.

Two time limits shape the schedule. Cremation cannot take place until 48 hours after death (RSA 325-A:18), which is worth knowing if relatives are travelling in for a short service. Separately, RSA 325:40-b says that once a funeral home has held a body for 48 hours without disposition, it must be embalmed, refrigerated below 40°F or sealed, so a refrigerated hold keeps embalming optional.

Public help comes from the municipality rather than a county or the state. When someone who was receiving assistance dies, the town or city overseers of public welfare must pay for a decent burial or cremation under RSA 165:3. The town can later try to recover that cost from the estate or from relatives who are legally liable, so the family should talk to the local welfare officer before it signs anything with a funeral home. Social Security adds only a $255 lump sum, and that goes to an eligible spouse or child who claims it within two years, not to the funeral home.

For veterans, the New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery (603-796-2026) handles its own burial requests, and the VA provides the headstone or marker there without charge. With a private plot estimated at about $4,400 on this page, eligibility for the state cemetery can change the total more than any other single choice.

A short checklist for New Hampshire families

  1. If the person was on public assistance, phone the town or city welfare officer first; RSA 165:3 help depends on contact before arrangements are fixed.
  2. Ask each funeral home for its General Price List and compare the direct cremation line against the $2,100 estimate here.
  3. Plan around the 48-hour cremation wait, and ask whether refrigeration is included so embalming stays your decision.
  4. For a veteran, call the state veterans cemetery before buying a private grave.
  5. Take any refusal to show prices to the OPLC at 603-271-2152.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in New Hampshire right now?

Current averages in New Hampshire come in at $8,800 for a traditional funeral, $6,800 for cremation with a service, and from $2,100 for direct cremation, with the traditional figure sitting near the $8,300 US median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $4,400 when a New Hampshire family chooses burial.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in New Hampshire?

Yes, by a wide margin. Direct cremation at about $2,100 is roughly $6,700 below the $8,800 traditional New Hampshire funeral, and the gap grows to about $11,100 once a $4,400 plot and its cemetery fees are counted. A memorial service can still be held after cremation.

What rights do New Hampshire families have when arranging a funeral?

In New Hampshire, as everywhere in the US, the FTC Funeral Rule requires an itemized General Price List, phone price quotes, and no fee for using a casket bought elsewhere. New Hampshire is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. Complaints about a New Hampshire funeral home go to the New Hampshire Office of Professional Licensure and Certification (603-271-2152).

How do I keep funeral costs down in New Hampshire?

Direct cremation runs about $2,100 in New Hampshire, and municipal, Catholic and Jewish cemeteries often cost at least $1,000 less than private memorial parks. A death between December and March can bring a frozen-ground charge, so ask for it in writing. If you buy ahead, New Hampshire requires any prearranged contract funded through a trust, insurance policy or annuity to be in writing and to list the seller's name, address and phone number and the purchaser's name and address, plus the beneficiary's if that is someone else (RSA 325:46-a). Comparing three General Price Lists still saves the most.

Does New Hampshire require embalming?

No. RSA 325:40-b requires that if disposition has not happened within 48 hours after the funeral home takes custody, the body be embalmed, kept below 40°F, or sealed in a leak-proof container. With refrigeration, embalming remains your choice.

New Hampshire funeral homes are licensed through the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification (603-271-2152), which is also where to report a charge for embalming you did not approve; this page estimates that charge at about $880 in New Hampshire.

Is New Hampshire cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Compare the New Hampshire figures in the cost table on this page with the national median of $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, or $6,280 with cremation (NFDA 2023). Prices inside New Hampshire vary by provider, so the General Price Lists you collect matter more than any average.

Which funeral option costs the least in New Hampshire?

Direct cremation carries the lowest price tag in New Hampshire, from roughly $2,100. Direct burial is the next step up at about $4,400, which is $4,400 below a traditional New Hampshire funeral.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in New Hampshire?

Phone several New Hampshire homes and ask for their prices; the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to answer by phone. If the person received public assistance, also call the town welfare officer, since RSA 165:3 may cover a basic burial or cremation. You may supply your own casket or urn without paying a handling fee.
