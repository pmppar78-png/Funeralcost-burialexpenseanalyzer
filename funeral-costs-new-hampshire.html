Funeral Costs in New Hampshire: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: Northeast

New Hampshire has written the strictest preneed funeral trust rules in the country — 100% of principal held in trust, cancellation and refund at any time — a statute that emerged from a wave of preneed fraud in the 1990s. It also has one of the highest cremation rates in the nation at 72%. Phaneuf Funeral Homes, operating out of Manchester since 1906 with locations across southern New Hampshire, along with Bennett Funeral Home in Concord and smaller independent firms in the Seacoast and Upper Valley, set the pricing. A traditional funeral averages $8,800 — 12% above the national figure — while direct cremation through Phaneuf's high-volume cremation service starts near $995, well below the state average of $2,100.

New Hampshire's 100% Preneed Trust Rule — Strictest in the Nation

In the late 1990s, a series of preneed funeral trust failures across New England — funeral homes pocketing prepayments and leaving families with no service when the time came — prompted New Hampshire to enact what remains the toughest preneed regulation in the United States. Under RSA 325 and the New Hampshire Board of Registration of Funeral Directors and Embalmers rules, any funeral home selling a preneed contract must deposit 100% of the principal into a separate trust account, and the purchaser retains the right to cancel at any time and receive a full refund of principal plus accrued interest. No "retention fee," no "administrative fee," no exceptions. Compare this with neighboring Vermont (75%), Massachusetts (variable), or Maine (85%) and the gap is substantial.

For consumers, this has two practical effects. First, New Hampshire is probably the safest state in the country to prepay for a funeral — the money is genuinely held in trust and legally retrievable. Second, because sellers cannot extract fees from the preneed principal, some New Hampshire funeral homes don't heavily market preneed contracts, preferring the simpler economics of at-need arrangements. If a New Hampshire provider will not answer a direct question about preneed trust handling, walk.

Phaneuf Funeral Homes and the Direct-Cremation Floor

Phaneuf Funeral Homes, founded in 1906 and now operated by the fifth generation of the same family, runs six chapels across southern New Hampshire along with its own crematories. Phaneuf has been the most visible force in pushing New Hampshire's direct-cremation price down — its advertised direct cremation often lists at $995-$1,395, well below the state average of $2,100. Bennett Funeral Home in Concord and J.S. Pelkey & Son in Kittery offer comparable rates for their markets. The state's 72% cremation rate (third highest nationally alongside Nevada and Montana) has effectively forced every New Hampshire provider to publish a competitive flat-rate cremation package — there is no hiding behind bundled pricing here.

The counterpoint: New Hampshire traditional funerals still average $8,800, 12% above the US median. Manchester and Nashua non-declinable service fees run $2,500-$3,000 at the larger firms, reflecting the Northeast's higher labor, real estate, and facility costs. That spread — cheap cremation, expensive traditional service — is one of the widest in the country.

Pine Grove, Valley Street, and the Municipal Cemetery Advantage

New Hampshire towns still operate municipal cemeteries at prices most states abandoned decades ago. Pine Grove Cemetery in Manchester, Blossom Hill Cemetery in Concord, Edgewood Cemetery in Nashua, and the historic burial grounds in towns like Exeter, Dover, and Portsmouth typically price plots well below private memorial parks — often $1,200-$2,500 versus $3,500-$5,500 at the commercial cemeteries. Because New Hampshire towns are geographically small and densely settled along the Merrimack Valley, almost every family has access to a municipal cemetery within a few miles. For veterans, the New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery at Boscawen provides no-cost burial for eligible veterans, spouses, and dependents — vault, marker, and perpetual care included.

New Hampshire DHHS Burial Assistance and Medicaid Spend-Down

New Hampshire operates a modest state-funded burial assistance program through the Department of Health and Human Services Division of Client Services. For decedents who were receiving Old Age Assistance, Aid to the Needy Blind, or Aid to the Permanently and Totally Disabled at the time of death, the program provides up to $750 toward funeral costs — a figure unchanged for years and now modest by any standard. Several New Hampshire counties add modest supplements. Of greater practical value: New Hampshire Medicaid allows enrollees to spend down into an irrevocable funeral trust of up to $8,500 (subject to reasonable-cost guidelines) without affecting Medicaid eligibility, commonly used in nursing home spend-down planning. The Medicaid trust is the workhorse; the DHHS burial assistance is a safety net. Consult the New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services at dhhs.nh.gov or a New Hampshire elder-law attorney before structuring either.

Small line items that become big ones

Many New Hampshire families are surprised by charges that appear after the initial quote. Here are the most common hidden funeral fees to watch for:

  • Casket handling fee — Some funeral homes add a surcharge for caskets purchased elsewhere, despite this being illegal under the FTC Funeral Rule
  • Mandatory embalming — Funeral homes may claim embalming is required. In New Hampshire, it is almost never legally required. Know your rights
  • Cemetery "perpetual care" fees — An ongoing maintenance charge, typically $200–$1,000, added on top of the plot price
  • Vault/liner surcharge — Cemeteries may require a specific vault brand or type, inflating costs by $500–$2,000+
  • Documentation and filing fees — Death certificates, permits, and filing fees can add $200–$600
  • Weekend/holiday premium — Services held outside business hours may carry a 15–25% surcharge
  • "Package" markups — Bundled packages often include services you don't need. Always compare line-by-line. What funeral homes don't tell you

Rules in Your Favor

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in New Hampshire must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in New Hampshire has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Your state attorney general's office can also assist with consumer protection complaints.

Frozen Ground and the White Mountains Spring Burial Tradition

Between mid-December and early April, the ground across most of New Hampshire freezes too hard for practical grave excavation, and a tradition of spring interment has developed across the White Mountains, North Country, and Upper Valley — towns from Lancaster to Colebrook to Pittsburg, and along the Connecticut River from Hanover through Lebanon and Claremont. Phaneuf, Bennett, and most New Hampshire funeral homes will cremate in winter and hold cremated remains through the thaw, often without storage fees for up to six months: the visitation and service happen in January, and the interment at Pine Grove, Blossom Hill, or a hillside cemetery in Bethlehem or Jefferson waits until May. Some Manchester and Nashua cemeteries maintain winter vaults for casketed burials at $500–$1,200, but in Coos County, Grafton County, and the Lakes Region around Squam and Winnipesaukee, winter vault access is limited enough that families frequently default to cremation. Ask specifically about "spring burial" pricing on the first call — New Hampshire funeral homes quote it differently than a summer interment, and the distinction can shift $800–$1,500 in either direction.

Commonly Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in New Hampshire right now?

Traditional funerals in New Hampshire average $8,800, cremation with a service runs $6,800, and direct cremation through Phaneuf Funeral Homes or Bennett starts around $2,100 — with Phaneuf's Simple Cremation routinely quoted at $995 plus state fees. The non-declinable basic services fee at established Manchester, Concord, and Nashua providers runs $2,500–$3,000, several hundred dollars higher than the national median. Always request the General Price List under RSA 325 before agreeing to any package.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in New Hampshire?

Yes, substantially. Direct cremation at $2,100 saves roughly $6,700 compared with an $8,800 traditional funeral plus burial. New Hampshire's cremation rate sits near 72%, well above the 60% national average and among the highest in the Northeast. Phaneuf, Bennett, J.S. Pelkey & Son in Kittery, and Cremation Society of New Hampshire have pushed direct-cremation floor prices down to $995–$1,395 in the Manchester–Concord–Nashua corridor.

What rights do New Hampshire families have when arranging a funeral?

Under the FTC Funeral Rule and New Hampshire RSA 325, every licensed funeral home must hand you an itemized General Price List, let you decline any service you do not want, accept a casket or urn bought from a third party with no surcharge, and avoid any false claim that embalming or a specific casket is legally required. The New Hampshire Board of Registration of Funeral Directors and Embalmers at oplc.nh.gov oversees licensing and complaints, and the state enforces the strictest preneed trust rule in the country at 100%.

How do I keep funeral costs down in New Hampshire?

Compare at least three New Hampshire General Price Lists line by line — Phaneuf, Bennett, and an independent or cooperative provider — and focus on the non-declinable basic services fee, which drives most of the price variation. Choose direct cremation ($995–$2,100) if cost is the priority. Municipal cemeteries such as Pine Grove in Manchester, Blossom Hill in Concord, and Edgewood in Nashua undercut private memorial parks by $1,000–$2,500 for a single plot. Ask about spring-burial pricing if the death falls between December and March — winter vault fees run $500–$1,200 extra. Then confirm NH DHHS burial assistance, VA benefits, and Social Security's $255 survivor payment.

Is New Hampshire cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

New Hampshire runs 12% above the $7,848 US median for a traditional funeral, landing at $8,800. The state sits below Massachusetts ($10,400) and Connecticut ($10,200) but above Maine ($8,200) and Vermont. Real estate along the Seacoast and Lakes Region, labor costs, and New Hampshire's strict 100% preneed trust requirement under RSA 325 all push prices slightly above the national average, though the direct-cremation floor established by Phaneuf keeps the low end competitive with northern New England neighbors.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in New Hampshire?

Request General Price Lists from three or more licensed New Hampshire providers — include at least one direct-cremation specialist like Phaneuf's Simple Cremation or Cremation Society of New Hampshire alongside a traditional full-service home. Contact Funeral Consumers Alliance of New Hampshire for vetted recommendations and price-list surveys. Check NH DHHS burial assistance eligibility up to $750 for OAA, ANB, and APTD recipients, and verify whether the decedent qualifies for free interment at the New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery in Boscawen. Municipal cemeteries consistently price below private memorial parks across the state.
