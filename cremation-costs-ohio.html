Cremation Costs in Ohio (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 55%

Ohio's cremation rate crossed 55% in the early 2020s and is tracking upward, with clear divisions between the big three metros — Cleveland, Columbus, Cincinnati — and the Appalachian southeastern counties that remain traditional. Schoedinger in Columbus, Brown-Forward in Cleveland, and Frederick Funeral Home in Cincinnati anchor the metro markets. Direct cremation in Ohio runs $1,700 on average; cremation with a full service lands near $5,600.

The Ohio Cremation Authorization Act, in plain English

Ohio's cremation legal framework sits in Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4717 — the Cremation Authorization Act — and is enforced by the Ohio Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors. The board inspects every Ohio crematory biennially and publishes the results. Key provisions families should know:

  • Written authorization required from the person with legal right of disposition, following the priority order in ORC 2108.70 (spouse, majority of adult children, etc.).
  • 24-hour minimum hold from time of death to cremation.
  • Pacemaker, defibrillator, and radioactive implant certification — funeral director must certify in writing that any such devices have been removed or disclosed.
  • Cremated remains release form — separate from the cremation authorization, documents chain of custody.
  • Coroner permit fee — Ohio counties charge $20-$35 for the cremation permit; typically itemized on the GPL.

Ohio metro pricing, by market

Ohio has enough geographic and economic variation that a single "Ohio cremation price" masks significant differences. Approximate 2026 direct cremation ranges:

  • Columbus metro: $1,395-$2,395. Schoedinger (founded 1855) anchors the high end; Cremation Society of Columbus and Heritage Cremation serve the budget end.
  • Cleveland metro: $1,495-$2,495. Brown-Forward, Jardine, and Saxton dominate legacy; Cremation Society of Ohio handles low-cost direct.
  • Cincinnati metro: $1,395-$2,295. Frederick Funeral Home, Weil, Vitt Stermer & Anderson set the range.
  • Dayton, Toledo, Akron: $1,495-$2,195. Mid-size market pricing sits between metro and rural.
  • Appalachian SE Ohio (Athens, Meigs, Vinton): $1,795-$2,695. Fewer providers; transport distance adds variance.

Ohio's cemetery tax district system

Ohio is one of a handful of states where local cemetery districts can be organized under ORC Chapter 759 and funded through property tax levies. These public cemeteries (common in rural Ohio townships) typically offer inurnment of cremated remains at significantly below-market rates — $200-$500 versus $1,200-$3,500 at commercial Ohio cemeteries. If the decedent had local ties to a township cemetery, ask about inurnment eligibility. The township trustees or cemetery board administers enrollment.

Cuyahoga County Medical Examiner hold. Deaths in Cleveland-area counties requiring ME review (approximately 25-30% of deaths) route through the Cuyahoga County Medical Examiner's office in Cleveland. Expect a 48-96 hour delay during peak periods. Other Ohio counties contract with the Cuyahoga ME or use the Montgomery County Coroner (Dayton) for overflow — ask the funeral director which route your case will take.

Veteran options in Ohio

Ohio has two active national cemeteries:

  • Dayton National Cemetery — one of the original 14 national cemeteries established in 1867; accepts cremated remains in columbarium niches.
  • Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery (Rittman, near Akron) — opened 2000; extensive columbarium capacity; accepts cremated remains.

Both provide no-cost interment, government-furnished niche covers, perpetual care, and military honors on request. The state also maintains five Ohio Veterans' Homes and accepts cremated remains at their affiliated cemeteries.

Ohio pre-need considerations

Ohio requires funeral homes to place 100% of pre-need funds into an approved trust or commissioned insurance policy (ORC 4717.31-35). That makes Ohio pre-need contracts relatively low-risk, but families should still:

  • Ask for the trust institution name and trust receipt number.
  • Confirm the contract is guaranteed-price vs. non-guaranteed (the difference can be $1,500+ after inflation).
  • Verify the provider's active licensure on the Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors website before signing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical price of cremation in Ohio?

Ohio cremation pricing lives in two tiers: direct cremation — transport, cremation, and return of ashes — starting around $1,700, and full-service cremation with a ceremony landing near $5,600. Urns ($50-$3,000), memorial venues ($500-$2,000), and scattering or inurnment fees ($200-$600) are separate line items most providers will quote on request.

What types of cremation are available in Ohio?

Families in Ohio can choose from three main types: direct cremation ($1,700) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service (ceremony held after cremation, often more flexible on timing); and traditional cremation ($5,600) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each option can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget.

Does Ohio have specific rules for cremation?

Yes. Ohio requires written authorization from the legal next of kin, a standard 24-48 hour waiting period, and in some counties a permit from the medical examiner before cremation can proceed. Pacemakers and certain implants must be removed first. The FTC Funeral Rule (federal) separately guarantees you do not need a casket for cremation — a basic cremation container suffices.

Where are ashes allowed to be scattered in Ohio?

Ohio scattering rules depend on the specific location. Private land is generally fine with the owner's permission; at sea requires being at least 3 nautical miles offshore per EPA rules; many state and national parks need a permit (and in some cases prohibit it entirely). Check the local ordinance before choosing a spot. Families in Ohio also often opt for columbarium niches, burial of the urn, or keepsake jewelry in place of scattering.

Why is cremation growing in Ohio?

Ohio has near the national average, with a cremation rate of 55%. Factors driving cremation adoption include lower cost compared to traditional burial (saving $5,400 or more), greater flexibility for memorial services, environmental considerations, and changing cultural preferences. The national cremation rate has exceeded 60% and continues to rise annually.

How quickly can cremation be completed in Ohio?

From start to ashes, most cremations in Ohio take 4 to 10 days. The cremation itself is 2-3 hours, but the mandatory 24-48 hour pre-cremation waiting period and the 3-7 business day processing window dominate the timeline. Direct cremation at $1,700 moves fastest since there is no viewing or service to schedule beforehand.

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Ohio?

Yes. Ohio funeral homes regularly arrange visitation and a ceremony before cremation takes place — the full-service option typically runs around $5,600. If the ceremony timing is flexible, a memorial service after cremation (at any venue, anytime) is another option and usually costs less than the full traditional route.
