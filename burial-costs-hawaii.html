Burial Costs in Hawaii (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: West

Hawaii produces the highest burial totals in the United States, and the reasons are structural: land is scarce, labor and materials arrive by ocean freight, and Native Hawaiian burial protections administered by the Department of Land and Natural Resources (DLNR) add an archaeological review layer not found in most states. A 2026 Hawaii burial bill falls between $21,450 and $30,250. Island geography also matters — a death on Kauai with burial on Oahu requires inter-island body transfer, which adds several hundred to several thousand dollars before the plot is even opened.

The end-to-end 2026 bill for a Hawaii burial lands between $21,450 and $30,250. That is roughly 40 percent above the mainland U.S. average, almost all of which traces to land, logistics, and the inter-island freight chain.

Iwi kupuna: why Hawaii burials carry a review step no other state has

Hawaii state law protects iwi kupuna (ancestral Native Hawaiian remains) under the oversight of the Department of Land and Natural Resources through the island-specific Burial Councils. When a cemetery expansion or a new burial ground breaks ground, DLNR review is triggered. For families purchasing a plot, the practical effect is small — cemeteries handle the compliance — but it contributes to why plot inventory is constrained and why new cemetery development is rare. Families whose loved one is Native Hawaiian and who wish to bury on ancestral land should contact the island Burial Council early; the process can take several months.

The inter-island logistics line

When a death occurs on a different island from the burial, the body must be transported by licensed air carrier. Typical 2026 inter-island body transfer:

  • Kauai to Oahu: $800-$1,400 depending on carrier and timing
  • Maui to Oahu: $900-$1,500
  • Big Island (Kona or Hilo) to Oahu: $1,100-$1,800
  • Lanai or Molokai to Oahu: $1,200-$2,000

These are not "hidden fees" — they are real line items a Honolulu funeral home must itemize on the General Price List under the FTC Funeral Rule. Ask for them in writing.

Scattering at sea is an alternative, not a rule. EPA rules require scattering of cremated remains at least three nautical miles offshore, and full-body burial at sea is separately regulated. For Hawaii families, a cremation followed by scattering often lands under $3,000 all-in — an order of magnitude below a traditional burial. Our comparison guide covers the tradeoff.

Oahu's main cemeteries and where they sit on price

  • National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (Punchbowl): Free to eligible veterans and qualifying spouses. Plots are limited; a columbarium niche is the more commonly available option.
  • Diamond Head Memorial Park: Premium Oahu pricing, plots $6,500-$11,000 in current sections.
  • Valley of the Temples Memorial Park (Kaneohe): Mid-market Oahu pricing, $5,000-$8,500 depending on section.
  • Hawaiian Memorial Park (Kaneohe): Oahu's largest private cemetery, $4,500-$7,500 typical.
  • Church and mission cemeteries (Catholic, LDS, Buddhist): Often $2,800-$4,500, with membership or affiliation sometimes required.

Hawaii's 70 percent cremation rate changes the negotiation

Hawaii has one of the highest cremation rates in the country — roughly 70 percent, versus a national average around 60 percent. Burial-focused funeral homes compete for a shrinking share of the market and often have more pricing flexibility than their General Price List suggests. Mentioning direct cremation as an alternative in an at-need conversation sometimes surfaces a better burial quote. Our direct cremation guide covers the cremation side of the comparison.

Practical moves for a Hawaii family

  1. Ask every Hawaii funeral home for the full General Price List. The FTC Funeral Rule applies.
  2. Price inter-island transfer separately if the death and burial are on different islands. Some carriers price lower than others.
  3. Consider church-affiliated cemeteries. Catholic diocese, LDS, Buddhist, and Japanese-American community cemeteries consistently price plots below commercial memorial parks.
  4. Buy the casket from a mainland online retailer — even with freight, the total is usually below a Hawaii funeral home's showroom price.
  5. Check Punchbowl eligibility for veterans. Niche availability changes; plot availability is rare.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Hawaii?

Hawaii has the most expensive burial market in the United States. A 2026 traditional service totals $21,450 to $30,250: funeral service $11,000, plot $5,500, casket $3,300, vault $2,200, opening-and-closing $2,750, and a $1,000-$3,000 marker. Inter-island body transfer — if the death and burial are on different islands — can add another $800-$2,000 on top.

Do I need a vault for burial in Hawaii?

Hawaii state law does not require a vault, but nearly every commercial Hawaii cemetery does. Volcanic and coralline soils settle unpredictably, which is why cemeteries here insist on a sealed vault or, at minimum, a concrete grave liner. Ask the specific cemetery whether a liner satisfies their policy — at some Valley of the Temples and Hawaiian Memorial Park sections it does, saving $600-$1,200.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in Hawaii?

Given Hawaii's high burial cost, many families instead opt for cremation with scattering at sea — the total often lands under $3,000. When traditional burial matters, the cheapest paths are a Catholic, LDS, or Buddhist cemetery membership plot, a direct burial through an independent funeral home, and a casket purchased from a mainland online retailer. Eligible veterans should check Punchbowl (National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific), where burial is free though plot availability is limited.

How do I compare cemetery costs in Hawaii?

Hawaii cemeteries are not bound by the FTC Funeral Rule's itemization requirement, so you have to ask for a written price sheet covering plot, opening-and-closing, vault policy, perpetual care, and membership or residency restrictions. Pricing spreads widely — a plot at Diamond Head runs $11,000 while a similar plot at a Buddhist mission cemetery in Kaneohe runs $3,500. Church-affiliated grounds consistently price below commercial memorial parks.

Once I add every line item, what does burial actually cost in Hawaii?

The full 2026 Hawaii stack totals $21,450 to $30,250, plus inter-island transfer if applicable. That breaks down as $11,000 funeral service, $5,500 plot, $3,300 casket, $2,200 vault, $2,750 opening-and-closing, and $1,000-$3,000 marker. Buying the casket from a mainland online retailer (even with shipping) and choosing a church cemetery over a commercial park consistently lands families in the lower half.

Does Hawaii have green burial cemeteries?

Green burial options exist on Oahu and the Big Island but remain limited. Some cemeteries offer green or hybrid sections where the body is buried without embalming and in a biodegradable container; the vault requirement is sometimes waived. Scattering at sea after cremation is a more common natural disposition choice in Hawaii, regulated by the EPA's three-nautical-mile offshore rule. Our green burial guide explains certification.
