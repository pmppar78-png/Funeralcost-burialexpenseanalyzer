Burial Costs in Hawaii (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: West

Hawaii has the highest burial costs in the United States, a reality driven by the islands' extreme land scarcity, isolation from the mainland, and the high cost of living that affects every aspect of life in the Aloha State. Cemetery space is severely limited across all major islands, and the cost of shipping caskets, vaults, and other burial supplies across the Pacific adds thousands of dollars to the process. At the same time, Hawaii's burial landscape is profoundly shaped by Native Hawaiian cultural traditions, the diverse heritage of its residents — including Japanese, Filipino, Chinese, Korean, Portuguese, and Samoan communities — and the spiritual connection many families feel to the land. The National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific at Punchbowl Crater in Honolulu is one of the most visited and revered burial sites in the nation.

What Drives Burial Costs in Hawaii

Extreme Land Scarcity

Hawaii's islands have very limited land available for cemetery development. On Oahu, where most of the state's population lives, cemetery space is critically scarce. This drives plot prices to levels rivaling Manhattan. Some families choose to have remains shipped to the mainland for burial where costs are lower.

Hawaiian Cultural Burial Traditions

Native Hawaiian burial traditions are deeply connected to the land (aina). The discovery of ancient Hawaiian burial sites frequently halts development projects, as these iwi (bones) are considered sacred. Modern Hawaiian families may incorporate both traditional and contemporary practices, including prayers, chanting, and specific burial orientations.

Notable Hawaii Cemeteries

The National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (Punchbowl) in Honolulu is among the most visited cemeteries in the world, honoring military veterans in the crater of an extinct volcano. Valley of the Temples Memorial Park on Oahu is a major private cemetery. Oahu Cemetery, established in 1844, is one of Hawaii's oldest.

Cemetery Types Available in Hawaii

  • Private memorial parks — The primary option on most islands, including Valley of the Temples and Hawaiian Memorial Park.
  • Veterans cemeteries — National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (Punchbowl) and state veterans cemeteries. Learn about veteran burial benefits
  • Church cemeteries — Various denominational cemeteries, including historic mission-era burial grounds.
  • Municipal cemeteries — Limited options on some islands.

Casket and Vault Options in Hawaii

Casket costs in Hawaii are elevated by shipping expenses. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, you can buy a casket from any retailer, though shipping to Hawaii adds $500 to $1,500. Despite this, online casket purchases can still save $1,000 to $5,000 compared to funeral home prices in Hawaii.

Consumer Rights for Burial in Hawaii

  • FTC Funeral Rule — Itemized pricing required.
  • Hawaii Department of Health — Oversees burial permits and regulations.
  • Embalming not required by Hawaii law.
  • Native Hawaiian burial protections — Hawaii has specific laws protecting ancient Hawaiian burial sites and providing for the proper treatment of discovered remains.

Green and Natural Burial in Hawaii

Hawaii's connection to the natural world makes green burial a philosophically appealing option, though dedicated green cemeteries are limited. Some cemeteries accommodate natural burial with biodegradable containers and no embalming. Hawaii's tropical climate means decomposition occurs relatively quickly in-ground. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Hawaii?

Hawaii has the highest burial costs in the nation. A traditional burial costs approximately $11,000 for the funeral service plus $5,500 for a plot, with totals ranging from $21,450 to $30,250. Extreme land scarcity and high import costs drive these prices.

Is a burial vault required in Hawaii?

Hawaii has no state law requiring vaults. Most cemeteries require them. Ask about less expensive grave liners as an alternative.

Why is burial so expensive in Hawaii?

Limited island land makes cemetery space extremely scarce. Caskets, vaults, and supplies must be shipped across the Pacific. Hawaii's overall high cost of living affects labor and service costs. Many families choose cremation as a more affordable alternative.

What is the Punchbowl cemetery?

The National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific, known as Punchbowl, is located in the crater of an extinct volcanic cone in Honolulu. It serves as a national cemetery for eligible veterans and is one of the most visited and revered burial sites in the nation. Learn about veteran burial benefits
