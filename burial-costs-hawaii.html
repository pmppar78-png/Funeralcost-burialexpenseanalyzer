How to Reduce Burial Costs in Hawaii

Burial costs in Hawaii can be managed with smart planning:

  • Compare cemetery prices directly: Cemeteries are not covered by the FTC Funeral Rule but most in Hawaii will share a price sheet on request. Ask for plot, vault, opening-and-closing, and perpetual care line-by-line, and set them against the Hawaii estimates of $5,500, $2,200 and $2,750.
  • Look for hybrid or natural burial sections: Several Hawaii cemeteries have added green or natural burial sections without advertising them prominently.
  • Consider certified conservation burial grounds: Hawaii is one of the more active states for true conservation burial. These grounds protect land in perpetuity and price simply, typically $4,400-$7,700 all-in versus $25,250+ for traditional burial in Hawaii.
  • Use Hawaii's high cremation rate when you shop: about 70% of families here choose cremation, so burial-focused funeral homes compete for fewer families, and mentioning that you are also pricing direct cremation can bring a better burial quote.
  • Compare the $3,300 Hawaii casket estimate with the casket buying guide and the list of online casket retailers.
  • Check headstone prices independently: Hawaii funeral homes and cemeteries mark up headstones.

For ways to bring a Hawaii burial bill down, see our affordable funeral options guide or payment assistance programs.

Regional Context for Burial in Hawaii

Scattering at sea, on private land, and in certain federal wilderness areas is more practical in Hawaii than in the inland US, and families in Honolulu increasingly choose cremation with a natural scattering over traditional burial. When burial is still the choice, Hawaii green burial grounds and Jewish and LDS cemeteries tend to offer the most competitive plot pricing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Hawaii?

A traditional burial in Hawaii costs approximately $11,000 for the funeral service plus $5,500 for a cemetery plot. When you add a burial vault ($2,200), opening and closing fees ($2,750), casket ($3,300), and headstone ($500-$5,000), the Hawaii total typically ranges from $25,250 to $29,750.

Do I need a vault for burial in Hawaii?

Grave liners (cheaper than full vaults) often satisfy the same requirement — ask the Hawaii cemetery what it accepts before paying $2,200 for a vault. Vault prices in Hawaii range from $800 to $10,000 depending on material and brand.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in Hawaii?

Direct burial is the lowest-cost path in Hawaii — it skips viewing, ceremony, and embalming. Green or natural burial is the next rung up, available at a growing number of Hawaii cemeteries with biodegradable containers and no embalming.

How do I compare cemetery costs in Hawaii?

Cemetery costs in Hawaii vary significantly even within the same city. In particular, green and hybrid burial sections in Hawaii cemeteries often sit 30-50% below traditional plots, and conservation burial grounds are more established here than in most regions.

Once I add every line item, what does burial actually cost in Hawaii?

A complete burial in Hawaii — funeral service ($11,000) + plot ($5,500) + casket ($3,300) + vault ($2,200) + opening-and-closing ($2,750) + headstone ($500-$5,000) — totals $25,250 to $29,750 in practice. Comparing two or three Hawaii funeral homes and two or three cemeteries independently is the single most reliable way to bring that number down.

Does Hawaii have green burial cemeteries?

There are green burial options in parts of Hawaii, though coverage is not statewide. Some Hawaii cemeteries have hybrid sections rather than fully dedicated grounds. Check our green burial guide and call local Hawaii cemeteries to confirm what they currently offer.

Burial rules and help in Hawaii

The Hawaii Department of Health (808-586-8000) licenses the funeral homes that arrange burials in Hawaii. It can also tell you which Hawaii office handles cemetery complaints.

Hawaii does not require a funeral director for a burial, by the Funeral Consumers Alliance's count of the nine states that do, so the family can work with a Hawaii cemetery directly on the plot, the grave opening and the container it accepts.

A Hawaii burial needs a disposition permit. If you switch to cremation instead, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (HRS §§ 338-23, 338-25).

Hawaii's state help does not fund a burial plot: unclaimed bodies are cremated with up to $1,600 paid to the provider, and an eligible assistance recipient's family can apply within 60 days for a lump sum the size of Social Security's (Haw. Rev. Stat. § 346-15). (Source: Haw. Rev. Stat. § 346-15; HAR ch. 17-1745.) Social Security's $255 payment to a surviving spouse or child is well short of the $5,500 Hawaii plot estimate on this page.

For Hawaii veterans, the plot and marker in a national cemetery are free, which can remove thousands from a burial bill. In Hawaii, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (808-532-3720, cremated remains only); state veterans cemeteries: Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery (808-369-3575); Kauai Veterans Cemetery (808-241-4460); Lanai Veterans Cemetery (808-565-7086); East Hawaii Veterans Cemetery (808-961-8311); Maui Veterans Cemetery (808-876-4534); Molokai Veterans Cemetery (808-553-3222); West Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery (808-961-8311) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps for a burial in Hawaii

  1. Compare at least two Hawaii cemeteries as well as two funeral homes, since the plot, grave opening and marker are priced separately.
  2. Ask the Hawaii cemetery whether it requires an outer burial container before you shop for one.
  3. Apply to Hawaii DHS within 60 days for the lump sum if the person received state assistance; it can go toward a plot.
  4. For a veteran, ask Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery about eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. A complaint call to 808-586-8000 is free if a funeral home misleads you about burial costs.
