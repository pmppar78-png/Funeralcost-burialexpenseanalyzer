What this helper can answer, and what it will not

The chat box above is a starting point when you do not know which guide to open. It is free, it is not a funeral home, and it is not a person. The paragraphs under it are here so you can see the limits before you type something painful into a form.

Questions it is built to handle

Cost questions should land on a sourced figure. A fair question is "what is the national median for a viewing and burial?" The answer we stand behind is $8,300 from the NFDA 2023 General Price List Study, latest available, not including the vault or the cemetery, and $6,280 for a viewing and cremation. Direct cremation in that study is $2,645 or $2,750 depending on who supplies the container. The helper should send you to average funeral cost or your state page rather than invent a local price.

Rights questions should land on the FTC Funeral Rule. You can ask for a General Price List over the phone, you can decline embalming, and you can bring a casket bought elsewhere with no handling fee. The long version is the FTC Funeral Rule guide. The helper does not know your state's cremation-waiting rule from memory. That rule is on the regulations directory and on your state page, which names the licensing board.

Benefit questions have official forms. Social Security's lump-sum death payment is $255, claimed on Form SSA-8 by an eligible spouse or child. Veteran burial allowances, markers, and national-cemetery graves are on the veteran guide, which follows VA publications. The helper should not quote a state aid cap. Those caps change, and the payment-assistance guide says so.

Questions it should refuse

  • It cannot tell you that you must use a particular funeral home, insurer, or casket site. A commercial link on this website never outranks a General Price List.
  • It cannot tell you whether a will is valid, whether a policy will pay, or whether a doctor did the right thing. Those are legal, insurance, and medical questions for licensed people.
  • It cannot see your contract. If you paste one in, treat the reply as a reading aid and still ask the funeral home to explain each line.
  • It does not know today's price at a named funeral home. Call and ask for the General Price List.
  • It is not a crisis line. If you may hurt yourself, call or text 988. If someone has just died and you need emergency help, call 911.

Example questions, and the page that answers them

  • "What should I do tonight?" Open the first-48-hours checklist.
  • "Is cremation cheaper where I live?" Use the calculator, then read why the totals differ.
  • "We have no insurance and no savings." Start with how to pay with no money, not with a loan page.
  • "Is a burial policy worth it at 75?" Read burial insurance for seniors and do the multiplication. The helper should not pick a carrier.
  • "The price feels high." Read what funeral homes don't tell you and take the worksheet to two more homes.

If the helper and a guide disagree, believe the guide. The guides name the NFDA release, the CFR section, or the government form. The helper is a map to those pages. It was described this way on 2026-10-22.

What happens to what you type

Each message goes from this page to our server, which passes it to OpenAI's API to write the reply. We do not save the conversation, and it is gone when you close or leave the page. To stop abuse, the server keeps a count of requests under a one-way hash of your IP address, not the address itself, and allows 20 messages in 10 minutes and 100 a day. Only the last 20 messages of a conversation are sent, and each is cut off at 2,000 characters. If a message mentions suicide, self-harm or hurting someone, the server skips the AI and replies with the 988 Lifeline and Crisis Text Line numbers. Leave out Social Security numbers and account numbers. The privacy policy has the full terms.
