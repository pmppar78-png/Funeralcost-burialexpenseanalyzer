Cremation Trends Around Georgia

How to Save on Cremation in Georgia

Cremation costs in Georgia can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Georgia: Request written General Price Lists.
  • Check cremation societies: Nonprofit cremation societies operate in several Georgia metros and often undercut full-service funeral homes by $300-$900 on direct cremation while meeting all Georgia regulatory requirements.

Before You Sign for Cremation in Georgia

When considering cremation in Georgia, keep these important points in mind:

  • Under O.C.G.A. § 31-21-7, the first say over a Georgia cremation belongs to the person's health care agent, then to anyone named in a notarized disposition affidavit, then the surviving spouse, and only after that to the children, parents and siblings.
  • Georgia lets fewer than half of the surviving children act when they have made reasonable efforts to notify the others and know of no opposition from more than half of them, which matters in families where some children cannot be reached quickly.
  • Ask how long the Georgia crematory usually takes to return the ashes once the permit is issued, and whether the quoted price includes delivery or only pickup.
Georgia families can supply their own urn; the Georgia Board of Funeral Service takes complaints when a home refuses one or tacks on a fee for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Georgia?

Cremation costs in Georgia range from $1,700 for direct cremation to $5,600 for a full funeral service followed by cremation.

Which cremation options exist in Georgia?

Georgia providers typically offer three tiers. Direct cremation at $1,700 is the simplest Georgia tier: no service or viewing, the body cremated and the ashes returned. Traditional cremation at $5,600 adds a full funeral home service before the cremation, $3,900 more than the direct tier in Georgia.

Does Georgia have specific rules for cremation?

In short, Georgia has no fixed minimum waiting period, but the crematory still needs the death certificate, a cremation permit and a signed authorization from the next of kin or designated agent before it can proceed. The statute behind the Georgia timing rule, whether a funeral director must be involved, and the licensing board's phone number are listed in the cremation rules section further down this page.

Can I scatter ashes in Georgia?

Georgia does not limit scattering to particular places. Under O.C.G.A. § 43-18-80, the person who authorizes the cremation signs a statement of ultimate disposition, so the funeral home knows in advance whether the remains will be buried, kept or scattered. Private land needs the owner's permission, and national park units such as Cumberland Island National Seashore require a permit or a designated area under 36 CFR 2.62.

If the remains are not picked up within 60 days, the funeral home may dispose of them, including by scattering, after giving 30 more days' notice; a 2025 amendment caps the fee for that disposition at $100. If a funeral home disposes of unclaimed remains without that notice, or charges more than the cap, report it to the Georgia State Board of Funeral Service at 404-424-9966.

Why do so many Georgia families choose cremation?

Cost is a major reason: the national median for a funeral with cremation is $6,280 versus $8,300 with burial (NFDA 2023), and direct cremation in Georgia costs less than either. Cremation also avoids buying a Georgia cemetery plot unless the family wants one.

How quickly can cremation be completed in Georgia?

From start to ashes, most cremations in Georgia take 4 to 10 days. Direct cremation at $1,700 moves fastest: with no viewing or service to schedule, the only wait is the Georgia cremation permit (Georgia sets no fixed waiting period; O.C.G.A. § 31-10-20; Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. r. 511-1-3-.23).

Can I hold a funeral service before cremation in Georgia?

Yes, and in Georgia it adds about $3,900: this site estimates $5,600 for a funeral followed by cremation (usually $4,480 to $7,280), against about $1,700 for direct cremation. That difference pays for the visitation, the ceremony and a casket for the service, which many homes will rent.

The state's guide to funeral homes notes that a viewing may lead a funeral home to ask for embalming, and that families can ask whether refrigeration will do instead. The local registrar's disposition permit (O.C.G.A. § 31-10-20) has to be issued before cremation.

Cremation rules and help in Georgia

Crematories and the funeral homes that sell cremation in Georgia answer to the Georgia State Board of Funeral Service (404-424-9966).

Georgia is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict family-directed care, so a family can go to a crematory directly after the local registrar issues the disposition permit.

Timing and permits: Georgia sets no waiting period before cremation, but a disposition permit from the local registrar is required (O.C.G.A. § 31-10-20; Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. r. 511-1-3-.23).

A Georgia county must cover a cremation when the deceased and close family are indigent (O.C.G.A. § 36-12-5), at an amount the county sets; Whitfield County, for example, caps cremation at $800 and burial at $2,100. (Source: O.C.G.A. § 36-12-5.) The $255 Social Security death payment to a qualifying Georgia spouse or child would cover only part of the $1,700 direct cremation estimate above.

Choosing cremation does not give up a Georgia veteran's cemetery benefit; urns can be interred in national cemeteries free of charge. Options in Georgia include Georgia National Cemetery (770-479-9300), Georgia Veterans Memorial Cemetery - Glennville (912-654-5398), and Georgia Veterans Memorial Cemetery - Milledgeville (478-445-3363). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Georgia

  1. Ask Georgia cremation providers to itemize transport, the container and the crematory fee before you choose one.
  2. Ask which office signs Georgia cremation permits where the death occurred, and how long it takes.
  3. Check the county's cremation rate under O.C.G.A. § 36-12-5 before booking; Georgia counties set different caps.
  4. Note the board's number, 404-424-9966, before you pay a cremation provider.
