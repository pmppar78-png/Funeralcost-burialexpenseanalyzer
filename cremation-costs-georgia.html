Cremation Costs in Georgia (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Cremation Rate: 45%

Georgia's cremation rate of 45% is growing steadily as families across the Peach State increasingly consider cremation's practical and financial advantages. Traditionally, Georgia has been a state where ground burial was deeply embedded in the culture, but that is changing -- particularly in the Atlanta metropolitan area, which has seen a surge in cremation providers and competitive pricing. Direct cremation in Georgia averages $1,700, a reasonable figure reflecting the state's moderate cost of living. Georgia requires a 24-hour waiting period and written authorization before cremation. Cremation is also gaining acceptance within Georgia's African-American community, contributing to the statewide trend.

What Drives Cremation Costs in Georgia

Georgia's cremation landscape is evolving rapidly:

  • 24-hour waiting period: Georgia requires a 24-hour waiting period after death before cremation, one of the shorter waits in the Southeast, helping to keep associated storage costs manageable.
  • Atlanta metro competition: The Atlanta metro area, home to over six million people, has seen significant growth in cremation providers, including standalone crematories and cremation-only businesses. This competition benefits consumers with more choices and better pricing.
  • Growing acceptance across communities: Cremation is gaining acceptance in Georgia's African-American community, which historically favored traditional homegoing services with burial. This cultural shift is contributing to the state's rising cremation rate and expanding the market.
  • Growing number of standalone crematories: Georgia has seen new cremation-focused businesses open in recent years, particularly in the Atlanta, Savannah, and Augusta areas. These providers typically offer lower prices than traditional funeral homes.
  • Rural vs. urban pricing: Cremation in rural Georgia is handled almost exclusively through traditional funeral homes. While prices may be slightly lower, there are fewer options for comparison shopping.
  • Coastal scattering options: Georgia's coastline, including the Golden Isles and Savannah area, provides beautiful settings for ocean and marsh scattering, adding to cremation's appeal for families in coastal communities.

Georgia Consumer Rights for Cremation

Georgia families have clear protections when arranging cremation:

  • FTC Funeral Rule: All Georgia providers must supply an itemized General Price List. You select only the services you need and are not required to buy a casket for cremation.
  • Georgia Secretary of State oversight: The Georgia Board of Funeral Service licenses and regulates cremation providers and handles consumer complaints.
  • Written authorization: Georgia law requires signed authorization from the next of kin or authorized agent before cremation.
  • Right to use your own urn: Providers cannot refuse a third-party urn or charge handling fees.

Scattering and Memorial Options in Georgia

Georgia provides several meaningful options for cremated remains:

  • Private property: Scattering on private property is permitted with the landowner's written consent. Georgia's rural landscape and family farms provide meaningful settings.
  • Coastal scattering: The Georgia coast, including the Golden Isles (Jekyll Island, St. Simons, Sea Island), offers beautiful settings for ocean scattering following EPA guidelines (3+ nautical miles from shore).
  • Chattahoochee National Forest: Scattering on national forest land in northern Georgia is generally permitted.
  • Church memorial gardens: A growing number of Georgia churches are establishing memorial gardens for cremated remains, often at modest or no cost to members.
  • Cemetery columbarium: Many Georgia cemeteries offer columbarium niches, typically $500 to $3,000.
  • Home retention: Georgia law allows families to keep cremated remains at home indefinitely.
Cost-Saving Tip: Georgia's cremation market is becoming more competitive each year, especially in metro Atlanta. Compare prices between traditional funeral homes and standalone cremation providers -- the difference can be significant. Also ask about pricing for services held at the cremation provider's facility rather than a rented venue, which can save hundreds of dollars.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Georgia?

Direct cremation in Georgia averages $1,700, ranging from $1,190 to $2,380. Cremation with a full funeral service averages $5,600. Atlanta metro pricing is competitive due to multiple providers, while rural areas may have fewer options. Additional costs include urns, memorial services, and scattering fees.

What types of cremation are available in Georgia?

Georgia offers direct cremation, cremation with memorial service, and traditional cremation with funeral service. The growing number of standalone crematories in Atlanta, Savannah, and Augusta is expanding options. Witnessed cremation is available at some providers.

What are Georgia's cremation regulations?

Georgia requires a 24-hour waiting period after death before cremation. Written authorization from the next of kin is mandatory. Pacemakers must be removed. The Georgia Board of Funeral Service oversees provider licensing.

Can I scatter ashes in Georgia?

Yes. Scattering on private property requires the owner's consent. Coastal scattering near the Golden Isles and Savannah follows EPA guidelines. Scattering on national forest land in north Georgia is generally permitted. Church memorial gardens are an increasingly popular option.

Is cremation accepted in Georgia's African-American community?

Cremation is gaining significant acceptance within Georgia's African-American community, though the traditional homegoing service with burial remains deeply valued. Many families are finding ways to honor homegoing traditions while choosing cremation -- for example, holding a full funeral service followed by cremation rather than burial. This cultural evolution is contributing meaningfully to Georgia's rising cremation rate.

Can I scatter ashes at the Georgia coast?

Yes. Ocean scattering off Georgia's coast, including near the Golden Isles, is permitted when following EPA guidelines (at least three nautical miles from shore with biodegradable containers). Charter services are available from Savannah, Brunswick, and St. Simons Island. Scattering in Georgia's coastal marshes and waterways may be subject to local regulations.
