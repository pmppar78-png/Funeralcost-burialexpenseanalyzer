Funeral Costs in Denver, Colorado (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 State: Colorado

Denver is at the forefront of a quiet revolution in American funeral practices. Colorado was one of the first states to legalize human composting, has one of the highest cremation rates in the country at 72%, and is home to nationally recognized green funeral providers. The Mile High City's outdoor culture, progressive regulations, and environmentally conscious population have created a funeral market unlike anywhere else. This guide covers Denver-specific pricing data and the innovative options available to Front Range families.

Colorado's Progressive Funeral Options

Colorado is one of the most consumer-friendly and innovative states in the country when it comes to funeral regulations. Denver-area families have access to options that are not available in most other states:

  • Human composting (natural organic reduction): Legalized in Colorado in 2021, this process transforms the body into nutrient-rich soil over 30-45 days. Several providers now serve the Denver metro area, with costs ranging from $5,000–$7,000. The resulting soil can be used in a garden, scattered in nature, or donated to conservation land. This option appeals strongly to Denver's environmentally conscious population.
  • Home funerals: Colorado allows families to care for their own deceased at home, transport the body, and handle all arrangements without a licensed funeral director. This can reduce costs to as little as $200–$500 for permits and supplies. Organizations like the Natural Funeral in Lafayette (just outside Denver) provide guidance and support for families choosing this path.
  • Green burial: Multiple cemeteries in the Denver metro and Front Range accept green burials — no embalming, biodegradable containers, no vault. Costs typically run $2,000–$4,000 total, far less than traditional burial.
  • Mountain ash scattering: Colorado's vast public lands offer countless meaningful locations for ash scattering. National Forest land generally allows scattering without a permit (away from trails and water). Rocky Mountain National Park requires a free permit.

Why Denver Leads in Cremation

Colorado has a cremation rate of 72%, and Denver's rate exceeds that. Several factors specific to the Front Range explain this:

  • Transplant population: Denver has grown rapidly, with many residents relocating from other states. Without family burial plots or local cemetery ties, these transplants gravitate toward cremation for its flexibility.
  • Environmental consciousness: Colorado's outdoor culture extends to end-of-life choices. Many Denver families view cremation (or green alternatives) as more environmentally responsible than traditional burial with embalming chemicals and non-biodegradable materials.
  • Mountain memorial culture: Scattering ashes at a favorite mountain trailhead, ski area, or along a river is deeply meaningful to many Colorado families and has become a cultural norm in the Denver metro.
  • Cost awareness: Denver's high cost of living has made residents especially cost-conscious. Direct cremation at $2,070 is significantly more affordable than traditional burial at $9,200 plus cemetery fees. Compare cremation and burial costs nationally

Denver Metro Pricing Variation

The Denver metro area spans from Boulder and Longmont in the north to Castle Rock in the south, and from the mountains in the west to Aurora and the plains in the east. Pricing varies accordingly:

  • Central Denver and Cherry Creek: Highest prices. Funeral homes in upscale areas charge 20-30% premiums for facility fees.
  • Aurora and Lakewood: Mid-range pricing, typically 10-15% below central Denver. Good selection of providers.
  • Thornton, Westminster, and Northglenn: Competitive pricing with good access. Often 15-20% below Denver proper.
  • Boulder: Surprisingly high funeral costs due to the city's elevated cost of living, but also home to some of Colorado's most innovative green funeral providers.
  • Outlying communities (Brighton, Castle Rock, Longmont): Most affordable options in the metro, with direct cremation sometimes available for under $1,700.

Compare: Denver vs. Other Colorado Cities

Denver is the most expensive metro in Colorado for funeral services, while Colorado Springs and mountain communities offer lower costs. Front Range providers generally charge less than resort-area providers in Vail, Aspen, or Steamboat Springs.

Denver-Area Cemetery and Memorial Options

  • Fort Logan National Cemetery: Located in southwest Denver, this is one of the most active national cemeteries in the country. Free burial for eligible veterans and spouses, including plot, opening/closing, headstone, and perpetual care. Check veteran eligibility
  • Fairmount Cemetery: Denver's oldest and most historic cemetery, established in 1890. Plots range from $4,000–$10,000 depending on section. A well-maintained, centrally located option.
  • Crown Hill Cemetery (Wheat Ridge): One of the largest cemeteries in the metro area. Plots from $3,500. Also offers green burial sections.
  • Natural burial sites: Several cemeteries along the Front Range have dedicated green burial sections. The Natural Funeral in Lafayette provides guidance on green burial and home funeral options throughout the Denver metro.
  • Mountain ash scattering: Free on most National Forest land. Rocky Mountain National Park requires a free permit. This is the most affordable memorial option, costing nothing beyond the cremation itself.

Your Rights as a Consumer in Denver

Colorado Consumer Protections Beyond the FTC Funeral Rule, Colorado's Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA) oversees funeral home licensing and investigates complaints. Colorado has no mandatory embalming requirement, no requirement to use a funeral director, and permits home funerals. Colorado also legalized human composting in 2021 and has some of the most permissive green burial regulations in the country. If you encounter problems, file a complaint with DORA at (303) 894-7855. Ways to Reduce Funeral Costs in Denver

  • Consider Colorado's unique options: Home funerals ($200–$500), green burial ($2,000–$4,000), and human composting ($5,000–$7,000) are all available and often more affordable than traditional services

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Colorado have one of the highest cremation rates in the US?

Colorado has a cremation rate of 72%, ranking among the top five states nationally. Denver's rate is even higher. Factors include the state's environmentally conscious culture, a large population of transplants without family burial plots, the outdoor-oriented lifestyle that favors nature-based memorials like ash scattering in the Rockies, and Colorado's progressive funeral regulations that support alternative options. Colorado was also the first state to legalize human composting (natural organic reduction) in 2021.

What is human composting and is it available in Denver?

Human composting, also called natural organic reduction, transforms the body into nutrient-rich soil over approximately 30-45 days. Colorado legalized this process in 2021, making it one of the first states to do so. Several providers now serve Denver-area families. The cost ranges from $5,000-$7,000, which is comparable to a mid-range cremation with service. The resulting soil can be used in gardens, scattered in nature, or donated to conservation land. It appeals to environmentally conscious Denver families.

Can I scatter ashes in the Rocky Mountains near Denver?

Yes, with some guidelines. On National Forest land (including much of the mountain areas west of Denver), ash scattering is generally permitted without a permit as long as it is done away from trails, water sources, and developed areas. Rocky Mountain National Park requires a free permit and specific location approval. Colorado state parks allow scattering with written permission. Many Denver families choose meaningful mountain locations along the I-70 corridor, near Breckenridge, or in Roosevelt National Forest.

Does Colorado require embalming or a funeral director?

No. Colorado does not require embalming under any circumstances. Colorado is also one of the most permissive states for home funerals — families can care for their own deceased, transport the body, and handle all arrangements without a licensed funeral director. A death certificate must still be filed, but no funeral home involvement is legally required. This makes Colorado one of the most consumer-friendly states for affordable, personalized funeral arrangements.

Are there green burial options near Denver?

Yes. Colorado's progressive regulations and environmental ethos make it a leader in green burial. Several cemeteries in the Denver metro area and nearby mountain communities accept green burials — no embalming, biodegradable caskets or shrouds, no vault. The Natural Funeral organization in Lafayette, CO (just outside Denver) is a nationally recognized provider of green and home funeral services. Green burial costs in the Denver area typically range from $2,000-$4,000.

How do funeral costs vary across the Denver metro area?

Denver proper and upscale suburbs like Cherry Creek, Greenwood Village, and Boulder have the highest funeral prices in the metro, while providers in Aurora, Lakewood, Thornton, and Commerce City tend to be 15-25% less expensive. Outlying communities like Castle Rock, Brighton, and Longmont offer even more competitive pricing. Getting quotes from providers in different parts of the metro can save $1,000-$2,500.
