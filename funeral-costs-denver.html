Funeral Costs in Denver, Colorado (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Colorado

Colorado is one of the few U.S. states where funeral directors are not individually licensed — the Office of Funeral Home and Crematory Registration regulates establishments rather than practitioners. That regulatory gap, combined with the Front Range's 72% cremation rate and mountain-winter interment delays, shapes how Denver families actually pay. Traditional services average $9,200, direct cremation clears at $2,070, and a plot at Fairmount or Crown Hill sits near $4,370.

Colorado's Unusual Funeral Regulator: Licensing Homes, Not Directors

Colorado is the only state in the country that does not license individual funeral directors or embalmers. The state's Office of Funeral Home and Crematory Registration inside the Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA) registers establishments and crematories — but the person arranging your mother's cremation may hold no state credential at all. In practice, the big Denver chains (Horan & McConaty, Olinger, Feldman Mortuary) employ NFDA-accredited staff voluntarily, but smaller operators vary. Ask every provider about training and check the DORA registration number before paying a deposit.

Your Rights in Denver

FTC Funeral Rule Protection Every funeral home in Denver must provide an itemized General Price List, let you choose only the services you want, and accept caskets purchased elsewhere. You are never required to buy a package. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

Winter Scheduling on the Front Range

Denver's elevation and January averages below 20°F push some families toward delayed interment. Fairmount, Crown Hill, and Mount Olivet can still open graves year-round, but opening fees in the December-through-February window typically run $250–$500 higher than summer pricing. Foothill cemeteries above 7,000 feet sometimes suspend burials outright — confirm before paying a plot deposit in winter. Mountain-pass transport from Denver to Glenwood Springs or Grand Junction adds $3–$5 per mile on most GPLs when I-70 is chained up.

El Paso County Indigent Burial and Colorado Safety Net

Colorado administers indigent burial assistance at the county level rather than statewide. In Denver proper, the Denver Department of Human Services covers removal, cremation, and disposition for qualifying decedents at a fixed contracted rate — typically through a preselected Horan & McConaty or similar contract. Adjacent counties (Jefferson, Arapahoe, Douglas, Adams) each run separate programs with different income thresholds. The $255 Social Security lump-sum benefit stacks with any county payment, as does a VA burial allowance for qualifying veterans.

Commonly Asked Questions

What are funeral costs in the Denver area?

Metro Denver averages $9,200 for a traditional service — roughly 15% above the $8,000 Colorado statewide figure. Direct cremation starts near $2,070, cremation with a service runs $7,130, and a standard plot at Fairmount or Crown Hill sits around $4,370. A full burial that includes casket, vault, headstone, and opening/closing can reach $16,330 once every line item is included.

Do more Denver families choose cremation or burial?

Cremation, overwhelmingly. Metro Denver follows Colorado's 72% cremation rate — one of the highest in the Mountain region — and the price gap explains why: $2,070 for direct cremation versus $9,200 plus a $4,370 plot for burial. Horan & McConaty, Olinger, and Feldman Mortuary all accommodate separate memorial services after cremation, which keeps the family gathering intact without the traditional-service price tag.

How do Denver funeral costs compare to the national average?

Denver's $9,200 traditional-service average is about 17% over the $7,848 U.S. median. Front Range real estate, higher labor costs than the Colorado Springs or Pueblo markets, and mountain transport add-ons all contribute. Direct cremation, however, tracks closer to the national figure — $2,070 in Denver versus roughly $1,800 nationwide.

What is the best way to compare Denver funeral homes?

Start by requesting written General Price Lists from Horan & McConaty, Olinger, and Feldman Mortuary — the three largest Denver operators — plus one independent or direct-cremation specialist. The FTC Funeral Rule requires every provider to hand over the GPL on request, no appointment needed. Because Colorado registers funeral establishments rather than individuals through DORA, verify the home's current registration number before committing a deposit.

How much is direct cremation in Denver?

Roughly $2,070 in metro Denver — Horan & McConaty's simple-cremation tier and smaller direct-cremation specialists along Colfax and Federal typically quote between $1,850 and $2,400. The price covers transport from place of death, the cremation itself, a basic container, and return of the cremated remains. No viewing, no ceremony, no embalming.

Are there affordable funeral options in Denver?

Direct cremation at $2,070 is the floor. Other options include direct burial at Riverside or one of the smaller municipal cemeteries, a memorial-only service at a church or family home (Colorado permits home funerals), and online caskets which undercut funeral-home inventory by 50–70%. Denver Department of Human Services covers indigent burials for qualifying residents, and VA burial benefits at Fort Logan National Cemetery eliminate plot costs entirely for eligible veterans.
