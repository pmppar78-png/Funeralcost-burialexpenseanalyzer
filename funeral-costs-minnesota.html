Funeral rules and help in Minnesota

If a funeral home in Minnesota refuses to show prices or pressures you, the licensing body is the Minnesota Department of Health, Health Regulation Division, reachable at 651-201-4200.

Minnesota is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict families from caring for their own dead. You can buy only the services you need, but someone must still file the Minnesota death certificate and get the permit.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Minnesota sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95, subd. 6a).

Minnesota counties must pay for the burial or cremation of people who die without the means to cover it (Minn. Stat. § 261.035); amounts are set by each county. (Source: Minn. Stat. § 261.035.)

Eligible Minnesota veterans get a grave, opening and closing, a liner and a headstone at no cost in a national cemetery. In Minnesota, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Fort Snelling National Cemetery (612-726-1127); state veterans cemeteries: Leech Lake Veterans Cemetery - Cass Lake (218-335-4463); Minnesota State Veterans Cemetery - Duluth (218-520-0120); Minnesota State Veterans Cemetery - Little Falls (320-616-2527). Source: VA National Cemetery Administration.

What to do next

  1. Ask every Minnesota provider for its General Price List; the FTC Funeral Rule says they must give it to you.
  2. Decide early whether you want embalming and a viewing in Minnesota; skipping them is often the largest saving.
  3. Check eligibility with the county human-services office first; some programs refuse claims after a contract is signed.
  4. For a veteran, ask Fort Snelling National Cemetery about eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. Keep the board's number, 651-201-4200, in case a provider will not honor its price list.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a typical Minnesota funeral cost in 2026?

Most Minnesota families paying for a full traditional service see the bill settle around $7,700 — 7% below the $8,300 national benchmark. Cremation with ceremony runs closer to $6,000, while a stripped-down direct cremation in Minnesota can land near $1,800. Cemetery plot fees in Minnesota add about $3,600. Actual prices shift a lot depending on which Minnesota provider you call first, which is why comparing is non-negotiable.

How much cheaper is cremation than burial in Minnesota?

Direct cremation in Minnesota starts around $1,800; a traditional funeral with burial averages $7,700, not counting the cemetery plot or vault. That is a difference of at least $5,900 for most families.

What are my consumer rights at Minnesota funeral homes?

The federal FTC Funeral Rule gives Minnesota families the right to an itemized price list, to prices over the phone, and to buy only the goods and services they want. Minnesota is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. The Minnesota Department of Health, Health Regulation Division, 651-201-4200, licenses Minnesota funeral homes and takes complaints.

What is the best way to reduce funeral expenses in Minnesota?

The biggest lever is comparison shopping — two or three GPLs from different Minnesota providers, compared line by line, consistently saves families $1,000 or more. Township and county cemeteries in Minnesota rarely appear in online searches but are often half the price of private cemeteries — call the clerk's office in the county where burial will occur. Independent, family-owned funeral homes still dominate Minnesota and tend to price more transparently than chain providers. At $1,800, direct cremation is the cheapest disposition option available in Minnesota.

Do I have to embalm a loved one in Minnesota?

The FTC Funeral Rule bars a Minnesota funeral home from saying embalming is required by law when it is not, and its price list must state that embalming is not required except in certain special cases. If a Minnesota provider says embalming is needed, ask which law or cemetery rule it is citing.

How do funeral costs in Minnesota compare to other states?

The state's traditional-funeral average of $7,700 sits 7% below the $8,300 national median, and the Midwest region overall trends toward moderate to lower pricing than the rest of the country.

What is the least expensive way to handle a funeral in Minnesota?

In Minnesota, direct cremation at about $1,800 is the most affordable path — just transport, cremation, and the return of the remains. If burial is preferred, direct burial (no viewing or ceremony) runs near $4,620 and is the cheapest burial option.

What is the best way to find a cheap funeral home in Minnesota?

Call several Minnesota funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Minnesota home cannot charge a fee for it.
