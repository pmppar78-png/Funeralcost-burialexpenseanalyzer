Funeral Costs in Minnesota: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 29, 2026 What changed: Rewrote the page by hand, including the section “What Each Part of a Minnesota Funeral Costs”. Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: Midwest

Our 2026 estimate for a traditional Minnesota funeral is about $7,700, some 7% under the $8,300 national median, with a service plus cremation near $6,000 and direct cremation near $1,800. Close to two in three Minnesota families (62%) now cremate. Below are the item prices, the county and veterans help, and the Minnesota rules on timing and who decides.

Minnesota's Funeral Market

The Federal Rule in Minnesota

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Minnesota must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

Report a Minnesota home that ignores the federal rule at ftc.gov; the Funeral Consumers Alliance can advise.

Why Minnesota Quotes Differ

Several things move a Minnesota quote.

Land and wages in the Twin Cities cost more than in rural counties, and many small towns have a single home. With 62% of families now cremating, cremation-only providers keep direct cremation prices competitive in the metro.

Arranging a Minnesota Funeral

Working through these in order saves time and money:

  1. Start with cremation or burial: cremation in Minnesota or burial in Minnesota.

What comes next after a death is covered in our what to do when someone dies guide and printable checklist.

Minnesota death certificates: $13, then $6

A certified Minnesota death certificate costs $13 for the first copy and $6 for every additional copy bought on the same order. Copies for a Veterans Affairs claim are free (Minnesota Department of Health, death records; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Where: any county vital records office can print certificates for deaths anywhere in Minnesota from 1997 on, often while you wait. The state Office of Vital Records takes mail at P.O. Box 64499, St. Paul, MN 55164-0499 (651-201-5970) but has no counter.
  • Who: a certified copy requires a "tangible interest", such as being the spouse, a child, a parent or the estate's representative. A noncertified copy, without that proof, costs the same $13.
  • Extras: handling a mailed state order ahead of the queue costs $20, and express delivery $21. The state does not refund a fee when no record turns up.

With each added copy at $6, five certificates come to $37 when ordered together, against $65 if bought one at a time. Funeral homes normally order the first set when they file the record.

Who licenses Minnesota funeral homes, and county and veterans help

In Minnesota, funeral homes are licensed by the Department of Health's Health Regulation Division, not a separate board. Call 651-201-4200 if a home will not show its prices or leans on you to buy more.

Minnesota families may handle much of the after-death work themselves; the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not place the state among the nine where a director is mandatory for some steps. The death record and disposition permit still have to be filed. Cremation requires that permit but no waiting period (Minn. Stat. § 149A.95, subd. 6a). Minnesota sets a deadline instead: final disposition within 14 days of death or of the coroner's release, with embalming, refrigeration or dry ice once it will take longer than 72 hours (Minn. Stat. § 149A.94). A person can also name a designee for final arrangements in a health care directive, and that choice outranks next of kin (Minn. Stat. § 149A.80). Funeral homes file the death record through the state's electronic system, MR&CS.

County burial help

Counties must pay for the burial or cremation of a person who dies without the money for it (Minn. Stat. § 261.035), and each county sets its own amount. (Source: Minn. Stat. § 261.035.) Ask the county human-services office for the figure in writing before the home sets its price.

Veterans' cemeteries

Burial at Fort Snelling National Cemetery in Minneapolis (612-726-1127) is booked by the funeral director, who calls the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office on 800-535-1117. Families deal directly with the state cemeteries at Duluth (218-520-0120), Little Falls (320-616-2527), Preston (507-765-7320) and Redwood Falls (507-709-4390), and with the tribal Leech Lake Veterans Cemetery at Cass Lake (218-335-4463).

Minnesota steps

  1. Request each Minnesota home's printed price list; federal law says you get one on the spot.
  2. Decide on embalming and a viewing early, since dropping them saves the most.
  3. Ring the county human-services office before signing to learn its § 261.035 amount.
  4. For a veteran, have the home check Fort Snelling or the nearest state cemetery first.
  5. Report price-list problems to the Health Regulation Division, 651-201-4200.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a Minnesota funeral in 2026?

About $7,700 for a traditional service, 7% under the $8,300 national figure. A service with cremation comes to around $6,000 and a direct cremation to around $1,800, and a burial adds about $3,600 for the plot. Prices vary widely between homes, so compare.

What does cremation save in Minnesota?

By at least $5,900. Direct cremation is about $1,800 and a traditional funeral with burial about $7,700, with the plot and vault still to come.

What rights do Minnesota families have?

Federal law gives you a written, line-by-line price list, quotes over the phone and the freedom to turn down any item you do not need, while the Funeral Consumers Alliance confirms Minnesota families can skip a funeral director for any given step. The Health Regulation Division of the Department of Health, 651-201-4200, licenses homes and takes complaints.

How can a Minnesota family pay less?

Line-by-line comparison of two or three General Price Lists from Minnesota funeral homes regularly saves $1,000 or more, and township or county cemeteries, which rarely show up online, can cost half what private ones do; the county clerk has the contacts. Direct cremation, about $1,800, is the cheapest option. Decide early who has authority: Minnesota gives it first to whoever the decedent appointed in a dated, signed document, including a health care directive, and a witnessed or notarized one wins over one that is not. A power of attorney does not count, since it ends at death. After that comes the spouse, then the adult children (Minn. Stat. § 149A.80).

Is embalming required in Minnesota?

Not as a rule. Minnesota requires embalming, refrigeration or dry ice only when disposition will take more than 72 hours (Minn. Stat. § 149A.94), and federal rules stop a home from telling you the law demands embalming when it does not. Ask any home that insists which law or cemetery rule it means.

Is Minnesota cheaper than other states?

A little cheaper. Our $7,700 Minnesota figure is 7% under the $8,300 national median, typical of the Midwest.

What is the cheapest Minnesota funeral?

Direct cremation, about $1,800, which covers collection, cremation and the return of the ashes. For a burial, direct burial runs near $3,850 before cemetery charges, with no viewing or ceremony.

Where are the cheaper Minnesota funeral homes?

Phone several homes; they must quote prices over the phone. A casket or urn from another seller must be accepted with no handling charge.
