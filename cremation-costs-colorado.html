Cremation Costs in Colorado (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Colorado cremation page reorganized around the county permit, family-directed cremation and transport charges, with unsourced distance and regional-average claims removed. Cremation Rate: 72%

Colorado cremates about 72% of its dead on this site's figures, and we put direct cremation at about $1,800 and cremation after a full funeral at about $6,200. Colorado is one of the states where a family may arrange a cremation without hiring a funeral director, and it sets no waiting period, but the county vital records office must issue a permit from a complete death certificate first. Starting January 1, 2027, the people who run cremations must hold a state license. This page covers the permit, state Burial Assistance and the veterans cemeteries.

What to Know Before Choosing Cremation in Colorado

Points to settle with a Colorado provider:

  • Written authorization. The person entitled to decide must sign before the crematory proceeds (C.R.S. § 12-135-307), and the county vital records office issues the permit.
Colorado's Disposition of Last Remains Act puts the person's own declaration first, then a personal representative or the person nominated in the will, then a spouse who was not legally separated, then a designated beneficiary, and then a majority of the adult children (C.R.S. § 15-19-106). If someone in a class will not act or cannot be found, the right moves on.

Keeping a Cremation in Colorado Affordable

Cremation costs in Colorado can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare General Price Lists. Every Colorado funeral home must give one on request and quote prices by phone, so line up the direct cremation entries.
  • Ask about mileage. Outside the Front Range a crematory can be a long drive from the place of death. Ask whether the quote covers that transfer and get any per-mile charge in writing.
  • Bring your own urn. The FTC Funeral Rule bars a Colorado funeral home from refusing an urn bought elsewhere or charging to use it.

Distance and Transport in Colorado

Colorado families in mountain and plains counties may be far from the nearest crematory, and providers handle that transport differently: some include it in the $1,800 base price and others add mileage. Ask each provider what is included, and if the family arranges the cremation without a funeral director, plan how the body will be moved and who will carry the permit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a Colorado cremation cost?

We estimate about $1,800 for a Colorado direct cremation and about $6,200 for a full funeral followed by cremation. An urn ($50 to $3,000), a memorial service ($500 to $2,000) and scattering or inurnment ($200 to $600) come on top.

What kinds of cremation services can I choose in Colorado?

Colorado families choose between three routes: direct cremation at about $1,800 with no service, cremation followed by a memorial held later at a place the family picks, or a full funeral with viewing before cremation. Any of them can be shaped around what the family wants.

What does Colorado require before a cremation?

Colorado lets a family arrange a cremation without a funeral director and sets no waiting period, but the crematory must have written authorization (C.R.S. § 12-135-307) and a permit from the county vital records office.

If a Colorado death falls under the medical examiner or coroner, that office has to release the body before any cremation. And under the federal Funeral Rule, you do not need to buy a full casket; an alternative cremation container is sufficient.

Complaints about how a crematory or funeral home handled these steps go to the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies (303-894-7800).

Can I scatter ashes in Colorado?

Usually, yes. The Office of Funeral Home and Crematory Registration within the Department of Regulatory Agencies says it has no rules on where remains may be scattered, so local ordinances and the landowner decide. Get the owner's consent on private land and ask the city or county before using a park or open space.

State law does govern the provider's side. C.R.S. 12-135-307 bars a crematory from commingling one person's remains with another's without written permission, and under 12-135-302 a crematory may scatter remains in a dedicated area if no one has claimed them after three years. At Rocky Mountain, Mesa Verde or Great Sand Dunes, the National Park Service requires a permit or a designated site (36 CFR 2.62).

Why is cremation so common in Colorado?

At about 72% on this site's figures, Colorado's rate is above the 63.4% NFDA projected nationally for 2025. Price plays a part: on our estimates direct cremation costs about $6,200 less than a traditional Colorado funeral with burial.

How long does a Colorado cremation take?

Most of the time is spent on paperwork. Colorado sets no fixed waiting period (C.R.S. § 12-135-307), so the permit from a complete death certificate is the main step, and direct cremation at about $1,800 has no service to schedule.

Can I hold a funeral service before cremation in Colorado?

Yes. In Colorado a funeral with viewing and then cremation costs about $6,200 on this site's estimates, typically $4,960 to $8,060, against about $1,800 for direct cremation. Most of the $4,400 difference is the visitation, staff for the ceremony, and embalming or refrigeration.

Colorado is one of the states that lets a family arrange cremation without hiring a funeral director, so a family can hold its own gathering at home or a church and then deliver the body with a signed authorization (C.R.S. 12-135-307). A rental casket keeps the cost of an open-casket service down.

How Colorado's disposition permit is issued

In Colorado the permit for a cremation comes from the local vital records office in the county of death, and only after it has a complete, correct death certificate. The state's guide is blunt about two points: a permit is never issued after remains have already been cremated, buried or removed from the state, and disposing of remains without one is illegal (Colorado Certificate of Death Completion Guide; checked October 29, 2026).

  • After hours: local registrars may name an alternate issuer, such as the coroner, police, the sheriff or hospital staff. Funeral directors cannot be alternates because that would be a conflict of interest.
  • Remains from another state: if a body arrives for cremation without a permit from the state it came from, the funeral home must contact the state vital records office.

Cremation rules and help in Colorado

To check that a cremation provider in Colorado is registered, or to report a problem, contact the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies at 303-894-7800. The person running a Colorado cremation has not needed an individual license so far; under Senate Bill 24-173 (C.R.S. 12-135-501), cremationists must be licensed from January 1, 2027, or hold a provisional license applied for before that date.

A Colorado family can arrange cremation without a funeral director (the Funeral Consumers Alliance omits Colorado from its nine-state list), but the crematory still wants the death certificate, the cremation permit and the next of kin's signed authorization. It must also give a receipt signed by both sides when the body is delivered and again when the ashes are released, and keep those receipts and a permanent cremation record for at least five years (C.R.S. 12-135-305).

Before a cremation can happen: Colorado lets families arrange cremation without a funeral director. There is no waiting period, but the crematory needs written authorization (C.R.S. § 12-135-307).

A Colorado cremation can qualify for state Burial Assistance of up to $1,500 if the person received Old Age Pension or another listed program, as long as the provider's total charge stays at or under $2,500. (Source: Colorado DHS Adult Financial Reference Guide (Burial Assistance).) Social Security's $255 lump sum is paid only to an eligible Colorado spouse or child, and only if claimed within two years of the death.

A Colorado veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. Options in Colorado include Fort Logan National Cemetery (303-761-0117), Fort Lyon National Cemetery (719-391-6920), and Pikes Peak National Cemetery (719-391-6920). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps for a cremation in Colorado

  1. Get written cremation prices from at least two Colorado providers and compare what each direct cremation price includes.
  2. Confirm who signs the Colorado cremation permit and when, so the memorial date is realistic.
  3. Keep a Colorado cremation quote under $2,500 if you want county human services to approve the $1,500 Burial Assistance payment.
  4. A cremation provider that misstates its prices can be reported at 303-894-7800.
