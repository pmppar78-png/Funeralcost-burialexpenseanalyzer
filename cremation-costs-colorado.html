By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 24, 2026 Cremation Rate: 72%

Direct cremation lands near $1,800, cremation with a ceremony near $6,200, and the 72% cremation rate means most providers compete seriously on price. Here is what the numbers look like now, what regulations apply, and what to ask every Colorado provider before you commit.

What to Know Before Choosing Cremation in Colorado

When considering cremation in Colorado, keep these important points in mind:

  • Authorization: Written authorization from the legal next of kin is required.
In Colorado you do not have to buy the urn from the crematory, the FTC Funeral Rule lets a family in Colorado bring a container, and the CO Dept. of Regulatory Agencies is who you call if a home in Colorado refuses it.

How to Save on Cremation in Colorado

Cremation costs in Colorado can be reduced with these strategies:

  • Compare at least 3 cremation providers in Colorado: Request written General Price Lists.
  • Account for transport distance: In rural Colorado, the nearest crematory may be 100+ miles from the funeral home. Ask whether the quote includes the transfer from the place of death, and get any mileage rate in writing.
  • Buy urns independently: Often lower than funeral home urn pricing. The FTC Funeral Rule protects your right to use any urn or container.

Direct cremation in Colorado can involve a longer transport leg than families expect, and some providers fold that into the $1,800 base price while others charge mileage. Ask specifically about transportation when comparing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cremation cost in Colorado?

Cremation costs in Colorado range from $1,800 for direct cremation to $6,200 for a full funeral service followed by cremation. Additional costs may include urns ($50-$3,000), memorial services ($500-$2,000), and scattering or inurnment fees ($200-$600). The total depends on the level of service you choose.

What kinds of cremation services can I choose in Colorado?

Three paths are standard in Colorado: (1) direct cremation — $1,800, just the cremation itself, no service; (2) cremation with memorial — cremation first, ceremony later at a location any of the three can be tailored to the family's specific wishes.

What legal requirements apply to cremation in Colorado?

The core rule comes from C.R.S. § 12-135-307. Colorado lets families arrange cremation without a funeral director. There is no waiting period, but the crematory needs written authorization.

If a Colorado death falls under the medical examiner or coroner, that office has to release the body before any cremation. And under the federal Funeral Rule, you do not need to buy a full casket — an alternative cremation container is sufficient.

Complaints about how a crematory or funeral home handled these steps go to the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies (303-894-7800).

Can I scatter ashes in Colorado?

Usually, yes. The Office of Funeral Home and Crematory Registration within the Department of Regulatory Agencies says it has no rules on where remains may be scattered, so local ordinances and the landowner decide. Get the owner's consent on private land and ask the city or county before using a park or open space.

State law does govern the provider's side. C.R.S. 12-135-307 bars a crematory from commingling one person's remains with another's without written permission, and under 12-135-302 a crematory may scatter remains in a dedicated area if no one has claimed them after three years. At Rocky Mountain, Mesa Verde or Great Sand Dunes, the National Park Service requires a permit or a designated site (36 CFR 2.62).

What is driving Colorado's cremation rate?

Colorado's 72% cremation rate reflects one of the highest cremation rates in the nation. The cost delta alone explains a lot — direct cremation costs an estimated $6,200 less than a traditional funeral in Colorado.

What is the timeline for cremation in Colorado?

The cremation itself runs 2-3 hours in Colorado. A direct cremation ($1,800) is the fastest end-to-end because no ceremony sits between death and cremation.

Can I hold a funeral service before cremation in Colorado?

Yes. In Colorado a funeral with viewing and then cremation costs about $6,200 on this site's estimates, typically $4,960 to $8,060, against about $1,800 for direct cremation. Most of the $4,400 difference is the visitation, staff for the ceremony, and embalming or refrigeration.

Colorado is one of the states that lets a family arrange cremation without hiring a funeral director, so a family can hold its own gathering at home or a church and then deliver the body with a signed authorization (C.R.S. 12-135-307). A rental casket keeps the cost of an open-casket service down.

Cremation rules and help in Colorado

To check that a cremation provider in Colorado is registered, or to report a problem, contact the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies at 303-894-7800. Individual funeral directors, embalmers and cremationists have not needed a Colorado license so far; Senate Bill 24-173 (C.R.S. 12-135-501) requires one starting January 1, 2027, and practitioners without the new qualifications can apply for a provisional license before then.

Colorado is not on the Funeral Consumers Alliance list of nine states that restrict families from caring for their own dead. You can buy only the services you need, but someone must still file the Colorado death certificate and get the permit.

Before a cremation can happen: Colorado lets families arrange cremation without a funeral director. There is no waiting period, but the crematory needs written authorization (C.R.S. § 12-135-307).

No verified statewide burial benefit is listed here for Colorado. The county social-services or human-services office in Colorado is the place to ask, and our assistance guide explains the federal options. Separately, Social Security pays Colorado survivors $255 once, if they are an eligible spouse or child.

A Colorado veteran who is cremated keeps the same burial benefit: the niche or plot and the marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. One option in Colorado is Fort Logan National Cemetery (303-761-0117). See cem.va.gov.

Next steps

  1. Request itemized prices from Colorado providers by phone or in person; under the FTC Funeral Rule you only pay for what you choose.
  2. Ask which office signs cremation permits in your Colorado county and how long it takes.
  3. Ask about Colorado county help before any contract is signed; it may not be available afterward.
  4. A complaint call to 303-894-7800 is free if a provider misleads you.
