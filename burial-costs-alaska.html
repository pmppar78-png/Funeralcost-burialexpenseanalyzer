Ways to Lower a Burial Bill in Alaska

Burial costs in Alaska can be managed with smart planning:

  • Bundled quotes obscure where the markup sits, and it sits in a different place depending on the Alaska provider.
  • Look for hybrid or natural burial sections: Several Alaska cemeteries have added green or natural burial sections without advertising them prominently.
  • Consider certified conservation burial grounds: Alaska is one of the more active states for true conservation burial. These grounds protect land in perpetuity and price simply, typically $3,920-$6,860 all-in versus $21,790+ for traditional burial in Alaska.
  • Use the competitive cremation market as leverage: With 72% of Alaska families choosing cremation, burial-focused providers often have pricing flexibility they do not advertise.

Regional Context for Burial in Alaska

Alaska cremation rates run far above the national average, and the consequence is a more competitive market for the remaining burial services: the Alaska funeral homes that still do a lot of burials often price aggressively to win that share. Do not assume that burial pricing in Alaska moves in lockstep with cost of living; shop it as if you were in a buyer's market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do Alaska families spend on burial?

Typical Alaska burials total between $21,790 and $26,290. Of that, the Alaska funeral service runs about $9,800, the cemetery plot $4,500, the casket around $2,940, the vault $1,800, opening-and-closing $2,250, and a headstone $500-$5,000.

Do I need a vault for burial in Alaska?

We found no Alaska statute that requires a vault, so the cemetery decides. The table on this page estimates about $1,800 for a vault or liner in Alaska, and many cemeteries accept the cheaper liner instead of a sealed vault.

Alaska does require a burial-transit permit before any burial (AS 18.50.250), and local rules govern where a body may be buried; the Municipality of Anchorage, for example, allows burial only in an approved cemetery. Ask the cemetery for its container rule before the funeral home orders anything.

What are the cheapest burial options in Alaska?

Direct (immediate) burial skips embalming, viewing and a ceremony at the funeral home, so it is usually the lowest-cost burial on an Alaska price list. You may supply your own casket in Alaska; the FTC Funeral Rule forbids a handling fee for it. Eligible veterans in Alaska can be buried in a VA national cemetery at no cost for the grave, opening and closing, liner and marker.

How do cemetery prices differ across Alaska?

Always request an itemized sheet from each Alaska cemetery covering plot, opening-and-closing, vault policy, perpetual care, and any residency or membership restrictions. A useful tip: green and hybrid burial sections in Alaska cemeteries often sit 30-50% below traditional plots, and conservation burial grounds are more established here than in most regions.

Once I add every line item, what does burial actually cost in Alaska?

A complete burial in Alaska (funeral service ($9,800) + plot ($4,500) + casket ($2,940) + vault ($1,800) + opening-and-closing ($2,250) + headstone ($500-$5,000)) totals $21,790 to $26,290 in practice.

Is green burial available in Alaska?

Not all cemeteries in Alaska offer green burial sections, so you may need to research options in your area. Our green burial guide covers the embalming and vault questions to raise with an Alaska cemetery.

Burial rules and help in Alaska

The Alaska Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing (mortuary science program) (907-465-2250) licenses the funeral homes that arrange burials in Alaska. It can also tell you which Alaska office handles cemetery complaints.

No funeral director is needed for an Alaska burial (the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not put Alaska among its nine states), so the family can settle the plot, the grave opening and the accepted container with the cemetery itself. Where filing the death certificate first would cause undue hardship, state regulations may allow the burial-transit permit to issue before that certificate is filed (AS 18.50.260).

A burial in Alaska goes ahead once the permit is issued; for cremation, there is no fixed waiting period, though a permit is required (AS 13.75.020).

For an Alaska burial, General Relief Assistance pays up to $1,250 for the funeral home's basic services and may also pay for a plot in the least expensive section and the opening and closing of the grave (7 AAC 47.130). (Source: 7 AAC 47.130.) Social Security's $255 payment to a surviving spouse or child is well short of the $4,500 Alaska plot estimate on this page.

For Alaska veterans, the plot and marker in a national cemetery are free, which can remove thousands from a burial bill. In Alaska, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: Fort Richardson National Cemetery (907-384-7075); Sitka National Cemetery (907-384-7075); tribal veterans cemetery: Metlakatla Veterans Memorial Cemetery (tribal, 907-886-1121) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Next steps for a burial in Alaska

  1. Get the funeral home's General Price List and the Alaska cemetery's own price sheet before you agree to a burial.
  2. Ask the Alaska cemetery for its rules on grave opening times, vaults and markers.
  3. Ask the Division of Public Assistance which least-cost plot it will approve before a family in Alaska buys one; General Relief pays only for the cheapest section.
  4. For a veteran, ask Fort Richardson National Cemetery about eligibility before buying a private plot.
  5. A complaint call to 907-465-2250 is free if a funeral home misleads you about burial costs.
