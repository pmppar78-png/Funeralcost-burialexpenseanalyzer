Funeral Costs in Louisiana: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: South

Louisiana's funeral market is defined by two things most states don't share: above-ground burial in the New Orleans area (a practical response to the water table, not tradition alone) and the Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors' casket-sale licensing rule, which the Fifth Circuit struck down in 2013 (St. Joseph Abbey v. Castille) after a decade-long fight by Benedictine monks. The headline 2026 numbers run $6,700 for a traditional service and $1,700 for direct cremation — below the national median — but the spread between providers in a given parish is wide. Major operators include Lake Lawn Metairie Funeral Home, Leitz-Eagan, Charbonnet Labat Glapion, Rhodes Funeral Home (serving New Orleans's Black community since 1884), Resthaven in Baton Rouge, and Rose-Neath across North Louisiana. St. Louis Cemeteries No. 1, 2, and 3 in New Orleans, Lafayette Cemetery in the Garden District, and Metairie Cemetery carry historical weight and premium pricing; most suburban and rural parishes still offer plots under $2,500.

Above-Ground Burial, the Water Table, and New Orleans Pricing

New Orleans, much of Jefferson Parish, and low-lying Plaquemines and St. Bernard Parishes sit at or below sea level with a water table that can reach within inches of the surface after a heavy rain. That is why above-ground tombs and wall vaults dominate the New Orleans cemetery landscape — not primarily tradition, but physics. Tomb interment at St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 (burial ground of Marie Laveau), Lafayette Cemetery No. 1 in the Garden District, St. Louis Cemetery No. 3 on Esplanade, or Metairie Cemetery typically runs $5,500-$12,000 for a family tomb or crypt, with single niche and columbarium options starting closer to $1,800. Above-ground interment also changes the disposition timeline: the "year and a day" rule permits reuse of a tomb after that interval, so family tombs hold many generations. Outside the New Orleans metro, standard in-ground burial pricing prevails — Resthaven Gardens in Baton Rouge, Forest Park in Shreveport, and Sunrise Memorial in Lafayette price plots $1,800-$3,500 in most sections.

The St. Joseph Abbey Case and Why It Matters to Louisiana Consumers

For years, Louisiana law restricted casket sales to licensed funeral directors, which the Benedictine monks of St. Joseph Abbey in Covington challenged in federal court after being cited for selling simple handmade pine caskets. In 2013 the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled in St. Joseph Abbey v. Castille that the restriction served no legitimate purpose beyond protecting funeral-director profits and struck it down. The practical effect for families: Louisiana funeral homes must accept any third-party casket — from the Abbey itself, from Costco, Walmart, or an online retailer like Titan Casket or Overnight Caskets — without a handling surcharge. The federal FTC Funeral Rule confirms that right nationally; Louisiana families now have both a federal and a state-specific case law tradition of casket-sale consumer choice. If a Louisiana provider claims state law requires their casket, they are misrepresenting both federal law and binding Fifth Circuit precedent.

Your Consumer Rights in Louisiana

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Louisiana must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in Louisiana has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Your state attorney general's office can also assist with consumer protection complaints.

Line Items Louisiana Families Miss on the First Read

  • Basic services of funeral director and staff — non-declinable. $1,800-$3,200 across Louisiana, with New Orleans-metro providers at the top of the band.
  • Tomb opening and closing — unique to above-ground burial, distinct from a standard grave opening. $400-$900 at historic New Orleans cemeteries, depending on tomb access and labor.
  • Wall vault / columbarium niche — a mid-tier option in New Orleans that avoids both in-ground water issues and family-tomb costs. $1,800-$3,500 at Greenwood Cemetery, Lake Lawn Park, or Metairie Cemetery.
  • Louisiana parish permits — Orleans, Jefferson, and East Baton Rouge parishes each have their own permit fees administered through the parish clerk of court. Bundled into some GPLs and charged separately on others.
  • Certified death certificates — $9 each from Louisiana Vital Records, with additional copies at a reduced rate. Many tasks require 8-12 copies.
  • Hurricane and evacuation contingencies — some Gulf Coast Louisiana providers build evacuation or deferred-service clauses into their pre-need contracts. Ask what happens if a named storm arrives between arrangement and service.

Payment Assistance in Louisiana

Louisiana has a patchwork of parish-level and state-administered funeral aid programs, plus federal benefits that apply anywhere:

  • Parish indigent burial — many Louisiana parishes, including Orleans, Jefferson, East Baton Rouge, and Caddo, administer modest indigent-burial funds through the parish coroner or welfare office. Budgets are limited and application usually precedes services.
  • Louisiana Crime Victims Reparations Board — administered by the Louisiana Commission on Law Enforcement, covers up to $5,000 in funeral and burial costs when death results from a crime.
  • Social Security lump-sum death benefit — a one-time $255 payment for eligible survivors. Details.
  • Veteran burial benefits — Louisiana veterans qualify for no-cost burial at Port Hudson National Cemetery in Zachary, Alexandria National Cemetery, or the Northwest Louisiana Veterans Cemetery in Keithville. Full veteran benefits.
  • Medicaid funeral assistance — Louisiana Medicaid will disregard a modest funeral and burial trust in asset calculations, allowing families to preserve funds for disposition. Medicaid funeral assistance.
  • Burial insurance — final expense policies covering $5,000-$25,000 are widely available in Louisiana. Compare the best burial insurance companies.
  • Crowdfunding — platforms like GoFundMe are heavily used after disasters in Louisiana. Crowdfunding guide.
  • Payment plans — some Louisiana funeral homes offer installment financing. Payment plan options.

What to Do When Planning a Funeral in Louisiana

When an immediate Louisiana funeral arrangement lands on you, the order of operations matters. This sequence tends to save both money and emotional bandwidth:

  1. Take a breath. Unless there are legal or medical time constraints, you typically have 24–72 hours before decisions must be finalized.
  2. Request General Price Lists from at least 2–3 funeral homes in your area of Louisiana. They are legally required to provide them. Questions to ask funeral homes
  3. Decide on disposition: cremation in Louisiana or burial in Louisiana. This is the single biggest cost decision.
  4. Choose only the services you need. Embalming, premium caskets, and elaborate arrangements are optional. What funeral homes don't tell you
  5. Explore payment assistance if cost is a concern: Social Security benefits, veteran benefits, Medicaid assistance, or crowdfunding.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in Louisiana right now?

Louisiana averages $6,700 for a traditional service and $1,700 for direct cremation statewide, running about 15 percent below the $7,848 U.S. median. New Orleans pulls pricing up by around 15 percent because of above-ground burial in historic cemeteries like St. Louis No. 1, Lafayette No. 1, and Metairie Cemetery, where tomb and wall-vault interment runs $5,500-$12,000. Shreveport, Baton Rouge, Lafayette, and Monroe track the statewide average closely. The GPL you receive from Lake Lawn Metairie will look very different from one issued by a rural Rose-Neath chapel or by a Cajun parish independent.

Does cremation save money compared with burial in Louisiana?

Direct cremation at $1,700 versus a $6,700 traditional funeral plus an average $3,200 plot — or a $5,500-$12,000 New Orleans tomb interment — creates a gap of $5,000 or more. Louisiana's 35 percent cremation rate remains below the national figure near 60 percent, shaped in part by Catholic tradition across Acadiana and the New Orleans archdiocese's continued emphasis on whole-body burial. Cremation Society of Louisiana, Heritage South Cremation, and low-cost direct-cremation specialists serve all the major metros.

What are my consumer rights at Louisiana funeral homes?

The FTC Funeral Rule gives every Louisiana consumer the right to a printed General Price List on request, the right to pick and choose services (packages cannot be forced), the right to use a third-party casket without a handling fee, and the right to decline embalming in most cases. Louisiana has its own binding layer through St. Joseph Abbey v. Castille (5th Cir. 2013), which specifically affirmed that third-party caskets — including handmade pine caskets from the Abbey — must be accepted. The Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors handles complaints against licensed providers.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Louisiana?

Three moves cut the most: compare at least three Louisiana GPLs side by side (Lake Lawn Metairie vs. Charbonnet Labat Glapion vs. Heritage South Cremation, for example), strip packages to only the services you actually want, and choose direct cremation at $1,700 if cost is the priority. In New Orleans specifically, consider a wall vault or columbarium niche at Greenwood, Lake Lawn, or Metairie ($1,800-$3,500) rather than a family tomb. Parish indigent-burial funds, Louisiana Crime Victims Reparations (up to $5,000), and veteran burial at Port Hudson National Cemetery or Alexandria National Cemetery are all worth checking.

Is embalming legally required in Louisiana?

No — Louisiana law does not require embalming as a matter of course. Refrigeration is an accepted alternative, and cremation or direct burial within 24-48 hours obviates the question. Some Louisiana funeral homes have internal policies requiring embalming for an open-casket viewing, but that is provider policy, not state law. The FTC Funeral Rule specifically prohibits providers from falsely claiming embalming is legally mandated. When a Louisiana provider cites state law to justify embalming, ask them to cite the statute — they cannot.

How do funeral costs in Louisiana compare to other states?

Louisiana's $6,700 traditional-funeral average sits about 15 percent below the national median of $7,848, in line with most Gulf South and Deep South states. Alabama ($6,400), Arkansas ($6,100), and Mississippi track slightly lower; Florida ($7,800) runs meaningfully higher because of its retiree demographics and metro density; Texas and Georgia sit close to the Louisiana line. The region-wide softness reflects lower real estate costs and a larger share of family-owned funeral homes than coastal markets.

Which funeral option costs the least in Louisiana?

Direct cremation at $1,700 is the floor, available across the state through Cremation Society of Louisiana, Heritage South Cremation, and independent cremation-only providers in New Orleans, Baton Rouge, Lafayette, and Shreveport. Direct burial (a simple in-ground burial without viewing or ceremony) runs about $3,350. In New Orleans, existing family tombs can often accept a new interment for just the opening-and-closing fee ($400-$900) and a simple disposition charge — dramatically less than purchasing new cemetery space. Home funerals are permitted under Louisiana law with proper permits.

Where do I look for low-cost funeral providers in Louisiana?

Pull GPLs from three Louisiana providers that span the range: a large independent or corporate chapel (Lake Lawn Metairie, Rose-Neath), a historic independent (Charbonnet Labat Glapion, Rhodes Funeral Home), and a direct-cremation specialist (Cremation Society of Louisiana, Heritage South Cremation). The Funeral Consumers Alliance of Greater New Orleans, affiliated with the national FCA, maintains periodic price surveys for the metro. St. Joseph Abbey in Covington continues to sell simple pine caskets that Louisiana funeral homes must accept following the 2013 Fifth Circuit ruling.
