Your Consumer Rights in Louisiana

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Louisiana must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Louisiana has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

Where Louisiana Families Can Find Money for a Funeral

  • A Louisiana parish pauper disposition is capped at its actual cost, and the parish coroner may set a lower maximum for the funeral home (La. R.S. 13:5715).
  • Louisiana survivors claim the one-time $255 from Social Security by phone (1-800-772-1213); only a widow, widower or eligible child qualifies, and the deadline is two years. Who signs the funeral contract is set by La. R.S. 8:655: a person the decedent named in a notarial testament or notarized declaration comes first, then the spouse, then a majority of adult children, grandchildren, parents and siblings in turn. When none of them can give authorization, a district court judgment is required, which adds delay and legal cost.
  • For a Louisiana veteran, the person who covered the funeral bill claims the VA allowance on Form 21P-530EZ: up to $1,032 for burial plus $1,032 for a plot when a non-service-connected death falls on or after October 1, 2026. Eligibility rules for Louisiana families are in our veteran burial benefits guide.

What Drives Funeral Costs in Louisiana

What you pay for a funeral in Louisiana depends on a cluster of local factors rather than any single number. Cost of living in Louisiana, how many funeral homes compete in your area, whether Louisiana state regulations require specific steps, and local real estate prices for funeral home facilities all feed into quotes. South pricing is generally lower than the US median of $8,300, and metro-versus-rural pricing in Louisiana routinely differs by 10-30%.

At 35%, Louisiana's cremation rate is 28.4 points below the 63.4% national rate that NFDA projected for 2025.

Funeral rules and help in Louisiana

If a funeral home in Louisiana refuses to show prices or pressures you, the licensing body is the Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors, reachable at 504-838-5109.

Louisiana is one of nine states where a licensed funeral director must be involved in some steps: filing the death certificate, moving the body, and being present at the final disposition (La. R.S. 37:848, as summarized by Funeral Consumers Alliance). A Louisiana family can still do the rest of a funeral itself, such as washing and dressing the body or holding the service at home.

For families comparing a funeral with cremation, note that Louisiana sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (La. R.S. 37:876-877; 13:5716).

Louisiana handles pauper funerals through the parish coroner, who arranges the disposition, preferably with a Louisiana-licensed funeral home; the parish or municipality where the person was domiciled pays the actual cost (the one where death occurred, if the person lived out of state), and the coroner may set a maximum (La. R.S. 13:5715). (Source: La. R.S. 13:5715(A)(2).) Social Security's one-time $255 death payment, paid only to a qualifying spouse or child, is small next to the $6,700 average Louisiana funeral in the table.

A Louisiana funeral home can reserve a veteran's grave at Louisiana National Cemetery (225-654-1988) or Port Hudson National Cemetery (225-654-1988, cremated remains only) through the VA's National Cemetery Scheduling Office (800-535-1117). The state-run veterans cemeteries, Central Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (337-238-6405), Northeast Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (318-728-4346), Northwest Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (318-925-0612), Southeast Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (985-646-6458), or Southwest Louisiana Veterans Cemetery (337-246-7094), take requests directly.

Next steps for a funeral in Louisiana

  1. Get written price lists from at least two Louisiana funeral homes before you agree to anything.
  2. Consider a Louisiana memorial service after cremation or burial, which avoids embalming and viewing fees.
  3. Call the coroner of the Louisiana parish where the person lived about a pauper disposition before a funeral home is hired.
  4. For a veteran, price Louisiana National Cemetery against a private Louisiana plot before choosing.
  5. Keep the board's number, 504-838-5109, in case a funeral home will not honor its price list.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in Louisiana right now?

Current averages in Louisiana come in at $6,700 for a traditional funeral, $5,400 for cremation with a service, and from $1,700 for direct cremation, with the traditional figure sitting 19% below the $8,300 US median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $3,200 when a Louisiana family chooses burial.

Does cremation save money compared with burial in Louisiana?

A direct cremation in Louisiana runs about $1,700, whereas a traditional funeral with burial averages $6,700 before adding the plot, so the gap can easily exceed $5,000. Louisiana's cremation rate of 35% sits below the 63.4% national rate NFDA projected for 2025.

What are my consumer rights at Louisiana funeral homes?

In Louisiana, as everywhere in the US, the FTC Funeral Rule requires an itemized General Price List, phone price quotes, and no fee for using a casket bought elsewhere. Louisiana law requires a licensed funeral director for filing the death certificate, moving the body, and being present at the final disposition (La. R.S. 37:848, per the Funeral Consumers Alliance). You can confirm a Louisiana license or file a complaint with the Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors at 504-838-5109.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Louisiana?

Three moves cut the most: compare at least three Louisiana General Price Lists side by side before choosing a provider, strip packages down to only the services you actually want, and choose direct cremation ($1,700) if cost is the priority.

Is embalming legally required in Louisiana?

Only after 30 hours without refrigeration. La. R.S. 37:848 requires a body held longer than 30 hours after death to be embalmed or kept continuously at 45°F or below; otherwise it must be buried or cremated within 30 hours. The Board's rule (51 La. Admin. Code Pt. XXVI, 103) also requires embalming or cremation before a body is moved out of Louisiana more than 24 hours after death, and it does not require embalming where religious practice forbids it.

For a body that stays in Louisiana and is refrigerated, the roughly $670 our Louisiana estimate puts on embalming is optional, and a funeral home needs your approval before it can charge it.

How do funeral costs in Louisiana compare to other states?

Louisiana's traditional-funeral average of $6,700 sits 19% below the $8,300 national median, and the South region overall trends toward moderate to lower pricing than the rest of the country.

Which funeral option costs the least in Louisiana?

Direct cremation carries the lowest price tag in Louisiana, from roughly $1,700. Direct burial is the next step up at about $3,350, which is $3,350 below a traditional Louisiana funeral.

Where do I look for low-cost funeral providers in Louisiana?

Start with the price lists of three Louisiana funeral homes and compare direct cremation, from about $1,700 here, and immediate burial. The State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors (504-838-5109) can confirm that a provider is licensed.

Body donation can remove most of the cost. LSU Health New Orleans' Bureau of Anatomical Services arranges and pays transport for registered donors who die within 200 miles of New Orleans; the family pays the mileage beyond that. Registering requires a form signed before a notary and two witnesses, so it has to be done in advance.
