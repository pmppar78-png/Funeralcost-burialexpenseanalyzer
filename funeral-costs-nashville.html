Funeral Costs in Nashville, Tennessee (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: Tennessee

Nashville funeral pricing has moved faster than almost any other southern metro. The city's population grew by 15% over the past decade, and a traditional funeral now averages $7,705 — about $1,000 above the Tennessee state figure. Marshall Donnelly Combs on Demonbreun, the historic firm that's buried much of Music City's establishment; Spring Hill Funeral Home on Gallatin Pike; and newer boutique cremation operators on the east side and in Franklin are the three pricing poles families weigh. Tennessee's 42% cremation rate leaves Nashville roughly split between traditional-service families and a growing cohort choosing direct cremation at $1,840 and a memorial service held at the church, the park, or the family home.

Understanding Nashville Funeral Pricing

Funeral costs in Nashville carry a metro premium of approximately 15% above the Tennessee state average. This is driven by higher real estate costs for funeral home facilities, higher labor costs, and greater demand for services in a dense metropolitan area. However, the competitive market in Nashville also means you have more providers to choose from, which gives you leverage when comparing prices.

The most effective way to find the best price is to request General Price Lists from 2 to 3 funeral homes in the Nashville area and compare them line by line. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, every provider must give you this list upon request — no appointment needed, no commitment required.

Your Rights in Nashville

FTC Funeral Rule Protection Every funeral home in Nashville must provide an itemized General Price List, let you choose only the services you want, and accept caskets purchased elsewhere. You are never required to buy a package. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

The Boutique Cremation Boom East of the Cumberland

Something distinctive is happening in East Nashville, Madison, and Donelson: a wave of small, cremation-focused operations — many owned by second-career operators who trained at places like Marshall Donnelly Combs before going independent — is publishing direct-cremation prices in the $895-$1,395 band, well below the city average of $1,840. These firms typically have no viewing chapel, no embalming suite, and a website rather than a showroom. They've absorbed younger, more transient Nashville families — the ones who moved from Chicago or Los Angeles for a tech or music job and have no multi-generational tie to Spring Hill Cemetery. The pricing pressure has forced the traditional houses to publish lower-tier cremation packages they didn't list five years ago.

Nashville's 42% Tennessee cremation rate lags behind metros like Portland or Phoenix but runs well above the state's rural counties. Davidson County's rate tracks closer to 50% — driven by the newer-resident demographic and by cemetery plot pricing in Williamson County that crossed $6,000 for desirable sections.

Tennessee Title 62 Chapter 5 and Your Recourse

Funeral establishments in Nashville are licensed under Tennessee Code Annotated Title 62, Chapter 5, and regulated by the Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers, based in Nashville itself. The Board requires preneed contracts to place at least 110% of the principal in a trust account (with 10% retained by the seller), publishes disciplinary orders quarterly on its website, and takes consumer complaints at tn.gov/commerce/regboards/funeral. Tennessee funeral law specifically permits refrigeration as an alternative to embalming for up to 72 hours for most causes of death — families commonly do not realize they can decline the $771 embalming fee when the timeline allows it.

A distinctive Tennessee wrinkle: the state recognizes the authority of a designated agent under a Tennessee durable power of attorney for health care to make funeral arrangements, even over the objection of next of kin — useful for blended families and estranged adult children where the decedent documented preferences in advance.

Nashville's Historic Cemetery Landscape

Nashville's three major cemeteries each carry different pricing logic. Mount Olivet on Lebanon Pike, consecrated in 1856 and containing the graves of Nashville governors, Confederate generals, and country music figures, quotes plots in the $3,500-$8,000 band depending on section. Spring Hill Cemetery on Gallatin Pike, the city's historic African-American burial ground tied to Meharry Medical College alumni and civil rights figures, prices more modestly at $2,200-$4,500. Nashville National Cemetery on Gallatin Pike — the 64-acre federal cemetery dedicated in 1866 for Union war dead — offers no-cost burial for eligible veterans including spouse and minor children, a benefit worth $6,000+ to eligible families. Middle Tennessee Veterans Cemetery on McCrory Lane in Pegram serves veterans who prefer a suburban setting.

Families working on a budget should know that Metro Nashville operates several municipal cemeteries (Greenwood South, Greenwood North, Mount Ararat) with plot fees well below the private parks — typically $1,200-$2,400 for a single grave — and that Williamson County's rural church cemeteries will sometimes sell plots to non-members for a donation.

Tennessee Funeral Payment Assistance — What's Actually Available

Tennessee does not operate a standalone state-funded funeral assistance program, which surprises Nashville families coming from states like Minnesota or New York that do. What's available: the Social Security lump-sum death payment of $255 for eligible survivors; VA burial allowances ($893-$2,000 service-connected) plus no-cost Nashville National Cemetery burial for veterans; TennCare (Tennessee's Medicaid) spend-down protection that lets a qualifying individual set aside up to roughly $6,000 in a preneed burial trust without affecting eligibility; and county pauper burial handled by the Davidson County Medical Examiner when no family is available and the estate is insolvent — a basic cremation paid from public funds. Applications for TennCare burial trusts run through the Tennessee Department of Human Services.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical funeral price in Nashville?

A traditional Nashville funeral averages $7,705 — about 15% above the Tennessee state figure of $6,700 and roughly at the $7,848 US median. Direct cremation starts near $1,840 at traditional firms but runs $895–$1,395 at East Nashville and Madison boutique cremation operators. Mount Olivet Cemetery plots run $3,500–$8,000, Spring Hill Cemetery $2,200–$4,500.

Is cremation or burial more common in Nashville?

Davidson County cremation runs near 50%, above the Tennessee statewide rate of 42%. Growth is concentrated in the newer-resident demographics of East Nashville and the suburbs. Boutique cremation firms often beat traditional houses by $400–$900 on direct cremation — Marshall Donnelly Combs and Spring Hill still lead traditional-service work.

Where does Nashville rank nationally for funeral pricing?

Nashville's $7,705 traditional average sits just below the $7,848 US median and about 15% above the Tennessee state figure. Williamson County (Franklin, Brentwood) prices higher; Rutherford and Wilson counties price 8–12% lower. The non-declinable basic-services fee at the major Nashville houses ($2,200–$2,800) is the main driver of the metro premium.

How do I compare funeral quotes in Nashville?

Pull the FTC-required General Price List from at least three Nashville providers — one traditional firm (Marshall Donnelly Combs, Spring Hill, or Woodlawn-Roesch-Patton), one East Nashville or Madison cremation boutique, and one suburban Rutherford or Wilson county provider. The Funeral Consumers Alliance of Middle Tennessee tracks regional pricing. Watch the non-declinable basic-services fee closely — it is where the largest variance shows up.

What is direct cremation in Nashville and how much does it cost?

Direct cremation in Nashville runs $895–$1,840 depending on provider. Boutique cremation firms on the east side of the Cumberland and in Madison advertise the low end; traditional funeral homes quote closer to $1,840. The service covers transport, cremation, and return of the cremated remains — no viewing, no embalming, no ceremony at the funeral home. Families commonly hold a memorial later at a church, park, or home.

How can Nashville families find low-cost funeral options?

Eligible veterans should use Nashville National Cemetery on Gallatin Pike or Middle Tennessee Veterans Cemetery at Pegram — both offer no-cost burial worth $6,000+ to eligible families including spouse. Metro Nashville municipal cemeteries (Greenwood South, Greenwood North, Mount Ararat) quote plots at $1,200–$2,400, well below Mount Olivet. For TennCare recipients, the preneed burial trust spend-down protection lets a qualifying individual set aside around $6,000 without affecting eligibility. East Nashville boutique cremation firms publish the lowest direct-cremation prices in the metro.
