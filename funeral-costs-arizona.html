Your Consumer Rights in Arizona

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Arizona must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide.

If you believe a funeral home in Arizona has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance.

What's Specific to Arizona (West)

Arizona's high cremation rate has reshaped its funeral home market — full-service traditional funerals are less than half of all arrangements here, so providers who still do many traditional services in Arizona tend to either cater to a specific community or compete aggressively on price. Ask for their breakdown of cremation versus burial volume when comparing; it predicts pricing posture.

Payment Assistance in Arizona

  • Families in Arizona who have no money for a funeral should reach the Arizona county social services office first; signing with a provider before the office approves can cost you the help.
  • The $255 Social Security death payment goes only to an eligible surviving spouse or child; in Arizona, as elsewhere, they apply by calling 1-800-772-1213 within two years of the death.
  • VA reimburses part of an Arizona veteran's funeral through Form 21P-530EZ, filed by whoever paid; deaths not connected to service on or after October 1, 2026 qualify for up to $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for the plot, as our veteran burial benefits guide explains.
  • More options for Arizona families, including Medicaid and charity help, are in our funeral payment assistance guide and how to pay for a funeral with no money.

Funeral rules and help in Arizona

The Arizona Department of Health Services, Bureau of Licensing for Professions & Occupations (BLPO) licenses funeral directors and establishments in Arizona; call 602-364-2079 to check a license or file a complaint.

Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Arizona among the nine states that require a funeral director for parts of after-death care, so a family may handle much of the work itself. The Arizona death certificate and disposition permit still have to be filed.

Cremation timing: Arizona sets no fixed waiting period before cremation, but a permit is needed first (A.R.S. § 32-1365.02).

No verified statewide burial benefit is listed here for Arizona. The county social-services or human-services office in Arizona is the place to ask, and our assistance guide explains the federal options. Social Security pays a one-time $255 to a qualifying surviving spouse or child in Arizona, as in every state.

For Arizona veterans, the plot and marker in a national cemetery are free, which can remove thousands from a burial bill. In Arizona, VA national cemeteries accepting burials: National Memorial Cemetery of Arizona (480-513-3600); Prescott National Cemetery (928-445-4860, cremated remains only); state veterans cemeteries: Arizona Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Camp Navajo (928-214-3474); Arizona Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Marana (520-638-4894); Monte Calvario Veterans Cemetery (520-879-6317). Source: VA National Cemetery Administration.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average price of a funeral in Arizona right now?

Current averages in Arizona come in at $7,200 for a traditional funeral, $5,800 for cremation with a service, and from $1,600 for direct cremation — with the traditional figure sitting 13% below the $8,300 US median. Cemetery and burial fees add roughly $3,400 when applicable.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Arizona?

In Arizona, direct cremation at $1,600 costs about $5,600 less than the $7,200 traditional funeral, and roughly $9,000 less once about $3,400 in cemetery costs is added.

What rights do Arizona families have when arranging a funeral?

Under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR 453), every Arizona funeral home must give you an itemized General Price List, quote prices by phone, and accept a casket or urn you bought elsewhere without a handling fee. Arizona is not among the nine states the Funeral Consumers Alliance lists as requiring families to hire a funeral director. The Arizona Department of Health Services, Bureau of Licensing for Professions & Occupations (BLPO), 602-364-2079, licenses Arizona funeral homes and takes complaints.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Arizona?

Conservation burial grounds and hybrid cemetery sections in Arizona typically cost 30-50% less than traditional plots in the same cemetery.

Does Arizona require embalming?

Arizona law does not require embalming in most situations.

Is Arizona cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Compare the Arizona figures in the cost table on this page with the national median of $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial, or $6,280 with cremation (NFDA 2023). Prices inside Arizona vary by provider, so the General Price Lists you collect matter more than any average.

Which funeral option costs the least in Arizona?

Direct cremation carries the lowest price tag in Arizona, from roughly $1,600. Direct burial is the next step up at about $3,600 and is still considerably cheaper than a traditional service.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Arizona?

Call several Arizona funeral homes and ask for prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires them to give. Buying the casket or urn elsewhere is allowed under the FTC Funeral Rule, and the Arizona home cannot charge a fee for it.
