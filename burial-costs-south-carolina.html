Bringing Down a South Carolina Burial Bill

Where South Carolina families can save:

  • Ask both for prices in writing. A South Carolina funeral home owes you its General Price List, and a perpetual care cemetery must give you a written list of required charges when you buy an interment right (S.C. Code § 40-8-100(G)).
  • Price a church cemetery. Church and family cemeteries set their own fees, so get their written prices next to a memorial park's before choosing.
  • Look at veterans cemeteries first. An eligible veteran can be buried at Beaufort, Florence or Fort Jackson National Cemetery or the state's M.J. Dolly Cooper cemetery with no charge for the grave or marker.
  • Compare a crypt with a grave. If the cemetery has a mausoleum, ask for the crypt price beside the plot, liner and opening charges, since it replaces all three.

Two Bills: Funeral Home and Cemetery

The funeral home's bill does not include the cemetery's, and South Carolina regulates them separately: funeral homes answer to the Board of Funeral Service, perpetual care cemeteries to the Perpetual Care Cemetery Board. Ask each funeral home for its prices by phone, which the FTC Funeral Rule requires it to give (16 CFR 453.2(b)(1)), and each cemetery for its list of charges.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does burial in South Carolina cost?

Our model puts a traditional South Carolina burial at $14,910 to $19,410. Inside that, the funeral home charges about $6,700 for its service and about $2,010 for its casket; the cemetery bills roughly $3,000 for the plot, $1,200 for the liner and $1,500 to open and close the grave. A headstone adds $500 to $5,000, and a monument dealer may quote less than the cemetery.

Cemeteries in South Carolina are regulated separately from funeral homes, by the Perpetual Care Cemetery Board, so ask each one for its own written prices. Funeral homes answer to the Board of Funeral Service (803-896-4429).

Is a vault required for a South Carolina grave?

State law does not call for one; the cemetery's rules decide. Our table puts the container at about $1,200, and a funeral home or vault dealer can usually sell one for less than the cemetery.

South Carolina's perpetual care cemetery law backs that up. Under S.C. Code § 40-8-100(F), a cemetery must post its policies and may not prevent the use of merchandise a lot owner bought from another source, as long as it meets the cemetery's regulations. When you buy an interment right, it must also give you a written list of required charges and of charges you may face later (§ 40-8-100(G)).

Which South Carolina burial costs least?

Direct burial, with no embalming, viewing or service, is the cheapest burial most funeral homes list. Buying the casket from another seller helps too: the FTC Funeral Rule bars a handling fee, and the cemetery cannot refuse merchandise bought elsewhere that meets its rules (S.C. Code § 40-8-100(F)).

Do South Carolina cemetery prices vary?

Plot, opening and closing, and any vault or liner requirement are priced cemetery by cemetery in South Carolina, and since the FTC Funeral Rule reaches funeral homes rather than most cemeteries, an itemized list may not be owed; ask for it all the same. Beaufort National Cemetery and Florence National Cemetery Annex are among the VA national cemeteries in the state. For a grave in an old family plot that now sits on someone else's land, S.C. Code § 27-43-310 obliges the landowner to allow ingress and egress to descendants, plot owners and people lawfully taking part in a burial, for purposes such as visiting and maintaining the graves.

With everything added, what is the South Carolina total?

Adding every South Carolina line gives $14,910 to $19,410: the plot at $3,000, the service at $6,700, a casket at $2,010, the vault at $1,200, the opening at $1,500 and a headstone at $500 to $5,000. Death certificates and other cash advance items come on top.

Can I have a green burial in South Carolina?

Ask each cemetery whether it allows burial without embalming or a vault in a biodegradable container; policies differ. Our green burial guide has the questions to ask.

South Carolina's disinterment permit

In South Carolina, moving a body to a different grave needs a disinterment and reinterment permit from the state's vital records office. Reburial in the same plot does not. The permit is issued once the State Registrar's form is signed by the next of kin and by the person doing the disinterment, or once there is a court order. One permit covers digging up, transport and reburial, but the work has to happen within one year or the family must apply again (S.C. Code Regs. 61-19 § 903; checked October 29, 2026).

  • Notary: the state's request form must be signed by the next of kin in front of a notary, and the funeral director signs to accept responsibility.
  • Exceptions: taking remains out of a receiving vault for final burial is not a disinterment. Deaths before 1915 do not need a permit.

South Carolina burial regulators and county help

The South Carolina Board of Funeral Service (803-896-4429) issues funeral home licenses and investigates complaints. Perpetual care cemeteries answer to the Perpetual Care Cemetery Board, and church or family cemeteries set their own rules.

The Funeral Consumers Alliance names nine states where a funeral director must handle the arrangements, and South Carolina is not one of them, so a family can deal directly with the cemetery about the plot, the opening and the container.

The burial goes ahead once the permit is issued. If the family decides on cremation instead, South Carolina requires a 24-hour wait, next-of-kin authorization and a coroner's permit (S.C. Code § 32-8-340).

County help

South Carolina has no state funeral payment. An unclaimed body goes to the county coroner if the anatomical board declines it; the estate pays first and the county only after that, and county councils may fund indigent burial as a public purpose (S.C. Code § 17-5-570; S.C. Code § 4-9-130). Social Security's $255 lump sum, paid to an eligible spouse or child, is a small fraction of the $3,000 plot estimate.

Veterans

Beaufort National Cemetery (843-524-3925), Florence National Cemetery Annex (843-669-8783), Fort Jackson National Cemetery in Columbia (803-699-2246) and the M.J. Dolly Cooper Veterans Cemetery in Anderson (864-332-8022) bury eligible veterans with the plot and marker free (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

South Carolina burial steps

  1. Get the cemetery's written list of charges as well as the funeral home's price list.
  2. Ask whether you can bring a headstone or vault from another seller; § 40-8-100(F) says the cemetery cannot block one that meets its rules.
  3. If no one can pay, ask the county coroner what the county covers.
  4. For a veteran, ask Fort Jackson or another veterans cemetery about eligibility before buying a plot.
  5. Complaints about a funeral home go to the Board at 803-896-4429.
