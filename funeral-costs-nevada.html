Funeral Costs in Nevada: What Families Pay in 2026

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Sources: NFDA, FCA, FTC Funeral Rule Region: West

Nevada funeral pricing tells a story about Las Vegas more than it does about Nevada. Palm Mortuary, founded in 1926 and now operating six Southern Nevada locations including the enormous Palm Eastern on Eastern Avenue, handles roughly half of all Clark County arrangements — a concentration you do not see in most states. Davis Funeral Homes, the Simple Tribute Mortuary direct-cremation brand, and Reno's Walton's Funerals & Cremations round out the rest. Nevada's 72% cremation rate is among the highest in the country, and the Las Vegas market has become one of the fiercest direct-cremation price wars anywhere, with flat-rate $795-$995 offerings now common. A traditional funeral still averages $8,000 statewide, but the gap between the cremation floor and the burial ceiling is wider here than nearly anywhere else.

The Palm Mortuary Effect and Las Vegas Market Concentration

Few American funeral markets are as concentrated as Clark County's. Palm Mortuary's six locations — Palm Eastern, Palm Northwest, Palm Downtown, Palm Cheyenne, and its Boulder Highway and Henderson chapels — reportedly handle a plurality of Southern Nevada deaths, and the company also owns four of the major Las Vegas cemeteries. That level of vertical integration lets Palm bundle service, casket, plot, and vault in ways smaller competitors cannot. The counterweight is a distinctive Las Vegas direct-cremation segment: firms like Simple Tribute Mortuary, Affordable Cremation & Burial, and Heritage Cremation Society advertise flat-rate direct cremation at $795-$1,095, pulling Nevada's direct-cremation average well below the statewide $1,800 figure for families who shop aggressively.

Las Vegas also handles a disproportionate share of out-of-state deaths — retirees who moved from California, Illinois, or New York but want to be buried back home. That shipping volume (typically $2,800-$4,500 to get remains to a distant funeral home, embalmed and in an approved shipping container) is a line item Nevada providers know how to price aggressively when asked.

Nevada Funeral and Cemetery Services Board — The Unified Regulator

Nevada is one of the few states where a single board — the Nevada State Funeral and Cemetery Services Board — regulates funeral homes, crematories, and cemeteries under one statute, NRS Chapter 642. This unified structure matters because cemetery pricing disputes and funeral home complaints go through the same body, unlike states where you have to file with two separate agencies. The Board requires preneed contracts to place 100% of the principal in trust and publishes disciplinary orders at funeral.nv.gov. One notable Nevada feature: the state has no mandatory waiting period before cremation — a decedent can be cremated immediately upon authorization and permit issuance, which helps families coordinating out-of-state memorials.

Nevada is also one of the few states that specifically licenses "direct disposition establishments" — crematory-only operations without chapels or embalming facilities — which is why flat-rate $795-$995 direct cremation is possible here and not in, say, New Jersey. The regulatory structure encourages the low-end competition.

Clark County Social Services and Nevada's Indigent Burial Program

Nevada does not operate a single statewide burial assistance program. Clark County Social Services administers an indigent burial and cremation program for decedents whose estates cannot cover disposition — typically covering a basic cremation, urn, and disposition of ashes at Palm Memorial Park's indigent section or Woodlawn Cemetery. Applications go through Clark County Social Services within a limited window after death. Washoe County offers a parallel program run through the Medical Examiner's office. For Medicaid recipients, Nevada allows up to $1,500 in a burial fund plus reasonable value in a prepaid irrevocable burial contract without affecting eligibility. Veterans should first check eligibility at the Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City or the Northern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Fernley — both offer no-cost burial for eligible veterans and qualifying family members, a benefit worth $6,000+.

Las Vegas Cemetery Landscape and the Out-of-State Transport Market

Palm Memorial Park, Bunkers Memory Gardens, Davis Memorial Park, and Woodlawn Cemetery are the principal Las Vegas burial grounds, with plots in the $3,800-$8,500 band for standard single graves. Clark County operates Woodlawn and a smaller indigent section; Paradise Memorial Gardens rounds out the mid-tier. Reno's Mountain View Cemetery, Sierra Memorial Gardens, and Our Mother of Sorrows Catholic Cemetery serve Northern Nevada. Across the rural state, small-town cemeteries in Ely, Winnemucca, Tonopah, and Pahrump typically price plots at $400-$1,500 — a meaningful saving for families with rural ties.

Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City, dedicated in 1990, holds more than 60,000 veterans and spouses; Northern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Fernley opened in 1997. Both provide no-cost burial, vault, marker, and perpetual care for eligible veterans — the single largest line-item saving available to qualifying Nevada families.

Small line items that become big ones

Many Nevada families are surprised by charges that appear after the initial quote. Here are the most common hidden funeral fees to watch for:

  • Casket handling fee — Some funeral homes add a surcharge for caskets purchased elsewhere, despite this being illegal under the FTC Funeral Rule
  • Mandatory embalming — Funeral homes may claim embalming is required. In Nevada, it is almost never legally required. Know your rights
  • Cemetery "perpetual care" fees — An ongoing maintenance charge, typically $200–$1,000, added on top of the plot price
  • Vault/liner surcharge — Cemeteries may require a specific vault brand or type, inflating costs by $500–$2,000+
  • Documentation and filing fees — Death certificates, permits, and filing fees can add $200–$600
  • Weekend/holiday premium — Services held outside business hours may carry a 15–25% surcharge
  • "Package" markups — Bundled packages often include services you don't need. Always compare line-by-line. What funeral homes don't tell you

Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule

Know Your Rights Under the FTC Funeral Rule Every funeral home in Nevada must comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule, which protects consumers nationwide. You have the right to: receive an itemized General Price List before making any decisions; choose only the services and products you want; purchase a casket or urn from a third party without penalty; decline embalming unless required by state law for specific circumstances; and receive a written estimate before services are performed.

If you believe a funeral home in Nevada has violated these rights, you can file a complaint with the FTC at ftc.gov or contact the Funeral Consumers Alliance for guidance. Your state attorney general's office can also assist with consumer protection complaints.

Shipping Out of Nevada: The Snowbird Death Logistics Premium

A meaningful fraction of Nevada deaths involve decedents who moved to Las Vegas or Henderson in retirement but intended to be buried in family plots in the Midwest, East Coast, or Southern California. Nevada funeral homes price this "ship-out" service as a specialized line item — typically $2,800-$4,500 for embalming, documentation, TSA-approved air tray and Ziegler case, and delivery to the airline. Families who don't know this workflow sometimes end up paying for a full Nevada funeral plus the full receiving-state funeral — a $14,000+ double-billing situation. The cleaner approach is to have Nevada handle only preparation and transport, and hold all services in the destination state.

Questions From Families

What is the average price of a funeral in Nevada right now?

A traditional Nevada funeral averages $8,000, slightly above the $7,848 US median. Cremation with a service runs about $6,200; direct cremation starts at $1,800 at traditional firms but Las Vegas flat-rate specialists like Simple Tribute, Affordable Cremation & Burial, and Heritage Cremation Society advertise $795–$1,095. Palm Memorial Park and Bunkers Memory Gardens price plots $3,800–$8,500.

Is cremation cheaper than burial in Nevada?

Dramatically. Direct cremation as low as $795 in Las Vegas versus an $8,000 traditional funeral plus a $3,800 plot leaves Nevada with one of the largest cremation-burial price gaps in the country. The state's 72% cremation rate is among the highest nationally. Palm Mortuary's market share in Clark County has pushed traditional-service pricing up while simultaneously fueling a ferocious direct-cremation price war at the low end.

How do I keep funeral costs down in Nevada?

Las Vegas is the most competitive direct-cremation market in the country — firms like Simple Tribute Mortuary and Affordable Cremation & Burial quote $795–$995 flat rate. Eligible veterans should use Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Boulder City or Northern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Fernley for no-cost burial — a $6,000+ saving. Clark County Social Services administers indigent burial/cremation for qualifying decedents. Rural-county cemeteries in Ely, Tonopah, Winnemucca, and Pahrump price plots at $400–$1,500. If shipping the decedent to another state, ask Nevada to handle preparation and transport only — don't pay for a full Nevada funeral plus a full receiving-state funeral.

Does Nevada require embalming?

No. Nevada does not require embalming in routine circumstances. Refrigeration is accepted, and Nevada uniquely allows immediate cremation upon authorization with no mandatory waiting period. Embalming is typically required only for out-of-state air transport — a separate scenario from local disposition.

Is Nevada cheaper or more expensive than the US average for funerals?

Nevada's $8,000 traditional average sits just above the $7,848 US median and well below California ($10,600), Hawaii ($11,000), or Washington ($8,800). Clark County (Las Vegas, Henderson) prices above the state average; Washoe County is similar; rural counties run 10–15% below. Direct cremation in Las Vegas is as cheap as anywhere in the country.

Which funeral option costs the least in Nevada?

Direct cremation at $795–$1,095 through Las Vegas specialists is the commercial floor — among the cheapest in the US. For eligible veterans, Southern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Boulder City or Northern Nevada Veterans Memorial Cemetery at Fernley burial is no-cost. Clark County Social Services covers basic indigent disposition for qualifying decedents.

How can I find affordable funeral homes in Nevada?

Pull at least three GPLs: Palm Mortuary or Davis Funeral Homes for a traditional-service quote, a direct-cremation specialist (Simple Tribute Mortuary, Affordable Cremation & Burial, Heritage Cremation Society) for the floor, and a smaller independent for a middle comparison. The Nevada State Funeral and Cemetery Services Board publishes disciplinary history at funeral.nv.gov. Funeral Consumers Alliance of Southern Nevada maintains price surveys for the Las Vegas metro.
