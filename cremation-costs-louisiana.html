Cremation Costs in Louisiana (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Cremation Rate: 35%

Louisiana's 35% cremation rate is the lowest in this series and among the lowest in the country, a number that tracks directly back to the state's deep Catholic tradition of above-ground tomb burial — the tiered wall vaults of St. Louis No. 1 in New Orleans, the family tombs at Metairie Cemetery, the Creole and Cajun burial customs in Lafayette and the River Parishes. The $1,700 direct-cremation average is held down by Lake Lawn Metairie Funeral Home, Greenwood Funeral Home and Cemetery on City Park Avenue, Rabenhorst Funeral Homes in Baton Rouge, and Martinez Funeral Home in Lafayette. Louisiana is also one of the few states that legally operates under a civil-law tradition, which shapes how the statutory authorization hierarchy works.

Lake Lawn, Rabenhorst, and the regional map

Lake Lawn Metairie Funeral Home and Cemetery (the company behind Metairie Cemetery's distinctive cast-iron gates and lyre-shaped drive) serves the Greater New Orleans metro. Greenwood Funeral Home on City Park Avenue has operated since 1852 and includes a cemetery section with above-ground vaults and an in-house crematory. Tharp-Sontheimer-Tharp and Leitz-Eagan cover specific Catholic and Jewish communities, respectively. In Baton Rouge, Rabenhorst Funeral Homes (four locations including East and Ascension) is the dominant operator; Welsh Funeral Home serves north Baton Rouge. Lafayette's market is led by Martin & Castille and Delhomme Funeral Home. Shreveport arrangements route through Rose-Neath Funeral Homes, Boone Funeral Home, and Centuries Memorial. The small direct-cremation-only segment includes Neptune Society Louisiana.

Louisiana's 24-hour wait and parish coroner system

Louisiana Revised Statutes Title 8 imposes a mandatory 24-hour waiting period between death and cremation — shorter than most neighboring states. Cremation authorization must be signed by the legal next of kin under the civil-law-influenced hierarchy. Unlike most states, Louisiana operates under a parish coroner system rather than a unified state medical examiner; each of the 64 parishes has its own coroner, typically a licensed physician. Orleans Parish, Jefferson Parish, East Baton Rouge Parish, and Lafayette Parish coroners handle most metro deaths and typically issue the cremation release within 24 to 48 hours. Rural parishes can take longer. The Louisiana Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors at lsbefd.la.gov licenses funeral establishments and crematories.

Bayou scattering, Gulf scattering, and above-ground tombs

Louisiana's geography of scattering is unusual. The Mississippi, Atchafalaya, Red, and Sabine Rivers are commonly used with no specific permit required. Gulf of Mexico scattering three nautical miles offshore — typically from Grand Isle, Fourchon, or Cameron charters at $400 to $1,500 — is permitted under EPA rules with 30-day written notice. Barataria Preserve in Jean Lafitte National Historical Park allows scattering with NPS superintendent approval. Kisatchie National Forest requires ranger-district permission. Louisiana State Parks require written approval. For families preferring the traditional Louisiana option, cremation urns can be placed in an above-ground family tomb at any of the state's historic Catholic cemeteries — a hybrid approach that honors both cremation and the parish-tomb tradition. Louisiana Veterans Cemeteries in Jackson, Leesville, Keithville, and Slidell offer free inurnment.

Louisiana-specific cost levers

  • Metro pricing varies sharply by parish. New Orleans metro providers often run at the top of the Louisiana range; Shreveport, Lake Charles, and Monroe providers typically fall well below the state average for direct cremation.
  • The Louisiana Cemetery Board at cemeteryboard.louisiana.gov separately regulates perpetual-care cemeteries — worth a check before committing to pre-need at a specific cemetery.
  • Parish-level indigent-cremation programs run through each parish coroner's office or human services department. Orleans Parish, East Baton Rouge Parish, and Caddo Parish all operate programs for residents below income thresholds.
  • Veterans qualify for one of Louisiana's four state Veterans Cemeteries or out-of-state national cemeteries, all offering free inurnment and a government-issued marker.

Answers to Common Questions

What is the typical price of cremation in Louisiana?

Direct cremation in Louisiana averages $1,700; a full-service cremation with viewing and ceremony runs $5,400. New Orleans metro providers like Lake Lawn Metairie and Greenwood typically price at the upper end of the range. Shreveport, Monroe, and Lake Charles providers usually fall below the state average. Flat-rate direct cremation through Neptune Society Louisiana can run below $1,500. Urns, memorial services, and inurnment fees add $50 to $3,000.

What types of cremation are available in Louisiana?

Families in Louisiana can choose from three main types: direct cremation ($1,700) — the most affordable option with no viewing or ceremony; cremation with memorial service (ceremony held after cremation, often more flexible on timing); and traditional cremation ($5,400) — a full funeral service before cremation. Each option can be customized to fit your family's needs and budget.

Does Louisiana have specific rules for cremation?

Louisiana Revised Statutes Title 8 requires a 24-hour waiting period between death and cremation — shorter than most neighboring states. Louisiana operates under a parish coroner system rather than a state medical examiner, so the specific coroner's office issuing the cremation release varies by parish. Cremation authorization must be signed by the legal next of kin under Louisiana's civil-law-influenced hierarchy. The Louisiana Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors at lsbefd.la.gov licenses funeral establishments and crematories.

Where are ashes allowed to be scattered in Louisiana?

The Mississippi, Atchafalaya, Red, and Sabine Rivers are all commonly used for scattering with no specific permit. Gulf of Mexico scattering three nautical miles offshore — from Grand Isle, Fourchon, or Cameron — requires EPA notification within 30 days. Barataria Preserve and Kisatchie National Forest require advance written permission. Louisiana families can also place cremation urns in an above-ground family tomb at any historic Catholic cemetery — a hybrid that honors both cremation and the parish-tomb tradition. Private property scattering is permitted with the landowner's consent.

Why is cremation growing in Louisiana?

Louisiana's 35% cremation rate is among the lowest in the nation, reflecting the state's deep Catholic tradition of above-ground tomb burial — the tiered wall vaults of St. Louis No. 1, the family tombs at Metairie Cemetery, and Creole and Cajun customs across Acadiana. Growth is driven by the $3,700 gap between direct cremation and full-service cremation, younger families in New Orleans and Baton Rouge shifting away from traditional tomb burial, and cost pressure from post-Katrina cemetery-maintenance costs.

How quickly can cremation be completed in Louisiana?

From death to ashes typically takes 5 to 10 business days in Louisiana. The mandatory 24-hour waiting period is the shortest in the region, but the parish coroner release — required before cremation — adds variable time depending on parish. Orleans, Jefferson, East Baton Rouge, and Lafayette Parish coroners typically clear within 24-48 hours; rural parishes can take longer. The cremation itself runs 2-3 hours, with processing adding 1-3 business days.

Is it possible to have a ceremony before cremation in Louisiana?

Yes. Full-service cremation in Louisiana averages $5,400 and includes visitation, a church or chapel service, and cremation instead of burial. Lake Lawn Metairie, Rabenhorst, Martin & Castille, and Rose-Neath all offer this format. Post-cremation memorial services are also common; families often arrange a small graveside or tomb-side committal when the urn is placed in a family tomb — a uniquely Louisiana hybrid.
