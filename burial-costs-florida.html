Burial Costs in Florida (2026)

Reviewed: February 2026 Region: South

Florida's burial market is one of the largest and most dynamic in the nation, shaped by the state's massive retiree population, extraordinary cultural diversity, and unique environmental challenges. From the sprawling memorial parks of South Florida — where Cuban, Haitian, and Caribbean burial traditions influence every aspect of the funeral — to the small-town cemeteries of the Panhandle and the historic burial grounds of St. Augustine, Florida families navigate a wide spectrum of burial costs and options. The state's low water table, particularly in South Florida, creates special challenges for in-ground burial and has made above-ground entombment a popular alternative in many communities.

North Florida and the Panhandle are considerably more affordable, with pricing similar to neighboring Southern states.

What Drives Burial Costs in Florida

Plot Prices by Area

South Florida (Miami, Fort Lauderdale, West Palm Beach): $3,000-$10,000+ per plot. Orlando/Central Florida: $2,000-$5,500. Tampa/St. Petersburg: $2,000-$5,000. Jacksonville: $1,500-$4,000. Panhandle (Pensacola, Tallahassee): $1,000-$3,000. The enormous gap between South Florida and the Panhandle reflects the state's dramatic regional cost-of-living differences.

Water Table and Environmental Challenges

Florida's high water table — particularly in South Florida, coastal areas, and near the Everglades — creates unique burial challenges. In some areas, graves can encounter groundwater at just a few feet below the surface. This makes above-ground mausoleum entombment a practical and popular alternative, particularly in the Miami-Dade area. Where in-ground burial is available, cemeteries often require heavy-duty sealed vaults to protect against water intrusion.

Cultural Burial Traditions

Florida's diversity brings a rich tapestry of burial customs. Cuban and Caribbean families in South Florida often emphasize elaborate viewings and multi-day mourning periods. Jewish communities follow traditions of prompt burial and simple pine caskets. Southern Baptist traditions predominate in North Florida and the Panhandle. This cultural diversity supports a wide range of funeral homes and cemeteries catering to specific communities.

Veterans Burial in Florida

Florida has one of the largest veteran populations in the country. The state is served by multiple national cemeteries including Barrancas National Cemetery (Pensacola), Florida National Cemetery (Bushnell), Jacksonville National Cemetery, Sarasota National Cemetery, South Florida National Cemetery, and St. Augustine National Cemetery.

Cemetery Types Available in Florida

  • Private memorial parks — The dominant model in Florida, from large SCI-operated cemeteries to independent parks.
  • Municipal cemeteries — Available in many Florida cities, often more affordable.
  • Catholic cemeteries — Archdioceses of Miami and other dioceses maintain extensive cemetery networks.
  • Veterans cemeteries — Multiple national cemeteries across the state. Learn about veteran burial benefits
  • Jewish cemeteries — Numerous options in South Florida serving the large Jewish population.
  • Mausoleum facilities — Especially popular in South Florida due to the high water table.

Casket and Vault Options in Florida

Florida's competitive funeral market offers a wide range of casket options. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, you can buy a casket from any retailer. Florida's large market means many online casket retailers have distribution centers in-state, allowing fast delivery and savings of 50-80%.

Consumer Rights for Burial in Florida

  • FTC Funeral Rule — Itemized pricing required. Choose only what you need.
  • Florida Board of Funeral, Cemetery, and Consumer Services — One of the most active state regulatory bodies, overseeing both funeral homes and cemeteries.
  • Embalming not required by Florida law when burial occurs within 24 hours or refrigeration is used.
  • Cemetery regulation — Florida has specific laws governing cemetery perpetual care funds, sales practices, and consumer disclosures.
  • Preneed contract protections — Florida requires that preneed funeral and cemetery contracts be backed by trust funds or insurance.

Green and Natural Burial in Florida

Green burial in Florida faces unique challenges due to the high water table, but options are growing. Several cemeteries now offer green burial sections, particularly in North and Central Florida where ground conditions are more favorable. Green burial eliminates embalming, uses biodegradable containers, and skips concrete vaults, saving 30-50%. Prairie Creek Conservation Cemetery and other natural burial providers offer eco-friendly options. Learn more about green burial options

Burial Options in Florida

  • Traditional burial — Full service with viewing, casket, and cemetery burial. The most common choice across Florida.
  • Direct burial — Burial without embalming, viewing, or ceremony. Saves $3,000 to $6,000 in Florida.
  • Green burial — Growing availability, especially in North and Central Florida. Learn about green burial
  • Mausoleum entombment — Popular in South Florida due to high water table. Prices range from $4,000 to $15,000+.
  • Lawn crypt burial — Pre-installed underground vaults, common at Florida memorial parks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Florida?

A traditional burial in Florida costs approximately $7,800 for the funeral service plus $3,600 for a cemetery plot. Total costs range from $14,640 to $20,580. South Florida is significantly more expensive than the Panhandle or North Florida.

Is a burial vault required in Florida?

Florida has no state law requiring vaults. Most cemeteries require them, and in areas with a high water table, sealed vaults may be mandatory to prevent water intrusion. Some green burial cemeteries waive vault requirements.

What are the cheapest burial options in Florida?

Direct burial in North Florida or the Panhandle is most affordable at $5,000-$8,000 total. Green burial saves 30-50%. Buying caskets online saves $1,000-$5,000. Florida's many veterans cemeteries provide free burial for eligible veterans.

Why is mausoleum entombment popular in Florida?

Florida's high water table, particularly in South Florida, makes in-ground burial challenging or impossible in some areas. Above-ground mausoleum entombment avoids these issues entirely. Many South Florida cemeteries offer indoor, climate-controlled mausoleums.

How many veterans cemeteries does Florida have?

Florida has six national cemeteries (Barrancas, Bushnell/Florida National, Jacksonville, Sarasota, South Florida, and St. Augustine) plus state veterans cemeteries. With one of the nation's largest veteran populations, these facilities provide free burial for eligible veterans and spouses. Learn more about veteran benefits
