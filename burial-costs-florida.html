Burial Costs in Florida (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 Region: South

Florida burial pricing in 2026 sits on top of one unusual fact: the state's water table. In low-elevation parts of Miami-Dade, Broward, and coastal Pinellas, a traditional in-ground burial may require a sealed vault and extra groundwater disclosure under Florida Department of Financial Services Division of Funeral, Cemetery and Consumer Services rules, while above-ground entombment at places like Woodlawn Park in Miami becomes the practical norm. A full Florida burial bill lands between $14,640 and $20,580 — but which side of that range a family sees has as much to do with geography and cemetery type as with the funeral home's General Price List.

Why the water table matters more in Florida than anywhere else

Florida's peninsular geology puts the water table within a few feet of the surface across much of South Florida and the Gulf coast. That creates two practical consequences for burial. First, sealed concrete or polymer vaults are effectively required at almost every cemetery south of Ocala — a grave liner alone will not hold a casket in place as groundwater rises. Second, several large cemeteries simply steer families toward above-ground entombment in mausoleum crypts. Woodlawn Park in Miami, Evergreen Cemetery in Jacksonville, and Greenwood Cemetery in Orlando all operate extensive mausoleum sections, and crypts can come in at or below the price of a plot-plus-vault combination once all the line items are added.

Florida's regulators — who watches what

The Florida Department of Financial Services Division of Funeral, Cemetery and Consumer Services licenses funeral directors, cemeteries, and pre-need contracts. The division publishes consumer complaints and takes disciplinary action — a check against a provider's name is a useful part of pre-need due diligence. Pre-need contracts in Florida are held in trust or funded by insurance; ask for the trust statement. Our FTC Funeral Rule guide explains the federal baseline, which applies on top of state rules.

Florida indigent assistance: The Department of Children and Families coordinates indigent burial programs at the county level, and Florida's Victim Compensation Program can reimburse up to $7,500 toward funeral and burial costs when death results from a covered crime. Both programs require paperwork submitted within specific windows.

Mausoleum versus in-ground: a quick numerical test

Families often assume above-ground costs more than in-ground. In Florida it frequently does not. Compare all-in:

  • Traditional in-ground (South Florida): plot $4,200 + sealed vault $1,680 + opening-and-closing $1,800 = $7,680 cemetery cost.
  • Mausoleum crypt (same cemetery): crypt $6,500-$8,500, opening-and-closing $800-$1,200, no vault line.

The ranges overlap. Always ask the cemetery for a crypt quote alongside the plot quote if mausoleum entombment is acceptable to the family.

Where Florida families actually save money

  1. Shop away from the beachfront. Inland cemeteries 20-30 miles from Miami, Fort Lauderdale, or Naples routinely price plots $1,500-$3,000 lower than coastal parks.
  2. Check Catholic diocese cemeteries. The Archdiocese of Miami and Diocese of St. Petersburg operate cemeteries with pricing below most commercial parks.
  3. Request the FTC-mandated General Price List from every funeral home before committing — service prices vary by $1,000-$2,000 inside the same county.
  4. Buy the casket online. Savings of $1,000-$2,000 are typical versus a mortuary's showroom.
  5. VA national cemeteries. Florida has four VA national cemeteries (Bushnell, Jacksonville, Lake Worth, Sarasota). Eligibility covers plot, opening-and-closing, and marker at no cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does burial cost in Florida?

A 2026 Florida burial runs $14,640 to $20,580 once the funeral service ($7,800), plot ($3,600), casket ($2,340), vault ($1,440), opening-and-closing ($1,800), and a marker ($1,000-$3,000) are stacked. Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, and coastal Collier County push toward the top of that band; the Panhandle and inland North Florida sit noticeably lower. The casket line is usually the fastest place to save — a third-party retailer purchase trims $1,000-$2,000.

Do I need a vault for burial in Florida?

Florida state law does not universally require a burial vault, but the state's high water table makes most South Florida and Gulf-coast cemeteries insist on a sealed vault rather than a grave liner. The Florida Department of Financial Services Division of Funeral, Cemetery and Consumer Services enforces groundwater-related disclosures on cemetery contracts, and in practice, the closer you get to sea level the more likely the sealed vault becomes mandatory. Inland and North Florida cemeteries more often accept a concrete liner.

What is the most affordable way to bury someone in Florida?

The shortest path to lower Florida burial costs is direct burial through an independent funeral home paired with an inland or Catholic-diocese cemetery. Green burial sections at certified grounds in North Florida and parts of the Panhandle skip the vault line entirely. Eligible veterans should check the four Florida VA national cemeteries — Bushnell, Jacksonville, Lake Worth, and Sarasota — where plot, opening-and-closing, and marker are provided at no cost.

How do I compare cemetery costs in Florida?

Florida cemeteries are regulated by the Division of Funeral, Cemetery and Consumer Services but are not covered by the FTC Funeral Rule's itemization requirement. You have to request a written, itemized price sheet covering plot, opening-and-closing, vault policy, perpetual care, and any residency or denominational restrictions. At places with mausoleum sections like Woodlawn Park Miami, Evergreen Jacksonville, and Greenwood Orlando, ask for a crypt quote alongside the plot quote — entombment sometimes undercuts in-ground once the vault is included.

Once I add every line item, what does burial actually cost in Florida?

The full 2026 Florida stack — funeral service $7,800, plot $3,600, casket $2,340, vault $1,440, opening-and-closing $1,800, marker $1,000-$3,000 — lands at $14,640 to $20,580. Families in South Florida almost always see the upper range; those in North Florida and the Panhandle typically come in under $17,000 when they shop the cemetery and funeral home separately.

Does Florida have green burial cemeteries?

Yes, but unevenly. Prairie Creek Conservation Cemetery in Gainesville is one of the country's best-known conservation burial grounds, and a growing number of Florida cemeteries maintain green burial sections with biodegradable containers and no vault requirement. Coverage is thinner in far South Florida, where water-table rules effectively require a vault even for natural burials. Our green burial guide covers how to vet a cemetery's green certification.
