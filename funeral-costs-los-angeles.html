Funeral Costs in Los Angeles, California (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Updated: April 2026 State: California

Los Angeles County has more than 400 licensed funeral establishments, and the 2026 headline number — $13,250 for a full traditional service — hides a spread that can exceed $8,000 between the lowest-cost direct-cremation specialists and the Westside chapels that handle celebrity and entertainment-industry services. Forest Lawn Memorial-Parks & Mortuaries (six LA-area locations including Glendale, Hollywood Hills, and Covina Hills), Rose Hills Memorial Park & Mortuary in Whittier (the largest cemetery by acreage in North America), Pierce Brothers Westwood Village, Hillside Memorial Park in Culver City, and Angelus-Rosedale in Mid-City handle the bulk of traditional volume. On the low-cost end, Neptune Society of Los Angeles, Cremation Society of Los Angeles, Valley Funeral Home & Crematory, and Tulip Cremation publish direct-cremation rates that undercut the $2,750 metro average by $800-$1,500. Plot scarcity is the hardest constraint: developed cemetery land in Los Angeles County is effectively closed, and new sections at Rose Hills now begin at $6,500 and push past $20,000 for feature lots.

What Drives LA's 25 Percent Premium

Los Angeles County funeral pricing sits 25 percent above California's state line, and the gap is stable year over year. Westside facilities — Pierce Brothers Westwood, Hillside Memorial Park, Mount Sinai Memorial Park in Hollywood Hills — carry the highest basic services fees in the country outside Manhattan, routinely $3,800-$5,200. South LA and Eastside providers including Angelus Funeral Home on Crenshaw (serving the historic Black community since 1925) and Cunningham & O'Connor in San Pedro run meaningfully lower, often $2,400-$3,200 for comparable services. The FTC Funeral Rule applies equally in all 88 LA County municipalities: any funeral home must deliver its General Price List on request, and cannot refuse a third-party casket or charge a handling fee for accepting one. Pulling GPLs from three geographically distinct providers — one Westside, one South LA, one Valley — typically reveals a $2,500-$4,000 spread.

The Plot Shortage at Forest Lawn and Rose Hills

Los Angeles County has not opened a new cemetery in decades. Hollywood Forever on Santa Monica Boulevard, Holy Cross in Culver City, Forest Lawn Glendale, Inglewood Park, and Rose Hills in Whittier are working with finite inventory. Pricing reflects scarcity: Forest Lawn Glendale plots now start near $7,500 and rise past $25,000 for view lots on the hillside sections; Hillside Memorial Park in Culver City — burial ground for Al Jolson, Jack Benny, and Aaron Spelling — prices single crypts from about $15,000. Rose Hills in Whittier, at 1,400 acres North America's largest cemetery by area, remains the comparatively affordable LA County option with plots from $4,800. Green burial is available at Joshua Tree Memorial Park (about two hours east) for families willing to drive, and natural-burial conservation cemeteries are expanding in Ventura and Santa Barbara counties as LA inventory tightens further.

Line Items Los Angeles Families Miss on the First Read

  • Basic services fee — non-declinable. Runs from $2,100 at a San Fernando Valley independent to $5,000+ at a Westside chapel.
  • County permit for disposition — required by the LA County Department of Public Health Vital Records. $20-$25 per permit, often passed through with a modest handling fee.
  • Traffic-escort fees — funeral processions in Los Angeles often require paid off-duty LAPD or Sheriff's escorts. $400-$900 depending on distance and time of day.
  • Cemetery endowment care — separate from the plot price, often 15-25 percent of plot cost, and California law mandates it be deposited into an endowment-care trust.
  • Cash advance items — obituaries in the Los Angeles Times or community papers like La Opinión ($300-$900), certified death certificates ($24 each from LA County Registrar-Recorder), clergy honoraria, sometimes floral arrangements.
  • International shipment — a large share of LA families ship remains to Mexico, the Philippines, Korea, Armenia, or Iran. Budget $4,000-$8,500 for international repatriation including consular documentation and airline freight.

LA County Indigent Disposition and Crime Victim Assistance

When a resident dies in Los Angeles County with no known next of kin or no family resources, the case goes to the LA County Department of Medical Examiner-Coroner's Decedent Affairs division, which arranges cremation at county expense and stores remains at the Los Angeles County crematorium. Unclaimed remains are eventually interred at the LA County Cemetery in Boyle Heights. For families who can demonstrate indigence but want to conduct their own service, the LA County Department of Public Social Services can sometimes contribute a modest burial or cremation allowance; eligibility is narrow. California's Victims of Crime program (CalVCB) covers up to $7,500 in funeral expenses when death results from a violent crime — meaningfully more than most states. The Social Security Administration's $255 lump-sum death benefit applies statewide. For veterans, Los Angeles National Cemetery in Westwood (established 1889) is no longer accepting full-casket burials, but offers columbarium and in-ground cremation inurnment; Riverside National Cemetery, about an hour east, still accepts casketed burials at no cost. The Funeral Consumers Alliance of the Los Angeles Area maintains one of the country's oldest price-survey committees, with periodic updates for metro-area providers.

Your Rights in Los Angeles

FTC Funeral Rule Protection Every funeral home in Los Angeles must provide an itemized General Price List, let you choose only the services you want, and accept caskets purchased elsewhere. You are never required to buy a package. Read the full FTC Funeral Rule guide.

Regional Context: Funeral Pricing in the West

Among the major California metros, Los Angeles sits between San Diego (slightly cheaper) and the Bay Area (meaningfully more expensive, with San Francisco and San Jose both clearing $13,700 for a traditional service). The $2,500-$4,000 spread inside LA County itself is wider than the spread between LA and most other West Coast metros, so the most consequential comparison for a local family is not geographic but provider-level. Rose Hills in Whittier, Forest Lawn Covina Hills, and San Fernando Mission Cemetery tend to anchor the more affordable end of full-service pricing; Hillside, Mount Sinai Hollywood Hills, and Pierce Brothers Westwood anchor the top.

California-Specific Compliance Items to Check

California regulates funeral and cemetery pricing through the Cemetery and Funeral Bureau under the Department of Consumer Affairs. Beyond the federal FTC Funeral Rule, California imposes additional protections: pre-need contracts must be fully trusted (100 percent of the principal, not a fraction), cemetery endowment care funds must be held in a separate trust and reported annually, and GPLs must be printed and delivered, not merely quoted verbally. If any LA County provider refuses a written GPL, adds a casket-handling fee, or claims California law requires embalming for a viewing (it does not, except in narrow circumstances under Title 16 regulations), those are violations you can report to the Cemetery and Funeral Bureau directly. Crematories are separately licensed under California Business and Professions Code, and any family can request a crematory license number before signing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical funeral price in Los Angeles?

Los Angeles County averages $13,250 for a traditional service, $9,750 for cremation with a service, $2,750 for direct cremation, and $6,500 for a cemetery plot. The realistic bracket runs from $1,195 (Tulip Cremation or Neptune Society direct cremation) through about $23,700 for a full traditional burial at a Forest Lawn or Hillside property with a premium plot and monument. International shipment adds $4,000-$8,500.

What is Los Angeles's cremation-versus-burial split?

LA County cremation runs close to 65 percent, tracking California's statewide figure. Cost is a primary driver — direct cremation through Neptune Society or Cremation Society of Los Angeles averages $1,500-$2,100 against $13,250 plus $6,500 for full traditional burial — but land scarcity matters equally. Cemetery inventory in LA County is functionally fixed, so plot costs only climb. Memorial services held after cremation, often at a family home, park, church, or community center, are the dominant hybrid.

How do Los Angeles funeral costs compare to the national average?

Los Angeles sits well above the national traditional-funeral average of $7,848 — roughly 70 percent higher. It's more expensive than San Diego and Sacramento, comparable to San Jose, and slightly below San Francisco. The biggest lever isn't geographic: the spread inside LA County itself — between a Westside chapel and a Valley or South LA independent — is $2,500-$4,000 on comparable services. That makes the GPL comparison unusually high-leverage in the LA market.

How do I compare funeral quotes in Los Angeles?

The FTC Funeral Rule requires every LA County funeral home to hand you an itemized General Price List on request — no appointment, no commitment, no fee. California additionally requires printed GPLs, not verbal quotes. Call at least one Westside provider (Pierce Brothers Westwood, Hillside Memorial Park, Mount Sinai Hollywood Hills), one South LA or Eastside provider (Angelus on Crenshaw, Cunningham & O'Connor in San Pedro), one Valley provider, and one direct-cremation specialist (Neptune Society, Tulip Cremation, Cremation Society of Los Angeles). The Funeral Consumers Alliance of the Los Angeles Area publishes metro price surveys.

What does direct cremation include in Los Angeles?

Direct cremation in LA covers transport from the place of death, the cremation itself at a California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau-licensed crematory, and return of the cremated remains in a temporary container. No embalming, no viewing, no service at the funeral home. Tulip Cremation, Neptune Society of Los Angeles, Cremation Society of Los Angeles, and Valley Funeral Home & Crematory publish rates starting near $1,195-$1,800 — meaningfully below the $2,750 metro average. Memorial services afterward can happen at any venue.

Are there affordable funeral options in Los Angeles?

The floor is direct cremation at $1,195 through Tulip Cremation or comparable providers. Other paths: direct burial without viewing at Rose Hills in Whittier (LA County's most affordable full-service cemetery), a memorial-only service separated from disposition, LA County Department of Medical Examiner-Coroner's indigent cremation program for qualifying unclaimed cases, and CalVCB funding up to $7,500 for crime-related deaths. Veterans qualify for no-cost burial at Riverside National Cemetery. Private assistance networks include Catholic Charities of Los Angeles and the Armenian, Korean, Mexican, and Iranian community funds that maintain emergency funeral assistance.
