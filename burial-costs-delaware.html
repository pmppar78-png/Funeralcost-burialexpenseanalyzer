Burial Costs in Delaware (2026)

By: Paul Paradis, Editor Last updated: October 29, 2026 Last reviewed: October 30, 2026 What changed: Delaware's burial guide rewritten in full, including the oversight and help section. Region: Northeast

Three figures set the price of a Delaware burial in 2026: about $8,600 for the funeral home's service, about $4,200 for a cemetery plot, and $500 to $5,000 for a headstone. Once a casket, a vault and the opening and closing of the grave are added, a Delaware family should plan on $19,660 to $24,160 in total, and the cemetery chosen can shift that by more than a thousand dollars.

How to Reduce Burial Costs in Delaware

Here is where a Delaware burial usually has room to come down:

  • Ask for line-item prices, not packages. Every Delaware funeral home has to give you a General Price List. Cemeteries do not, but most will hand over an itemized price sheet if you ask for that specifically.
  • Check for a winter charge. Some cemeteries in the region add a frozen-ground fee from December through March or keep the casket in a receiving vault until spring, so ask whether a Delaware cemetery does either.
  • Include church and town cemeteries. Delaware's denominational, municipal and other nonprofit cemeteries are worth adding to the shortlist next to the commercial memorial parks.
  • Look beyond Wilmington. Driving 20 to 30 minutes from Wilmington into a less costly county can bring the plot price down a good deal while keeping the grave close enough to visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a burial cost in Delaware?

Plan on $19,660 to $24,160. The pieces, by our estimate: about $8,600 for the funeral home's service, $4,200 for the plot, roughly $2,580 for a casket, $1,680 for a vault and $2,100 to open and close the grave, with a headstone from $500 to $5,000.

Do Delaware cemeteries require a burial vault?

Most do, through their own rules rather than any Delaware law; the vault or liner keeps the grave from settling. Prices run from about $800 to $10,000, so ask the cemetery whether a basic liner is enough.

What lowers the price of a Delaware burial?

Three things help most. Choose direct (immediate) burial, which leaves out embalming, a viewing and a service and is normally the cheapest burial a Delaware funeral home sells. Buy the casket elsewhere if it costs less; under the FTC Funeral Rule the funeral home cannot charge a fee for handling it. And for an eligible veteran, a VA national cemetery provides the grave, opening and closing, liner and marker at no cost.

Why do Delaware cemetery prices vary so much?

Every cemetery sets its own plot price, opening and closing fee and vault or liner rule. Because the FTC Funeral Rule covers funeral homes and not most cemeteries, a Delaware cemetery need not give you a price list, but asking for one in writing usually gets it.

Is anything charged beyond the Delaware burial estimate?

Yes. Cash advance items, such as certified copies of the death certificate, an obituary and flowers, are paid out by the funeral home and billed on top of the $19,660 to $24,160 Delaware estimate.

Is a green or natural burial an option in Delaware?

Yes, at some cemeteries. A green section uses a biodegradable container with no embalming and no vault, and it usually costs less than a conventional burial. Where no green ground is close by, some Delaware families choose a regular plot and simply skip embalming.

Delaware's five-day rule and the burial permit

Delaware expects a body to be buried, cremated, reduced or placed in a receiving vault within five days of death. Past 24 hours it must be embalmed, refrigerated or kept in a sealed casket, and refrigeration is capped at 15 days unless an investigation, religious practice or a wait for a national cemetery or burial at sea requires more (16 Del. Admin. Code 4602, §§ 4.1, 10.1, 11.1; checked October 29, 2026).

  • The permit: the funeral director prints and signs the burial-transit permit in the state's electronic system after filing the death record, and gives it to whoever runs the cemetery.
  • Missing paperwork: if the cemetery has not received the permit within 10 days after a burial, it must tell the State Registrar in Dover.
  • Depth: in any public or private burial ground, the top of the outer case must be at least 18 inches below the natural ground surface.

Delaware burial oversight and where to get help

Funeral homes handling burials in Delaware are licensed by the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation (302-744-4500). The Delaware Cemetery Board in the Division of Public Health keeps a cemetery registration and runs the Distressed Cemetery Fund, but it has no enforcement power (29 Del. C. §§ 7901A and 7904A); it refers consumer complaints elsewhere, including to the Department of Justice's Consumer Protection Unit (800-220-5424).

No funeral director is required for a Delaware burial; the Funeral Consumers Alliance does not list Delaware among the nine states that insist on one. A family can work out the plot, the grave opening and the container with the cemetery directly. Once the permit is issued the burial can proceed, and cremation has no set waiting period, though it too needs a permit (16 Del. C. § 3159).

Public help with burial costs

For someone who was on TANF, General Assistance or Medicaid, Delaware DSS pays up to $100 toward burial, sent to the funeral vendor, and a separate DSS service buries the indigent or unclaimed at public expense. (Source: 16 Del. Admin. Code 2000-2020; Delaware DSS indigent burial service.) The $255 Social Security payment goes only to a surviving spouse or child and is far below the $4,200 plot we estimate for Delaware.

A veteran's plot and marker in a national cemetery cost nothing. Delaware has two state veterans cemeteries: Delaware Veterans Memorial Cemetery in New Castle County (302-834-8046) and in Sussex County (302-934-5653) (source: VA National Cemetery Administration).

Steps to take in Delaware

  1. Price at least two cemeteries and two funeral homes; the plot, the grave opening and the marker are each priced on their own.
  2. Find out whether the cemetery requires an outer burial container before you buy one.
  3. Ask the Division of Social Services whether its indigent burial service applies before anyone buys a plot.
  4. For a veteran, ask the New Castle County or Sussex County veterans cemetery about eligibility before paying for a private grave.
  5. If a funeral home misleads you on burial prices, call 302-744-4500; there is no charge for the complaint.
